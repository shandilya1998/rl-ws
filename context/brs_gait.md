## Reward function defects found (brs_base_env_cfg.py and mdp/rewards.py)

1. rew_foot_clearance target_height 0.12 is below the standing height of the Link6 body frame (about 0.124 m, the frame sits at the ankle, the sole extends 0.124 m below it), so the term is maximised by swinging the foot fast at ground level, it actively rewards the observed fast flat shuffle, and raising its weight from 2 to 20 amplifies the defect. The comment in the config assumes a 0.03 m foot radius geometry that belongs to TRON1, not to BRS. A correct target for 8 cm of sole clearance is about 0.20 in Link6 frame height.
2. feet_air_time (custom variant) pays (last_air_time - 0.2) only on first contact, clamped above at 0.3, at weight 500, so every exploratory short step costs up to -100 raw (-1.0 per step after dt scaling) while never stepping costs exactly zero, a textbook negative barrier that locks in the no lift local optimum. The IsaacLab biped variant feet_air_time_positive_biped is dense, non negative, and encodes single stance directly.
3. keep_ankle_pitch_zero_in_air returns its maximum value 1.0 whenever both feet are in contact (the weighted sum is empty), so at weight 1.0 it pays a small permanent bonus for keeping both feet planted.
4. feet_regulation uses base_height_target 1.1 as the exponential length scale, exp(-h/1.1) is essentially constant (about 0.9) over the whole 0 to 0.15 m foot height band, so the term penalises swing foot velocity almost as strongly as ground level foot velocity and fights the clearance and air time terms, the commented out original used 0.025 times the desired height as the scale, which is the correct order.
5. pen_flat_orientation even at the new weight -5 contributes at most about -0.05 per step at a 45 degree lean while the two tracking terms contribute up to +0.65 per step, the lean therefore buys more tracking reward than it costs in orientation penalty, the run that produced the video had it at -1.
6. There is no termination on orientation and low_height terminates only below 0.5 m, half the nominal height, so bent over and collapsed postures remain alive and keep collecting tracking reward, isaaclab.envs.mdp.terminations.bad_orientation exists and is already importable through the project mdp package.
7. The curriculum reduce_tracking_rewards_std drives the linear tracking std down to 0.09, an extremely sharp kernel that further raises the value of leaning into the command against a weak posture penalty.
8. No symmetry mechanism exists anywhere in the stack for this task, no mirror loss, no augmentation, no phase variable, and the commands are slow (lin_vel_x -0.1 to 0.5 before iteration 6000), slow commands are known to favour shuffling gaits.
9. GaitReward in mdp/rewards.py is a complete Walk These Ways style phase clock reward (force and velocity shaping against desired contact states from frequency, offset, duration parameters) but is unused and requires a gait parameter command term to drive it.
10. The vendored rsl_rl PPO at /ws/rsl_rl supports symmetry_cfg with use_data_augmentation and use_mirror_loss (Mittal et al. 2024 interface, RslRlSymmetryCfg in isaaclab_rl), so symmetry training is plumbing work only.

## DataLogger note

scripts/rsl_rl/play.py now logs feet contact forces and feet velocities through the module global FEET_LINK_NAMES, which defaults to "ankle_.*" (TRON1), any BRS evaluation must set it to "Link6[LR]".

Extended on 2026-07-20 with feet heights, so that the discriminating test of the ninth pass can actually be run. Two keys are written, feet_frame_heights, the body frame origin height that foot_clearance_reward reads, and feet_sole_clearances, the lowest world height of the sole points that foot_clearance_reward_v2 reads, computed by DataLogger._sole_clearance which carries the v2 batched transform verbatim. The sole table is a second module global SOLE_OFFSETS, defaulting to None, under which only the frame height is logged and the clearance key stays empty, so TRON1 and every existing caller are unaffected. The SD_BRS1 twelve point table is provided beside it as SD_BRS1_SOLE_OFFSETS and must be assigned to SOLE_OFFSETS together with FEET_LINK_NAMES = "Link6[LR]". Logging both heights is deliberate, their difference is the tilt that the frame height alone cannot distinguish from a lift, and the geometry was re-verified this pass, a level foot at frame height 0.204 measures exactly 0.080 of clearance, and a grounded foot measures 0.000 at pitches of 0, 20, 30, 40 and 45 degrees while its frame stands at 0.1240, 0.1690, 0.1860, 0.1973 and 0.2007 respectively, reproducing the fifth pass figures.

The signature to look for in the dump, restating the ninth pass test in terms of the logged keys, is a nonzero feet_contact_forces magnitude on a foot whose feet_sole_clearances sits near 0.08, which is forged contact through self collision since a genuinely grounded sole measures at most 0.7 mm, and nonzero force on both feet during the ballistic phases identified by base vertical velocity.

## Reward scale conversion basis and corrected reference ratios (2026-07-17, third pass)

A references section was added to ../plans/GAIT_STRATEGY.md as section 7, and verifying it against the live sources exposed three numerical errors in the earlier drafts of that document, all now corrected. The errors and the principle that resolves them are recorded here so they are not reintroduced.

The principle. RewardManager.compute evaluates value = func × weight × dt (IsaacLab/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/managers/reward_manager.py:144), so for any reward function saturating at 1 the configured weight is exactly the reward rate per second, and weights are directly comparable across configurations regardless of control rate. This task pays linear tracking at 50 per second, the IsaacLab G1 reference pays 1.0, and van Marum pays 0.15 plus 0.15 for its two velocity components, so G1 weights map onto this task by a factor of 50 and van Marum weights by a factor of about 167. Event terms that fire once rather than continuously (is_terminated) are exempt from the rate reading and must be converted through forfeited income, since for them the dt multiplication yields a raw penalty of weight × dt.

Error 1, corrected. Earlier drafts claimed the G1 config sets flat orientation at half its linear tracking weight. It does not, G1 sets flat_orientation_l2 to -1.0 (rough_env_cfg.py:135) against track_lin_vel_xy_yaw_frame_exp at 1.0 (rough_env_cfg.py:24), that is parity, and it is half the ANGULAR tracking weight of 2.0. The van Marum ratio is roll and pitch orientation 0.2 against combined linear velocity 0.3, that is two thirds. The implied bracket for pen_flat_orientation here is therefore -33 (van Marum) to -50 (G1 parity), not the -25 previously proposed, and the phase 1 table now recommends -35 as the conservative entry point. The section 2.3 claim that -5 is "a factor of four to ten below" the references is now stated as roughly seven to ten times below.

Error 2, corrected. The phase 1 table proposed is_terminated at -200 "as in G1", copying the literal reference weight across a 50x scale gap. At this task's dt of 0.01 that yields a raw penalty of 2.0, about three hundredths of a second of tracking income, effectively a no op. The correct conversion, G1's -200 at dt 0.02 is a raw penalty of 4.0 against G1's combined tracking rate of 3.0 per second, hence about 1.3 s of forfeited ideal income and about 6.7 percent of a perfect 20 s episode. Reproducing that here (combined tracking rate 65 per second, dt 0.01) needs a raw penalty near 85, hence weight about -8000, which cross checks at about 6 percent of a perfect episode. Recorded caveat, a -85 single step spike against a typical 0.65 per step reward may inflate value loss variance, fall back to -4000 if Loss/value_function degrades.

Error 3, corrected. The proposed joint_deviation_l1 weight of -2.5 did not follow from any reference, the G1 ratio is 0.1 against tracking 1.0 (rough_env_cfg.py:57), which maps to -5 here. Also corrected, the rew_no_fly rationale previously said the current 4.5 is "seven times weaker" than van Marum, the correct figure is about four times weaker, van Marum prices feet contact at 0.1 against 0.3 combined linear velocity, one third, which maps to about 16 here, and the proposal of 15 stands.

Verified as already correct and unchanged, feet_air_time_positive_biped at 12.5 (G1 ratio 0.25 × 50), pen_base_height at about -10 (van Marum 0.05/0.3 × 50 ≈ 8), and feet_slide at the existing -5 (G1 ratio 0.1 × 50), which the config already matches.

## Root cause of the shuffle, the tilted foot clearance exploit (2026-07-17, fifth pass, run 2026-07-17_07-49-02)

The run 2026-07-17_07-49-02 carries phase 0 plus most of phase 1 (clearance target 0.20 std 0.035, feet_air_time_positive_biped 12.5 threshold 0.4, no_fly 15, flat_orientation -35, low_height 0.65, keep_ankle require_airborne True, feet_regulation foot_radius 0.124 scale 0.03, lin_vel_x (-0.3, 0.8)). At iteration ~2500 the posture is fixed (pen_flat_orientation only -0.376 against a -35 weight implies about 6 degrees of lean) and tracking is fast and high, but the shuffle persists and rewards have plateaued. The root cause is now established and is a reward hacking exploit rather than an optimisation failure.

Useful identity for reading these plots, Episode_Reward/x = episode_sum / max_episode_length_s, and for a term held at value v with weight w over a full episode this equals v × w, so every logged Episode_Reward is a reward RATE per second and the logged numbers invert directly into physical quantities.

The decisive evidence, from /ws/context/artefacts/plots-latest at iteration ~2570.
1. rew_no_fly = -0.033 at weight 15 implies a mean no_fly value of -0.0022, and since no_fly = 1×single_contact - 5×no_contact, both single stance and flight are essentially zero, the robot is in DOUBLE SUPPORT about 99.9 percent of the time.
2. feet_air_time = 2e-5 against a theoretical max of 5.0, confirming single stance essentially never occurs, feet_air_time_positive_biped is gated on exactly one foot in contact so it pays nothing.
3. rew_keep_ankle_pitch_zero_in_air = 1e-4, the require_airborne gate added this pass is working correctly and is reporting that no foot is ever airborne.
4. rew_foot_clearance = 10.07 and still climbing, out of a theoretical max of 40 (it sums over both feet, 2 × 1.0 × 20). Substantial clearance reward is being earned while no foot ever leaves the ground.

Points 1 and 4 are only jointly possible if the clearance reward is being collected without lifting a foot. The mechanism, verified two independent ways. foot_clearance_reward measures asset.data.body_pos_w[Link6].z, the LINK FRAME origin height, and the Link6 frame sits at the ankle while the sole plate is 0.3156 m long and 0.19 m wide, so tilting the foot about the ankle raises the frame while the sole edge stays on the ground. Computed over the collision mesh Link6R.STL (47857 vertices, rotating the vertex cloud and taking the lowest point), the frame height while still touching the ground is 0.124 m flat, 0.170 m at 20 degrees of pitch, 0.186 m at 30 degrees, 0.197 m at 40 degrees, 0.201 m at 45 degrees, with a maximum of 0.2245 m at 48 degrees of pitch and -30 degrees of roll. The configured target_height of 0.20 m with std 0.035 is therefore hit at kernel 0.994 to 0.999, essentially the maximum, by a foot resting on its toe or heel edge at about 40 to 45 degrees. Independently, inverting the logs, feet_slide -3.36 at weight 5 gives a mean per foot contact speed of 0.336 m/s, and feet_regulation -0.0098 at weight 0.2 then implies a height gate of 0.217, hence h = frame_z - 0.124 = +0.046 m and a frame height of 0.170 m while in contact, which corresponds to about 20 degrees of foot pitch. A flat grounded foot would give h = 0 exactly, so h > 0 while in contact is direct log side proof that the feet are tilted.

The behaviour is therefore skating on tilted feet, the policy rocks both feet onto their edges to about 20 to 45 degrees, which reads as 0.17 to 0.20 m of "clearance" to the reward, slides them along the ground at about 0.34 m/s each to satisfy the tanh speed gate of the clearance term, and translates without ever entering single stance. It secures 87 percent of linear tracking (43.5 of 50) and 74 percent of angular tracking with no stepping at all.

Why the term worked on TRON1 and fails here. TRON1 has a point foot of radius 0.03, and a sphere's centre height equals its clearance under any orientation, so the link frame height is an exact proxy. The BRS sole foot breaks that assumption, pure tilt moves the frame by up to 0.10 m, comparable to the whole intended swing clearance, so the proxy cannot distinguish lifting from tilting. This is the same family as the foot_radius defect but deeper, it is not a wrong constant but a wrong measured quantity, and no choice of target_height repairs it, 0.12 rewarded ground sweeping and 0.20 rewards toe rocking.

Regression introduced by the feet_regulation change of the previous pass, recorded honestly. The height gate now releases exactly where the exploit lives, gate values on a grounded foot are 0.921 old versus 1.000 new when flat, but 0.884 old versus 0.216 new at the observed 20 degrees, 0.872 versus 0.127 at 30 degrees, and 0.864 versus 0.088 at 40 degrees, so the anti slide brake on a tilted grounded foot was weakened by 4x to 10x. The change is correct for a flat foot and wrong for a tilted one, for the same reason as the clearance term, it derives clearance from the frame height. Both terms need the true sole clearance, not the frame height.

The alternation machinery has been dead in every run to date, which is why air time and no fly read zero across all experiments and why no repair of the clearance measurement alone could induce stepping.

## ROOT CAUSE FOUND, permanent shank into foot self penetration in the URDF (2026-07-20, tenth pass, supersedes the forged foot to foot mechanism of the eighth and ninth passes)

The user's DataLogger plot (artefacts/plots-latest-2/clearance_and_forces.png, env 0 of run 2026-07-17_11-01-29) settled the mechanism and refuted the specific claim that the two feet press each other. The user correctly objected that no feet collision is visible in the videos, and they were right.

What the plot shows. Contact force magnitude on Link6R and Link6L never reaches zero at any point, sitting between roughly 2000 and 5000 N throughout, while sole clearance on the same feet oscillates cleanly between 0 and about 0.10 m against the 0.08 m target and body frame height oscillates between 0.13 and 0.25 m. Two features of the force are decisive beyond its being nonzero. First, Fz is NEGATIVE, between about -2000 and -5000 N, and ground contact can only push a foot up, never down. Second, the magnitude is 6.8 times the entire robot's weight PER FOOT, the URDF masses summing to 59.85 kg for a weight of 587 N. That is a penetration resolution force, not a ground reaction.

The defect, verified at mesh level. Link4 (shank, frame at the knee) and Link6 (foot, frame at the ankle) permanently interpenetrate, on both legs, and PhysX does not filter their collision because they are NOT directly jointed. The ankle is modelled as two revolute joints, AnkleRoll from Link4 to Link5 and AnklePitch from Link5 to Link6, with Link5 a 0.072 kg intermediate link whose own mesh is only 1.9 cm tall and whose AnklePitch origin is (0,0,0) so that Link5 and Link6 frames are coincident. Articulation self collision filtering excludes only parent child pairs, so the physically adjacent shank and foot are treated as a colliding pair. At the zero pose the overlap box is 0.2376 x 0.1364 x 0.0710 m, about 2301 cm^3, and 3195 vertices of Link4R lie strictly inside the convex hull of Link6R (3128 for the left leg). The discriminating control is Link1 against Link3, which share an overlapping bounding box at the hip but have ZERO vertices inside each other's hull, being merely coaxial, and correctly generate no such force. Self collision is enabled at sd_brs1_identified_cfg.py:147 and recorded in the run at env.yaml:109 and :139.

Why the force is downward and constant. Link4 sits above Link6, so PhysX separates them by pushing the shank up and the foot down, giving the observed negative Fz. The penetration is unescapable because the two links are held in relative position by the ankle joints, so the force is present at every foot height, which is exactly what the plot shows and is the single most diagnostic feature in it.

Consequences, which explain every anomaly recorded in the previous passes. Every contact keyed term on Link6 is permanently saturated, because a true ground reaction of at most about 600 N is buried under a permanent spurious bias five times larger and the terms threshold at 1 N. no_fly therefore reads permanent double support and never charges its -75 per second flight tax nor pays single stance income, feet_air_time never accrues air time and reads 1.8e-5 against a possible 5 per second, feet_slide charges genuinely airborne feet as sliding, and the optional contact gate on foot_clearance_reward_v2 never opens, which is precisely why run 2026-07-17_10-59-30 froze with clearance at 0.000001 and a maximum of 0.00055 over 30000 iterations. pen_undesired_contacts monitors Link[1-4][LR] and so counts Link4L and Link4R permanently, contributing to its 5.84 bodies. The alternation machinery has therefore been dead in EVERY experiment in this work stream, which is the standing explanation for air time and no fly reading zero throughout, and no reward redesign could ever have repaired it.

Correction to the ninth pass. The arithmetic of the ninth pass stands, both feet report contact 99.97 percent of the time and one foot is genuinely at 0.080 m of sole clearance, and the conclusion that the contact reading is not ground contact stands. The mechanism asserted there, the raised foot pressing the opposite foot or leg, is WRONG. No inter leg contact is required at all, each foot is permanently colliding with its own shank. The reward side reasoning that ungated stance agnostic clearance favours parking a foot at the target height remains a real secondary defect but is no longer the primary cause.

Fix options, none implemented. (1) Disable articulation self collision, one line at sd_brs1_identified_cfg.py:147 and the articulation_props flag, which removes the spurious force immediately and is not a fidelity regression since the current state is already unphysical, at the cost of allowing genuine thigh to thigh interpenetration. (2) Add an explicit filtered pair between Link4 and Link6 through UsdPhysics FilteredPairsAPI, preserving legitimate self collision, which is the correct fix but needs USD post processing after the URDF import. (3) Shrink or replace the Link4 collision geometry with a primitive that stops above the ankle, which also preserves thigh collision and is robust, at the cost of editing the asset. Recommended order is (2), falling back to (1) for a fast confirmation run since it is reversible and one line. Whichever is chosen, the contact terms should be re verified with the same DataLogger plot before a long run, the signature of success is Fz going to exactly zero whenever sole clearance exceeds about 1 cm.

The decisive arithmetic, why holding beats walking. Modelling a gait as two single support phases of length T_ss and two double support phases of T_ds per cycle, and allowing the swing foot either to pass through the clearance band or to PARK inside it, the three gait terms pay as follows. Park and hold at T_ss 2.5 s pays air 3.62, no_fly 14.71 and clearance 13.66 for a total of 31.99 per second, which reproduces the logged 3.43, 13.63 and 13.94 totalling 31.01 to within three percent and validates the model. A genuine walk at T_ss 0.40 s pays 1.61, 12.00 and 5.02 totalling 18.62. Walking therefore costs 13.3 per second, which is sixteen percent of the 85.6 per second gross income, and no amount of further training at a learning rate of 3e-5 will cross a barrier of that size.

Why each of the three terms prefers the hold, stated separately since the remedies differ. no_fly has no grace window, so every instant of double support is unpaid, and since a walk cannot avoid double support the term strictly prefers minimising the number of transitions, hence long phases. feet_air_time_positive_biped clamps at the threshold rather than peaking at it, so its per phase mean rises monotonically towards 0.4 as T_ss grows, 0.20 at T_ss 0.4 s against 0.38 at 2.5 s, and every foot swap additionally RESETS both accumulators and costs a dip, so alternation is doubly penalised. foot_clearance_reward_v2 is stance agnostic and velocity multiplied, so a foot parked at the target height and waved earns the kernel continuously, whereas a genuine swing passes through the band for only about forty five percent of a much shorter phase.

Which defect is load bearing, from a sweep of the candidate repairs against both strategies. Adding a 0.2 s grace window to no_fly leaves the hold ahead by 9.9 per second. Adding that plus a peaked air time kernel of width 0.15 centred on 0.4 s leaves the hold ahead by 7.7. Only when the clearance term stops paying a parked foot does the ranking invert, and the clearance gate applied alone inverts it by a mere 0.32 per second, which is inside noise, whereas the full package gives walking a margin of about 9 per second. The conclusion for sequencing is that the clearance term is necessary but not sufficient and the three must move together.

Corroborating trend evidence for the reward hacking reading. Across training rew_foot_clearance climbs from 0.08 to 13.94 while every effort and quality penalty worsens monotonically, pen_action_smoothness from -0.22 to -1.65, pen_joint_torque from -0.12 to -1.25, pen_ang_vel_xy from -0.06 to -0.41 and pen_flat_orientation from -0.30 to -0.88. An agent paying steadily rising costs to farm one rising term is the standard signature of reward hacking rather than of skill acquisition.

The hip roll oscillation visible in the video and quantified by pen_hip_deviation (7.7 degrees mean) and pen_ang_vel_xy (func value 0.670, meaning RMS angular velocity of about 0.82 rad/s) is a direct consequence of the narrow 0.21 m stance width. During single support, the CoM must lie over the stance foot's support polygon. With a stance foot at only 0.105 m from the midline, the CoM shift needed is small, and the robot achieves it primarily through torso roll rather than lateral hip translation. A 5.7 degree roll of the torso shifts the CoM laterally by about 1.15 times sin(5.7 degrees) = 0.114 m, which is sufficient for the 0.105 m shift, confirming that roll alone can balance the narrow stance.

Widening the stance to 0.32 m raises the required lateral shift to 0.16 m, which would need about 8 degrees of roll to accomplish purely through torso rotation, exceeding the 5.7 degree operating point and incurring substantially more pen_flat_orientation cost (-50 weight is steep at 8 degrees). The policy should therefore redistribute the lateral balance from roll toward hip sway, which is kinematically available through hip roll actuation (the joint, not the base roll) combined with ankle inversion.

Three ordered interventions are recommended.

First, raise min_feet_distance from 0.21 (run) or 0.30 (working tree) to 0.32 m. This is a pure config change in `cfg/SF/brs_base_env_cfg.py` line 815, no function edited, no backwards compatibility concern since pen_feet_distance is not called from any other task.

Second, raise pen_ang_vel_xy weight from -2.0 to -5.0. The current -2.0 gives a penalty of 1.34/s against a tracking income of 39.29/s (lin + ang), a ratio of 3.4%, far below the G1 reference where ang_vel_xy_l2 is set to -0.05 against track_lin_vel_xy 1.0, a ratio of 5%. At -5.0 the penalty would be about 3.35/s, a ratio of 8.5%, closer to the reference proportion and enough to shift the roll versus sway tradeoff. This is also a pure config change.

Third, if the first two changes are insufficient, a new reward function `lateral_com_over_stance_foot` would reward the base y-position aligning with the stance foot y-position during single support. This is the most principled approach since it directly rewards the desired behaviour, but it requires a new function in `mdp/rewards.py` and should be held in reserve until the config-only changes are evaluated.
