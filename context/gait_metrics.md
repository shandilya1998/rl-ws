# gait_metrics.md — the measurement of a legged locomotion policy

Created 2026-08-04. This document records the factual basis of the statistics pipeline specified in [../plans/GAIT_STATISTICS_PLAN.md](../plans/GAIT_STATISTICS_PLAN.md), namely the exact format of the play dumps, the inventory of statistics the SD_BRS1 and co-optimisation work streams have computed, the reference implementations validated against the surviving runs, and the instrumentation defects that govern every computation. Its purpose is that a future session need not re-derive an instrument that has already been written and checked, which is the cost the record has paid in every pass since the twentieth.

## 1. The dump format, verified 2026-08-04

`DataLogger.plot` at `tron1-rl-isaaclab-cozum/scripts/rsl_rl/play.py:388` writes two files under `<run>/data/<seed>/`, and the shapes below were read directly from `IsaacLab/logs/rsl_rl/sd_brs1_flat/2026-07-31_10-21-10/data/42/` and `2026-08-03_11-19-11/data/42/`.

`dump.npy` is a pickled dictionary. Every per step series is stored under a leading singleton axis, so `joint_velocities` has shape `(1, 3001, 32, 10)` and must be indexed at zero before use. The two commanded channels are the exception, stored flat by the branch at `play.py:399`, so `commanded_linear_velocity` is `(3001, 32, 2)` and `commanded_angular_velocity` is `(3001, 32)`. The three name lists are exempt from both the wrap and the stack by the `_METADATA_KEYS` tuple at `play.py:105`, so `joint_names` reads as a list of ten strings rather than as a nesting of one.

`rewards.npy` is a pickled dictionary of `(T, E)` arrays, one per reward term plus `total_reward`. The run `2026-07-31_10-21-10` carries twenty six terms and `total_reward`.

The total body mass is recoverable as `robot_mass[0, 0].sum()`, which for SD_BRS1 is 59.852306 kilograms and therefore a body weight of 587.15 newtons at 9.81 metres per second squared.

## 2. THE UNIT TRAP in rewards.npy, established 2026-08-04

Every per term series in `rewards.npy` is a RATE PER SECOND equal to `func × weight`, and `total_reward` is a PER STEP value equal to the sum of those rates times the control period. This has not been recorded anywhere before and it is easy to get wrong in either direction.

The mechanism is in `RewardManager.compute` at `/ws/IsaacLab/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/managers/reward_manager.py`. Line 150 forms `value = term_cfg.func(...) * term_cfg.weight * dt` and line 152 accumulates that into the buffer the method returns, which is what `env.step()` yields and therefore what `DataLogger.log_reward` stores as `total_reward`. Line 157 then stores `value / dt` into `_step_reward`, which is the array the per term loop at `play.py:380` reads.

Verified numerically on `2026-08-03_11-19-11`, the mean of `total_reward` is 0.6011485 against a summed term mean of 60.114845, a ratio of 0.010000001 which is exactly the control period. A budget that adds the two together, or that treats a term series as a per step reward, is wrong by a factor of one hundred.

The consequence for analysis is that a logged term value divides directly by its weight to give the unweighted function value, with no `dt` bookkeeping, which is the same identity the third pass of [brs_gait.md](brs_gait.md) established for the TensorBoard `Episode_Reward` scalars. The two are the same quantity computed over different populations, the TensorBoard figure over the training distribution and the dump figure over the evaluation one.

## 3. Reference implementations, validated by reproduction

The implementations below were prototyped on 2026-08-04 against the surviving dumps and reproduce the figures the record carries. They are the specification the module of [../plans/GAIT_STATISTICS_PLAN.md](../plans/GAIT_STATISTICS_PLAN.md) section 4.3 implements, and any change to that module must continue to reproduce them.

Contact is the norm of `feet_contact_forces` above one newton. A touchdown is a rising edge of that indicator. The peak contact force is the maximum over the eight control steps following a touchdown, which is eighty milliseconds at the SD_BRS1 control period. The touchdown frame vertical speed is the magnitude of `feet_velocities[..., 2]` at the last airborne sample. The sole approach speed is the backward difference of `feet_sole_clearances` across the step ending at touchdown, negated so that descent is positive. The absolute cost of transport is the mean over steps of the summed magnitude of `joint_powers`, divided by the product of body weight and the mean planar base speed. A swing is a contiguous airborne interval bounded by a lift off and a touchdown.

## 4. Statistics the record has computed, the consolidated inventory

Seven families were named in the request that motivated the pipeline and all seven were located in the record but one. The velocity error moments, the per joint moments and peaks, the per foot force velocity and clearance moments, the feet distance decomposition, the command correlations, and the reward budget with its unweighted comparison are each computed in the twentieth or the twenty second pass. The error between the expected and the measured base position has never been computed anywhere, and section 5 establishes why.

Twenty three further statistics appear in the record and were not named in the request. The temporal decomposition carries the median and mean cycle period, the stance duty per foot, and the double support, single support and flight fractions. The contact event family carries the touchdown frame velocity, the sole approach velocity, the peak force in body weights, the loading rate, and the fraction of touchdowns above a force threshold. The swing family carries the apex, the apex position within the normalised swing, the peak count per swing, and the correlation against a raised cosine reference. The energetic family carries the mean absolute joint power, the net mechanical power, the absolute and net costs of transport, and the opposed power between a joint pair. The actuator margin family carries the limit residence fraction, the effort saturation fraction, and the excursion beyond a declared limit. The symmetry family carries the Robinson index of the root mean square torque and of the range of motion.

Three statistics come from the co-optimisation stream rather than the gait stream. [cmaes.md](cmaes.md) section 7 computes the per iteration mean and standard deviation of each design parameter across the population, and the analogue here is that every behavioural statistic must be computable per design group, since a co-optimisation policy is evaluated across environments carrying different morphologies. The dump already carries the descriptor, `robot_mass`, `robot_inertia` and `robot_material_properties` being logged per environment at `play.py:323` through `:331`. Inspection of `2026-08-03_11-19-11` confirms the mechanism, the material properties resolving to thirty two distinct rows across thirty two environments under friction randomisation while the masses resolve to a single row, that run randomising the surface and not the body. The within generation fitness range of [cmaes.md](cmaes.md) section 5 has no play dump analogue. The variance decomposition lesson of [knowledge_base.md](knowledge_base.md) transfers directly, that a statistic aggregated over a population conceals whether its spread is within an environment across time or across environments at one time, so the principal quantities should carry both decompositions.

## 5. Channels the dump does not carry, and what they cost

Five gaps were established on 2026-08-04 and each blocks a statistic the record wants.

There is no orientation channel of any kind. No base quaternion, no projected gravity, no foot quaternion. The consequence is that the torso tilt quoted throughout the record, 7.3 degrees root mean square for `2026-07-28_06-37-24` and 5.6 for its sibling, was inverted from `pen_flat_orientation` through its weight and has never been measured. It also means the expected base position cannot be integrated, since `commanded_linear_velocity` is expressed in the base frame and rotating it into the world requires the yaw, which is why that family of the request has never been computed. And it means the foot yaw alignment term that section 2.7 of [../plans/GAIT_EFFICIENCY_PLAN.md](../plans/GAIT_EFFICIENCY_PLAN.md) prescribes cannot be evaluated before it is wired. The members required are verified to exist, `root_quat_w` at `/ws/IsaacLab/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/assets/articulation/articulation_data.py:1021`, `projected_gravity_b` at `:788`, and `body_quat_w` at `:1061`.

There are no actuator or joint limits. The saturation and limit residence fractions the twentieth and twenty second passes both report were computed against constants transcribed into a throwaway script, the ankle roll effort ceiling of 131 newton metres among them. The members are verified at the same file, `joint_effort_limits` at `:391`, `joint_vel_limits` at `:388`, `joint_pos_limits` at `:382`, `soft_joint_pos_limits` at `:398` and `default_joint_pos` at `:134`.

There is no episode boundary marker, so a reset is invisible. Inspection of `2026-08-03_11-19-11` finds exactly one reset per environment, detectable as a base position discontinuity reaching 19.56 metres at step 2998, and the commanded velocity resamples independently at steps 1088 and 2007 in environment zero. A cycle period, a swing segment or a base position error spanning a boundary is meaningless, and until the marker exists the boundary must be inferred from a position discontinuity, a threshold of 0.2 metres separating the two cases cleanly. The members required are verified at `/ws/IsaacLab/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/managers/termination_manager.py:102`, `:117` and `:107`.

There is no record of the reward weights, and they cannot reliably be recovered from the run's dumped `params/env.yaml` outside the simulation container, since that file carries Python object tags requiring `bipedal_locomotion` to be importable. A safe loader pattern exists in the repository at `scripts/analysis/compare_experiments.py`, which registers a permissive constructor on a `yaml.SafeLoader` subclass, and it is the fallback where a weight must be read from an existing run. Logging the weight at play time is the reliable route.

There is no history maximum of the contact force. The dump records the instantaneous value while the impact reward reads `net_forces_w_history`, verified at `/ws/IsaacLab/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/sensors/contact_sensor/contact_sensor_data.py:84`, whose maximum sees transients falling between two control steps. The twenty first pass measured the discrepancy at a factor of twelve on the excess above threshold, so every peak contact force this project has quoted is a lower bound.

## 6. The `SOLE_OFFSETS` defect, found 2026-08-04

`scripts/rsl_rl/play.py` documents a module global `SOLE_OFFSETS` at line 113, defaulting to `None`, under which only the body frame height is logged and the sole clearance channel stays empty, so that TRON1 and every existing caller are unaffected. The fortieth line of [brs_gait.md](brs_gait.md) records the same contract. The code does not implement it.

The guard at `play.py:299` tests `SD_BRS1_SOLE_OFFSETS is not None`, which is a twelve row table literal declared at line 120 and therefore always true, and `_sole_clearance` at `play.py:349` transforms that same literal rather than the configured global. The SD_BRS1 sole geometry is consequently applied to whatever robot is played. `SOLE_OFFSETS` is dead code, read nowhere.

No conclusion in the record is affected, every figure quoted having come from an SD_BRS1 run for which the table is correct. The consequence is confined to any TRON1 dump written since the seventh pass, whose `feet_sole_clearances` channel was computed from a sole that robot does not have and must not be trusted. The repair is two lines and is specified in section 4.1.5 of [../plans/GAIT_STATISTICS_PLAN.md](../plans/GAIT_STATISTICS_PLAN.md), and it must be taken together with an assignment of `SOLE_OFFSETS = SD_BRS1_SOLE_OFFSETS` in the SD_BRS1 configuration of that module, since otherwise the repair silences the channel for the robot that needs it.

## 7. Metrics the literature prescribes and this project has never computed

Recorded here in summary, with the full survey and its citations in [../plans/GAIT_STATISTICS_PLAN.md](../plans/GAIT_STATISTICS_PLAN.md) section 2 and the register entries in [literature.md](literature.md) cluster 14.

Four stability measures, the zero moment point margin within the support polygon, the margin of stability against the extrapolated centre of mass, the capture point excursion, and the nonlinear pair of the maximum Lyapunov exponent and the maximum Floquet multiplier. The second is the measure of choice in the biomechanical literature because it accounts for velocity as well as position, and its mediolateral component is the one associated with falls.

Two variability measures, the stride to stride coefficient of variation of the cycle period and of the step width, whose warrant is the clinical result that stride time variability predicts falls where the mean does not. The record contains the observation these would have flagged, a median cycle period of exactly 1.000 seconds coexisting with a tenth percentile of 0.720.

Two smoothness measures, the dimensionless jerk and the spectral arc length, the second being preferred for its robustness to noise and its independence of movement duration.

One kinetic measure, the loading rate, which separates a collision from a weight acceptance where the peak force alone cannot, and one conservation check, the stance impulse.

One momentum measure, the whole body angular momentum about the centre of mass, whose regulation to a small range is the established property of human walking and whose horizontal component bears directly on the yaw authority of a machine with fixed hip yaw joints.

One energetic refinement, that the cost of transport must be reported in all three of its conventions, absolute, net and positive, since they diverge by an order of magnitude on an antagonistic gait and a single figure conceals whether the expense lies in the trajectory or in the manner of producing it.

## 7a. A symmetry index must not be pooled across environments, established 2026-08-04

Measured rather than taken from the literature, and recorded because it inverts the reading of the family. Forming the Robinson index from statistics pooled over every environment cancels the asymmetry, since the left and the right pooled quantities each take the union over environments whose asymmetries point in opposite directions. On run `2026-07-28_06-37-24` the ankle roll range of motion index reads 4.68 percent when the range is taken over the pooled population, minus 5.17 as a signed mean of the per environment indices, 13.4 as a mean of their magnitudes, and 14.09 in environment zero alone, which is the panel the exported plots display.

A pooled index near zero is therefore not evidence of symmetry. The index must be formed per environment and averaged as a magnitude, and the signed mean reported beside it so that a consistent limp, which appears in both, is distinguishable from a scattered one, which appears only in the magnitude. This also explains why the twentieth pass could report a frontal ankle roll index of 41 percent while a pooled recomputation reports single figures, the two being different statistics rather than a discrepancy.

## 8. Corrections and open items

The commanded double support fraction of a gait clock with an anti phase offset is exactly `2 × duration − 1`, so a duration of 0.6 commands 20.0 percent and not the 19.6 the twentieth pass recorded, that figure having been an artefact of evaluating the clock on the discrete episode step grid. Recorded by the twenty first pass and repeated here because the pipeline computes it.

The swing reference is the raised cosine `A sin²(π φ)` and not the half sinusoid `A sin(π φ)`. A half sinusoid attains its maximum vertical velocity at both ends of the swing, 0.66 metres per second at this robot's swing duration, which is the collision the impact phases were paying to remove. Recorded by the twenty first pass.

Open, the swing segmentation convention that closes the four residual discrepancies of section 3. Open, whether the margin of stability should be computed against a support polygon reconstructed from the sole offsets, which would require logging them, or accepted as the conservative lower bound the foot origin gives. Open, whether the per design grouping of section 4 should be a dimension of the output record or a separate invocation of the module.

## 9. Corrections of 2026-08-05, from the review of the statistics plan

Five entries above are superseded or contradicted by the revision the plan received on this date, and each is corrected here rather than in place so that the reasoning which produced the earlier claim survives.

Section 5 states that the reward weights cannot reliably be recovered from a run's dumped `params/env.yaml` outside the simulation container, since the file carries Python object tags requiring `bipedal_locomotion` to be importable. That is true only of `yaml.unsafe_load`, and it is false of the document itself. A `yaml.SafeLoader` subclass carrying three multi constructors, for the `python/name`, `python/object` and `python/object/apply` tag prefixes, together with one constructor for `python/tuple`, parses the document whole without importing anything, resolving the anchors and aliases natively as PyYAML does. Prototyped on this date against `IsaacLab/logs/rsl_rl/sd_brs1_flat/2026-08-03_11-19-11/params/env.yaml`, which yielded twenty four top level keys, a `sim.dt` of 0.005 against a `decimation` of 2 and therefore a control period of 0.01 seconds derived rather than assumed, and all twenty six reward terms with their weights and the fully qualified import path of each `func`. The parsed term names and their order match the twenty six keys of that run's `rewards.npy` exactly, position for position, which follows from `RewardManager._prepare_terms` iterating the configuration instance dictionary in declaration order at `/ws/IsaacLab/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/managers/reward_manager.py:222`. The consequence is that a stored run is self describing, and section 4.1 of the plan specifies the module `scripts/analysis/experiment_params.py` that reads it, both for the weights and for the reconstruction of a replay through a new `--use_run_params` flag on `play.py`.

Section 1 describes the leading singleton axis as the dump format. The plan now abolishes it at the source in its section 4.2.7, `DataLogger.plot` saving the stacked `(T, E, ...)` array under every per step key, so that after implementation only the eleven metadata keys are stored flat and no key carries a wrap. The two commanded channels do not move, having always been stored flat. The table of section 1 above therefore describes every dump written to date and the column headed "shape after unwrapping" becomes the shape as stored. A legacy dump is distinguishable without a version marker, its per step values being Python lists where a current dump holds numpy arrays, and `stats.normalise_legacy_dump` converts one to the other. It was also found that `plot` already computed the correct stacked form into a `write_data` dictionary at `play.py:404` and then discarded it, saving the wrapped `data` instead, so the change deletes a redundant construction rather than adding one.

Section 5 states that there is no history maximum of the contact force. The plan now moves the existing `feet_contact_forces` channel onto that maximum rather than adding a second channel beside it, storing the full force vector at the instant of the history peak so that the shape stays `(T, E, F, 3)` and every dashboard panel continues to draw. The cadence was verified on this date, `scene.update` being called inside the decimation loop at `/ws/IsaacLab/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/envs/manager_based_rl_env.py:197` so that the history rolls once per physics step, which with the SD_BRS1 history length of four at `brs_base_env_cfg.py:79` spans twenty milliseconds against a control period of ten. No guard is needed, since `net_forces_w_history` falls back to `net_forces_w.unsqueeze(1)` when the history length is zero at `contact_sensor.py:310`. The depth is logged as metadata under `contact_force_history_length`, which is the marker distinguishing a dump whose forces are true peaks from one whose forces are the lower bound this document records.

Section 6 specifies the `SOLE_OFFSETS` repair as belonging to section 4.1.5 of the plan. That repair is withdrawn from the plan and deferred to the TRON1 correction pass, on the reasoning that `play.py` is at present exercised only against SD_BRS1, for which the behaviour is correct, and that the repair would otherwise change a dump nobody reads and require validation against a robot nobody plays. Everything section 6 records about the defect stands unaltered, and the operative consequence is now a reading rule, that the presence of a `feet_sole_clearances` channel is not evidence that the robot has a sole.

Section 5 states that there is no record of the reward weights. That remains true of every dump written to date. The plan's section 4.2.4 logs them into `rewards.npy` under the reserved key `_weights`, and the parsed weight of the paragraph above is the fallback for a dump predating that. The two answer different questions, the logged weight being what the replay applied and therefore the correct divisor for that dump's own series, and the parsed weight being what the training applied, so a disagreement between them is a finding rather than a nuisance.

All four constructions were run before being written down, the code blocks of the plan being extracted verbatim and executed on this date against `2026-08-03_11-19-11`. The parameter reader returned the figures quoted above. `normalise_legacy_dump` converted that run's dump into the uniform shape, `joint_velocities` reading `(3001, 32, 10)` and `commanded_linear_velocity` staying `(3001, 32, 2)`, dropping only the degenerate `latent_space_output` of shape `(1, 0)`. The reduced `get_env_data` applied to the normalised dump returned arrays identical element for element to those the present `get_env_data` returns from the legacy dump, across every channel of environment seven, which is the evidence that the shape change costs no historical dump. And `from_dump` built a container reporting a body mass of 59.852301 kilograms with `has_peak_contact_force` correctly false, taking all twenty six weights from the parameter reader where the dump carried none.

One further discovery bears on the analysis rather than on the plan. `_METADATA_KEYS` at `play.py:105` is referenced nowhere but its declaration and its single use in `plot`, verified by grep over the workspace on this date, so moving it into `stats.py` as the public `METADATA_KEYS` affects no external consumer. And `get_env_data`, present in all four live dashboards, dispatches on dimensionality with branches for two, three and four dimensions only, returning an empty array for anything else, so a channel carrying two trailing axes beyond the component axis would be dropped silently. No present channel reaches that shape, so nothing in the record is affected, but the reduction the plan specifies removes the hazard along with the branch.

## 10. Corrections of 2026-08-06, from the second review of the statistics plan

The second review narrowed the pipeline in five ways that supersede entries above, and the execution of the plan's own code on this date established a sixth fact that supersedes part of section 3.

Section 9 records the leading singleton axis as removable with `stats.normalise_legacy_dump` converting a legacy dump to the current shape. That helper is withdrawn. Every dump the record rests upon is to be regenerated by replay under the extended logger, so the plan carries no provision for reading a dump written before the change, and the dashboards no longer normalise at load. A dump in the legacy shape is now readable only by the two surviving copies under `/ws/IsaacLab/logs/rsl_rl_backup/`, which are retained for that purpose.

Section 9 records the parameter reader as feeding a new `--use_run_params` flag on `play.py`. There is no flag. The replay is configured from the run's own `params/env.yaml` on every invocation, since no evaluation wants the working tree's configuration in preference to the run's, and a default reproducing the present conduct would leave the manual step standing for every invocation that omitted the flag. A checkpoint directory carrying no such file still plays, falling back to the tree with a warning.

Section 9 records the parsed weight as the fallback for a dump carrying no logged `_weights`. That fallback is removed from `stats.from_dump`, which now takes only the dump, the reward dump and the control period. It had no caller once the statistics became computable in one place, and after the change above the logged weight is the parsed weight by construction. `rewards.npy` is itself no longer optional to any reader, being a mandatory member of a dump, written unconditionally by `DataLogger.plot` and read unconditionally by `from_dump`, which raises `ValueError` where it is absent.

The statistics are computed in exactly one place. `stats.py` is imported by `scripts/rsl_rl/play.py` and by nothing else, the record set is written to `statistics.npy` at the close of a play from the arrays held in memory, and every dashboard reads that file without recomputing. The `compute_from_paths` entry point and the dashboards' compute on load fallback are both withdrawn, the consequence being that a play whose statistics block failed is recovered by replaying rather than by recomputing.

## 12. Two defects found in the shipped pipeline, 2026-08-07, from the Phase 3 evaluation

All seven runs of the SD_BRS1 series were replayed on 2026-08-06 and 2026-08-07 and now carry a `statistics.npy`, so the regeneration the previous section anticipated has happened and the four run comparison of the twenty-fifth pass of `brs_gait.md` is drawn entirely from it. Two defects surfaced in the course of that comparison. Both are in the data the pipeline is given rather than in its arithmetic, so both are one line fixes at the point of dump, and both silently produce a plausible number rather than an obviously wrong one, which is why they survived the two reviews of the plan.

### 12.1 THE ACTUATOR CEILINGS ARE THE SIM DEFAULTS, so the whole saturation family is inert

The dump records `joint_effort_limits` as 1.0e9 N·m for all ten joints and `joint_velocity_limits` as 5.939e36 rad/s. These are Isaac Lab's unbounded `effort_limit_sim` and `velocity_limit_sim`, not the identified actuator limits declared in the `IdentifiedActuatorCfg` blocks of `/ws/tron1-rl-isaaclab-cozum/exts/bipedal_locomotion/bipedal_locomotion/assets/config/sd_brs1_identified_cfg.py`, which are HipYaw 351, HipRoll 500, HipPitch 500, KneePitch 600, AnkleRoll 131 and AnklePitch 262 N·m.

Three records per joint per channel are therefore worthless in every run on disk. `p99 as fraction of ceiling` reads about 1.3e-5 percent everywhere, which is the true value divided by roughly two million. `fraction above 98 pct of ceiling` is exactly 0.0 for every joint in every run. `fraction exceeding ceiling` is likewise 0.0. `has_limits` in the metadata reads True, so a consumer has no signal that the family is unusable.

The defect matters more than its size suggests, because the ankle roll saturation is the oldest unresolved finding in this record and the pipeline cannot see it. Computed by hand against the identified ceilings, the ankle roll p99 torque is 99.6, 99.7, 100.0 and 93.3 percent of its 131 N·m limit across the four runs, which is the most saturated actuator in the machine by a factor of one and a half over the next. The pipeline reports 1.2e-5 percent.

The remedy is to dump `actuator.effort_limit` rather than `effort_limit_sim`, or where only the sim value is reachable, to fall back to the actuator configuration. Until it lands, every saturation figure quoted anywhere in this record was transcribed by hand and must be treated as such.

### 12.2 THE FOOT SEPARATION IS DUMPED IN THE WORLD FRAME, so two of its three components are meaningless when the robot turns

`scripts/rsl_rl/play.py:407` logs `feet_distance` as `body_pos_w[:, 0, :] - body_pos_w[:, 1, :]`, a world frame vector, and `feet_distance_statistics` at `scripts/analysis/stats.py:751` names its three components fore and aft, lateral and vertical without rotating any of them. The lateral component equals the stance width only when the robot's heading is zero. This robot turns continuously under a heading controller, its yaw covering the whole circle with a standard deviation of 1.76 radians over a fifteen second evaluation, so the reported fore and aft and lateral components are a rotating mixture of the stride and the stance width.

The error is large and it inverts a conclusion. On run 2026-08-05_07-53-35 the shipped statistic reports a lateral separation of mean 0.1916 m, fifth percentile 0.0202 and minimum 0.0000, from which the record twice concluded that the feet crossed the midline. Rotated into the base frame by the yaw of the dumped `base_quaternion` the same data give mean 0.2591, fifth percentile 0.2169, minimum 0.0907 and NOT ONE SIGN CHANGE in 96032 samples.

The decomposition is validated on both components independently, which is the check that should be added as a regression test. A true stance width never changes sign, the left foot remaining left of the right, and the base frame lateral does not. A true stride crosses zero once per half cycle, and the base frame fore and aft crosses at 0.0185 per step, which is one crossing every 0.54 s against a half cycle of 0.46 s. Its p99 of 0.4371 m agrees with the 0.4461 m predicted from the measured 0.9242 s cycle at 0.4827 m/s. In the world frame BOTH components cross zero and both have a mean magnitude near 0.19 to 0.20 m, which is the diagnostic signature of the defect and is what a reviewer should look for.

Three consumers are affected and one is not. The `stance` family's fore and aft and lateral records are wrong, and so are the separation correlations built on them. The `stability` family's centre of pressure and base of support records at `stats.py:1024` and `stats.py:1044` take the horizontal components and are wrong for the same reason. The `variability` family's step width at `stats.py:1242` takes the lateral component and is wrong. The vertical component is unaffected, and the planar norm is rotation invariant and therefore also unaffected, which is why `total separation` and `planar separation` may be quoted as they stand.

CORRECTION dated 2026-08-07, made the same day, which relocates the remedy and exonerates the reward code. The defect above is one of INTERPRETATION and not of representation, and stating it loosely would condemn code that is correct.

A separation is a vector between two points and a change of basis does not alter it. What a rotation alters is the tuple of components, and therefore any quantity read off a single component. Every rotation invariant may be computed from the world frame components exactly, and this was verified rather than asserted. On run 2026-08-05_07-53-35 the planar norm computed from the world components and from the base frame components agree to 5.96e-8 m at worst over 96032 samples, and the full 3D norm to 8.94e-8, which is float32 resolution. The two horizontal components taken singly differ by up to 0.4827 m on a vector whose length never moved.

`feet_distance` at `/ws/tron1-rl-isaaclab-cozum/exts/bipedal_locomotion/bipedal_locomotion/tasks/locomotion/mdp/rewards.py:581` THEREFORE REQUIRES NO CHANGE in either branch. Its default branch takes `torch.norm` of the world frame planar difference at line 630, a rotation invariant, and is already correct. Its `lateral_only` branch at lines 622 to 627 already rotates by `math_utils.yaw_quat` before taking a component, which is the only place a rotation is needed. No reward in the tree has ever measured the wrong quantity here.

`play.py:407` likewise requires no change. A world frame vector is a faithful and lossless record, and `base_quaternion` is dumped beside it, verified present at shape (3001, 32, 4) in all seven runs of the SD_BRS1 series, so the yaw needed to rotate it is already on disk for every separation ever recorded.

THE REMEDY BELONGS IN `stats.py`, and belongs there for a practical reason as well as a correctness one. Rotating inside the statistics corrects every existing dump RETROACTIVELY and requires no replay, whereas changing the dump would correct only runs recorded afterwards and would destroy the world frame reading for any consumer wanting it. `feet_distance_statistics` should take `math_utils.yaw_quat` of the dumped base quaternion, apply its inverse to the separation, and only then name the components, with the same treatment applied at `stats.py:1024`, `stats.py:1044` and `stats.py:1242`. Where both frames are wanted they should be reported under distinct names rather than one being inferred from the other.

A REGRESSION TEST is available and should be added with the fix, since both frames produce plausible numbers and only the invariants distinguish them. A true stance width never changes sign, the left foot remaining left of the right, and the base frame lateral does not change sign once in 96032 samples where the world frame one changes at a rate of 0.0107 per step. A true stride crosses zero once per half cycle, and the base frame fore and aft crosses at 0.0185 per step against a half cycle of 0.46 s. And the planar norm must be identical in both frames to float precision, which is the check that would have caught a rotation applied in the wrong direction.

### 12.3 A note on the swing plateau statistic, which is sound but cannot carry a fine gate

`swing profile / fraction above 90 pct of apex` at `stats.py:582` is computed from a mean profile resampled onto 21 points at `stats.py:555`, so it can take only multiples of 1/21, which is 4.76 percentage points. It is a correct statistic and a useful diagnostic, and it is not a quantity on which a gate stated to a hundredth can be adjudicated. The Phase 3 gate of 0.22 fell between the two achievable values of 0.1905 and 0.2381, and the run landed on the higher. Where a fine threshold is wanted the continuous companion at `stats.py:585`, `rms error against raised cosine`, is the record to use, and it moved from 0.0271 m to 0.00798 over the same change that moved the quantised measure by one step.

## 13. The implementation of 2026-08-07, both defects fixed and one third found

Sections 12.1 and 12.2 recorded two defects and section 12.2 carried a correction locating the second one's remedy. ### 13.1 The actuator ceilings, `play.py`

`asset.data.joint_effort_limits` is written from the actuator's `effort_limit_sim` at `/ws/IsaacLab/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/assets/articulation/articulation.py:1772`, and for an EXPLICIT actuator model that value defaults to `ActuatorBase._DEFAULT_MAX_EFFORT_SIM` rather than to the identified ceiling, which is why every dump up to 2026-08-05 records 1.0e9 N·m. The identified ceilings live on the actuator itself as `effort_limit` and `velocity_limit`, declared at `actuator_base.py:56` and `:76`, and `effort_limit` is the value `clip_effort` actually clips against at `actuator_base.py:381`, so it is the only ceiling a saturation statistic may be computed from.

`DataLogger._log_actuator_limits` now assembles a per joint vector by scattering each actuator's limits into its `joint_indices`, leaving any joint no actuator claims as NaN so that an absent ceiling reads as absent rather than as a plausible number. The sim ceilings are RETAINED under `joint_effort_limits_sim` and `joint_velocity_limits_sim`, since a torque clipped by the actuator model and one clipped by the solver are different events, and since a reader comparing an old dump against a new one can then see both figures and tell which convention it was on.

Verified by recomputing the whole pipeline on the Phase 3 dump with the identified ceilings supplied. `p99 as fraction of ceiling` moves from about 1.3e-5 percent on every joint to HipRoll 26.0, HipPitch 19.5, KneePitch 20.5, ANKLEROLL 93.3 and AnklePitch 56.5 to 61.5, which reproduces the hand computation of the twenty-fifth pass exactly. The ankle roll saturation is visible to the pipeline for the first time.

### 13.2 The base frame, `play.py` and `stats.py`

THREE channels are now rotated into the base frame by the yaw of the root quaternion, `feet_distance`, `feet_velocities` and `feet_contact_forces`, through a shared `DataLogger._to_base_frame`. Only the yaw is used, the pitch and roll being discarded so that a leaning torso does not appear to narrow the stance, which is the convention `feet_distance`'s `lateral_only` branch already adopts at `mdp/rewards.py:625`.

The two velocity and force channels were not named in section 12.2 and belong to the same defect. `foot velocity x`, `foot velocity y`, `contact force x` and `contact force y` at `stats.py:696` onward were world frame components carrying base frame names, exactly as the separation was.

NOTHING THAT READS A NORM OR A VERTICAL COMPONENT MOVES, and this was verified rather than asserted. Recomputing the Phase 3 dump in both conventions, `planar separation` reads 0.31002 in both, `peak contact force` 1.55138 in both, and `raised cosine correlation` 0.94471 in both, while the vertical component is untouched to 0.0 exactly and the planar norm agrees to 1.19e-7, which is float32 resolution. What moves is `lateral separation magnitude`, 0.1916 to 0.25907, and `step width` coefficient of variation, 57.47 percent to 15.05, the second being the clearer signal that the old figure was not a gait property at all.

A `frame_convention` dictionary is now written into the dump, naming the frame of every per body vector channel, and `stats.py` carries it into the metadata as `frame_convention` with a derived boolean `feet_axes_are_base_frame`. A dump lacking the key predates the change and is world frame. The key is written rather than the convention being assumed, because a consumer that guesses wrong here produces plausible numbers rather than an error, which is how the defect survived two reviews.

### 13.3 A third defect, `stability_statistics` mixed two frames and now mixes none

Found while implementing the above. The margin of stability was computed as `xcom - support` entirely in the world frame and its components were then labelled fore and aft and lateral, which is the section 12.2 defect in a second place. Worse, it reconstructed the support from `data.base_com_position`, a world frame position, plus half the recorded separation, so moving the separation to the base frame would have made it mix two frames outright.

The remedy is algebraic rather than a rotation. The margin is a difference of two points both expressed relative to the same base position, so that position CANCELS identically.

```
xcom    = com + v / omega
support = com                (double stance)
        = com + half         (foot 0 loaded)
        = com - half         (foot 1 loaded)
delta   = xcom - support = v / omega  -/+ half
```

`base_linear_velocity` is already the base frame velocity at the point of logging and `half` is now the base frame half separation, so the whole construction is base frame with no rotation performed anywhere, and the dependence on `base_quaternion` is removed with it. Verified equal to the previous form on random data to 4.44e-16, which is double precision identity, so the refactor changes the FRAME of the answer and not the answer.

The centre of pressure offset reads the same `half` and is corrected by the same change, being a relative offset already.

A GUARD accompanies both. Where the dump does not declare `feet_distance` to be base frame, the margin of stability and the centre of pressure records are WITHHELD rather than computed, since serving them would mix a base frame velocity against a world frame separation. On the Phase 3 dump as it stands on disk the pipeline therefore returns 3336 records rather than 3386, the fifty absent ones being exactly those, and `feet_axes_are_base_frame` reads False. After regeneration both return.

### 13.4 A fourth defect, the dead `SOLE_OFFSETS` switch

`play.py` declared `SOLE_OFFSETS = None` as the per robot selector and then guarded the sole clearance block on `SD_BRS1_SOLE_OFFSETS`, the constant table, which is never None. The switch therefore selected nothing and the SD_BRS1 sole geometry was applied to every robot played, which for a point footed TRON1 would have produced a clearance measured against a sole that robot does not have. The guard now tests `SOLE_OFFSETS` and the table is assigned to it beside the `FEET_LINK_NAMES` declaration, so the SD_BRS1 behaviour is preserved exactly and the switch works for any other robot. This was recorded in the auto memory as an open defect and is now closed.

### 13.5 What is NOT changed, and why

`feet_distance` at `mdp/rewards.py:581` is untouched in both branches, its default taking a yaw invariant planar norm and its `lateral_only` branch already rotating. No reward in the tree ever measured the wrong quantity. `foot roll` and `foot pitch` at `stats.py:731` are left in the world frame deliberately, since whether a sole is flat is a question about the ground and not about the torso. `odometry_statistics` already rotated the command by the measured yaw and is untouched. `base_com_position` remains world frame, `base_linear_velocity` and `base_angular_velocity` remain base frame, and both quaternions remain world frame orientations from which any frame may be recovered.
