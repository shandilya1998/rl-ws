# isaaclab_newton.md, the Newton and MuJoCo Warp port of the workspace

Currency, 2026-09-11. Written against IsaacLab at commit 412fb31 of the `develop` branch, cloned to `IsaacLab-newton/` and carrying VERSION 3.0.0, read alongside the incumbent `IsaacLab/` checkout at VERSION 2.3.0. Every claim about either tree carries a `path:line` citation relative to the workspace root `/ws`, and every claim drawn from the literature or from vendor publication carries a bracketed marker resolved in the bibliography at the end.

## 1. Purpose and scope

The workspace presently runs four task families, the TRON1 sole foot, point foot and wheel foot configurations, the SD_BRS1 biped, the K-Scale sole foot biped and the quadruped, on a single physics backend, the PhysX solver reached through Omniverse Kit. That coupling is total. It fixes the runtime to Isaac Sim, it fixes the contact model to patch based Coulomb friction, and it fixes the design co-optimisation loop to a mechanism, the respawning of USD prims, that exists only because PhysX must cook collision geometry before it can simulate it. The purpose of this document is to establish what the Newton engine offers in place of that arrangement, what the IsaacLab 3.0 line has already built to expose it, and precisely which lines of this workspace must change so that the physics backend becomes a selectable property of a run rather than a property of the codebase.

The goal is not to replace PhysX. It is to reach the state the IsaacLab 3.0 architecture was designed for, in which one task definition runs under PhysX for continuity with the historical runs, under Newton with the MuJoCo Warp solver for throughput and for contact fidelity, and under whichever further solver a particular question demands, with the choice made on the command line and recorded in the run's dumped `params/env.yaml`. Sections 2 through 5 establish what Newton is and how it compares. Sections 6 through 8 establish the architecture and the API and feature differences. Sections 9 and 10 inventory this workspace and enumerate the required changes. Section 11 stages the work, and section 12 records the defects that a staged port deliberately leaves standing.

## 2. Newton, its origin and its shape

Newton is an open source, GPU accelerated physics simulation engine built upon NVIDIA Warp and aimed at roboticists and simulation researchers [1]. It was contributed to the Linux Foundation on 2025-09-29 by Disney Research, Google DeepMind and NVIDIA, and is developed in the open under the Apache 2.0 licence [2]. Its dependency footprint is modest by the standards of the incumbent stack, requiring Python 3.10 or later and a CUDA 12 driver at version 545 or newer [1]. The significance of the governance arrangement is practical rather than ceremonial, because the same three parties own the MuJoCo lineage, the Warp compiler and the Isaac Sim platform, and Newton is the point at which those three lines are made to meet.

The engine's data model is deliberately thin and is worth stating exactly, because the whole of the porting argument in section 10 turns on it. A `ModelBuilder` is the construction entry point. It produces a `Model`, which encapsulates the physical structure, parameters and configuration of the simulation world, that is the bodies, the joints, the shapes and their physical properties, together with the structural and static arrays and the labels that index them [3]. A `State` holds the evolving quantities, the positions and velocities that a solver advances each step, with optional extended attributes for derived quantities. A `Control` holds the actuation inputs, the joint targets and forces. A `Contacts` object holds the active contact set produced by collision detection, and is optional in the sense that whether it exists at all depends on the active solver and the chosen collision path [3].

Two properties of that model matter more than any feature list. The first is that the `Model` is built once. There is no documented runtime path that rebuilds topology, and the IsaacLab integration confirms the absence, since `NewtonManager` constructs its builder from the stage during initialisation at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab_newton/isaaclab_newton/physics/newton_manager.py:1941` and its only teardown, `clear()` at `newton_manager.py:1127`, nulls the builder, the model, the solver, both states, the control, the contacts, the collision pipeline, every sensor registry and the captured CUDA graph, which is to say it is a full restart rather than a rebuild. The second is that Newton supports differentiable simulation for machine learning and optimisation [3], a property that PhysX does not offer and that bears directly on the co-optimisation work stream, since a differentiable forward model admits gradient based design search in place of the present evolutionary search.

## 3. The solvers available under Newton

Newton is a multi solver framework rather than a single engine, and the distinction between a backend and a solver is one the IsaacLab documentation is careful to draw, stating that a backend integrates a physics runtime and owns the simulation lifecycle and data exchange whereas a solver is the numerical method that advances the physical state within that backend, so that Newton is not synonymous with MuJoCo Warp but rather hosts it alongside Kamino, VBD, MPM and other solver paths (`IsaacLab-newton/docs/source/concepts/physics_backends.rst:23-25`). The Newton overview lists eight solver backends, XPBD, VBD, MuJoCo, Featherstone, SemiImplicit, Kamino, ImplicitMPM and Style3D [3].

The IsaacLab integration exposes a subset of these, and the subset is legible directly from the manager configuration modules under `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab_newton/isaaclab_newton/physics/`. Six solver families are present. `MJWarpSolverCfg` declares `solver_type = "mujoco_warp"` at `mjwarp_manager_cfg.py:34`. The Kamino family appears as `KaminoPADMMSolverCfg` and `KaminoDVISolverCfg` at `kamino_manager_cfg.py:396` and `:408`, both descending from a base that declares `solver_type = "kamino"` at `:244`, and supported by seven auxiliary configuration classes covering the P-ADMM and DVI modes, the dynamics, the constraints, the forward kinematics solver, the collision detector and the materials. `FeatherstoneSolverCfg` declares `solver_type = "featherstone"` at `featherstone_manager_cfg.py:39`, `XPBDSolverCfg` declares `solver_type = "xpbd"` at `xpbd_manager_cfg.py:38`, `MPMSolverCfg` declares `solver_type = "implicit_mpm"` at `mpm_manager_cfg.py:31`, and `VBDSolverCfg` appears at `vbd_manager_cfg.py:21`.

Of these, only two are presented to the task author as named presets. The preset table records `newton_mjwarp` as Newton physics with the MuJoCo Warp solver and `newton_kamino` as Newton physics with the Kamino solver, the latter marked beta and limited to selected tasks and compatible assets (`IsaacLab-newton/docs/source/concepts/backends_and_presets.rst`, physics presets table). The maturity assessment for the Newton backend as a whole is stated plainly as beta, with multiple solvers and MuJoCo Warp as the primary validated path (`physics_backends.rst`, backend selection table). This is the honest reading of the state of the integration, and it should govern expectations, since the remaining four solver families are reachable by configuration but are not backed by validated task presets.

The division of labour among the solvers is worth recording, because it determines which of them this workspace will ever use. MuJoCo Warp and PhysX articulations both work in reduced joint coordinates, whereas Kamino solves rigid multi-body systems in maximal coordinates with constraints and reconciles body poses against reduced joint state through forward kinematics on reset, a path gated by `KaminoPADMMSolverCfg.use_fk_solver` (`IsaacLab-newton/docs/source/concepts/solver_differences.rst`, coordinates section). For legged locomotion in reduced coordinates with frictional foot contact, MuJoCo Warp is the correct target and Kamino is a comparator rather than a candidate. VBD and MPM address deformables and material point continua, and Featherstone and XPBD are, respectively, a classical articulated body solver and a position based dynamics solver, none of which this workspace requires.

## 4. MuJoCo Warp as the validated path

MuJoCo Warp is the GPU implementation of the MuJoCo contact and constraint model written against NVIDIA Warp, and Newton incorporates it as its primary backend [1]. Its execution model is the property that makes it attractive at the environment counts this workspace runs. A batch of simulations is specified by a world count, `nworld`, and most model fields carry a leading batch dimension of one so that a single compiled model is shared across every world, with selected fields expanded per world through the `batch_sizes` argument of `put_model` when domain randomisation requires it [4]. That is precisely the mechanism the workspace already documented for mjlab, where the equivalent of disabling PhysX physics replication is not a per environment articulation but a per world model field write (`context/mjlab.md:136`), and it is the mechanism on which section 10.13 rests.

Memory and computation are bounded explicitly rather than grown on demand, and the semantics of the two bounds differ in a way that is easy to misread. The reference states that it is possible for the number of contacts per world to exceed `nconmax` provided the total number of contacts across all worlds does not exceed `nworld` multiplied by `nconmax`, whereas the number of constraints per world is strictly limited by `njmax` [4]. So `nconmax` is a pooled budget and `njmax` is a per world ceiling, and a contact rich environment in an otherwise quiet batch will survive the former and fail against the latter. The reference further recommends that the operations comprising a step be captured as a CUDA graph when the step will be called many times [4], which IsaacLab does and which section 6 shows to be conditional on the actuator configuration.

The solver controls exposed through `MJWarpSolverCfg` are enumerable and are reproduced here because section 10.6 must choose values for all of them. The capacity bounds are `njmax`, default 300 at `mjwarp_manager_cfg.py:37`, and `nconmax`, default `None` at `:40`. The convergence controls are `iterations`, default 100 at `:43`, `ls_iterations`, default 50 at `:46`, and `tolerance`, default 1e-6 at `:122`, with `ls_parallel` at `:100` selecting a parallel line search. The model controls are `solver`, default the string `"newton"` at `:49`, `integrator`, default `"euler"` at `:52`, `impratio`, default 1.0 at `:87`, and `cone`, default `"pyramidal"` at `:90`. The collision controls are `ccd_iterations`, default 35 at `:93`, and `use_mujoco_contacts`, default `True` at `:108`, which selects between MuJoCo's internal contact path and Newton's own collision pipeline. Three diagnostic controls deserve note, `disable_contacts` at `:58`, `disable_sensors` at `:61`, and `save_to_mjcf` at `:81`, the last of which dumps the generated MuJoCo model and is the only reliable way to confirm what the importer actually produced.

Two of those defaults are traps for a reader migrating from PhysX. The first is `ccd_iterations`, which the documentation is explicit about, stating that it is a convex GJK and EPA convergence limit and not a PhysX style continuous collision detection switch, so a task that depended on PhysX continuous collision detection needs an independently validated timestep and collision strategy on Newton rather than a raised iteration count (`solver_differences.rst`, friction and contact section). The second is `cone`, where `"pyramidal"` is the fast choice and `"elliptic"` the more accurate one, and `impratio` above one improves soft contact stability (`IsaacLab-newton/docs/source/migration/migrating_to_isaaclab_3-0.rst`, Newton solver parameter table).

The substep arrangement is a third quantity with no PhysX analogue. MuJoCo Warp and Kamino each run `NewtonCfg.num_substeps` solver substeps per physics tick, so the solver timestep is the simulation timestep divided by the substep count, and more substeps change integration work rather than translating a PhysX iteration setting into a Newton equivalent (`solver_differences.rst`, timesteps section). Substeps do not by themselves refresh contacts drawn from Newton's pipeline. When that pipeline is active, `NewtonCfg.collision_decimation` re-collides within a physics tick only when it is positive and strictly less than `num_substeps`, and its default of zero collides once at the start of each tick, an arrangement that does not apply at all when MuJoCo Warp uses its internal contact path (`solver_differences.rst`, same section).

Feature parity against the reference MuJoCo implementation is stated in the negative and should be treated as a live constraint rather than a historical one. The reference says that MuJoCo Warp supports most of the main simulation features of MuJoCo with a few exceptions, and that it will raise an exception if asked to copy to device a model whose field values reference unsupported features [4]. The failure mode is therefore loud rather than silent, which is the desirable arrangement, but it means asset validation must happen before any training campaign rather than during one.

## 5. Speed and accuracy, what is published and what must be measured

An honest comparison must separate what has been published from what this workspace would have to establish for itself, because the published figures do not answer the question the workspace is asking.

What is published concerns MuJoCo Warp against MuJoCo XLA, the JAX implementation of the same model. The MuJoCo Warp announcement states that simulations of more complex scenes such as manipulation tasks run more than one hundred times faster in MuJoCo Warp than in MuJoCo XLA, without naming the hardware [5]. NVIDIA's later account quantifies the same comparison, reporting MuJoCo Warp as up to one hundred and fifty two times faster for locomotion and three hundred and thirteen times faster for manipulation than MuJoCo XLA on a GeForce RTX 4090, and adds that an RTX PRO 6000 of the Blackwell series is a further forty four percent faster than the 4090 for MuJoCo Warp [6]. These are large numbers and they are also irrelevant to the decision at hand, because MuJoCo XLA is not the incumbent here. They establish that MuJoCo Warp is the fast member of the MuJoCo family, not that it is faster than PhysX.

Exactly one published figure compares the two engines that matter. NVIDIA reports up to sixty five percent faster in-hand dexterous manipulation with MuJoCo Warp against PhysX when MuJoCo Warp is used as the IsaacLab next generation backend [6]. In-hand manipulation is a contact dense, low body count regime, and legged locomotion at four thousand and ninety six environments is neither of those, so the figure does not transfer. No published, methodologically stated comparison of steps per second between PhysX and Newton MuJoCo Warp for legged locomotion at a fixed environment count was found. The IsaacLab performance dashboard exists and filters by physics preset and renderer [7], and the mjlab project publishes nightly physics throughput at four thousand and ninety six parallel environments on an RTX 5090 [8], but both render their figures through client side script and neither carries a PhysX comparison in retrievable form. The correct position is therefore to state the absence and to supply the measurement, not to quote a number the document cannot source.

The measurement is available and cheap, because IsaacLab 3.0 consolidated the benchmark suite behind a backend agnostic entry point whose physics choice is a preset token. The runtime benchmark steps an environment with random actions and emits a typed bundle, the training benchmark dispatches a real training run for a selected reinforcement learning library, the startup benchmark profiles the five startup phases under `cProfile`, and a play benchmark new in 3.0 loads a checkpoint and measures inference throughput together with the policy's reward, episode length and success rate (`migrating_to_isaaclab_3-0.rst`, benchmark workflows section). The paired invocation is the same command twice with one token changed, `isaaclab benchmark runtime --task TASK presets=physx` against `presets=newton_mjwarp`, and the same for `benchmark training --rl_library rsl_rl`. Section 11 makes that pairing a gate rather than an afterthought.

Accuracy admits no such single number, and the documentation declines to offer one, arguing instead that PhysX and the Newton solvers can use the same USD asset while producing different trajectories because they construct contacts, represent state, stabilise constraints and allocate work differently (`solver_differences.rst`, opening). The structural differences are worth stating because they predict where the divergence will appear in this workspace. PhysX uses patch based Coulomb friction in which nearby contacts can be correlated into a friction patch, whereas MuJoCo Warp uses MuJoCo's contact model with a selectable pyramidal or elliptic friction cone and a frictional impedance ratio (`solver_differences.rst`, friction section). Restitution differs in kind rather than in degree, PhysX applying it through per material properties and a scene level bounce threshold with an optional stabilisation pass that can make reported contact sensor forces inaccurate, whereas MuJoCo Warp expresses contact compliance through its model and solver formulation (`solver_differences.rst`, restitution section). The consequence stated for porting is that equal material coefficients do not guarantee equal slip, grasp stability or contact counts, and that collision geometry and contact behaviour must be revalidated before friction is tuned at all (`solver_differences.rst`, friction porting implication).

For a workspace whose reward function is built almost entirely from foot contact events, that consequence is the dominant one. Every foot clearance, foot landing velocity, feet air time, feet slide, feet impact force and undesired contact term reads the contact sensor, and the contact model beneath those readings is changing. The accuracy question for this port is therefore not which engine is more accurate in the abstract but whether the gait the reward function selects under MuJoCo Warp is the gait it selected under PhysX, which is a question only a paired evaluation of the existing checkpoints can answer. Section 11 places that evaluation before any retraining.

## 6. The IsaacLab 3.0 multi-backend architecture

IsaacLab 3.0 is not IsaacLab 2.3 with a Newton option bolted on. The release separates backend specific simulation code from the core API and reorganises the source tree accordingly (`migrating_to_isaaclab_3-0.rst:30-31`). Where the incumbent checkout carries six packages under `IsaacLab/source/`, namely `isaaclab`, `isaaclab_assets`, `isaaclab_contrib`, `isaaclab_mimic`, `isaaclab_rl` and `isaaclab_tasks`, the `develop` tree carries fifteen, adding `isaaclab_newton`, `isaaclab_physx`, `isaaclab_ov`, `isaaclab_ovphysx`, `isaaclab_experimental`, `isaaclab_ppisp`, `isaaclab_tasks_experimental`, `isaaclab_teleop` and `isaaclab_visualizers`. The core `isaaclab` package keeps the backend agnostic contracts, and the three backend packages mirror its module structure so that `isaaclab.assets.articulation` has a counterpart at `isaaclab_physx.assets.articulation`, `isaaclab_newton.assets.articulation` and `isaaclab_ov.assets.articulation` (`IsaacLab-newton/docs/source/concepts/backend_architecture.rst`, factory dispatch section).

Three backends are offered behind those common interfaces. PhysX requires Isaac Sim and Omniverse Kit, uses the Temporal Gauss-Seidel solver by default with Projective Gauss-Seidel available, is described as the established reference, and carries static GPU scene capacities so that contact rich or large vectorised scenes may require explicit buffer sizing. Newton is Warp native, does not require Isaac Sim for kit-less execution, is marked beta, and offers multiple solvers with MuJoCo Warp as the primary validated path. OvPhysX is an experimental kit-less PhysX path configured primarily through USD, and the documentation warns that it must not be combined with a Kit runtime in the same process (`physics_backends.rst`, backend selection table and OvPhysX section). For this workspace the material consequence is that Newton makes an Isaac Sim free runtime possible, which removes the Omniverse dependency from every headless training container.

### 6.1 Factory dispatch

Selection happens once, at construction, and adds nothing to the simulation hot path. Every factory inherits from `isaaclab.utils.backend_utils.FactoryBase`, whose `__new__` maps the name of `SimulationContext.physics_manager` to one of the recognised backend keys, derives the backend module path from the factory's own module path, lazily imports that module and caches the implementation class in a registry (`backend_architecture.rst`, factory dispatch section). The layering beneath is consistent, a base class in `isaaclab` defines the public contract as `BaseArticulation` or `BaseContactSensor`, a factory class inherits from both `FactoryBase` and that base, and the backend packages supply the implementations, with the data classes following the same pattern so that `ArticulationData` is itself a `FactoryBase` subclass. The practical consequence for this workspace is large and favourable, that `from isaaclab.assets import Articulation, ArticulationCfg` continues to mean what it meant, and no import in `environments/` needs to name a backend.

Renderers and visualisers are selected independently of physics, the renderer from its own configuration through `isaaclab.renderers.Renderer` and the visualiser from a `visualizer_type` field, so that a camera environment can pair Newton physics with the Newton Warp renderer while the visualiser is chosen separately (`backend_architecture.rst`, portable renderer section). Live simulation data reaches renderers and visualisers through a common Warp native path, the physics manager exposing a `SceneDataBackend` which a `SceneDataProvider` owned by the simulation context converts and remaps for backend independent consumers (`backend_architecture.rst`, same section). Each backend implements `isaaclab.physics.PhysicsManager`, initialising from a `SimulationContext`, updating kinematics with `forward()`, advancing with `step()`, resetting with `reset()` and releasing with `close()`, and dispatching `PhysicsEvent` callbacks, `MODEL_INIT` during scene construction, `PHYSICS_READY` after physics initialisation and `STOP` during shutdown (`backend_architecture.rst`, physics manager lifecycle). That `MODEL_INIT` fires during scene construction and not thereafter is the fact section 10.13 turns upon.

### 6.2 Presets, the configuration mechanism

A preset is a named, tested configuration alternative selected by a Hydra token without a leading dash, so that `physics=newton_mjwarp`, `renderer=newton_renderer` and `presets=rgb` are appended to an otherwise unchanged command (`backends_and_presets.rst`, mental model and selector sections). Resolution proceeds in four stages, each preset configuration's own `default` choice, then global choices from `presets=`, then a preset targeted at a specific path such as `env.sim.physics=newton_mjwarp`, then scalar Hydra overrides such as `env.sim.dt=0.002`, and the critical semantic is that a preset replaces a complete configuration section rather than merging fields from two alternatives (`backends_and_presets.rst`, defaults section).

Task authors expose the alternatives by subclassing `isaaclab_tasks.utils.PresetCfg`, whose typed fields are the named variants and whose field called `default` is used when no override is given, and for a multi-backend task the wrapper belongs in the `physics` field of `isaaclab.sim.SimulationCfg` (`backends_and_presets.rst`, task author section). The standard names follow a convention, `isaacsim_physx` for the concrete Isaac Sim PhysX configuration and the default for tasks whose established default is PhysX, `physx` for automatic PhysX family selection through `PhysxAutoCfg`, `newton_mjwarp`, `newton_kamino` and `ovphysx`, with the renderer side offering `isaacsim_rtx`, `rtx`, `newton_renderer` and `ovrtx`. The documentation is emphatic on one point of discipline, that adding backend variants should not silently change a task's established default, and that a task retaining PhysX by default should declare `default: PhysxCfg = isaacsim_physx` (`migrating_to_isaaclab_3-0.rst`, step one important note). That discipline maps exactly onto this workspace's own rule that no change may alter the behaviour of an existing caller which did not ask for the change, and section 10.6 adopts it.

The canonical example for this workspace is upstream's own locomotion velocity task, whose `RoughPhysicsCfg` at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab_tasks/isaaclab_tasks/core/velocity/velocity_env_cfg.py:53-74` declares five alternatives, `isaacsim_physx` as `PhysxCfg(gpu_max_rigid_patch_count=10 * 2**15)`, `ovphysx` as the same capacity on `OvPhysxCfg`, `physx` as the automatic selector over those two, `newton_mjwarp` as a `NewtonCfg` carrying `MJWarpSolverCfg(njmax=1000, nconmax=300, cone="pyramidal", impratio=1.0, integrator="implicitfast", use_mujoco_contacts=False)` with `collision_cfg=NewtonCollisionPipelineCfg(max_triangle_pairs=2_500_000)`, `num_substeps=2` and `default_shape_cfg=NewtonShapeCfg(margin=0.0, ke=160000.0, kd=1100.0)`, and `newton_kamino` as `KaminoPADMMSolverCfg(max_contacts_per_world=64)`. Its `default` is `newton_mjwarp`, so upstream locomotion has already moved its default off PhysX. That configuration is the starting point for this workspace's own presets and not merely an illustration, since it is the only rough terrain legged locomotion preset upstream has validated.

### 6.3 ProxyArray, the data access change

Every public asset and sensor data property now returns `isaaclab.utils.warp.ProxyArray` rather than `torch.Tensor`, a wrapper over the underlying `wp.array` exposing a `.warp` accessor for the array itself and a cached, zero copy `.torch` accessor for a tensor view (`backend_architecture.rst`, portable asset interfaces). The stated intent is explicit data interop, so that a Warp kernel and a torch operation each receive the representation it wants without a copy.

The practical severity of this change is much lower than the migration guide implies, and the distinction is worth establishing precisely because it determines whether the port is a rewrite or a tidying. The implementation at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/utils/warp/proxy_array.py` carries a comprehensive deprecation bridge. `__getattr__` at `:209` forwards any attribute not defined on the class to the torch view, so tensor instance methods such as `clone()`, `cpu()`, `to()` and `float()` all work. `__getitem__` and `__setitem__` at `:227` and `:236` support the full range of torch indexing including boolean masks and fancy indexing. `__torch_function__` at `:262` unwraps proxies so that any `torch.*` function accepts one. The arithmetic and comparison operators are all implemented from `:294` onward. `__cuda_array_interface__` at `:182` and `__array_interface__` at `:194` are delegated to the warp array so that `wp.launch` accepts a proxy directly. Every one of those bridges emits a single process wide `DeprecationWarning` through `_warn_implicit` at `:250` and then stays silent.

Two qualifications must be carried forward. The first is that the class docstring at `:37-38` asserts that tensor instance methods such as `clone()` are not forwarded, which contradicts the `__getattr__` implementation twenty lines below it, and a contradiction between documentation and implementation is precisely the sort of thing that gets resolved in favour of the documentation in a later release. The bridge is therefore a migration aid with an announced removal and not a supported interface. The second is a silent semantic difference rather than a break, that `shape` and `dtype` at `:155` and `:160` return the warp shape and the warp dtype, not the torch ones, so a `wp.vec3f` array of N elements reports a shape of `(N,)` while its `.torch` view reports `(N, 3)`. Code that reads a component count off a data property's shape will read the wrong number without error.

### 6.4 The CUDA graph fast path

The single most consequential performance mechanism in the Newton integration is not a solver setting. `ManagerBasedEnv` queries `self.sim.physics_manager.handles_decimation()` at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/envs/manager_based_env.py:205`, and when it returns true the environment's `step()` folds the entire decimation loop into a single `sim.step()` call rather than looping `decimation` times, taking the branch at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/envs/manager_based_rl_env.py` guarded by `self._physics_handles_decimation`. The base `PhysicsManager` returns false at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/physics/physics_manager.py:580`, and `NewtonManager` returns `cls._is_all_graphable()` at `newton_manager.py:3515`, whose own conditions at `:3364-3366` are that an articulation took the `use_newton_actuators=True` branch and that either no actuator adapter was needed or every actuator in the adapter is CUDA graph safe.

The chain therefore runs from the actuator configuration to the step structure to the throughput. A task whose actuator models are all natively supported gets its whole policy step captured as one CUDA graph. A task carrying one unsupported explicit actuator model, or one that sets `SimulationCfg.use_newton_actuators=False`, does not, and reverts to a Python loop over substeps. Section 10.7 shows that this workspace currently sits on the wrong side of that boundary and what it costs to cross it.

## 7. API differences

The differences below are ordered by their reach across this workspace rather than by their position in the upstream guide. Each entry states the incumbent form, the 3.0 form and the enforcement, that is, whether the old form raises, warns or silently changes meaning.

### 7.1 Changes that silently change meaning

The quaternion convention changed from WXYZ to XYZW, so that the identity becomes `(0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 1.0)` rather than `(1.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0)`, and the stated rationale is alignment with Warp, PhysX and Newton, which removes internal conversions (`migrating_to_isaaclab_3-0.rst`, quaternion format section). Every quaternion function in `isaaclab.utils.math` now expects and returns XYZW, among them `quat_mul`, `quat_apply`, `quat_from_euler_xyz`, `euler_xyz_from_quat`, `quat_from_matrix` and `matrix_from_quat`, and `convert_quat` has been removed outright. Code that reaches a quaternion only through those functions is self consistent and needs no change. Code that indexes quaternion components itself produces wrong rotations without any error, and the same applies to hard coded values in asset configurations, goal poses and controller targets. Two tools exist, a static scanner at `scripts/tools/find_quaternions.py` which parses Python by abstract syntax tree and offers an interactive fix mode, carrying an explicit warning not to run it over IsaacLab's own packages, and a runtime detector enabled by `export WARN_ON_TORCH_QUATF_ACCESS=1` which makes every `.torch` read of a `wp.quatf` typed proxy emit a warning naming the call site, implemented at `proxy_array.py:122-147`.

Contact force property semantics changed, and this is the most dangerous entry in the table for this workspace because it changes numbers rather than raising. In 3.0 `net_forces_w` denotes the total contact force, normal plus friction. Newton reports that quantity directly, whereas PhysX and OvPhysX cannot compute it and instead return the corresponding normal force quantity and warn. The same substitution applies to `net_forces_w_history` against `net_normal_forces_w_history`, to `force_matrix_w` against `normal_force_matrix_w`, to `force_matrix_w_history` against its normal counterpart, and to `friction_forces_w`, for which PhysX returns only the filtered `friction_force_matrix_w` (`migrating_to_isaaclab_3-0.rst`, contact force property names). PhysX raises `NotImplementedError` when `net_friction_forces_w` is accessed at all. The explicit names `net_normal_forces_w`, `net_friction_forces_w`, `normal_force_matrix_w` and `friction_force_matrix_w` and their history variants are identical across backends and are the correct target. The friction channels are gated by the new `ContactSensorCfg.track_friction_forces`, default false at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/sensors/contact_sensor/contact_sensor_cfg.py:39`.

Explicit actuator groups now keep the solver effort limit. In 2.x IsaacLab raised the solver effort limit to one times ten to the ninth on joints driven by an explicit actuator so that only the model clipped the effort, whereas 3.0 keeps the authored or configured `joint_effort_limit`, so effort submitted by an explicit model is clipped a second time by the solver, and the remedy is to set `joint_effort_limit` at least as large as `actuator_effort_limit` (`migrating_to_isaaclab_3-0.rst`, actuator limit behaviour change). For a workspace whose actuator parameters were identified against hardware, a second undeclared clip is a quiet falsification of the identification.

`ArticulationData.gear_ratio` was removed rather than deprecated, on the grounds that it was legacy `DCMotor` telemetry that was no longer updated and always read one (`migrating_to_isaaclab_3-0.rst`, actuator property relocation note).

### 7.3 Changes that warn

The write method split is the entry where the upstream guide and the upstream code disagree, and the code governs. The guide states that the previous `write_*_to_sim(data, env_ids)` methods have been removed (`migrating_to_isaaclab_3-0.rst`, write method index and mask split). They have not. `write_root_pose_to_sim` survives at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/assets/rigid_object/base_rigid_object.py:757` as a forwarder that emits a `DeprecationWarning` and calls `write_root_pose_to_sim_index`, and `write_joint_state_to_sim` survives identically on both concrete backends, at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab_physx/isaaclab_physx/assets/articulation/articulation.py:4763` where it forwards to the separate position and velocity index writers, and at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab_newton/isaaclab_newton/assets/articulation/articulation.py:3864` where it forwards to a combined `write_joint_state_to_sim_index`. The new contract is that `write_*_to_sim_index(data, env_ids)` takes partial data of shape `(len(env_ids), ...)` while `write_*_to_sim_mask(data, env_mask)` takes full data of shape `(num_envs, ...)` with a boolean selector, and the arguments are keyword only. Note that the combined joint state writer exists on Newton but not on PhysX, so the backend portable replacement is the pair of position and velocity index writers.

Actuator ownership moved from `Articulation` to a backend neutral `isaaclab.actuators.ActuatorCollection` reached as `Articulation.actuators`, with keyword only setters. The command setters move, `set_joint_position_target` to `actuators.target_command.set_position_index`, `set_joint_velocity_target` to `set_velocity_index` and `set_joint_effort_target` to `set_effort_index`, each with index and mask variants. The telemetry properties move, `data.joint_pos_target` to `actuators.target_command.position`, `data.joint_vel_target` to `.velocity`, `data.joint_effort_target` to `.effort`, `data.computed_torque` to `actuators.computed_effort` and `data.applied_torque` to `actuators.applied_effort`. `ArticulationData.soft_joint_vel_limits` explicitly stays where it is. Group topology became construction time state, so assignment to or deletion from `robot.actuators` at runtime raises `TypeError` (`migrating_to_isaaclab_3-0.rst`, actuator collection section).

Actuator limit fields were renamed to joint qualified names, `effort_limit` to `actuator_effort_limit` as the model's rated limit, `effort_limit_sim` to `joint_effort_limit` backed by `ArticulationData.joint_effort_limits`, and `velocity_limit_sim` to `joint_velocity_limit` backed by `ArticulationData.joint_vel_limits`, with the former names accepted through the 3.x line and removed in 4.0. A group carrying both a deprecated name and its canonical replacement warns and selects the canonical value when the two agree and raises `ValueError` when they conflict. Six runtime group properties were removed rather than renamed, `effort_limit_sim`, `velocity_limit_sim`, `armature`, `friction`, `dynamic_friction` and `viscous_friction`, each replaced by a read from articulation data and a matching indexed writer, and the dynamic friction pair is PhysX and OvPhysX only because Newton has no corresponding joint property (`migrating_to_isaaclab_3-0.rst`, actuator limit migration).

`root_physx_view` is deprecated in favour of `root_view`, whose concrete type is backend specific, and the documentation is careful to note that `get_material_properties()` reads each rigid shape's static friction, dynamic friction and restitution through the PhysX Tensor API while Newton selections and OvPhysX bindings use different access methods (`migrating_to_isaaclab_3-0.rst`, migration example commentary).

`XformPrimView` became `FrameView` with the old name kept as an alias, and the rename runs through the hierarchy, `BaseXformPrimView` to `BaseFrameView`, `UsdXformPrimView` to `UsdFrameView`, `FabricXformPrimView` to `isaaclab_physx.sim.views.FabricFrameView` and `NewtonSiteXformPrimView` to `isaaclab_newton.sim.views.NewtonSiteFrameView`, with the `FrameView` factory dispatching by active backend.

The old full state `Imu` sensor was renamed `Pva`, for pose, velocity and acceleration, and a new lightweight `Imu` reporting only `ang_vel_b` and `lin_acc_b` took its name. The `gravity_bias` field was removed from both, `Pva` reporting raw kinematic acceleration without gravity because it derives acceleration by finite differencing velocities, and `Imu` unconditionally including gravity queried from the simulation, which matches a real accelerometer. The observation functions renamed with the sensor, `imu_orientation` and `imu_projected_gravity` becoming `pva_orientation` and `pva_projected_gravity` (`migrating_to_isaaclab_3-0.rst`, IMU to PVA migration).

The schema configuration classes were split into solver common bases in `isaaclab.sim.schemas` and backend specific subclasses in `isaaclab_physx.sim.schemas` and `isaaclab_newton.sim.schemas`, with the 2.x names kept as deprecated aliases scheduled for removal in 4.0. `RigidBodyPropertiesCfg` becomes `RigidBodyBaseCfg` plus `PhysxRigidBodyPropertiesCfg`, `JointDrivePropertiesCfg` becomes `JointDriveBaseCfg` plus `PhysxJointDrivePropertiesCfg`, `CollisionPropertiesCfg` and `ArticulationRootPropertiesCfg` follow the same pattern, `RigidBodyMaterialCfg` becomes `RigidBodyMaterialBaseCfg` plus `PhysxRigidBodyMaterialCfg`, the `MeshCollisionPropertiesCfg` family becomes `MeshCollisionBaseCfg` plus a `Physx*` family, and the two tendon configurations become PhysX only. Two fields on `JointDriveBaseCfg` were renamed so that the snake case name maps identically onto the USD attribute, `max_velocity` to `max_joint_velocity` for `physxJoint:maxJointVelocity` and `max_effort` to `max_force` for `drive:<axis>:physics:maxForce`, and setting both the old and the new field on one instance is silent, the canonical field winning (`migrating_to_isaaclab_3-0.rst`, schema configuration refactor).

`RigidObjectCollection` renamed its `object_*` surface to `body_*` throughout, both methods and roughly forty data properties, with one collision worth noting, that `write_object_velocity_to_sim` and `write_object_com_velocity_to_sim` both map onto `write_body_com_velocity_to_sim`.

The ray caster family moved from a PyTorch and USD backend to native Warp kernels, `attach_yaw_only` being deprecated in favour of `ray_alignment` taking `"base"`, `"yaw"` or `"world"`, the kernel `raycast_dynamic_meshes_kernel` gaining a leading `env_mask` argument which the Python wrapper injects, and the `RayCaster.meshes` class cache being rekeyed from `prim_path` to `(prim_path, device)` so that a mesh built on one device is not reused by a sensor on another.

`ViewerCfg` and the `viewer` field on the environment configurations are deprecated behind a one release shim, replaced by `sim.default_visualizer_cfg` for eye and lookat and by `sim.visualizer_cfgs` for the visualiser list, with asset tracking expressed as `KitVisualizerCfg(origin_type="asset", origin_track_path="robot")` in place of the former `origin_type="asset_root"` and `asset_name`. `ViewportCameraController` is deprecated with a shim that raises a `DeprecationWarning` at construction, camera tracking having moved into `KitVisualizer` (`migrating_to_isaaclab_3-0.rst`, viewport camera configuration).

### 7.4 The new API surface worth adopting

Several 3.0 additions are not migrations but capabilities this workspace should take up deliberately.

Backend neutral body property access replaces the PhysX tensor API for the properties this workspace randomises. Reads are `asset.data.body_mass.torch`, `asset.data.body_inertia.torch` of shape `(num_envs, num_bodies, 9)` and `asset.data.body_com_pose_b.torch`, and writes are `asset.set_masses_index`, `asset.set_inertias_index` and `asset.set_coms_index`, each taking the property, `body_ids` and `env_ids`, as used by the stock event terms at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/envs/mdp/events.py:766`, `:919` and `:992`.

Joint and body ordering became configurable, `ArticulationCfg.joint_ordering` and `ArticulationCfg.body_ordering` accepting `None` for backend native order, `"physx"`, `"mjwarp"`, `"robot_schema"` or an explicit complete name permutation, with `isaaclab.assets.apply_articulation_ordering_preset(robot_cfg, "mjwarp")` setting both at once (`joint_and_body_ordering.rst`, public and backend order). The documentation warns that both must be set, since setting only `joint_ordering` leaves bodies in backend order and mismatches a checkpoint whose body vectors follow the source convention. `None` is the zero conversion default, whereas a non-identity map on Newton records a fixed per step reorder of joint positions and velocities and body poses and velocities inside the CUDA graph captured region, so it carries a baseline per step cost independent of access frequency.

The environment configuration gained `compute_final_obs`, which captures the terminal observation before a same step autoreset and publishes it as `extras["final_obs"]` for value bootstrapping, defaulting to false so that the previous behaviour is preserved (`IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/envs/manager_based_rl_env_cfg.py:45-55`). Section 10.10 shows this to be the sanctioned replacement for a custom environment subclass this workspace maintains.

The environment configuration also gained `play_mode()`, a method carrying the playback overrides that a task previously duplicated, used upstream at `velocity_env_cfg.py` to disable the terrain level initialisation, shrink the terrain generator and remove the pushing events.

Newton's native data API is available for code willing to own its own lifecycle. `NewtonManager.get_model()`, `get_state_0()`, `get_control()` and `get_contacts()` return the engine owned objects, assets expose a `root_view` which is a `newton.selection.ArticulationView`, and generic `get_attribute` and `set_attribute` calls reach model attributes by string name, after which the manager must be notified with `add_model_change(ModelFlags.X)`, while state writes that change generalised coordinates require `invalidate_fk()` followed by `forward()` (`IsaacLab-newton/docs/source/concepts/native-physics-api/newton.rst`). Solvers may swap state buffers so `get_state_0()` must be reacquired, and a selection survives a buffer swap because it describes model indices but must be recreated after a model rebuild.

## 8. Feature differences across the three backends

The following are the differences that bear on this workspace, established from the develop tree rather than from the beta release notes, which are behind on at least one material point.

Newton implements every sensor this workspace uses. The sensor directories under `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab_newton/isaaclab_newton/sensors/` carry `contact_sensor`, `frame_transformer`, `imu`, `joint_wrench`, `pva` and `ray_caster`, matching the PhysX set except for the camera, which lives only in the core package because rendering is selected independently of physics. The Newton contact sensor supports filtered reporting against filter objects, which the three K-Scale foot pair sensors require, and its configuration warns about and disables exactly two base fields, `track_contact_points` when no filter objects are configured and `max_contact_data_count_per_prim` unconditionally, at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab_newton/isaaclab_newton/sensors/contact_sensor/contact_sensor_cfg.py:35-48`. Neither is set by this workspace.

Newton implements the whole non-tendon articulation surface. Every `NotImplementedError` in `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab_newton/isaaclab_newton/assets/articulation/articulation.py` is a fixed or spatial tendon method, and the two explicit messages at `:3545` and `:3678` both read that spatial tendons are not supported yet, with the data class recording at `articulation_data.py:1516` that spatial tendons are not supported. The data class raises only on four fixed tendon and four spatial tendon properties, at `:548`, `:557`, `:566`, `:588`, `:597`, `:606` and `:615`. This workspace uses no tendons.

Domain randomisation support is now broad but not uniform, and the differences are recorded in the stock event terms themselves. Mass randomisation works on both backends through `set_masses_index`, with the `recompute_inertia` flag scaling the default inertia tensors by the mass ratio at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/envs/mdp/events.py:766-781`. Inertia randomisation works on both through `set_inertias_index`. Centre of mass randomisation works on both but carries a Newton caveat stated in the term's own docstring at `events.py:930-936`, that on Newton with MuJoCo Warp runtime centre of mass changes may cause simulation instability because `notify_model_changed(BODY_INERTIAL_PROPERTIES)` does not fully recompute the mass matrix after `body_ipos` changes, and that the term should be used with caution until that is fixed upstream, and additionally that Newton takes a position only vector where PhysX takes the full seven element pose. Material randomisation works on Newton and is not limited by buckets, the Newton implementation at `events.py:254-266` recording that Newton can assign arbitrary friction and restitution per shape but uses a single friction coefficient, so that `dynamic_friction_range` and `num_buckets` are ignored, and that under the Kamino solver contact materials are deduplicated globally by the pair of friction and restitution at build time so that no per environment variation is possible at all. Joint parameter randomisation works on Newton for static friction, viscous friction, armature and position limits, but Newton has no dynamic joint friction, the term branching on the backend at `events.py:1693` and reading `joint_dynamic_friction_coeff` only under PhysX at `:1703`. Fixed tendon randomisation is unavailable on Newton, the term raising with four distinct messages at `events.py:1980`, `:2018`, `:2035` and `:2051`.

Joint friction carries different units on the two backends and the base contract says so, warning at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/assets/articulation/base_articulation_data.py:473-477` that the physical meaning and units of `joint_friction_coeff` depend on the concrete backend and solver and that values must not be assumed comparable. Newton stores an absolute joint friction force or torque despite the name, copied by the MuJoCo Warp solver into MuJoCo's `dof_frictionloss`, so that setting it to 0.2 configures a dry friction loss limit of 0.2 newton metres on a revolute degree of freedom (`IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab_newton/isaaclab_newton/assets/articulation/articulation_data.py:406-418`), and `joint_viscous_friction_coeff` is passive joint damping in newton metre seconds per radian (`:421-427`). PhysX stores a static friction effort in the same units from Isaac Sim 5.0 onward and a legacy unitless coefficient before that (`IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab_physx/isaaclab_physx/assets/articulation/articulation_data.py:421-428`). On the Isaac Sim versions this workspace would target, therefore, the two happen to agree, but the agreement is a coincidence of version rather than a contract.

Velocity limits are the sharpest feature gap and the one most likely to change learned behaviour. The asset preparation guide states that `actuator_velocity_limit` is the actuator's physical rated speed which IsaacLab can use in actuator or task logic, observations, rewards and terminations, but that MuJoCo Warp does not parse it into the solver model or enforce it while stepping, and that `joint_velocity_limit` requests a solver side hard clamp which IsaacLab writes to Newton's `Model.joint_velocity_limit` but which MuJoCo Warp drops while Kamino honours it, so that a task needing a speed bound under MuJoCo Warp must implement and validate it in task or control logic (`IsaacLab-newton/docs/source/how-to/prepare_asset_for_newton.rst`, validate actuators and limits). This workspace sets `velocity_limit` at twenty two sites and `velocity_limit_sim` at ten, and under MuJoCo Warp none of the latter will be enforced by the solver.

Native actuator execution is the default and is restrictive. With `SimulationCfg.use_newton_actuators` true, each supported explicit actuator configuration becomes a `NewtonActuator` USD prim executed by Newton inside the solver, or by PhysX and OvPhysX through a shared host adapter during `write_data_to_sim()`. Exactly six configurations are supported, `IdealPDActuatorCfg` mapping to `NewtonPDControlAPI` with `NewtonMaxEffortClampingAPI`, `DCMotorCfg` to `NewtonPDControlAPI` with `NewtonDCMotorClampingAPI`, `DelayedPDActuatorCfg` to ideal PD with `NewtonActuatorDelayAPI`, `RemotizedPDActuatorCfg` to delayed PD with `NewtonPositionBasedClampingAPI`, and the two actuator network configurations to `NewtonNeuralControlAPI`. The documentation warns that every explicit actuator configuration must be supported and that an unsupported one raises an error before native authoring, and notes separately that under native execution delay is fixed because the schema stores only `max_delay`, so `min_delay` is ignored and a `DelayedPDActuator` no longer randomises its delay between resets (`IsaacLab-newton/docs/source/concepts/actuators.rst`, native actuators). On Newton, `robot.actuators[name]` returns the Newton actuator object rather than an IsaacLab model, Newton merges structurally identical joints into one actuator so several groups may share an object, and group scoped access in public joint order is available through `read_group_parameter` and `write_group_parameter` from `isaaclab.actuators.newton`.

For explicit actuators the solver stiffness and damping are zero on every backend because the actuator model owns the gains, so reading `data.joint_stiffness` or `data.joint_damping` on an explicitly actuated joint returns zero (`actuators.rst`, actuator pipeline). This workspace reads both as privileged observations at eleven sites each, and on those joints it has been reading zeros already under PhysX, which is a pre-existing condition rather than a porting consequence, but it becomes visible when the observation is revalidated.

Two features remain PhysX only and this workspace uses neither, the deformable object path and the surface gripper, the latter having moved to `isaaclab_physx.assets`. Continuous collision detection is PhysX only in substance, exposed through `PhysxCfg.enable_ccd` but disabled by IsaacLab whenever GPU dynamics is enabled (`solver_differences.rst`, friction and contact).

## 10. The change report

The changes are ordered by the depth of the rework rather than by the count of affected lines, since a one line change that invalidates a run archive costs more than a hundred mechanical substitutions. Each item states the required change, cites the affected code, and names the choice that honours the workspace rule that no change may alter the behaviour of an existing caller which did not ask for the change.

### 10.1 Installation and the version boundary

The upstream guidance is to start from a fresh 3.0 checkout and a Python 3.12 environment rather than upgrading the packages inside an existing 2.x environment (`migrating_to_isaaclab_3-0.rst:45`). The incumbent environment is Python 3.11, evident from the bytecode caches under `co_optimisation/co_optimisation/__pycache__/` which carry the `cpython-311` tag, so the interpreter moves as well as the library. The dependency set moves with it, `IsaacLab-newton/pyproject.toml` pinning `newton[sim]==1.6.0rc1`, `warp-lang==1.17.0`, `torch>=2.11`, `numpy>=2`, `gymnasium>=1.2.0`, `rsl-rl-lib==5.4.1` and `usd-exchange==2.3.0`, and requiring Python at least 3.12 and below 3.13. The `develop` branch targets Isaac Sim 6.1 (`IsaacLab-newton/README.md`, version dependency table).

Three consequences follow that the workspace must plan for rather than discover. The `rsl_rl` pin at 5.4.1 must be reconciled against the workspace's own `rsl_rl/` submodule and against the three algorithm implementations that subclass its runners and its PPO, namely `himloco/himloco/algorithms/him_ppo.py`, `co_optimisation/co_optimisation/algorithms/copt_ppo.py` and `co_optimisation/co_optimisation/runners/copt_on_policy_runner.py`. The Newton pin is a release candidate, so the port targets a prerelease and must record the exact version in each run's dumped configuration. And the kit-less mode is reached with a bare `uv run isaaclab`, whereas `--extra isaacsim` is required for the PhysX path and `--extra ovphysx` for OvPhysX, so the container images bifurcate, a small Newton only image and the existing full Isaac Sim image.

The recommendation is to build the Newton environment beside the incumbent rather than over it, since the whole value of a multi-backend port is the ability to reproduce a historical PhysX run, and that ability is lost the moment the 2.3.0 environment is destroyed.

### 10.2 Import renames

Thirteen sites must change `AdditiveGaussianNoiseCfg` to `GaussianNoiseCfg` and `AdditiveUniformNoiseCfg` to `UniformNoiseCfg`. These are aliases of the same classes in 2.3.0, so the substitution is semantically null and can be made in the incumbent tree first, which removes thirteen breakages from the port at zero behavioural cost. This is the cheapest available step and should be taken immediately regardless of the port's schedule.

Four sites must change `isaaclab_tasks.manager_based.locomotion.velocity` to `isaaclab_tasks.core.velocity`, of which the consequential one is the star import at `mdp/__init__.py:1`. Because that import supplies every stock term by name, a wrong path there fails loudly at import rather than silently at runtime, which is the desirable arrangement.

Seven sites should move `isaaclab.sim.RigidBodyMaterialCfg` to `isaaclab_physx.sim.spawners.materials.PhysxRigidBodyMaterialCfg` where the material is bound to a PhysX only property, or to `isaaclab.sim.spawners.materials.RigidBodyMaterialBaseCfg` where only universal fields are used. The alias survives in 3.0, so this is not urgent, but the alias is scheduled for removal in 4.0 and the split is exactly the distinction the port needs to make explicit anyway, since `friction_combine_mode` and `restitution_combine_mode` are PhysX concepts that MuJoCo Warp does not share.

### 10.3 The quaternion convention

Two classes of site exist and they require opposite treatment.

Sites that reach a quaternion only through `isaaclab.utils.math` are self consistent and require no change. These are the eight `quat_apply` calls, eight `quat_apply_inverse` calls, four `yaw_quat` calls and three `euler_xyz_from_quat` calls in `rewards.py` at lines 95, 481, 800, 1095, 1203, 1225, 1316, 1318, 1325, 1396, 1416 and 1433, and the single `yaw_quat` call at `play.py:446`. Under 3.0 the data and the function change convention together, so the composition is unchanged. This should be verified rather than assumed, by running with `WARN_ON_TORCH_QUATF_ACCESS=1` set and triaging every reported call site, which is the procedure the migration guide prescribes.

Sites that unpack quaternion components by index break silently and must all change. There are three in the analysis layer, at `scripts/analysis/stats.py:354` inside `_yaw_from_quaternion`, at `:752` in the per foot orientation block and at `:1001` in the base orientation block, each of the form `w, x, y, z = quat[..., 0], quat[..., 1], quat[..., 2], quat[..., 3]`. There are three in the self tests, at `scripts/analysis/kscale_feet_heading_selftest.py:281` and `:294` and at `scripts/analysis/kscale_feet_distance_v2_selftest.py:137`, which are shim implementations of `euler_xyz_from_quat` and `quat_apply` written in the Hamilton convention. And there is one in the unit tests, at `environments/tests/rewards_foot_landing_vel_v3_test.py:29` with its identity at `:113`.

The dump archive is the harder problem and it is not solved by editing `stats.py`. `play.py:370` appends `asset.data.root_quat_w` and `:457` appends `asset.data.body_quat_w`, and `stats.py:122` and `:124` document the resulting arrays as WXYZ. After the port those arrays are XYZW, and nothing in the dump distinguishes the two, so `stats.py` would silently misread every pre-port dump or every post-port dump depending on which convention it assumed. The workspace has been here before, the gait statistics pipeline having already rendered every dump written before 2026-08-06 unreadable, and the remedy then was to replay. The remedy now should be cheaper. The recommendation is to write an explicit convention field into the dump, for instance `quaternion_order` taking the value `"wxyz"` or `"xyzw"`, and to make `stats.py` branch on it with the absence of the field read as `"wxyz"` so that every existing dump keeps its present meaning. That is precisely the optional argument whose default reproduces the old behaviour that the workspace's own rule prefers, and it is set explicitly only by the new writer.

The defect left standing is that a dump written between the port and the addition of the convention field is unlabelled and therefore ambiguous. The field must land in `play.py` in the same change as the convention flip, not after it.

Hard coded quaternions in asset configurations were searched for and none was found outside the tests, so `scripts/tools/find_quaternions.py` should be run over `environments/` and `co_optimisation/` to confirm the negative rather than to effect a fix. The guide's warning against running it over IsaacLab's own packages applies, so the `--path` argument must name only this workspace's directories.

### 10.4 ProxyArray

No site in this workspace requires a change in order to run under 3.0, because the deprecation bridge covers every pattern used, the attribute forwarding at `proxy_array.py:209` covering the twelve `.to(device)`, ten `.cpu()`, three `.unsqueeze()` and several `.clone()` calls, the indexing at `:227` covering the fifteen contact history slices, and `__torch_function__` at `:262` covering the `torch.*` calls. This is the single largest divergence between the apparent and the actual cost of the port and it should be stated plainly, that the ProxyArray change is a deprecation with a removal horizon rather than a break.

That said, the bridge should not be relied upon, for two reasons. The class docstring at `:37-38` contradicts the implementation on whether instance methods are forwarded, and the guide states that both the `__torch_function__` bridge and the `wp.to_torch(proxy)` shim will be removed in a future release. The recommendation is therefore to append `.torch` at every site as ordinary maintenance, in a single mechanical pass, with the pass deferred until after the behavioural gates in section 11 have been passed, so that a functional regression is never confounded with a mechanical one.

Three refinements deserve to be taken up rather than merely tolerated. Where a reward term computes a reduction over a data property, the warp array can be passed straight to a Warp kernel through `__cuda_array_interface__`, which removes the torch round trip entirely, and `rewards.py` is where that would pay. Where a site needs a component count, `.torch.shape` must be used rather than `.shape`, because the latter reports the warp shape. And the twelve sites that call `.to(device)` on a property already resident on the simulation device are performing a null transfer whose only cost is the deprecation path, and they should simply drop the call.

### 10.6 Simulation configuration and the presets

Three sites set `self.sim.physx.gpu_max_rigid_patch_count = 2**19`, at `environments/environments/tasks/locomotion/cfg/quadruped/base_env_cfg.py:1502` and at `environments/environments/tasks/locomotion/cfg/SF/kscale_base_env_cfg.py:1066` and `:1096`. The attribute no longer exists. Two sites set the removed render configuration, `scripts/rsl_rl/train.py:135` assigning `env_cfg.sim.render.rendering_mode = "balanced"` and `:136` assigning `env_cfg.sim.render.dlss_mode = 0`.

The structural change is to introduce a preset class per task family and to place it in the `physics` field of `SimulationCfg`. Following the workspace rule that an addition must not alter an existing caller, and the identical upstream instruction that adding backend variants should not silently change a task's established default, each preset must declare `default` as the PhysX variant that reproduces the present run exactly. The shape is therefore

```python
@configclass
class TronSolefootPhysicsCfg(PresetCfg):
    isaacsim_physx = PhysxCfg(gpu_max_rigid_patch_count=2**19)
    newton_mjwarp = NewtonCfg(
        solver_cfg=MJWarpSolverCfg(
            njmax=1000, nconmax=300, cone="pyramidal", impratio=1.0,
            integrator="implicitfast", use_mujoco_contacts=False,
        ),
        collision_cfg=NewtonCollisionPipelineCfg(max_triangle_pairs=2_500_000),
        num_substeps=2,
        debug_mode=False,
        default_shape_cfg=NewtonShapeCfg(margin=0.0, ke=160000.0, kd=1100.0),
    )
    default = isaacsim_physx
```

with the Newton member seeded from upstream's own rough terrain locomotion values at `velocity_env_cfg.py:57-70` rather than from a guess, since those are the only validated legged locomotion figures available. The `gpu_max_rigid_patch_count` value carries into the PhysX member unchanged, which preserves the present behaviour precisely, and it has no Newton counterpart because Newton bounds contacts by `njmax` and `nconmax` instead.

Four quantities then need choosing for the Newton member and none of them has a defensible default. `num_substeps` sets the solver timestep as `sim.dt` divided by the count, so at the present `sim.dt` of 0.005 a substep count of two gives a solver step of 2.5 milliseconds. `njmax` is a strict per world constraint ceiling while `nconmax` is a pooled contact budget across worlds [4], so the busiest environment governs the former and the batch mean governs the latter, and both must be measured at the busiest reset rather than estimated, which is what `NewtonCfg.debug_mode` exists for. `cone` trades the fast pyramidal approximation against the accurate elliptic one, and for a foot contact task the elliptic cone is the physically defensible choice and the pyramidal one the throughput choice, so the comparison belongs in the validation matrix rather than in a default. And `impratio` above one improves soft contact stability, which a foot with a compliant sole may require.

`sim.render_interval` survives and is set to two or three times the decimation across the configurations. `sim.log_dir` survives at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/sim/simulation_cfg.py:120`. The two removed render settings move into a renderer configuration, `dlss_mode` onto `IsaacRtxRendererGlobalSettingsCfg` at `isaac_rtx_renderer_cfg.py:48`, and since both were set purely for training video quality they should be dropped from `train.py` and expressed in the renderer preset instead, which also stops a PhysX only rendering setting from being assigned on a Newton run.

Five new `SimulationCfg` fields should be set deliberately rather than left at their defaults, `physics_material` which upstream binds to the terrain material at `velocity_env_cfg.py`, `use_newton_actuators` whose value section 10.7 determines, `visualizer_cfgs` and `default_visualizer_cfg` which section 10.11 determines, and `logging_level`.

### 10.7 Actuators, and the custom identified model

This is the item with the largest ratio of consequence to line count.

`environments/environments/actuators/actuator_pd.py:18` declares `IdentifiedActuator(DCMotor)`, whose constructor at `:23-25` calls `self._parse_joint_parameter` three times to resolve `friction_static`, `activation_vel` and `friction_dynamic`, and whose `compute` at `:28-40` calls the `DCMotor` implementation and then subtracts `friction_static * tanh(joint_vel / activation_vel) + friction_dynamic * joint_vel` from the joint efforts, assigns `applied_effort` and nulls the position and velocity channels. Its configuration is `IdentifiedActuatorCfg(DCMotorCfg)` at `environments/environments/actuators/actuator_cfg.py:14`. Four asset configuration modules use it, `solefoot_identified_cfg.py` for the four TRON1 joint groups, `sd_brs1_identified_cfg.py` for the BRS1 groups, `quadruped_identified_cfg.py` and `kscale_identified_cfg.py`. It is, in other words, the actuator model of every identified robot in the workspace.

Two 3.0 problems arise. The mechanical one is that `_parse_joint_parameter` was removed, so the three constructor lines must become `resolve_joint_parameter(self.cfg.friction_static, 0.0, joint_names, num_envs, device)` and so forth. The structural one is that with the default `use_newton_actuators=True`, every explicit actuator configuration must be one of the six natively supported ones or an error is raised before native authoring, and `IdentifiedActuatorCfg` is not among them.

Two resolutions exist and they are not equivalent.

The first is to set `SimulationCfg.use_newton_actuators=False`, which restores the IsaacLab execution path and lets `IdentifiedActuator` run as written. The cost is stated in section 6.4 and is severe. `NewtonManager._is_all_graphable()` returns false when the Newton actuator path was never activated, at `newton_manager.py:3364-3365`, so `handles_decimation()` returns false at `:3515`, so the environment does not fold the decimation loop into a single captured step, so the principal throughput mechanism of the Newton backend is forfeited. Choosing this path is choosing to port to Newton and decline its performance.

The second is to express the friction model with Newton's native joint properties and use a stock `DCMotorCfg`. The mapping is close to exact. Newton's `joint_friction_coeff` is an absolute joint friction torque copied into MuJoCo's `dof_frictionloss`, so that 0.2 configures a dry friction loss limit of 0.2 newton metres on a revolute degree of freedom (`isaaclab_newton/.../articulation_data.py:406-418`), which is what `friction_static` is in newton metres. Newton's `joint_viscous_friction_coeff` is passive joint damping in newton metre seconds per radian (`:421-427`), which is what `friction_dynamic` is. What has no counterpart is `activation_vel`, the velocity at which the smoothed Coulomb term saturates, because MuJoCo applies its own regularisation to `dof_frictionloss` rather than exposing a tanh width. The two models therefore agree in the limit of small `activation_vel` and diverge near zero velocity, which is exactly the regime a standing biped occupies.

The recommendation is the second resolution, with the divergence treated as a quantity to measure rather than to assume away. Concretely, keep `IdentifiedActuatorCfg` and `IdentifiedActuator` untouched so that every PhysX run reproduces, and add a Newton variant of each identified actuator group expressed as a stock `DCMotorCfg` whose `joint_friction_coeff` and `joint_viscous_friction_coeff` carry the identified values, selected by the same `PresetCfg` that selects the backend. That satisfies the workspace rule, since no existing caller changes, and it keeps the fast path. The identification data must then be replayed against the Newton variant to establish whether the loss of the tanh width matters at the velocities the gait actually visits, and the result recorded.

The defect left standing is that `IdentifiedActuator` remains unusable under Newton with the fast path enabled, and that anyone selecting `use_newton_actuators=False` to use it silently loses the CUDA graph capture with no warning from IsaacLab. That should be recorded in the actuator module's own docstring so that the next reader does not rediscover it.

Three further actuator changes are mechanical but numerous. Fifty eight configuration sites use the deprecated limit names, twenty two of `velocity_limit`, twenty two of `effort_limit`, ten of `velocity_limit_sim` and four of `effort_limit_sim`, and they map onto `actuator_velocity_limit`, `actuator_effort_limit`, `joint_velocity_limit` and `joint_effort_limit` respectively. The deprecated names are accepted through 3.x, so this is not blocking, but the rename is worth doing eagerly because it forces the distinction between the model's rated limit and the solver's clamp to be stated, and that distinction is the subject of the behaviour change in section 7.1. Each group carrying an explicit actuator must be audited for the second clip, since the solver now keeps the authored `joint_effort_limit` rather than raising it, and the identified efforts were fitted against a single clip.

The nineteen `armature` sites and seven `friction` sites are group properties that were removed from the runtime rather than renamed, and they remain valid as configuration fields resolved at construction, so they do not change. What changes is the runtime read, which must now go through `data.joint_armature` and `data.joint_friction_coeff` with the corresponding indexed writers.

Finally, the twenty two `velocity_limit` and ten `velocity_limit_sim` values will not be enforced by MuJoCo Warp at all, since it neither parses `actuator_velocity_limit` into the solver nor honours the `joint_velocity_limit` that IsaacLab writes to `Model.joint_velocity_limit` (`prepare_asset_for_newton.rst`, validate actuators and limits). Whether that matters depends on whether the present policies actually reach those limits. If they do, the bound must be reimplemented in the actuator model or as a reward term, and if they do not, the limits were decorative under PhysX as well. That measurement should be taken before the port rather than after, because it is cheap and it settles a question the port would otherwise raise as a regression.

### 10.8 Sensors and the contact force semantics

The contact sensor configurations port without structural change. Newton implements filtered contact reporting, so the three K-Scale foot pair sensors at `environments/environments/tasks/locomotion/cfg/SF/kscale_base_env_cfg.py:121-136` work as written, and neither of the two base fields the Newton configuration rejects is set here. Two refinements are worth adopting from upstream, writing the aggregate sensor prim path as `"{ENV_REGEX_NS}/Robot/[^/]*"` rather than `"{ENV_REGEX_NS}/Robot/.*"` so that nested prims are not matched, and setting `global_world_only=True` on the height scanner as upstream now does at `velocity_env_cfg.py:117`.

The semantic change is the serious item. The nineteen reads of `net_forces_w_history`, the four of `net_forces_w` and the two of `force_matrix_w_history` change meaning between backends, reporting normal force under PhysX with a warning and normal plus friction force under Newton. Every contact based reward and termination in the workspace reads one of those properties, which is to say `feet_impact_force`, `foot_landing_vel` in three versions, `foot_clearance_reward` in four versions, `feet_air_time` in two versions, `feet_hold_penalty`, `no_fly`, `NoFlyWithGrace`, `filtered_contacts`, `undesired_contacts`, `illegal_contact` and `illegal_contact_instrumented`.

The correct change is to name the quantity explicitly as `net_normal_forces_w_history` and `net_normal_forces_w`, which is identical on both backends and which reproduces the present PhysX behaviour exactly. That is the substitution which satisfies the workspace rule, since it changes no caller's behaviour. It is a mechanical edit at twenty five sites in `rewards.py`, `terminations.py`, `observations.py` and `play.py`.

Having made that substitution, the friction channel becomes an opportunity rather than a hazard. Several of the reward terms are attempting to detect a physical event, foot slip most obviously through `feet_slide` and `wheel_slide_penalty`, for which the friction force is the direct measurement and the normal force only a proxy. Newton reports `net_friction_forces_w` directly once `ContactSensorCfg.track_friction_forces` is enabled, whereas PhysX raises `NotImplementedError` on that property and offers only the filtered matrix. A friction based slip term would therefore be Newton only, which is a legitimate thing for a backend specific preset to select, and it should be recorded as a future refinement rather than attempted in the port.

The five reads of `ray_hits_w` become ProxyArray reads and are covered by section 10.4.

The `Imu` rename does not reach this workspace, since `observations.py:15` imports `Imu` for a type hint only and no configuration instantiates an IMU sensor. Should one be added, the distinction matters, `Pva` for the full state without gravity and `Imu` for angular velocity and linear acceleration with gravity always included.

### 10.9 Domain randomisation and the retirement of the PhysX tensor API

Three of the eight PhysX tensor sites are replaceable by stock terms, three by backend neutral calls and two have no replacement.

The project's `randomize_rigid_body_mass_inertia` at `events.py:78-127` samples masses, forms the ratio against the current masses and scales the inertia tensors by that ratio. The stock `randomize_rigid_body_mass` does precisely this when `recompute_inertia` is set, computing the ratio against the stored default masses and scaling the default inertia tensors, at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/envs/mdp/events.py:766-781`. One difference matters and favours the stock term, that it randomises from stored defaults on every call rather than from the previously randomised values, which makes repeated invocation idempotent in distribution where the project's version compounds. The recommendation is therefore to delete the project term and switch the fourteen `randomize_rigid_body_mass` and two `randomize_rigid_body_mass_inertia` configuration sites onto the stock term with `recompute_inertia=True`. This is a behaviour change, so it must be made as a deliberate, recorded decision rather than as part of the port, and the compounding difference must be checked against the interval at which the term fires.

The project's `randomize_rigid_body_coms` at `events.py:129-168` is likewise superseded by the stock `randomize_rigid_body_com`, which detects the backend at `events.py:950` and passes a three element position to Newton against a seven element pose to PhysX at `:992-994`. The three configuration sites should move onto the stock term. The Newton caveat at `:930-936` must be carried into the decision, since a mass matrix that is not fully recomputed after a centre of mass write is a correctness problem and not a performance one, and the honest reading is that centre of mass randomisation should be held at startup mode only under Newton until the upstream fix lands.

`robot_material_properties` at `observations.py:113-121` has no replacement and requires a backend branch. Under PhysX it returns `get_material_properties()` flattened, three values per collision shape being static friction, dynamic friction and restitution. Under Newton the equivalent data are two model attributes read through the selection API, `shape_material_mu` and `shape_material_restitution`, reached as the stock material randomisation term reaches them at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/envs/mdp/events.py:295-296`, and mapping public body identifiers onto backend shape ranges requires `asset.backend_num_shapes_per_body` and `asset.map_body_ids_to_backend` as that term does at `:302-304`.

The consequence is that the observation width changes, from three times the shape count to twice the shape count, and the shape ordering becomes the backend ordering. A change in observation width is a change in the policy interface, and the policy transfer guide is explicit that observation group and term order, tensor width, history length, units, frames, clipping and corruption behaviour must match exactly for a checkpoint to transfer (`transfer_policies_between_physx_and_newton.rst`, checkpoint compatibility table). So a PhysX trained checkpoint whose critic observes `robot_material_properties` cannot be evaluated under Newton at all without retraining the critic. Since the term appears in eleven configuration sites and always in the critic group, this is a real constraint on the validation matrix of section 11.

Two mitigations are available and the choice between them should be deliberate. The first is to add an optional argument to `robot_material_properties` selecting which channels to emit, defaulting to the present three channel PhysX form, and to have the Newton preset request a two channel form, accepting that the critic must be retrained. The second is to emit a fixed three channel form on both backends by duplicating the Newton friction coefficient into both the static and the dynamic slot, which preserves the width and therefore the checkpoint, at the cost of feeding the critic a channel that carries no information under Newton. The first is honest and the second is convenient, and the second is defensible only as a transitional device during the PhysX to Newton transfer evaluation, after which it should be removed. Whichever is chosen must be recorded in the term's docstring, since a reader cannot otherwise tell why a two channel quantity occupies three channels.

`robot_center_of_mass` at `observations.py:123-131` is not referenced by any configuration, so its `get_coms()` call is dead and should be either ported to `asset.data.body_com_pose_b` or deleted.

`play.py:479` calls `get_material_properties()` inside the dump path, and the same branch applies, with the additional consequence that the dumped array's channel count changes, which the dump contract must record alongside the quaternion convention of section 10.3.

The stock `randomize_joint_parameters` at five configuration sites works on Newton for static friction, viscous friction, armature and position limits, and the workspace's own `randomize_joint_friction_model` at five sites must be read against the Newton friction model, since Newton has no dynamic joint friction at all.

`apply_external_force_torque_stochastic` at `events.py:22-76` uses `asset.instantaneous_wrench_composer.add_forces_and_torques`, which survives, declared on the base classes at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/assets/articulation/base_articulation.py:463` and `.../rigid_object/base_rigid_object.py:102` and implemented in the Newton backend at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab_newton/isaaclab_newton/assets/rigid_object/rigid_object.py`. No change is required.

### 10.10 The HIM environment override

`environments/environments/tasks/locomotion/envs/him_env.py:6` declares `HIMManagerBasedRLEnv(ManagerBasedRLEnv)` whose only substantive content is a reimplementation of `step()`. Comparison against the 2.3.0 base shows the override to be a near copy differing in two respects, that it computes observations with `update_history=False` before the reset and publishes the result as `extras["terminal_obs"]`, and that it computes them again with `update_history=True` at the end.

The 3.0 base `step()` at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/envs/manager_based_rl_env.py` has changed in seven ways that the override does not reproduce. It branches on `self._physics_handles_decimation` to fold the entire decimation loop into one `sim.step()`, which is the Newton CUDA graph fast path. It reads `self.sim.is_rendering` as a cached property in place of the two calls `self.sim.has_gui()` and `self.sim.has_rtx_sensors()`. It passes `skip_app_pumping=not self.render_enabled` to `sim.render()`. It casts `reset_env_ids` to integer. It captures the terminal observation under `self.cfg.compute_final_obs` and publishes it as `extras["final_obs"]`. It honours a visualiser driven reset through `self.sim.consume_reset_request()`. And it ticks the video recorders with `for recorder in self.video_recorders: recorder.step()`.

The recommendation is to delete the subclass. `ManagerBasedRLEnvCfg.compute_final_obs`, documented at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/envs/manager_based_rl_env_cfg.py:45-55` as capturing the observation before a same step autoreset and storing it under `extras["final_obs"]` so that wrappers can report it as the true terminal observation for value bootstrapping, is precisely the capability the override was written to provide, and it is provided as a configuration flag defaulting to false. Setting it to true and reading `extras["final_obs"]` in the HIM runner removes an eighty line reimplementation of a method that has since gained seven features, and it restores the decimation fusion without which the Newton port is pointless.

The consumer must be updated in the same change, since the key name differs. The HIM runner reads `extras["terminal_obs"]`, and the search for that key should be extended to `himloco/himloco/runners/him_on_policy_runner.py` and `co_optimisation/co_optimisation/runners/copt_on_policy_runner.py` before the deletion.

The defect left standing, should the subclass be retained instead, is that it silently disables the Newton fast path and silently drops video recorder ticks and visualiser resets, none of which raises. That is the worst class of defect and it argues strongly for deletion.

### 10.11 Video recording, the viewer and the visualisers

Four mechanisms change together and the workspace uses all four.

`train.py:164` and `play.py:787` call `gym.make(render_mode="rgb_array")` and `train.py:185` and `play.py:807` wrap the result in `gym.wrappers.RecordVideo`. The wrapper pattern is no longer supported and is replaced by `env_cfg.video_recorders = [VideoRecorderCfg(source=..., output_dir=..., video_length=...)]`, whose sources are `"visualizer"` for automatic selection, the three explicit visualiser forms and `"sensor:<name>"`, and whose `eye` and `lookat` fields were removed in favour of `sim.default_visualizer_cfg`.

`train.py:66` and `play.py:66` set `args_cli.enable_cameras` whenever `--video` is passed. The relation between the flags changed, `--headless` no longer suppressing visualisers and the two being independent, with `--viz kit --enable_cameras --headless --video` being the exact equivalent of the 2.x `--video` behaviour, and with `--video` alone still working by auto creating a headless Kit visualiser and printing a notice to that effect (`migrating_to_isaaclab_3-0.rst`, headless video recording).

`scripts/rsl_rl/highest_terrain_camera_wrapper.py` is the hardest of the four. Its `_track_highest_terrain` reads `base_env.viewport_camera_controller`, assigns `vcc.cfg.env_index` and calls `vcc.update_view_to_asset_root("robot")` so that the recorded video follows whichever environment sits on the lowest terrain difficulty. `ViewportCameraController` is deprecated behind a shim that raises a `DeprecationWarning` at construction, and camera tracking moved into `KitVisualizer` through the `origin_type` and `origin_track_path` fields of `KitVisualizerCfg`. Those fields are configuration rather than runtime state, so the wrapper's per step retargeting has no direct equivalent, and reproducing it requires either mutating the visualiser configuration between steps or a per step visualiser API that the documentation does not advertise. The honest position is that this capability is at risk, and the recommendation is to reduce it to a static choice for the port, tracking a fixed environment through `KitVisualizerCfg(origin_type="asset", origin_track_path="robot")`, and to record the loss of the terrain following behaviour as a deliberate omission to be revisited.

The Newton visualiser is itself a new capability worth taking up, since `newton_gl` uses pyglet's EGL backend and auto detects a missing display, so it records headlessly with no `--enable_cameras` and no Kit at all, whereas `newton_rtx` cannot render headlessly and returns nothing from `render_rgb_array()`. For a workspace whose training runs are headless and whose video is diagnostic rather than presentational, `--viz newton_gl` with a `VideoRecorderCfg(source="visualizer:newton_gl")` is both simpler and cheaper than the Kit Replicator path it replaces.

### 10.12 URDF conversion

`co_optimisation/co_optimisation/runners/usd_generator.py:403-415` constructs a `UrdfConverterCfg` per CMA-ES individual and reads `UrdfConverter(cfg).usd_path`. Three of its ten arguments change.

`usd_file_name=f"biped_{idx}.usd"` is no longer honoured, the importer determining the name from the robot and writing to `{usd_dir}/{robot_name}/{robot_name}.usda`, so both the argument and the `usd_path` read must change. `collider_type="convex_hull"` no longer exists, replaced by `collision_type="Convex Hull"`, a change of field name and of value spelling. `make_instanceable`, which this call does not pass but which the base class carries, is ignored and assets are always instanceable.

The remaining seven arguments survive, `asset_path`, `usd_dir`, `link_density`, `merge_fixed_joints`, `fix_base`, `self_collision`, `joint_drive` and `force_usd_conversion`, with `merge_fixed_joints` now implemented as a URDF XML preprocessing step that removes fixed joints and merges the child link's visual, collision and inertial elements into the parent with composed transforms, which is a different implementation of the same contract and therefore a thing to verify on the generated geometry rather than to assume.

Two new arguments matter to a Newton port and both default favourably. `run_multi_physics_conversion`, default true at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/sim/converters/urdf_converter_cfg.py:204`, generates MuJoCo attributes alongside PhysX ones, which is what makes a single generated asset usable by both backends. `run_asset_transformer`, default true at `:190`, produces a layered asset rather than a flat file, the structure being an interface `robot_name.usda` over `payloads/base.usda`, `payloads/instances.usda`, `payloads/geometry.usd`, `payloads/materials.usda` and a `payloads/Physics/` directory containing `physics.usda` for the neutral format, `physX.usda` for the PhysX attributes and `mujoco.usda` for the MuJoCo attributes. `physics_variant` on the base configuration selects among `PhysicsVariant.PHYSICS`, described as backend portable physics covering joints, articulation roots, mass and the Newton schemas, `PHYSX`, `MUJOCO` and `NONE`, at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/sim/converters/asset_converter_base_cfg.py:18-31`.

That layered output is a change of kind rather than of degree for the co-optimisation loop, which presently treats a generated individual as one file path. A generated individual is now a directory tree, and the generator's bookkeeping, its output directory layout under `log_dir/copt_usds` and its disk accounting, must be revised accordingly. Given that the CMA-ES configuration in `scripts/rsl_rl/train.py:203` sets two hundred and fifty six individuals, and that the workspace's root filesystem is already full, the increase in inode and byte count per individual is a practical constraint and should be measured on a single individual before a generation is attempted.

The MJCF importer also changed and the change is worth recording even though this workspace does not presently use it, because it produces assets with nested rigid bodies, applying `RigidBodyAPI` to each link prim individually rather than once at the top level, which matches how MuJoCo represents bodies and which will break downstream logic that assumes a flat rigid body hierarchy (`migrating_to_isaaclab_3-0.rst`, MJCF importer note). Should the port ever take the shorter route of authoring MuJoCo models directly rather than converting URDF, that is the structural difference to expect.

### 10.13 Co-optimisation under Newton

This section was rewritten on 2026-09-14 and reverses the conclusion of its first draft. That draft reasoned from the absence of a Newton respawn mechanism to the absence of runtime morphology variation, and recommended holding co-optimisation on PhysX. Reading the Newton world model and the IsaacLab Newton cloner establishes the opposite, that heterogeneous environments are a first class capability of both, that the respawn mechanism is absent because it is unnecessary rather than because the capability is missing, and that Newton is the better substrate for this work stream rather than the worse one. The first draft's recommendation is withdrawn and the reasoning that produced it is preserved here so that the correction is legible.

#### 10.13.1 What the loop actually varies

The design search varies two scalars per individual, `thigh_length_scale` and `shank_length_scale`, each over the interval from 0.75 to 1.25, across a population of two hundred and fifty six (`tron1-rl-isaaclab-cozum/scripts/rsl_rl/train.py:203`, `:209-210`). Those two scalars reach the simulation through `_apply_link_extents` at `tron1-rl-isaaclab-cozum/co_optimisation/co_optimisation/runners/usd_generator.py:419-452`, which the docstring of `update.py` confirms as the canonical list, and they touch exactly five quantities on each scalable link.

The first is the box extent, written as `<box size>` on both the visual and the collision geometry at `usd_generator.py:429-436`. The second is the recentring of the geometry and inertial origins to the new half length, at `:438-446`. The third is the mass, computed as density multiplied by the box volume at `:458-460`. The fourth is the inertia, the diagonal moment of a solid box about its centre of mass with the off diagonals zeroed, at `:461-470` and `:471-494`. The fifth is the anchor of the child joint, moved to the new link end at `:496-517`.

Three properties of that list govern everything below. Every quantity after the first is derived analytically from the box extents and a fixed per link density, so there is no mesh to reprocess, no convex hull to recompute and no inertia to integrate numerically. The geometry is a primitive, the method asserting as much in its own docstring with the words assumes box links only. And the topology never changes, the body count, the joint count, the joint types and the kinematic tree being identical across every individual in every generation. A design change is therefore a change of numbers in fixed sized arrays, which is the most favourable case that exists for a batched simulator.

#### 10.13.2 What the present implementation does, and why it is shaped that way

The live loop is not the single respawn the first draft described. It is two tiered, and reading `copt_on_policy_runner.py` establishes the division precisely.

The first tier is `_respawn_population` at `copt_on_policy_runner.py:539-567`, which spawns one USD per design through `MultiUsdFileCfg` and `spawn_multi_usd_file` with `replicate_physics=False`. Its own docstring states why it exists, that it is the only pathway which gives each design its own USD reference arc and therefore its own USD instancing prototype. It runs once at the start of training, and thereafter only when the population size changes.

The second tier is `apply_link_length_params` at `co_optimisation/co_optimisation/utils/update.py:132`, called from `_update_morphology` at `copt_on_policy_runner.py:689`, and it is what runs every generation. It stops the simulation, authors the new box extents on each design's geometry prototype through the `Sdf` layer API, writes the mass, inertia and child joint anchor on whichever layer actually defines each of them, and then performs a single `sim.reset()` followed by an `env.reset()`.

The reason for the two tiers is stated in the same docstring and is worth quoting because it is the whole of the difficulty. The in place edit reaches prototypes rather than composed prims, so where a single asset backs every environment the entire population resolves to one prototype, the per design writes overwrite one another, and every environment ends on the design authored last. The respawn exists solely to manufacture two hundred and fifty six distinct prototypes so that the cheap path has two hundred and fifty six distinct targets to write to.

That is a USD composition constraint. It is not a physics constraint, not a PhysX constraint and not a constraint of the problem. It arises because Isaac Sim represents many copies of one robot by sharing a single prototype subtree, and because sharing is precisely what a heterogeneous population must not do. Everything awkward in the present implementation, the reference arc manufacture, the prototype resolution, the per attribute authoring layer lookup, the stop and reset cycle, the sensor callback re-registration and the manager rebinding, descends from that one fact.

#### 10.13.3 Why the shape of the problem changes under Newton

Newton does not share prototypes. Its documentation states that multiple independent simulations, referred to as worlds, live within a single `Model`, that model arrays remain flat across all worlds rather than being separated per world, and that Newton stores entities sequentially with parallel world assignment arrays such as `body_world` recording which world each body belongs to, a design chosen so that GPU operations parallelise across entire entity sets without world awareness [10].

The consequence is that environment seven's thigh and environment eight's thigh are two different entries in `body_mass`, two different entries in `shape_scale` and two different entries in `joint_X_p`. They are not two instances of one prototype. They are two bodies. Independent per environment morphology is therefore not a feature that Newton must provide, it is the default consequence of how Newton lays out a model, and the reason Newton offers no respawn mechanism is that there is nothing a respawn would accomplish.

Newton offers three documented ways to build such a model, manual scoping with `begin_world()` and `end_world()` delimiting the entities of each world, `add_world()` incorporating a pre-built `ModelBuilder` as a complete new world in one operation, and `replicate()` producing N copies of one builder each as its own world with optional spatial offsets [10]. Only the third of these requires the worlds to be identical, and the documentation warns separately that a replicated source acts as a template whose entries may be shared across copies, so mutating one in place writes through to every copy and is unsupported [11]. The first two impose no such constraint. Per world assets, which the release notes record as arriving in Newton 1.5.0, extend the same principle to meshes, height fields, materials and textures, and IsaacLab pins 1.6.0rc1, released 2026-09-10 [12].

#### 10.13.4 Path A, a heterogeneous build through the cloner IsaacLab already has

The IsaacLab Newton integration exposes this capability and the co-optimisation loop's existing spawn configuration already speaks its language.

`isaaclab.cloner.clone_plan.num_spawn_variants` at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/cloner/clone_plan.py:104-122` returns one variant per asset configuration for a `MultiAssetSpawnerCfg` and one per USD path for a `MultiUsdFileCfg`, returning the length of the path list when that field is a list rather than a string. The resulting `ClonePlan` carries `sources`, `destinations` and a `clone_mask` which its docstring at `:275-279` describes as the flat prototype to environment mapping.

That plan reaches Newton through `_build_newton_builder_from_mapping` at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab_newton/isaaclab_newton/cloner/replicate.py:93`, which takes `sources` as a sequence and calls `build_source_builders` to construct one `ModelBuilder` per source before handing the set to `replicate_builder_mapping` at `:162`. That function, at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab_newton/isaaclab_newton/cloner/newton_clone_utils.py:264-277`, takes `sources` as a sequence and `mapping` as a two dimensional array whose second axis is the world count, and it takes a homogeneous fast path only under the conjunction recorded at `:288-296`, namely a single source, a single mapping row, a fully populated mask, no per world builder hooks, and both destinations and environment identifiers supplied. Every other case falls through to a general path that assigns worlds from distinct sources.

The conclusion is direct and it is the most useful single finding in this section. A `MultiUsdFileCfg` carrying a list of two hundred and fifty six generated USD paths, which is exactly what `_respawn_population` already builds, is a supported IsaacLab 3.0 configuration that produces two hundred and fifty six genuinely distinct Newton worlds in one model. The first tier of the present architecture therefore ports almost unchanged, and what it produces on Newton is better than what it produces on PhysX, because the worlds it produces are independent by construction rather than by prototype manufacture.

One caveat attaches and must be checked rather than assumed. A second builder entry point exists at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab_newton/isaaclab_newton/physics/newton_manager.py:1941`, `instantiate_builder_from_stage`, which scans the children of `/World` for names matching the pattern `[Ee]nv_(\d+)` and, when it finds them, imports only `env_paths[0]` as a prototype and replicates it to every environment with a mapping of shape one by the world count, at `:2008-2052`. That path would silently collapse a heterogeneous population to two hundred and fifty six copies of individual zero. It is the fallback for a stage that was not built through the cloner, and the IsaacLab scene uses `/World/envs/env_0` rather than `/World/env_0`, so the pattern should not match and the cloner path should govern. Confirming which of the two entry points a co-optimisation scene actually takes is the first verification experiment of this section, because the failure is silent and would present as a population whose fitness has no variance.

#### 10.13.5 Path B, runtime per world model field writes

The cheap per generation update, the analogue of `apply_link_length_params`, becomes a set of writes into the flat model arrays. Newton's `ModelFlags` enumerates which arrays each notification covers, and the correspondence with the five quantities of section 10.13.1 is exact [13].

The box extent maps to `shape_scale`, a per shape three vector, under `SHAPE_PROPERTIES`. The geometry origin recentring maps to `shape_transform`, a per shape transform, under the same flag. The inertial origin recentring maps to `body_com` under `BODY_INERTIAL_PROPERTIES`. The mass maps to `body_mass`, with `body_inv_mass` under the same flag. The inertia maps to `body_inertia`, with `body_inv_inertia` under the same flag. The child joint anchor maps to `joint_X_p`, the parent side joint frame, under `JOINT_PROPERTIES`. `SHAPE_PROPERTIES` additionally covers `shape_collision_radius`, which is Newton's bounding radius and which a change of extent invalidates, so it must be written alongside the scale rather than left stale.

Three of these six arrays are already reachable through the public backend neutral API, `asset.set_masses_index`, `asset.set_inertias_index` and `asset.set_coms_index`, whose Newton implementations write per environment by construction, `set_masses_index` at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab_newton/isaaclab_newton/assets/articulation/articulation.py:2268` taking masses of shape `(len(env_ids), len(body_ids))` and the corresponding data property declaring a shape of `(num_instances, num_bodies)` at `articulation_data.py:704-712`. That the public shape carries an environment axis is itself the confirmation that Newton's body arrays are per environment rather than shared.

The other three require the native escape hatch, which the documentation sanctions for code willing to own its own lifecycle. The pattern is the one the stock material randomisation term uses at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/envs/mdp/events.py:295-296`, obtaining a binding with `asset._root_view.get_attribute("shape_material_mu", model)` and writing through it, followed by `NewtonManager.add_model_change(ModelFlags.SHAPE_PROPERTIES)` at `:371`. The same generic accessor reaches `shape_scale`, `shape_transform` and `joint_X_p`. Mapping public body identifiers onto the backend shape ranges requires `asset.backend_num_shapes_per_body` and `asset.map_body_ids_to_backend`, as that term demonstrates at `:302-304`.

After the writes, three notifications are required and the second and third are easy to forget. `NewtonManager.add_model_change` must be called once per flag touched, the manager accumulating them in a set at `newton_manager.py:1454` and draining them into `solver.notify_model_changed` at the head of the next step at `:1015-1019`. A change to `joint_X_p` alters the kinematic tree, so `NewtonManager.invalidate_fk()` at `:1457` must be called and the next `forward()` allowed to run before any state is read. And a change to link length moves every body below the changed link, so the reset state must be recomputed rather than reused, which on this robot means the nominal standing height changes with the leg length and the episode initial pose must be derived from the new geometry.

Against the PhysX path this removes the simulation stop, the Fabric deactivation and reactivation, the physics cooking, the timeline event round trip, the sensor callback re-registration and the manager rebinding, which is to say it removes all nine steps of `respawn.py` and the stop and reset cycle of `update.py`. What remains is a handful of kernel launches and one flag drain.

#### 10.13.6 The recomputation contract and the risk that governs it

A solver caches quantities derived from the model, and a morphology write invalidates them. This is where the approach can be wrong rather than merely slow, so it deserves to be stated sharply.

The clearest available account of the contract is mjlab's, because mjlab drives MuJoCo Warp directly and therefore had to enumerate it. Its `RecomputeLevel` enumeration at `/ws/mjlab/src/mjlab/managers/event_manager.py:25-50` defines three levels above none. `set_const_fixed` recomputes `body_subtreemass` and suffices after a change to `body_gravcomp`. `set_const_0` recomputes `dof_invweight0`, `body_invweight0`, `tendon_length0`, `tendon_invweight0` and `actuator_acc0`, and is required after a change to `dof_armature`, `body_inertia`, `body_pos`, `body_quat` or `qpos0`. `set_const` is the full recomputation and is required after a change to `body_mass` or `body_ipos`. The enumeration carries a note that every level above none is expensive, involving forward kinematics and mass matrix factorisation, and that terms requiring recomputation should therefore run in startup or reset event modes rather than per step. A morphology update touches mass, inertia, centre of mass and body position together, so it requires the most expensive level, and at a cadence of once per four hundred and eighty policy iterations that cost is negligible.

mjlab also records a second obligation that no solver performs automatically. After writing `geom_size` it recomputes `geom_rbound` and `geom_aabb` itself, in `_recompute_geom_bounds` at `/ws/mjlab/src/mjlab/envs/mdp/dr/geom.py:33-118`, with an explicit per primitive formula, the bounding radius of a box being the norm of its three half extents and its axis aligned bounding half size being the half extents themselves. The presence of `shape_collision_radius` in Newton's `SHAPE_PROPERTIES` list [13] suggests Newton expects the same of its caller.

The risk that governs the whole approach is stated by IsaacLab itself, in the docstring of the stock centre of mass randomisation term at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/envs/mdp/events.py:930-936`, which warns that on Newton with MuJoCo Warp runtime centre of mass changes may cause simulation instability because `notify_model_changed(BODY_INERTIAL_PROPERTIES)` does not fully recompute the mass matrix after `body_ipos` changes, and that the term should be used with caution until this is fixed upstream. A morphology update writes `body_com` on every scaled link. If the mass matrix is not refactorised, the loop optimises a design against a stale inertia, which is a correctness failure and not a performance one, and it would not announce itself. Section 10.13.9 makes settling this the gating experiment, and section 10.13.7 names the fallback should it fail.

A third question is load bearing and could not be settled from documentation. MuJoCo compiles a single model, and MuJoCo Warp gives most model fields a leading batch dimension of one shared across worlds, expanding selected fields per world only when asked through the `batch_sizes` argument of `put_model` [4]. Newton's model is per world flat, so `SolverMuJoCo` must be performing that expansion for any field that differs between worlds. The circumstantial evidence is strong, since the solver's published mapping arrays are two dimensional over worlds, `mjc_geom_to_newton_shape` having shape `[nworld, ngeom]` and `mjc_body_to_newton` shape `[nworld, nbody]` [14], and since per world assets are an advertised capability of the release IsaacLab pins [12]. It is nonetheless circumstantial, and whether the expansion covers geometry extents specifically is the second gating experiment.

#### 10.13.7 What IsaacLab does not yet expose, and the three extension points

Three gaps separate the present integration from the construction above, and each has an identified seam.

The public asset API covers mass, inertia and centre of mass but not shape scale, shape transform or joint anchor. The seam is the generic selection accessor, `root_view.get_attribute(name, model)` and `set_attribute(name, model, value)`, which the native data API documents as reaching engine properties that have no dedicated selection method, with the caution that direct writes bypass IsaacLab caches and shape and order validation. Code taking this route owns its own index mapping, and `backend_num_shapes_per_body` together with `map_body_ids_to_backend` is how the stock terms do it.

IsaacLab never raises `ModelFlags.JOINT_PROPERTIES`. The four flags it uses are `BODY_INERTIAL_PROPERTIES` at twenty seven sites, `SHAPE_PROPERTIES` at eight, `MODEL_PROPERTIES` at four and `JOINT_DOF_PROPERTIES` at four, and `JOINT_PROPERTIES`, which is the flag covering `joint_q`, `joint_X_p` and `joint_X_c` [13], appears nowhere. The seam is that `NewtonManager.add_model_change(change)` at `newton_manager.py:1452-1454` accepts any flag and simply adds it to the set that is drained into the solver, so the flag is reachable even though no IsaacLab code path raises it. That it is unexercised by IsaacLab's own tests is a risk to be discharged by a direct experiment.

Per world construction is reachable but the injection point is not obvious. The cleanest is the public context manager `newton_builder_world_hook` at `IsaacLab-newton/source/isaaclab_newton/isaaclab_newton/cloner/replicate.py:63-90`, which temporarily registers a callback receiving the builder, the world index, the world position and the world orientation during replication, and which removes only its own callback on exit so that hooks owned by other callers survive. The same underlying list, `NewtonManager._per_world_builder_hooks` at `newton_manager.py:561`, is the established extension pattern, the material point method objects appending to it at `isaaclab_newton/assets/mpm_object/mpm_object.py:111-112` and the contributed deformables at `isaaclab_contrib/deformable/deformable_object.py:244-245`. A hook that applies the individual's box extents to the builder for its assigned world would express the whole population at build time without generating a single USD file.

That last observation deserves emphasis, because it points at a simplification the PhysX path cannot reach. On PhysX the population must become two hundred and fifty six USD files because the spawner consumes USD. On Newton the population could become two hundred and fifty six sets of numbers applied to one builder, and the URDF regeneration, the URDF to USD conversion and the layered asset output of section 10.12 would all disappear from the loop. Whether that is worth building depends on whether the runtime write path of section 10.13.5 succeeds, since if it does the build time path is needed only once.

#### 10.13.8 Path C, the differentiable route

Newton advertises support for differentiable simulation for machine learning and optimisation [3], and the design parameters of this loop are continuous, low dimensional and enter the model through arrays that a Warp kernel writes. A gradient of the episode return with respect to the two length scales, if obtainable, would replace a population of two hundred and fifty six evaluated for four hundred and eighty iterations with a direct ascent, which is a different order of sample efficiency.

Three cautions apply and they are sufficient to keep this out of the port. Differentiability through a contact rich locomotion rollout is a research problem rather than an engineering one, the gradients being both discontinuous at contact events and badly conditioned over long horizons. The MuJoCo Warp reference lists differentiability via Warp among the features it does not yet support, so the primary validated solver path is not the differentiable one and a differentiable design search would have to use a different Newton solver, which changes the physics the design is optimised against. And the workspace has an established result about the interaction between a changing morphology and on policy learning, recorded at [copt_ppo_nonstationarity.md](copt_ppo_nonstationarity.md), which a change of search method does not make irrelevant. This belongs in `plans/` as a research direction, and it is recorded here only because the port creates the possibility and because the possibility should inform how the field write layer of section 10.13.5 is factored, namely as a differentiable kernel rather than as an opaque update.

#### 10.13.9 A recommended construction

The work divides into three experiments and then an implementation, and the experiments are cheap, independent of the rest of the port, and answer questions that determine whether the implementation is worth starting.

Experiment one establishes that heterogeneous worlds survive the build. Construct a scene of four environments with two distinct box geometries through a `MultiUsdFileCfg` carrying two USD paths, run under `physics=newton_mjwarp`, and read `NewtonManager.get_model().shape_scale` back. Four distinct entries confirm the cloner path, and four identical entries confirm the `instantiate_builder_from_stage` prototype collapse of section 10.13.4 and locate the fix. Settle this before anything else, because the whole construction rests on it and because the failure is silent.

Experiment two establishes that a runtime write propagates. On the same scene write a new `shape_scale` for one environment only, raise `SHAPE_PROPERTIES`, step, and confirm both that the reported contact geometry changed for that environment and that the others are unaffected. Repeat for `body_mass` and `body_inertia` under `BODY_INERTIAL_PROPERTIES` and for `joint_X_p` under `JOINT_PROPERTIES`, the last being the one IsaacLab never exercises. Use `MJWarpSolverCfg.save_to_mjcf` to dump the compiled MuJoCo model before and after, since that is the only direct way to see what the solver actually holds.

Experiment three settles the mass matrix question of section 10.13.6. Write a known inertial change to one environment, then compare a measured quantity that depends on the mass matrix, the simplest being the acceleration produced by a fixed applied torque from a fixed state, against the value the same environment gives after a full restart with the new geometry baked in at build time. Agreement discharges the risk. Disagreement means the runtime inertial path is unsound, and the fallback is then the build time path of section 10.13.7, paying a model rebuild once per generation, which at a cadence of four hundred and eighty iterations is a cost worth measuring before it is feared.

If the three experiments pass, the implementation is smaller than the code it replaces. A single event term, or a single runner method, computes the five derived quantities for each individual from its two length scales by the same closed form arithmetic that `usd_generator.py` already implements, writes them into the six model arrays under the environment identifiers assigned to that individual, raises three flags, invalidates the forward kinematics and recomputes the reset pose. The URDF regeneration, the USD conversion, the prototype manufacture, the layer resolution, the stop and reset cycle and the manager rebinding all leave the loop. `respawn.py` and `update.py` become PhysX only paths retained behind the physics preset, which satisfies the workspace rule that no existing caller changes, and the co-optimisation task gains a `newton_mjwarp` preset alongside its `isaacsim_physx` default rather than replacing it.

If experiment three fails, the fallback is the build time path, and if experiment one fails in a way that cannot be fixed in configuration, both paths reduce to the same requirement, a contribution to `instantiate_builder_from_stage` or an override of it. In no case is the conclusion of the first draft correct, that co-optimisation should be held on PhysX indefinitely, because the capability exists at both the Newton and the IsaacLab layer and the only open questions are about propagation rather than about representation.

#### 10.13.10 Risk register

The silent collapse of a heterogeneous population to the first individual, should the prototype path of `instantiate_builder_from_stage` be taken, would present as a design search with no fitness variance and would be easy to mistake for a converged search. Experiment one, and an assertion on the distinctness of `shape_scale` retained in the production code.

The stale mass matrix after an inertial write, by IsaacLab's own admission at `events.py:930-936`, would present as a design search optimising against wrong dynamics. Experiment three, and the build time fallback.

`ModelFlags.JOINT_PROPERTIES` is unexercised by IsaacLab, so the joint anchor path has no upstream coverage. Experiment two, and a willingness to carry a patch.

Direct model writes bypass IsaacLab's caches and its shape and order validation, by the native data API's own caution, so a wrong index is silent rather than raised. Mitigate by deriving every index through `map_body_ids_to_backend` and `backend_num_shapes_per_body` rather than by arithmetic, and by asserting array shapes at the write site.

The reset pose depends on the leg length, so a morphology change that does not recompute it starts each episode with the feet at the wrong height, which on this robot already begins each episode clear of the ground. This is a pre-existing coupling rather than a new one, but the runtime path removes the reset that formerly masked it.

MuJoCo Warp enforces neither the rated actuator velocity limit nor the solver joint velocity limit, by section 10.7, and the co-optimisation loop also varies actuator parameters through `apply_actuator_params` at `copt_on_policy_runner.py:630`. The actuator half of the design vector therefore needs the same audit as the morphology half, and its Newton path is `write_group_parameter` from `isaaclab.actuators.newton` rather than a model array write.

### 10.14 The analysis layer and the dump contract

`scripts/analysis/stats.py`, `dashboard.py`, `experiment_params.py` and the six `kscale_*` self tests import no IsaacLab symbol and are therefore untouched by the library change. Their entire exposure is through the numpy dump contract, and that contract changes in three ways.

The quaternion convention of the `base_quaternion` and `feet_quaternions` arrays flips, as section 10.3 sets out, and the remedy is an explicit convention field read with a WXYZ default.

The contact force arrays change meaning, since `play.py:440` dumps `net_forces_w_history` and that quantity becomes normal plus friction under Newton. Applying the explicit renaming of section 10.8 to the dump path holds the contract fixed, which is the right answer, and the dumped array should keep the name that describes what it contains.

The material properties array changes width, from three channels per shape to two under Newton, as section 10.9 sets out, and the dump must record the channel count.

The recommendation across all three is a single dump level metadata block naming the backend, the solver, the quaternion order, the contact force quantity and the material channel layout, written by `play.py` and read by `stats.py` with defaults that reproduce the present interpretation. That keeps every existing dump readable, which matters because the run tree under `IsaacLab/logs/` is transient and the surviving evidence is what has already been moved into `context/artefacts/`.

### 10.15 Symmetry augmentation

`environments/environments/tasks/locomotion/mdp/symmetry/kscale.py` mirrors concatenated observation vectors by term name and width, so the quaternion change does not reach it, no term in either group being a raw quaternion.

Two of its mirror kinds are backend sensitive. `_CRITIC_KIND` maps `robot_material_properties` to the kind `material` at `:130`, and the builder comment at `:98-102` states that the term width is three times the total shape count, which is the PhysX layout. Under Newton it is twice the shape count and the shape order is the backend order rather than the public body order. `_NUM_SHAPES_PER_BODY` is presently `None` at `:102`, so `_build_material_perm` returns `None` at `:186-187` and the mirror falls back to identity, which makes the defect latent rather than live. It must nevertheless be re-derived before the material mirror is enabled on either backend, and the comment must be corrected, because a reader following its instruction to fill the list from a one time runtime print of the term width would derive a PhysX specific permutation and apply it silently under Newton.

`_INERTIA9` at `:113` assumes a nine element inertia layout, which matches the 3.0 `data.body_inertia` shape of `(num_envs, num_bodies, 9)`, so it is unaffected.

The `brs.py` symmetry module should be read against the same two questions before the port is declared complete.

## 11. A staged port with validation gates

The staging below is ordered so that each stage is independently valuable and independently revertible, and so that no stage confounds a mechanical change with a behavioural one. The gates are the point of the arrangement, since a port that cannot demonstrate equivalence has not been completed, it has merely been written.

### Stage 0, work that costs nothing and can be done today

Rename the thirteen noise alias imports to `GaussianNoiseCfg` and `UniformNoiseCfg` in the incumbent tree. The classes are identical under 2.3.0, so this is a semantically null edit that removes thirteen breakages from every later stage.

Measure whether the present policies reach the velocity limits that MuJoCo Warp will not enforce. A single playback run with the joint velocity recorded against `data.joint_vel_limits` settles it, and the answer determines whether section 10.7 owes a reimplementation or a deletion.

Run `scripts/tools/find_quaternions.py --path environments/ --path co_optimisation/` from the 3.0 checkout against this workspace's directories only, to confirm that no hard coded quaternion exists outside the tests.

### Stage 1, the environment

Build the 3.0 checkout and its Python 3.12 environment beside the incumbent rather than over it, resolve the `rsl_rl` pin at 5.4.1 against the three subclassing algorithm implementations, and build both container images, the bare Newton one and the `--extra isaacsim` one.

Gate. `uv run isaaclab --help` and `uv run python scripts/environments/list_envs.py --show_presets` both succeed, and the incumbent 2.3.0 environment still runs one existing training command to completion of a single iteration.

### Stage 2, compile the task tree against 3.0 on PhysX only

Apply the import renames of section 10.2, the simulation configuration changes of section 10.6 with `default` declared as the PhysX variant and no Newton variant yet present, the single write method change of section 10.5, the actuator constructor change of section 10.7 restricted to `resolve_joint_parameter`, the contact force renaming of section 10.8, and the removal of the two render settings from `train.py`. Delete the HIM environment override per section 10.10 and set `compute_final_obs=True`. Leave the PhysX tensor API sites alone, since PhysX still provides them.

Gate. Every one of the four task families trains for fifty iterations under `physics=isaacsim_physx` and the reward curves lie within run to run variance of the 2.3.0 baseline over the same seed. This is the stage that proves the 3.0 port is behaviour preserving, and it must be passed before Newton is introduced at all, because a divergence discovered after both changes cannot be attributed.

### Stage 3, asset preparation for Newton

Follow the asset preparation procedure for each robot before any Newton run is attempted (`prepare_asset_for_newton.rst`). Audit every dynamic link for positive mass, a correct centre of mass and a positive definite inertia, treating a placeholder inertia warning as a modelling failure rather than a warning. Confirm that `UsdPhysics.CollisionAPI` is applied only to intended collision geometry, and inspect the resolved per shape MuJoCo contact dimensionality, `condim`, choosing the smallest model that represents the physics, one for frictionless, three for tangential friction, four with torsional and six with rolling. Regenerate the assets through the 3.0 URDF converter with `run_multi_physics_conversion=True` so that the MuJoCo attributes are authored, and use `MJWarpSolverCfg.save_to_mjcf` to dump and read the model MuJoCo Warp actually compiled rather than trusting the importer.

Gate. The paired smoke test passes for every robot, `uv run --extra isaacsim python scripts/environments/zero_agent.py --task TASK --num_envs 4 --viz none physics=isaacsim_physx` against the same command with `physics=newton_mjwarp`, with each run crossing several resets, and with no non-finite state, no first step impulse, no robot to terrain penetration and no importer or solver warning on either side.

### Stage 4, introduce the Newton presets

Add the `newton_mjwarp` member to each task family's physics preset, seeded from upstream's rough terrain locomotion values, and add the Newton actuator variants of section 10.7 as parallel preset members leaving the identified PhysX actuators untouched. Add the backend branch to `robot_material_properties` per section 10.9 and record the channel decision. Adopt the upstream sensor refinements, the `[^/]*` prim path and `global_world_only=True`.

Gate. Each task family reaches a hundred iterations under `physics=newton_mjwarp` without a capacity overflow, with `NewtonCfg.debug_mode` enabled to read the iteration cap usage and with `njmax` and `nconmax` raised until the busiest reset fits. Record the final capacities per family, since they are per robot properties rather than global ones.

### Stage 5, the throughput and fidelity measurement

Run the paired benchmark for each family, `isaaclab benchmark runtime --task TASK presets=isaacsim_physx` against `presets=newton_mjwarp`, and then `isaaclab benchmark training --rl_library rsl_rl` for both, at the environment counts the workspace actually trains at rather than at a convenient small count. Record the figures in this document, since section 5 establishes that no published figure answers the question and the workspace's own measurement is therefore the only evidence that will exist.

Run the sim to sim transfer matrix of the policy transfer guide for at least the TRON1 sole foot and the K-Scale biped, the four cells being a PhysX baseline, a PhysX checkpoint under Newton, a Newton baseline and a Newton checkpoint under PhysX, using `env.scene.robot.joint_ordering` and `env.scene.robot.body_ordering` set to the training backend on the cross runs and set on both axes rather than one. Both robots are branched, so the ordering overrides are expected to be necessary, as they are for the upstream G1 and ANYmal-D (`transfer_policies_between_physx_and_newton.rst`, validated transfer examples). The signature to watch for is a policy that falls within a few dozen steps in every environment rather than degrading gracefully, which indicates a scrambled axis and not solver dynamics.

Gate. The cross backend cells survive on a timescale comparable to their source baseline, and the gait statistics of section 10.14 are computed for all four cells so that the difference is characterised rather than merely survived. The critic observation width question of section 10.9 may make a full transfer impossible for the families that observe material properties, and if so that must be stated rather than worked around.

### Stage 6, the mechanical tidying

Append `.torch` at every data property site, drop the null `.to(device)` calls, move the seven material configuration sites onto the split schema classes, rename the fifty eight deprecated actuator limit fields, and switch the sixteen mass and three centre of mass randomisation sites onto the stock backend neutral terms while recording the compounding difference. None of this changes behaviour if the earlier gates passed, and all of it must be done before the 4.0 line removes the aliases.

### Stage 7, the co-optimisation port

Run the three experiments of section 10.13.9, which are independent of every stage above and can in fact be run first, since they need only a four environment scene and answer questions that determine how much of section 10.13 is worth building. Experiment one confirms that a heterogeneous `MultiUsdFileCfg` population survives the build as distinct Newton worlds rather than collapsing to the first individual. Experiment two confirms that a runtime write to `shape_scale`, `body_mass`, `body_inertia` and `joint_X_p` propagates into the compiled solver model under the corresponding flags, `JOINT_PROPERTIES` being the one IsaacLab never exercises. Experiment three confirms that an inertial write refactorises the mass matrix, which is the risk IsaacLab's own centre of mass term records against itself.

Gate. All three pass, or the fallback each failure selects is chosen deliberately and recorded. Only then implement the field write layer, which replaces `respawn.py`, `update.py` and the per generation USD regeneration with one closed form computation and six array writes. Retain both PhysX paths behind the physics preset so that every historical co-optimisation run reproduces, and write the implementation up as a plan in `plans/` with this section as its premise.

## 12. Defects left standing and deliberate omissions

The following are known and are being left in place by the staging above rather than overlooked, and each is recorded so that another implementation may opt into a fix deliberately.

`IdentifiedActuator` remains unusable under Newton with the CUDA graph fast path enabled, and a reader who reaches for `use_newton_actuators=False` to make it work will lose that fast path with no warning from IsaacLab. The condition is a silent performance cliff rather than an error, and the note belongs in the actuator module's docstring.

The tanh regularisation width `activation_vel` has no Newton counterpart, so the Newton actuator variant diverges from the identified model near zero velocity, which is the regime a standing biped occupies. The divergence is to be measured against the identification data rather than assumed negligible.

Centre of mass randomisation on Newton writes an inertial property whose mass matrix is not fully recomputed, by the stock term's own admission, so it is held at startup mode only until the upstream fix lands. The same admission is the gating risk of the co-optimisation port, for the reason section 10.13.6 gives, that a design search optimising against a stale inertia fails silently rather than loudly.

The terrain following behaviour of the training video camera is reduced to a static environment choice, because per step retargeting of the visualiser has no advertised 3.0 equivalent. The capability is lost rather than ported.

The material property mirror in the symmetry module carries a PhysX specific width in its guiding comment and is presently disabled by a `None`, so the comment would mislead the next reader into deriving a PhysX permutation and applying it under Newton. The comment is to be corrected in the same pass as the observation branch.

MuJoCo Warp enforces neither the rated actuator velocity limit nor the solver joint velocity limit, so any behaviour the present policies derive from those bounds is unreproduced under Newton. Whether any such behaviour exists is a stage zero measurement.

Newton offers no dynamic joint friction, so the workspace's `randomize_joint_friction_model` cannot randomise the static and dynamic coefficients independently under Newton, and its Newton behaviour must be documented rather than silently reduced.

The published performance comparison this document could find is a single in-hand manipulation figure [6], and the Isaac Lab paper itself describes the Newton integration as upcoming rather than measured [9], so every throughput claim about this workspace's own tasks must come from the stage five measurement and from nowhere else.
