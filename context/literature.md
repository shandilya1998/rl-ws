# literature.md — Literature Survey for the Learned-Model Co-Optimisation Architecture

This file records the verified literature survey performed on 2026-07-02 in
support of the learned-model extension of the COPT pipeline (encoder-decoder
privileged estimator trained jointly with PPO inside the CMA-ES morphology
loop). All citations were verified against arXiv, OpenReview, PMLR, Science
Robotics, Nature, AAAI, the Royal Society, and project pages. The main design
document consuming this survey is `../plans/COPT_LEARNED_MODEL.md`.

## Cluster 1: Privileged learning and estimator architectures in legged locomotion

- Lee, J., Hwangbo, J., Wellhausen, L., Koltun, V., Hutter, M. (2020). Learning
  quadrupedal locomotion over challenging terrain. Science Robotics 5(47),
  eabc5986. Teacher policy trained with privileged terrain and contact
  information, distilled into a proprioception-only student deployed zero-shot
  on ANYmal. Canonical two-stage privileged-learning recipe that the proposed
  estimator compresses into a single stage.
- Kumar, A., Fu, Z., Pathak, D., Malik, J. (2021). RMA: Rapid Motor Adaptation
  for Legged Robots. RSS 2021, arXiv:2107.04034. Base policy conditioned on a
  latent encoding of privileged environment parameters (mass, friction,
  terrain), adaptation module regresses the latent from proprioceptive history.
  Direct precedent for conditioning the actor on a low-dimensional latent of
  privileged physical parameters including body-parameter variation.
- Ji, G., Mun, J., Kim, H., Hwangbo, J. (2022). Concurrent Training of a Control
  Policy and a State Estimator for Dynamic and Robust Legged Locomotion. IEEE
  RA-L 7(2), 4630-4637, arXiv:2202.05481. Policy and supervised state estimator
  (base velocity, foot height, contact probability) trained concurrently in one
  loop with the estimator output fed to the policy. Closest single-stage
  precedent for a supervised regression head on privileged dynamic quantities.
- Long, J., Wang, Z., Li, Q., Cao, L., Gao, J., Pang, J. (2024). Hybrid Internal
  Model: Learning Agile Legged Locomotion with Simulated Robot Response. ICLR
  2024, arXiv:2312.11460. HIMLoco learns a hybrid internal embedding (explicit
  velocity plus implicit stability latent) from proprioceptive history,
  optimised jointly with PPO through an auxiliary contrastive objective in a
  single phase. Stated inspiration for the proposal, the MSE decoder is a
  regression-based sibling of HIM's contrastive loss.
- Nahrendra, I. M. A., Yu, B., Myung, H. (2023). DreamWaQ: Learning Robust
  Quadrupedal Locomotion With Implicit Terrain Imagination via Deep
  Reinforcement Learning. ICRA 2023, arXiv:2301.10602. Asymmetric actor-critic
  with a context-aided estimator network, a variational encoder-decoder trained
  jointly with PPO whose latent conditions the actor. The most direct
  architectural precedent for the proposal.
- Miki, T., Lee, J., Hwangbo, J., Wellhausen, L., Koltun, V., Hutter, M. (2022).
  Learning robust perceptive locomotion for quadrupedal robots in the wild.
  Science Robotics 7(62), eabk2822, arXiv:2201.08117. Attention-based recurrent
  encoder fusing exteroceptive height samples with proprioception under
  privileged learning. Supports feeding a sparse height map into the encoder.

## Cluster 2: Auxiliary and representation losses reducing RL sample complexity

- Jaderberg, M., Mnih, V., Czarnecki, W. M., Schaul, T., Leibo, J. Z., Silver,
  D., Kavukcuoglu, K. (2017). Reinforcement Learning with Unsupervised
  Auxiliary Tasks. ICLR 2017, arXiv:1611.05397. UNREAL, auxiliary control and
  prediction heads sharing the policy trunk, about 10x faster learning on
  Labyrinth. Foundational evidence that auxiliary prediction gradients shape
  shared representations and cut sample complexity.
- Laskin, M., Srinivas, A., Abbeel, P. (2020). CURL: Contrastive Unsupervised
  Representations for Reinforcement Learning. ICML 2020, arXiv:2004.04136.
  Contrastive auxiliary loss on the encoder of a model-free agent, large
  data-efficiency gains, supports single-optimiser joint representation and RL
  objectives.
- Yarats, D., Zhang, A., Kostrikov, I., Amos, B., Pineau, J., Fergus, R. (2021).
  Improving Sample Efficiency in Model-Free Reinforcement Learning from Images.
  AAAI 2021, 35(12), 10674-10681, arXiv:1910.01741. SAC+AE, a regularised
  autoencoder whose shared encoder receives gradients from both reconstruction
  and Q-learning. Most direct evidence that an MSE reconstruction decoder
  combined with the RL loss on one encoder is stable and sample-efficient.
- Schwarzer, M., Anand, A., Goel, R., Hjelm, R. D., Courville, A., Bachman, P.
  (2021). Data-Efficient Reinforcement Learning with Self-Predictive
  Representations. ICLR 2021, arXiv:2007.05929. SPR, predicting future latent
  states as an auxiliary objective, 55 percent relative improvement on Atari
  100k. Supports auxiliary losses forcing the latent to carry dynamics
  information.
- Lyle, C., Rowland, M., Ostrovski, G., Dabney, W. (2021). On the Effect of
  Auxiliary Tasks on Representation Dynamics. AISTATS 2021, PMLR 130,
  arXiv:2102.13089. Theoretical analysis of how auxiliary prediction tasks
  shape TD representations. Principled grounding for privileged-dynamics
  targets inducing task-aligned latents.
- Caron, M., Misra, I., Mairal, J., Goyal, P., Bojanowski, P., Joulin, A.
  (2020). Unsupervised Learning of Visual Features by Contrasting Cluster
  Assignments. NeurIPS 2020, arXiv:2006.09882. SwAV, the swapped-assignment
  objective over a learned prototype bank with Sinkhorn-Knopp normalisation of
  the assignment, which HIMLoco (cluster 1) adopts verbatim as the contrastive
  half of its estimator and which the vendored MorAL clone therefore inherits.
  The source of the loss dissected in `../tron1-rl-isaaclab-cozum/context/HIM.md`
  and cited by `../tron1-rl-isaaclab-cozum/context/MorAL.md`. Not to be confused
  with the Stephane Caron entries in cluster 6, a different author.

## Cluster 3: Dynamics and model information for sample efficiency

- Chua, K., Calandra, R., McAllister, R., Levine, S. (2018). Deep Reinforcement
  Learning in a Handful of Trials using Probabilistic Dynamics Models. NeurIPS
  2018, arXiv:1805.12114. PETS, probabilistic ensemble dynamics models matching
  model-free asymptotic performance with orders of magnitude fewer samples.
- Janner, M., Fu, J., Zhang, M., Levine, S. (2019). When to Trust Your Model:
  Model-Based Policy Optimization. NeurIPS 2019, arXiv:1906.08253. MBPO, short
  learned-model rollouts inside model-free optimisation with usefulness
  guarantees. The proposal takes the safer middle path, dynamics prediction as
  a representation-shaping signal only, avoiding compounding rollout error.
- Hafner, D., Pasukonis, J., Ba, J., Lillicrap, T. (2023/2025). Mastering
  Diverse Domains through World Models (DreamerV3). arXiv:2301.04104, Nature
  640 (2025). Latents shaped by dynamics prediction are sufficient statistics
  for control across more than 150 tasks.

## Synthesis

The locomotion line (Lee, Kumar, Ji, Miki, Nahrendra, Long) shows privileged
simulation quantities are best exploited by encoding them into a latent that
conditions the actor, and the field has moved from two-stage distillation
toward single-stage concurrent estimators trained jointly with PPO, DreamWaQ
and HIMLoco providing the exact encoder-decoder-plus-auxiliary-loss template.
The representation line (Jaderberg, Laskin, Yarats, Schwarzer, Lyle)
establishes empirically and theoretically that auxiliary reconstruction and
prediction losses sharing an encoder with the RL objective in a single
optimiser reliably reduce sample complexity. The model-based line (Chua,
Janner, Hafner) shows dynamics knowledge is the dominant lever on sample
efficiency, imported here safely as a prediction target rather than a rollout
generator. The co-design line (Sims through Strgar and Kriegman) identifies
per-candidate controller adaptation as the bottleneck of morphology
optimisation and shows design-conditioned policies amortise learning across
bodies. The proposed encoder over link lengths, masses, terrain, and history,
with a decoder regressing torques, accelerations, inertia, and contact
quantities, sits at the intersection of all four lines.

## Cluster 7: Gait quality, symmetry, and posture shaping for bipeds (added 2026-07-17 for GAIT_STRATEGY.md)

- Siekmann et al., Sim-to-Real Learning of All Common Bipedal Gaits via Periodic
  Reward Composition, ICRA 2021, arXiv:2011.01387. Probabilistic phase clock
  intervals gate penalties on foot force during swing and foot velocity during
  stance, a (frequency, offset, duration) parameterisation spans standing,
  walking, hopping, running, skipping, transferred to Cassie. The unused
  GaitReward class in this repo's mdp/rewards.py is the Walk These Ways variant
  of exactly this construction.
- van Marum et al., Revisiting Reward Design and Evaluation for Robust Humanoid
  Standing and Walking, arXiv:2404.19173 (Digit). Minimally constraining reward,
  Table I weights, xy velocity 0.15 each, roll-pitch orientation 0.2 (heavier
  than each velocity term), single-foot-contact 0.1 with a 0.2 s grace window,
  base height 0.05, feet airtime sum(t_air - 0.4) at touchdown weight 1.0 (the
  only sparse term, deliberately the largest weight), base acceleration 0.1,
  action difference 0.02, torque 0.02. Velocity-only rewards yield two-footed
  hopping, the single-foot-contact reward is reported as the most reliable,
  tuning-free fix, clocks are rejected because they impede disturbance
  rejection, PPO is extended with a mirror loss, LSTM (64,64), 50 Hz control.
- Mittal et al., Symmetry Considerations for Learning Task Symmetric Robot
  Policies, ICRA 2024, arXiv:2403.04359. Compares symmetry data augmentation
  against mirror loss on PPO, the interface is implemented verbatim in the
  vendored rsl_rl (symmetry_cfg with use_data_augmentation, use_mirror_loss,
  data_augmentation_func) and surfaced as RslRlSymmetryCfg in isaaclab_rl.
- Yu, Turk, Liu, Learning Symmetric and Low-Energy Locomotion, SIGGRAPH 2018.
  Mirror symmetry loss added to the policy objective plus energy terms and
  curriculum produce symmetric gaits without any reference motion.
- Gait-Conditioned RL with Multi-Phase Curriculum for Humanoid Locomotion,
  arXiv:2505.20619. Human-inspired reference-free terms, contact pattern,
  foot clearance, straight knee during stance, anti-phase arm-leg swing via a
  Z-axis angular momentum penalty, three-phase curriculum walk, stand and
  transitions, then run.
- Peng et al., AMP, SIGGRAPH 2021, arXiv:2104.02180, and HumanMimic (Tang et
  al., arXiv:2309.14225, Wasserstein adversarial imitation for humanoids).
  Discriminator style rewards over retargeted mocap replace hand-tuned gait
  shaping when human-likeness is the objective, at the cost of a dataset,
  retargeting, and adversarial training stability work.
- IsaacLab reference configs (local checkout, G1Rewards in
  isaaclab_tasks/.../velocity/config/g1/rough_env_cfg.py). Tracking 1.0 and
  2.0, feet_air_time_positive_biped weight 0.25 threshold 0.4 (dense,
  non-negative, single-stance gated), flat_orientation_l2 -1.0 (half the
  linear tracking weight), joint_deviation_l1 on hip yaw-roll, arms, torso,
  is_terminated penalty -200, commands lin_vel_x (0.0, 1.0). These ratios are
  the field's working defaults for upright biped posture.
- Su et al., arXiv:2509.09106, LIPM-guided stability reward, already surveyed
  in cluster 6 and planned in /ws/plans/LIPM_REWARD.md, complements rather than
  replaces the posture and gait terms above.

## Cluster 8: Cadence, contact scheduling, and the anti-hold structure of gait rewards (added 2026-07-20 for run 2026-07-20_07-52-14)

Cluster 7 was surveyed to answer why a biped will not leave double support. This cluster answers the opposite question, why a biped that has reached single support will not leave it, which is the failure of run 2026-07-20_07-52-14.

- van Marum et al., arXiv:2404.19173, Table I retrieved in full from the HTML version on 2026-07-20 and now recorded exactly, since the earlier note paraphrased it. x and y velocity 0.15 each, yaw orientation 0.1, roll and pitch orientation 0.2, feet contact 0.1, base height 0.05, feet airtime 1.0, feet orientation 0.05, feet position 0.05, arm 1.03, base acceleration 0.1, action difference 0.02, torque 0.02. Two structural facts matter here and neither was recorded before. First, the feet airtime term is SPARSE and touchdown triggered, sum over feet of (t_air - 0.4) times the touchdown indicator, and it carries the LARGEST weight in the table, so their reward pays for step events and pays nothing at all to a robot that holds a foot up without landing it. Second, their feet contact term is 1 if single contact occurred at least once in the preceding 0.2 s, a GRACE WINDOW, so brief double support during weight transfer is not punished. Our no_fly has no such window and therefore prices every instant of double support at zero, which is what drives the transition count down. Their reported velocity only failure mode is two footed hopping. Their three arguments against periodic clocks are the undefined clock input during standing and transitions, the incentive towards low foot velocities in standing mode which impedes disturbance rejection, and the constraint on free foot motion during recovery from a disturbance.
- Humanoid-Gym, Gu et al., arXiv:2404.05695. A periodic stance mask I_p(t) defines the planned contact schedule, two double support and two single support phases per cycle, and the contact pattern reward phi(I_p(t) - I_d(t)) rewards the measured contacts for matching it, at weight 1.0 which is the largest term in their set. The clock pair sin(2 pi t / C_T), cos(2 pi t / C_T) is supplied as a policy input. This is the most direct available answer to the requirement that something in the reward set specify WHEN each foot should be down, and unlike an air time term it cannot be farmed by holding, since holding is off schedule by construction. The paper does not state how C_T is chosen, which must therefore be set from the robot's own pendulum time constant.
- Learning Sim-to-Real Humanoid Locomotion in 15 Minutes, arXiv:2512.01996. Argues that the customary twenty plus term humanoid reward can be reduced below ten terms, and notably uses foot height TRACKING against a reference swing profile rather than a Gaussian on height multiplied by foot speed. That formulation is immune to the parked and waved foot exploit, because a foot that remains high is off the reference just as a foot that never rises is. The paper does not itself discuss degenerate gaits, so this is an inference from its reward form and should be labelled as such.
- Gait-Conditioned RL with Multi-Phase Curriculum, arXiv:2505.20619, already in cluster 7, adds here that a gait conditioned reward routing scheme keeps gait specific contact and push off terms from interfering across walking, running and standing, and that the policy emits gait frequency as an action constrained to [0.7, 1.3] with an action smoothness term on it. Relevant if a commanded cadence is later made adaptive rather than constant.
- Denoising World Model Learning, arXiv:2408.14472. Constructs its reward from the complementary roles of foot force and foot velocity, promoting high foot velocity during swing and appropriate foot force during stance. This is the Siekmann periodic construction restated, and it is the form the unused GaitReward class in this repository already implements.
- Standing caution recorded from this survey. Neither van Marum's sparse touchdown term nor Humanoid-Gym's contact mask rewards the step COUNT directly. van Marum's (t_air - 0.4) actually pays more for one long hold followed by a landing than for several short swings, so it is not by itself an anti hold device, its protection comes from being touchdown gated combined with the grace window on the contact term. Only the clock based family prices cadence explicitly. This distinction was not obvious from the abstracts and is the main reason the full texts were retrieved.

## Cluster 9: Symmetry enforcement in RL legged locomotion (added 2026-07-22 for the SD_BRS1 limp, run 2026-07-21_06-03-36)

This cluster answers why the GaitReward run alternates its feet yet still limps, and grounds the symmetry data augmentation plan in /ws/plans/SYMMETRY_PLAN.md. All equations and numbers below were read from fetched PDFs, the two saved locally are the primary paper and the Abdolhosseini paper.

- Su et al., "Leveraging Symmetry in RL-based Legged Locomotion Control", arXiv 2403.17320, IROS 2024, the paper and SymmLoco code the user referenced. It models the robot's sagittal symmetry as the order two reflection group C2 equal to {e, g} with g squared equal to e, and proves that when the transition density, the initial state density and the reward are all C2 invariant, the optimal policy is equivariant, g applied to the optimal action equals the optimal action at the reflected state, and the optimal value is invariant. It compares THREE methods, a vanilla PPO baseline, PPOaug which is data augmentation, and PPOeqic which is a hard equivariant network built with MorphoSymm and ESCNN, and it DELIBERATELY EXCLUDES the mirror loss, citing the Mittal note that the loss is outperformed by augmentation. Verified results, the unconstrained baseline scores a Robinson symmetry index four times higher than PPOaug and eight times higher than PPOeqic, and PPOeqic is the most sample efficient and accurate in simulation. The decisive recommendation for us, for TASK SPACE symmetry such as door pushing the hard equivariant policy excels and even transfers from one trained side to both, but for INTRINSIC MOTION symmetry, real bipedal walking where actuator and mass asymmetries break the perfectly symmetric assumption, the hard constraint becomes brittle under distribution shift and the softer PPOaug is more robust because it adapts to the robot's real asymmetries. The SD_BRS1 is a real biped with an identified and therefore asymmetric inertial model, so data augmentation is the grounded first choice. A temporal gait phase signal psi is added with g applied to psi equal to psi plus 0.5 modulo 1, a half cycle offset, which also sidesteps the neutral state problem by ensuring the reflected state differs from the original even in a symmetric pose.
- Abdolhosseini et al., "On Learning Symmetric Locomotion", Motion in Games 2019, ACM DOI 10.1145/3359566.3360070, not on arXiv, full ACM PDF read. This is the paper that catalogues the FOUR practical methods, named DUP, LOSS, PHASE, NET. DUP duplicates every transition through the mirror and adds both to the update, the data augmentation route. LOSS adds the auxiliary mirror penalty. PHASE learns half a cycle and replays it mirrored, suited to periodic imitation. NET is the hard equivariant network. Reported symmetry indices lower is better, Walker2D baseline 3.97, DUP 3.77, LOSS 2.56, NET 2.00, Cassie baseline 9.27, PHASE 4.49, NET 5.15, LOSS 15.72, so no single method wins everywhere, LOSS is the most consistent soft method, NET the only exact one, DUP the weakest enforcer but the cheapest to add. Two cautions, symmetry enforcement gives no reliable learning speedup and can even slow it, contradicting the naive 2x expectation, and a strictly symmetric policy cannot leave a symmetric neutral pose, the neutral state problem, so training must start from a noised non neutral posture.
- The mirror loss form, introduced by Yu, Turk, Liu, "Learning Symmetric and Low-Energy Locomotion", arXiv 1801.08093, adopted verbatim by Abdolhosseini as equation two, L_sym(theta) equals the sum over t of the squared norm of pi_theta(s_t) minus M_a applied to pi_theta applied to M_s(s_t), where M_s mirrors the state and M_a mirrors the action, added to the PPO objective with a scalar weight, both papers use 4. This is exactly the loss IsaacLab's RslRlSymmetryCfg computes when use_mirror_loss is set.
- Mittal et al., "Symmetry Considerations for Learning Task Symmetric Robot Policies", arXiv 2403.04359, the IsaacLab symmetry note, evaluated on ANYmal. Its central and directly load bearing point for our implementation, when a mirrored sample enters the PPO surrogate the importance ratio must keep the ORIGINAL transition's old action log probability in the denominator, not the mirrored sample's own probability under the old policy, since the latter can be arbitrarily small and would make the ratio and its variance explode. It provides a state visitation ratio correction for the induced distribution shift, and it reports empirically that data augmentation reaches faster convergence and better behaviour than the symmetry loss, which is why the primary paper drops the loss. The rsl_rl at /ws/rsl_rl already satisfies this, it tiles old_actions_log_prob from the originals rather than recomputing it on the mirrored action, so the mirrored row is scored against the original old probability.
- Ordonez Apraez et al., "On discrete symmetries of robotics systems, a group theoretic and data driven analysis", arXiv 2302.10433, RSS 2023 and IJRR 2024, the MorphoSymm library. Establishes the group theoretic framework and the principle that a morphological symmetry propagates to a representation acting on all proprioceptive and exteroceptive measurements, states, actions, dynamics, optimal policy and value. Four of its authors co author the primary paper. SymmLoco builds its reps from this library's a1.yaml, group C2, joint permutation and a per joint reflection vector whose signs are determined by the joint frame orientations rather than by an abstract roll versus pitch rule, which is exactly why the SD_BRS1 HipPitch flips despite being a pitch joint, its left and right frames carry opposite axes.
- The biped sagittal sign table, derived from reflection physics and consistent with the MorphoSymm principle. A polar vector, base linear velocity or projected gravity, flips only its lateral y component, multiply by [1, -1, 1]. A pseudovector, base angular velocity, flips its in plane roll and yaw components and keeps pitch, multiply by [-1, 1, -1]. The velocity command flips lateral and yaw, multiply by [1, -1, -1]. The joints swap left for right with a sign flip on the degrees of freedom whose axis leaves the sagittal plane. The gait phase [sin, cos] negates both channels because a left right swap shifts the shared clock by exactly half a cycle while the command offset is 0.5.
- The gait symmetry metric to log, the Robinson symmetry index, SI equal to 2 times the absolute difference of the left and right measurements divided by their sum, times 100, lower is more symmetric, used by both the primary and foundational papers, originally Robinson, Herzog, Nigg 1987.
- Later phase notes, weakly grounded and marked as such. No fetched paper links the symmetry family directly to knee flexion, the stiff leg fix is generic reward shaping not a symmetry mechanism. For standing on a zero velocity command inside a periodic gait, Siekmann et al., arXiv 2011.01387, treats standing as a first class gait by setting the contact schedule so both feet may remain in stance at zero commanded velocity, the clean formulation for the standing shuffle of observation 3.

## Cluster 10: Natural gait, knee flexion, upright posture, and actuation smoothness (added 2026-07-23 for the SD_BRS1 baseline run 2026-07-22_11-36-53)

This cluster grounds `/ws/plans/NATURAL_GAIT_PLAN.md`, the plan for the three naturalness defects of the first successful SD_BRS1 walker, the locked knee, the forward lean, the non smooth actuation.

Knee and natural gait. The straight STANCE knee is energetically correct, established in passive dynamic walking, McGeer, Passive Dynamic Walking, IJRR 9(2) 1990, and confirmed by emergence under pure effort objectives, Heess et al., Emergence of Locomotion Behaviours in Rich Environments, arXiv 1707.02286. The pathology is the missing SWING flexion, human gait flexes the knee in swing to shorten and clear the leg, Winter, Biomechanics and Motor Control of Human Movement. The reference free fix is phase gated joint posture shaping, Peng, Bao, Zhou, Gait-Conditioned RL with Multi-Phase Curriculum, arXiv 2505.20619, which adds a straight knee during stance term at weight 0.1 on the Unitree G1 with no motion capture, its one fully published biomechanical term being the yaw angular momentum R = minus(L_z)^2 minus 0.4(L_la,z minus L_ra,z)^2 at weight 5.0. The imitation fix is DeepMimic, Peng et al., arXiv 1804.02717, tracking a reference with pose, velocity, end effector, and centre of mass terms and reference state init, or its phase free adversarial form AMP, Peng et al., arXiv 2104.02180, style reward r_S = max(0, 1 minus 0.25(D(s,s') minus 1)^2), shown for legged sim to real by Escontrela et al., arXiv 2203.15103, with a lower cost of transport than hand designed style rewards.

Posture. The projected gravity penalty flat_orientation_l2 is the legged_gym standard, Rudin et al., Learning to Walk in Minutes, arXiv 2109.11978, symmetric and reference free. Weight calibration from van Marum et al., Revisiting Reward Design and Evaluation for Robust Humanoid Standing and Walking, arXiv 2404.19173, IROS 2024, an orientation to linear tracking ratio near two thirds, and the IsaacLab G1 sets it at parity, which brackets our term at minus thirty three to minus fifty against the 50 tracking weight, so minus thirty five is adequate and the lean is a shaping problem. Note that van Marum AVOIDS clock based rewards, arguing they hinder disturbance recovery, a caveat against leaning further on the GaitReward clock for posture.

## Cluster 11: Reward kernel failure, nominal pose, and termination shaping (added 2026-07-27 after the Phase A knee reward failed in run 2026-07-23_11-31-57)

This cluster answers why a correctly weighted Gaussian knee reward produced literally nothing, and what working systems do instead. It supersedes the cluster 10 assumption that a swing gated flexion reward is the right instrument.

THE HEADLINE, negative and decisive. NO mainstream bipedal or humanoid codebase obtains knee flexion from a reward. Every one carries it in the DEFAULT JOINT POSE of the articulation. IsaacLab `isaaclab_assets/robots/unitree.py` G1 knee 0.42, hip pitch -0.20, ankle pitch -0.23, H1 knee 0.79, hip pitch -0.28, ankle -0.52. unitree_rl_gym G1 knee 0.30, hip pitch -0.10, ankle -0.20. Walk These Ways (Margolis arXiv 2212.03238) Go1 calf -1.5, thigh 0.8 to 1.0. The invariant across every configuration surveyed is that the default is NEVER a straight knee. Where a reward touches joint posture it is `joint_deviation_l1`, the summed absolute deviation FROM that already flexed default, never a reward pulling AWAY from a straight one. IsaacLab G1 applies it to hip yaw and hip roll at -0.1, arms -0.1, torso -0.1, and DELIBERATELY NOT to hip pitch or the knee, the two DOF that must swing. Digit, the closest true biped analogue, does add a knee deviation but only at -0.2, with hip yaw -0.2, hip roll -0.1, `flat_orientation_l2` -2.5 and a `bad_orientation` termination at limit_angle 0.7 rad. The established remedy for compensatory OFF AXIS hip rotation is therefore a small deviation penalty on hip roll and yaw at about -0.1, never a penalty on hip pitch amplitude, which is the primary swing actuator.

KERNEL WIDTH must be sized to the error the policy CURRENTLY produces, not to the task range. legged_gym sets `tracking_sigma` 0.25 in SQUARED error units, an effective tolerance near 0.5 m/s chosen from the achievable tracking error. Peng et al., Learning Agile Robotic Locomotion Skills by Imitating Animals, RSS 2020, arXiv 2004.00784, weights five exponential kernels whose interior coefficients span 0.1 (joint velocity) through 5 (joint angle) and 20 and 10 (root pose) to 40 (end effector Cartesian), each set from that quantity's natural error scale, so a single width is never reused across quantities. Humanoid-Gym arXiv 2404.05695 is the instructive contrast, it keeps ALL defaults at zero but drives the knee from a phase conditioned reference `sin_pos = sin(2 pi phase)` with a knee excursion amplitude of about 0.34 rad, tracked by `exp(-2 ||diff||) - 0.2 clamp(||diff||,0,0.5)`, so the target FOLLOWS the policy and the kernel is wide, a 0.3 rad error still returning 0.55. A reference tracking reward can work from a zero default, but only when the target is never far from the current pose.

STRUCTURAL ALTERNATIVES to a peaked kernel. Kim et al., Not Only Rewards But Also Constraints, IEEE T-RO, arXiv 2308.12517, replaces kernel shaped terms with CONSTRAINT COSTS that are zero inside a desired range read from the URDF limits and grow outside it, collapsing ten or more hand tuned terms to effectively one coefficient. A constraint cost cannot vanish the way a distant Gaussian does. Potential based shaping, Ng, Harada and Russell, ICML 1999, `F(s,a,s') = gamma Phi(s') - Phi(s)` is necessary and sufficient for policy invariance, with Wiewiora, JAIR 19, 205, adding that EPISODIC tasks need `Phi(terminal) = 0` or the return is biased, which matters where episodes terminate early. But Jeon et al., arXiv 2307.10142, benchmarking PBRS on humanoid locomotion, finds only MARGINAL convergence gains and that the real benefit is robustness to term scaling, so PBRS is not a cure for a vanishing gradient. Curriculum on the TARGET is the third route, Bengio et al., ICML 2009, with a concrete scheduled instance in Babadi et al., MIG 2019, arXiv 1907.11842, whose termination threshold anneals 0.75 to 0.5 across training, strict early and relaxed as competence grows.

TERMINATION. DeepMimic's own ablation shows early termination matters enormously for acrobatics and NOT AT ALL for plain walking, backflip 0.791 against 0.730 and sideflip 0.823 against 0.717 but walk 0.980 against 0.981, so a high termination rate on a walking task is better read as a symptom than a cause. More importantly, NO IsaacLab humanoid reference config uses a HEIGHT termination at all, the base velocity template terminates only on time out and illegal contact with the base body and G1, H1 and Digit merely override that contact body to the torso. He et al., H2O, IROS 2024, arXiv 2403.04436, uses projected gravity on x or y above 0.7 plus a base height below 0.3 m, a floor far BENEATH the standing height rather than just below it. A height threshold set just under the stance height truncates precisely the crouched states a policy must explore to discover that knee flexion is viable, which compounds a vanishing reward with a data availability problem.

## Cluster 12: Provenance of the contact history index, and the ancestry of this codebase (added 2026-07-27)

Not a reward design cluster but an implementation archaeology one, recorded because it establishes this project's ANCESTRY, which is useful well beyond the immediate question.

THE LINEAGE. The direct IsaacLab ancestor of `environments` is `Andy-xiong6/bipedal_locomotion_isaaclab` (github), which carries the identical directory skeleton and the "LimX Point Foot's locomotion task" docstring. The Isaac Gym sibling for the same TRON1 hardware is `limxdynamics/tron1-rl-isaacgym`, formerly `pointfoot-legged-gym`. Both descend from `leggedrobotics/legged_gym`. This repo's own genesis commit `b5217a9` (2025-07-14) is a VENDOR SQUASH from jenkins_ci@limxdynamics.com whose entire message is a version string, so nothing before it has a surviving rationale.

THE VERDICT on the `[:, -1]` contact index, a PORTING BUG on five grounds. IsaacLab DOCUMENTS the ordering, `contact_sensor_data.py:84-91`, first index most recent, last index oldest. No first party IsaacLab code reads a trailing index anywhere, only `[:, 0]` or a max or mean over the whole axis, and the framework's own debounce primitives `compute_first_contact` and `compute_first_air` use tracked contact and air time scalars rather than slicing the history. The Isaac Gym ancestors have NO history axis at all, `self.contact_forces` is instantaneous in legged_gym, unitree_rl_gym and humanoid-gym alike, and the LimX biped debounce is `contact_filt = contact OR last_contacts` with a manually cached previous step, temporally correct by construction, which is exactly the semantics the port was reproducing. The direct IsaacLab ancestor has `no_fly` at `[:, 0]` correct throughout and lacks `keep_ankle_pitch_zero_in_air` entirely. And the local git history shows `no_fly` being REGRESSED from correct to incorrect by commit `a2f1e2d` as an unremarked side effect, with a later commit fixing a different bug in the same neighbourhood without noticing.

GENERAL LESSON worth keeping. When porting a legged_gym derived reward to IsaacLab, the contact representation CHANGES SHAPE, from an instantaneous `(N, B, 3)` tensor plus a hand cached previous step to a rolling `(N, T, B, 3)` history whose newest sample is at index ZERO. The python habit that `[-1]` means most recent is exactly wrong here. Any reward that asks whether a foot is on the ground right now should use `[:, 0]`, and any that wants debouncing should either OR `[:, 0]` with `[:, 1]` or reduce by max over the whole axis, the latter being what all first party IsaacLab code does.

Smoothness for sim to real. The strongest instrument is CAPS, Mysore et al., Regularizing Action Policies for Smooth Control with RL, arXiv 2012.06644, ICRA 2021, two policy space regularisers, temporal L_T = ||pi(s_t) minus pi(s_{t+1})||_2 and spatial L_S = ||pi(s_t) minus pi(s_bar)||_2 with s_bar drawn from N(s, sigma), added as J minus lambda_T L_T minus lambda_S L_S, reporting a 96 percent smoothness improvement and an 80 percent power reduction on a real quadrotor, damping the state to action map directly rather than paying for roughness through a reward penalty. The reward side is the legged_gym action rate and torque penalties already in the stack, plus the one term the stack lacks, a torque rate penalty. Deeper fidelity is the actuator network, Hwangbo et al., Learning agile and dynamic motor skills for legged robots, Science Robotics 4(26) 2019, closing the torque transfer gap. The GaitReward clock is itself from Siekmann et al., arXiv 2011.01387, shown to give smooth hardware transferable bipedal gaits.

## Cluster 13: Swing trajectory shaping, impact pricing, and yaw authority (added 2026-07-31 for /ws/plans/GAIT_EFFICIENCY_PLAN.md)

This cluster answers three questions the earlier clusters left open, why a clearance reward buys a plateau rather than an arc, how the published systems keep a foot from striking the ground hard, and how a biped with no vertical axis actuator turns. It grounds the six phase programme of `/ws/plans/GAIT_EFFICIENCY_PLAN.md`, written against the four run ablation of 2026-07-28 to 2026-07-30 recorded in the twentieth pass of `brs_gait.md`.

THE BIOMECHANICAL STANDARD, now recorded with figures so it need not be searched again. Adult walking at a comfortable 1.33 m/s runs at a cadence of 113 steps per minute with a stride length of 1.41 m, dividing the gait cycle into 62 percent stance, 38 percent swing and 24 percent DOUBLE SUPPORT. The vertical ground reaction force peaks between 1.0 and 1.5 body weights in walking, rising with speed, against 2.0 to 2.9 in running (Nilsson and Thorstensson, Acta Physiologica Scandinavica 136(2), 1989), and it is double humped, the first hump being weight acceptance over roughly a quarter of stance rather than a single collision. Minimum toe clearance occurs at or very near MID SWING at under 2.5 cm, so the human swing clearance profile has an interior MINIMUM, not a plateau, and that event is the one most associated with tripping. Stance width is 1.0 to 1.3 times hip width (Perry, Gait Analysis, 1992), the figure the nineteenth pass of `brs_gait.md` already applied. The dimensionless cost of transport is near 0.2 for human walking, 0.055 and 0.08 for the Cornell and Delft passive walkers against an estimated 0.05 for the mechanical component of human walking, and roughly 1.6 to 2 for the Honda humanoid, so a learned policy may land anywhere across a forty fold range and nothing in a velocity tracking objective selects the efficient end.

SWING SHAPING, and why the present form cannot work. A Gaussian kernel on foot height multiplied by a tanh of foot speed, the legged_gym derived form this repository carries as `foot_clearance_reward_v2`, has an integrand depending on the instantaneous height alone, so its maximiser over a swing of fixed duration is the trajectory reaching the target soonest, holding longest and leaving latest. A reward on an EXTREMUM determines only that extremum, whereas a reward on a REFERENCE determines the whole path. Humanoid-Gym arXiv 2404.05695 is the cleanest published alternative, keeping every joint default at zero and driving the leg from a phase conditioned sinusoid with a knee excursion near 0.34 rad, tracked by `phi(theta - theta_target, 2)` at weight 1.5, the LARGEST term in its table, so a foot high at the wrong moment is penalised exactly as one low at the wrong moment. Seo et al. arXiv 2512.01996 carry a foot height TRACKING term against a reference swing profile in a reward set reduced below ten terms, and defer their formulas to code, so they may be cited for the form but not for a weight. Mind Your Steps, arXiv 2606.08253, supplies a third variant, a swing window in which clearance accrues only if the correct foot breaks contact within the allotted interval, authors not verified.

IMPACT PRICING, two complementary mechanisms. Humanoid-Gym carries a large contact force term of the form minus max(F minus 400, 0) clipped at 100, at weight minus 0.01, which is exactly zero over the whole range a well behaved stance produces and therefore cannot distort the phase it is not meant to govern. The ground reaction force aware line of work (QuietWalk, arXiv 2604.23702) penalises the per foot normal force directly and reports that such a penalty must be introduced on a CURRICULUM, small at first and raised as competence grows, because a policy that has not yet learned to walk cannot afford it. The complementary mechanism penalises the DESCENT VELOCITY before contact rather than the force after it, preferred where the simulator's contact force is unreliable, and is exactly the form of `foot_landing_vel` already in `mdp/rewards.py` and already wired into TRON1 PF. Its advantage is that its gradient reaches the swing trajectory that caused the collision rather than the stance that follows it. The biomechanical warrant for pricing impact at all is McGeer's passive walking result, that the collision at heel strike is where a walking gait loses its energy, so a policy that increases its collision velocity pays twice, once to inject the energy and once to arrest it.

DOUBLE SUPPORT AND THE GRACE WINDOW, restated from cluster 8 because it is now load bearing rather than incidental. Van Marum et al. arXiv 2404.19173 define their single foot contact term as 1 if single contact occurred at least once in the preceding 0.2 s, so brief double support during weight transfer is NOT punished. A single support reward without such a window prices every instant of double support at zero and therefore drives the transition count downward, which is precisely what `no_fly` does here. At this task's 0.01 s control period their window is twenty steps, deeper than the four sample contact sensor history, so implementing it requires a stateful term rather than an argument.

BOOSTER GYM, arXiv 2506.15132, newly surveyed and the most complete recent reference table for a 50 Hz humanoid controller. Velocity tracking 1.0, 1.0 and 0.5 for the two linear and the yaw components. Base height minus 20, orientation minus 5, angular velocity xy minus 0.2. Torque minus 2e-4, TORQUE TIREDNESS ||tau/tau_max||^2 minus 1e-2, power max(tau q_dot, 0) minus 2e-4, joint velocity minus 1e-4, joint acceleration minus 1e-7, base acceleration minus 1e-4, action rate minus 1.0. Feet swing indicator plus 3.0, feet slip minus 0.1, FEET YAW ||psi_feet minus psi_base||^2 minus 1.0, FEET ROLL minus 0.1, feet distance max(d_ref minus d, 0) minus 1.0. Vertical velocity minus 2.0, joint position limit INDICATOR minus 1.0, collision minus 1.0. Three of these have no counterpart in the SD_BRS1 set and are prescribed by the plan, the torque tiredness term because the same absolute torque is unremarkable at a hip and saturating at an ankle so an absolute penalty cannot see the ankle roll saturation at all, the feet yaw term at twice the linear tracking weight, and the feet roll term which is van Marum's feet orientation under another name.

YAW AUTHORITY IN A BIPED WITH NO VERTICAL AXIS ACTUATOR. SD_BRS1 declares both `HipYaw` joints fixed, so yaw can be obtained only from the ground, as a friction moment under the stance sole or as the reaction to a change in whole body angular momentum about the vertical. The yaw compensation literature establishes the same physics from the disturbance side, the swinging leg generating a yaw moment the stance foot must absorb through friction, sufficiently so that mechanisms have been designed to cancel it (Bevel-geared mechanical foot, a bioinspired robotic foot compensating yaw moment of bipedal walking, Advanced Robotics, DOI 10.1080/01691864.2021.2017343, authors not verified, the publisher page returned 403 and the citation is recorded by title and DOI alone). The corollary for a turning COMMAND is that yaw authority is bounded by the sole friction moment and by the swing leg angular momentum, and that both are enlarged by a wider stance, which lengthens the moment arm of differential foot placement, and the second by permitting lateral foot placement rather than only fore and aft. The frontal plane balance literature adds that the lateral centre of mass excursion required per step is half the stance width, so a narrow stance is balanced by torso roll alone whereas a wide stance requires deliberate lateral motion, and the foot placement estimator line establishes that recovery from a lateral disturbance requires the foot to be placed further laterally than the centre of mass, which a narrow nominal stance leaves no room to do.

A CAUTION on stance width regulation, discovered by measurement rather than from the literature and recorded here because no surveyed paper states it. Both Booster Gym's feet distance term and this repository's `feet_distance` hinge on a scalar separation, and where that scalar is the PLANAR norm rather than the lateral component, a long stride satisfies the hinge while the lateral separation is zero. On SD_BRS1 the term read a mean penalty of 0.00014 while the feet stood laterally closer than their own sole width for 40 percent of the cycle and crossed the midline. Any configuration adopting this term should confirm which axis it measures before trusting it to regulate stance width.

CORRECTION to the caution above, dated 2026-08-07, which withdraws its measurement while leaving its algebra intact. The 40 percent figure and the claim that the feet crossed the midline were computed on a WORLD frame separation, which `scripts/rsl_rl/play.py:407` dumps and `scripts/analysis/stats.py:751` labels lateral without rotating. That quantity equals the stance width only at zero heading, and this robot turns continuously, so the reported lateral separation is a rotating mixture of the stance width and the stride. Rotated into the base frame the feet never cross, the lateral separation on run 2026-08-05_07-53-35 having a mean of 0.259 m, a fifth percentile of 0.217, a minimum of 0.091 and no sign change in ninety six thousand samples, against 0.27 percent of steps below the 0.194 m sole width where the world frame figure reports 47.9. The ALGEBRAIC objection stands unaltered, a planar norm still cannot regulate a stance width, and so does the advice to confirm the axis. What is withdrawn is the claim that this particular robot's stance was pathological. The general lesson is sharper than the one originally recorded, being that a per axis separation statistic must be confirmed to be in the BASE frame and not merely on the right axis, since a world frame reading of the correct axis is as wrong as the correct frame on the wrong one.

THE FRONTAL PLANE STEPPING SOURCE, fixed 2026-08-07, having stood in cluster 13 and in `/ws/plans/GAIT_EFFICIENCY_PLAN.md` as an unattributed body of work. Dingwell and Cusumano, Humans use multi-objective control to regulate lateral foot placement when walking, PLoS Computational Biology, 2019, DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1006850, fit competing control models to treadmill walking and find that lateral stepping is regulated as a MULTI OBJECTIVE problem strongly dominated by STEP WIDTH, which takes roughly 93 percent of the control effort against roughly 7 for absolute lateral position, and that this contrasts with the single objective speed control the same authors previously established in the fore and aft direction. The lateral separation is therefore the quantity biology regulates and the planar norm is not a quantity anything regulates, which is the biomechanical form of the algebraic objection above. It also supplies a design consequence, that lateral position is deliberately left loosely regulated, so a reward set which pins the lateral placement as tightly as it pins the step width is imposing a control structure human walking does not use.

CURRENT PRACTICE ON THE FEET DISTANCE TERM, surveyed 2026-08-07 for the rescoping of Phase 4. The convergent form computes the lateral separation of the feet in the ROBOT's own frame and penalises it below a minimum, with reported minima of 0.25 m for the Unitree H1 and 0.18 for the smaller G1 (Ren, Huang, Wang, Wang, Ben, Long, Yang, Pang and Luo, VB-Com, Learning Vision-Blind Composite Humanoid Locomotion Against Deficient Perception, arXiv:2502.14814, 2025, the reward's exact expression and weight not recovered, the retrieved document exceeding the fetch limit). Base frame PER AXIS separation terms are now standard rather than variant, one loco manipulation reward set carrying a fore and aft foot separation term on the magnitude of the base frame longitudinal difference at weight −5.0 beside a lateral root centring term at the same weight, and carrying NO lateral foot separation hinge, which is recorded because the absence bears on how universal the form is (Zhang, Yuan, Gurunath, Gupta, Omidshafiei, Agha-mohammadi, Vazquez-Chanlatte, Pedersen, He and Shi, FALCON, Learning Force-Adaptive Humanoid Loco-Manipulation, arXiv:2505.06776, 2025). The COUNTERVAILING consideration is that humanoids are now trained to cross supports narrower than their own nominal stance, one policy traversing a 25 cm beam while carrying a feet separation term in its gait group, so a lateral floor admitting no exception forecloses a capability the field currently pursues (Xie, Bai, Shi, Yang, Ge, Zhang and Li, Humanoid Whole-Body Locomotion on Narrow Terrain via Dynamic Balance and Reinforcement Learning, arXiv:2502.17219, 2025, its reward table naming the term but stating neither expression nor weight). The synthesis, which no surveyed paper states and which section 4.4 of the gait efficiency plan adopts, is a DISJUNCTIVE hinge, requiring the lateral separation to exceed a threshold and, where it does not, requiring the total separation to exceed a larger one, so that the stance width is regulated without a crossed placement being forbidden outright.

## Cluster 14: The measurement of a legged gait, stability margins, symmetry, smoothness and variability (added 2026-08-04 for /ws/plans/GAIT_STATISTICS_PLAN.md)

Every earlier cluster was surveyed to justify a change to a reward. This one is surveyed to judge the policy a reward produced, which is a different question and draws on a different literature, principally clinical gait analysis and the humanoid balance line rather than the reinforcement learning line. It grounds the statistics pipeline of `/ws/plans/GAIT_STATISTICS_PLAN.md` and the corroborated inventory of `gait_metrics.md`.

THE ENERGETIC CONVENTION, and why one figure is not enough. The dimensionless cost of transport must be reported in three conventions because they diverge by an order of magnitude on an antagonistic gait. The ABSOLUTE convention sums the magnitude of the joint power, charging a joint for braking as well as for driving, and is correct for an actuator that cannot regenerate. The NET convention sums algebraically and measures the power the motion itself demands. The POSITIVE convention clips the algebraic sum at zero and is the one the quadrupedal benchmarking literature adopts when it reports the inverse cost of transport as a headline metric beside the success rate and the velocity tracking reward (Benchmarking Model Predictive Control and Reinforcement Learning Based Control for Legged Robot Locomotion in MuJoCo Simulation, arXiv:2501.16590, authors not verified). SD_BRS1 run 2026-07-28_06-37-24 measures 2.835 absolute against a net figure near 0.25, a ratio of 9.5, and that ratio IS the defect, ninety percent of the actuation being antagonistic circulation. Cluster 13 already carries the scale, 0.2 for human walking and 0.055 to 0.08 for the passive walkers.

STABILITY MARGINS, four measures answering different questions. The ZERO MOMENT POINT criterion (Vukobratović and Borovac, Zero-Moment Point, Thirty Five Years of its Life, International Journal of Humanoid Robotics 1(1), 2004, pages 157 to 173) states that the contact remains unilateral while the point of vanishing horizontal ground reaction moment lies within the support polygon, so the distance from that point to the nearest edge is a margin with the dimension of length. The MARGIN OF STABILITY (Hof, Gazendam and Sinke, The condition for dynamic stability, Journal of Biomechanics 38(1), 2005, pages 1 to 8) defines the extrapolated centre of mass as the position plus the velocity divided by the pendulum eigenfrequency, and the signed distance from that point to the base of support boundary as the margin. It is the biomechanical measure of choice because it accounts for velocity as well as position, so a centre of mass well inside the polygon but travelling fast toward its edge is correctly reported as unstable. Its MEDIOLATERAL component is the one associated with falls, which joins directly to the cluster 13 finding that the lateral excursion required per step is half the stance width. The CAPTURE POINT (Pratt, Carff, Drakunov and Goswami, Capture Point, A Step toward Humanoid Push Recovery, Humanoids 2006) is the same construction read forward, being the ground point at which a step brings the body to rest, and coincides with the divergent component of the motion under the linear inverted pendulum, which ties it to the template model of cluster 6. The NONLINEAR pair, the finite time maximum Lyapunov exponent for local dynamic stability and the maximum Floquet multiplier for orbital stability with a value below unity indicating a contracting stride to stride map, is the family the clinical literature uses to separate fallers from non fallers where the spatiotemporal parameters do not discriminate. The foundational application to human locomotion is attributed throughout that literature to Dingwell and colleagues, whose individual papers were not fixed during this survey.

SYMMETRY. The symmetry index of Robinson, Herzog and Nigg (Use of force platform variables to quantify the effects of chiropractic manipulation on gait symmetry, Journal of Manipulative and Physiological Therapeutics, 1987) expresses the difference between limbs as a percentage of their mean and remains the most commonly used construction. Its known weakness is that it is UNBOUNDED as the mean approaches zero, so it must be applied to magnitudes such as a root mean square torque or a range of motion rather than to a signed series that legitimately passes through zero, and normalised variants exist where the denominator is small. This workspace already uses it, the twentieth pass of `brs_gait.md` reporting 4.7 percent at the hip pitch and 41 at the ankle roll range of motion, and the contrast is what makes the measure useful, since it LOCALISES a residual asymmetry to a plane rather than scoring the gait as a whole. The HARMONIC RATIO offers a complementary spectral construction, comparing the even against the odd harmonics of the stride frequency in the trunk acceleration, with the documented caution that it has been paraphrased variously as smoothness, harmony, rhythmicity and dynamic stability and should therefore be reported beside a symmetry index rather than in place of one. No individual work fixed.

SMOOTHNESS. The SPECTRAL ARC LENGTH (Balasubramanian, Melendez-Calderon and Burdet, A Robust and Sensitive Metric for Quantifying Movement Smoothness, IEEE Transactions on Biomedical Engineering, 2012) takes the arc length of the normalised Fourier magnitude spectrum of a speed profile and is preferred over dimensionless jerk because it is robust to measurement noise and independent of movement duration, whereas jerk based measures vary counterintuitively with both. Its relevance here is that the SD_BRS1 joint traces are impulse trains, with accelerations reaching 1900 rad/s² at the 99th percentile and torque changing by 470 N·m within one control step, and neither the action rate penalty nor the torque rate penalty measures that character against any standard a reader can compare. The caution that belongs with any smoothness metric is that one which improves under a reward penalty but not under the policy space regularisation of cluster 11 (Mysore et al., arXiv:2012.06644) is measuring the penalty rather than the gait.

VARIABILITY. The stride to stride COEFFICIENT OF VARIATION of the temporal parameters is the standard measure, and the result that grounds it is that stride time variability predicts falls in community living older adults where the mean stride time does not (Hausdorff et al., Gait variability and fall risk in community-living older adults, a 1-year prospective study, Archives of Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation, 2001, first author as stated by the publisher record). Step width variability carries the same property in the frontal plane. The measure transfers to a policy evaluation without modification and answers a question no percentile answers, whether the gait is a LIMIT CYCLE or a sequence of recoveries, and the record contains exactly the observation it would have flagged, a median cycle period of 1.000 s coexisting with a tenth percentile of 0.720.

ANGULAR MOMENTUM. Popovic, Hofmann and Herr (Angular momentum regulation during human walking, biomechanics and control, ICRA 2004) established that whole body angular momentum about the centre of mass is regulated to a small range throughout walking, segment to segment cancellation accounting for up to 95 percent in the sagittal plane, 70 in the frontal and 80 in the horizontal, and that the centroidal moment pivot coincides with the centre of pressure whenever the horizontal moment about the centre of mass vanishes, so the separation of those two points measures the departure from regulation. For SD_BRS1 the HORIZONTAL component is the one that matters, cluster 13 having established that a machine with fixed hip yaw joints must obtain every yaw moment from the ground, so a swing leg generating angular momentum the stance sole cannot absorb through friction is precisely the yaw tracking failure the measurements report.

KINETICS BEYOND THE PEAK. The LOADING RATE, being the rise of the vertical ground reaction force through its first hump divided by the time taken, separates a collision from a weight acceptance where the peak alone cannot, human walking reaching its first peak over roughly a quarter of stance (Nilsson and Thorstensson, cluster 13) against the 733 body weights per second the twentieth pass measures. The STANCE IMPULSE, being the time integral of the normal force, must equal the momentum change the step requires and therefore serves as a conservation check on the contact model.

A METHODOLOGICAL FINDING with no published source, recorded because the systematic review of deep reinforcement learning for legged robot locomotion (Instruments 10(1), 2026, MDPI, authors not verified, 27 studies from 2018 to 2025) establishes that reward shaping and training stability dominate the performance obtained without stating how a comparison should therefore be conducted. This workspace supplies two rules the literature does not. Runs must be compared at MATCHED ITERATION COUNTS with the curriculum values verified equal, since the tracking error does not converge downward while a curriculum contracts the reward kernel and widens the command range. And a play dump's reward channels carry the LIVE TREE's weights rather than the trained ones, since `play.py` rebuilds through `parse_env_cfg`, so a per term function value must be recovered by reconstruction rather than by division through an assumed weight.

## Cluster 18: Foot orientation about the vertical, and the reward treatment of a live hip yaw joint (added 2026-08-26 for section 5 of /ws/tron1-rl-isaaclab-cozum/plans/kscale_integration.md)

THE OCCASION. The KScale biped walks with its feet rotated out of the plane of travel, its legs crossing at the shank, and the run at `IsaacLab/logs/rsl_rl/kscale_flat/2026-08-25_10-44-21` shows its off axis hip joints at 4.2 times the per joint excursion the SD_BRS1 runs at under the identical term at the identical weight. The behaviour is absent from the SD_BRS1 for a mechanical reason rather than a reward one, that robot declaring both hip yaw joints `type="fixed"` so that the axis does not exist. This cluster gathers the literature on regulating a foot's direction about the vertical, which is thinly reported precisely because the exemplars from which this repository's reward vocabulary descends are quadrupeds and point footed or fixed hip yaw bipeds that have no such axis to regulate.

THREE FAMILIES OF TREATMENT. The first regulates the joint. IsaacLab's Unitree G1 applies `joint_deviation_l1` to hip yaw and hip roll together at minus 0.1 and deliberately not to hip pitch or knee, `.../config/g1/rough_env_cfg.py:57-61`, which cluster 10 already records. The Digit configuration, the closest true biped analogue in that tree, SEPARATES the two axes and prices the vertical one at twice the fore and aft one, `joint_deviation_hip_roll` minus 0.1 against `joint_deviation_hip_yaw` minus 0.2, `.../config/digit/rough_env_cfg.py:97-105`. The second regulates the foot in task space and Booster Gym is its reference, arXiv 2506.15132, already surveyed in cluster 13 for its torque tiredness term. The third folds foot orientation into a broader posture objective, van Marum arXiv 2404.19173 carrying feet orientation at 0.05 against a table whose largest entry is 1.0, and Humanoid-Gym arXiv 2404.05695 carrying no foot yaw term at all.

BOOSTER GYM, A CORRECTION AND AN EXTENSION TO CLUSTER 13. Table II was retrieved in full from the HTML version on 2026-08-26 and the released implementation and configuration were read alongside it. The feet yaw row is ||psi_feet minus psi_base||^2 at minus 1.0, against velocity tracking of 1.0 for each linear axis and 0.5 for the yaw rate. Cluster 13 recorded this as being carried at twice the linear tracking weight, and the docstring of `feet_yaw_alignment` in this repository repeats that reading. It is minus 1.0 against a SINGLE linear component's 1.0, so it equals one linear component and is twice the YAW tracking weight. Dated correction, 2026-08-26, the original reasoning retained above.

The more consequential finding is that the single tabulated row is a PAIR of terms in the released work, priced separately and EQUALLY. `envs/t1.py` carries `_reward_feet_yaw_diff`, the squared wrapped difference between the two feet's yaws and entirely independent of the base, and `_reward_feet_yaw_mean`, the squared wrapped difference between the base yaw and the MEAN of the two feet's yaws, with a branch cut repair of pi added to the mean where the two feet straddle the wrap. `envs/T1.yaml` sets `feet_yaw_diff: -1.` and `feet_yaw_mean: -1.`, so each of the pair carries the tabulated weight. That file also carries `feet_roll: -0.1`, `feet_distance: -1.`, `feet_slip: -0.1`, `feet_swing: 3.`, `torque_tiredness: -1.e-2` and default joint angles of hip pitch minus 0.2, knee 0.4 and ankle pitch minus 0.25, the last being an independent corroboration of the KScale nominal pose of minus 0.1, 0.4 and minus 0.3 from a robot of comparable scale.

ORTHOGONALITY, the reason the decomposition is substance and not presentation. Writing the per foot errors against the base as e_1 and e_2, the MEAN form and the differential are independent coordinates and the SUMMED form of e_1^2 plus e_2^2 is not. A pure splay of plus and minus e gives a mean form common mode of exactly ZERO and a differential of 4e^2, attributing the fault entirely to the mode that names it, where the summed form gives 2e^2 and so registers a splay on the common mode as well. A pure common rotation gives e^2 under the mean form and 2e^2 under the summed. The splay to common ratio is therefore 4 to 1 under the mean form and 3 to 1 under the summed with equal mode weights, and only the mean form permits the two modes to be weighted, interpreted or ablated independently. Any implementation wishing to give each mode a physical meaning must adopt the mean form. Verified by execution, 2026-08-26.

DATED CORRECTION, 2026-09-28, TO THE PARAGRAPH BELOW. The prediction it closes with is falsified by measurement and the reasoning that produced it is retained above the correction rather than edited, in the manner rule 17 of `/ws/CLAUDE.md` requires. Three KScale runs of 2026-09-24 and 2026-09-25 were read with the two hip yaw angles correlated against one another directly, and the correlation of the RAW angles is POSITIVE in all three at 0.424, 0.374 and 0.557, with the standard deviation of their sum exceeding that of their difference at 0.2276 against 0.1449, 0.2141 against 0.1445 and 0.2727 against 0.1455. The common mode is therefore the larger of the two by a factor of 1.47 to 1.87 in amplitude and the two legs yaw TOGETHER rather than apart, which is the opposite of the prediction. The correlation of that common mode with the base yaw rate is weak and negative at minus 0.156, minus 0.168 and minus 0.328, so the common rotation is not buying yaw and the argument below, that a common mode carries no reaction against the torso and buys no yaw whatever, is not contradicted. What is contradicted is the inference from it, that a policy therefore has no reason to produce one. The measurement says only that it does produce one, and this workspace has not established why. The practical consequence is recorded because it changes a term rather than only a belief, the mean form of `feet_yaw_alignment` attributing a pure splay four times the weight of a pure common rotation as the orthogonality paragraph above establishes, so on this robot the term under prices exactly the mode that dominates, and section 21.6 of `/ws/tron1-rl-isaaclab-cozum/plans/kscale_gait_shaping.md` lowers its `differential_scale` in consequence. A second measurement of the same runs bears on where the fault lives rather than which mode it occupies, the splay standing at 12.06 to 14.75 degrees in swing against 0.07 to 3.42 in stance, so a term that does not gate on the contact state spends most of its gradient on the phase in which least is wrong. That does not contradict the paragraph below on the gate, which argues only that the gate is unnecessary under the mean form for the reason that a turn moves both feet together, and which remains correct as an argument about the cost of an ungated term rather than about its benefit.

WHY THE FAULT IS DIFFERENTIAL RATHER THAN COMMON, a corollary of the yaw authority argument of cluster 13 that neither that cluster nor cluster 14 drew, because neither robot then had a live hip yaw. A torque applied between the pelvis and one thigh about the vertical acts equally and oppositely upon the other, so a policy generating yaw INTERNALLY does so by counter rotating the two legs, which is the differential mode exactly. A common mode rotation of both legs the same way carries no reaction against the torso and buys no yaw whatever, so a policy exploiting the joint route has no reason to produce one. The prediction is that a free hip yaw biped rewarded for yaw rate should exhibit a predominantly DIFFERENTIAL foot yaw fault, and the KScale bears it out.

THE GATE IS UNNECESSARY UNDER THE MEAN FORM, measured rather than argued. A base referenced foot yaw term charges a planted foot for an error it cannot correct, the foot being held by friction while the base yaws over it, and at a 0.62 stance fraction and a terminal yaw command of 0.9 rad/s the accrual reaches 0.558 rad over one stance interval. That reasoning is sound under the SUMMED form and void under the mean form, because a well executed turn moves both feet the same way and their mean tracks the base, so the lag of the stance foot cancels against the lead of the swing foot. Simulating a steady turn under ideal placement against a 1.0 Hz clock with stance duration 0.62 and anti phase offset 0.5, with a 0.10 rad dead band, the common mode's time averaged value is 0.0000 at commanded yaw rates of 0.3, 0.6 and 0.9 rad/s, and the entire residual tax is differential and bounded by 0.0299 raw, which is between 23 and 80 times smaller than the static fault such a term is meant to price. Neither Booster Gym nor van Marum gates on the contact state and neither needs to.

REFERENCE FRAME, A NEGATIVE RESULT. No surveyed source references the foot to the COMMANDED HEADING. Every one references it to the robot's own base. Where a configuration commands heading directly, as this repository's biped tasks do with `heading_command=True`, the two coincide in expectation and the base referenced form is preferable on three grounds, that it measures the quantity actually at fault rather than the body against the world, that it does not charge the base's own heading error twice over when a yaw tracking term already prices it, and that it stays well defined when the commanded velocity is zero.

THE STANCE FOOT CANNOT COMPLY. A term of this family, referenced to the base and ungated, charges a planted foot for an error it cannot correct, the foot being held by friction while the base yaws over it. At a 0.62 stance fraction and the terminal yaw command of 0.9 rad/s the accrual reaches 0.558 rad over one stance interval, which exceeds the pathological signal such a term is meant to read. Neither Booster Gym nor van Marum gates on the contact state, so both must carry enough weight to dominate the legitimate excursion they also charge. Gating the term on the SWING converts it from a demand that a foot be aligned into a demand that a foot be PLACED aligned, which is the behaviour actually wanted and needs far less weight.

THE BIOMECHANICAL TOLERANCE. The foot progression angle, the angle between the long axis of the foot and the line of progression, is the direct human analogue. Cibulka, Winters, Kampwerth, McAfee, Payne, Roeckenhaus and Ross, Predicting foot progression angle during gait using two clinical measures in healthy adults, a preliminary study, International Journal of Sports Physical Therapy 11(3), 2016, pages 400 to 408, measured sixty healthy adults and report a mean of 3.3 degrees of toe out with a standard deviation of 5.6 degrees and a range from 9.7 degrees of toe in to 14.3 degrees of toe out. Human walking does not hold the foot exactly along the line of progression, it holds it within roughly a tenth of a radian, so a reward demanding exact alignment demands more than natural and one standard deviation of that distribution, 0.098 rad, is the natural scale for a dead band.

WHY A FREE HIP YAW IS EXPLOITED. Cluster 13 established that a biped with fixed hip yaw obtains every yaw moment from the ground, as a friction moment beneath the sole or as the reaction to a change in whole body angular momentum, and cluster 14 records Popovic, Hofmann and Herr, Angular momentum regulation during human walking, ICRA 2004, for the regulation of that momentum and the segment to segment cancellation of some eighty per cent of its horizontal component. The corollary, which neither cluster drew because neither robot then had the joint, is that a machine whose hip yaw is FREE has a third and much cheaper route to a commanded yaw, namely to counter rotate the legs about the vertical and let the reaction turn the torso, requiring no friction margin, no change in foot placement and no reorganisation of the gait. A policy rewarded for yaw rate finds it early. The KScale measurement bears this out, the off axis hip excursion correlating with yaw tracking at a partial correlation of 0.927 once linear tracking is controlled for, against 0.648 the other way. A narrow sole aggravates it from the other side, the KScale's 0.0846 m width against the SD_BRS1's 0.194 m giving a proportionally smaller friction moment about the vertical, so the ground route is dearer on this robot exactly where the joint route is cheaper.

A FRAME CAUTION, discovered by measurement and recorded here because no surveyed source states it. Every published implementation of this term obtains a foot's heading from the yaw component of an Euler decomposition of the foot link's world quaternion, which is correct only where that link's forward axis is its own x and its vertical is its own z. Where a foot link frame is permuted, as the KScale's is with width on x, vertical on y pointing downward and fore and aft length on z, the extracted yaw is not the toe heading. Measured over 405 configurations spanning the full ankle pitch, ankle roll and hip yaw travel, the two differ by between 70.16 and 109.84 degrees and never agree, reading exactly 90 degrees at a nominal pose whose feet point exactly forward. The Euler decomposition is well conditioned throughout, the smallest cosine of the extracted pitch being 0.9354, so the defect is a frame convention error rather than a numerical one and would not announce itself as instability. Taking the heading instead by rotating a NAMED LINK AXIS into the world and calling atan2 on its horizontal projection removes the dependence rather than parameterising around it. The published implementations are NOT at fault on their own robots. Booster Gym's T1 leg chain carries identity origin rotations throughout, `resources/T1/T1_locomotion.urdf` declaring the ankle pitch axis as 0 1 0 and the ankle roll as 1 0 0, so its foot link frame is canonical with x forward and z upward and its Euler yaw IS the toe heading. The defect appears only on transfer to a robot whose foot link is permuted, which is precisely the transfer this workspace performs.

HIP YAW SIGN CONVENTION, checked on both robots because a differential term read in joint space would invert under anti directed axes. Booster Gym's T1 declares both `Left_Hip_Yaw` and `Right_Hip_Yaw` with `axis xyz="0 0 1"` and `origin rpy="0 0 0"` over identical limits of plus and minus 1.0 rad. The KScale hip yaw axes composed to the root frame read minus 0.0998, 0.0000, plus 0.9950 on the right and minus 0.0998, minus 0.0000, plus 0.9950 on the left, and driving each alone gives a toe heading gain of 0.995 of the SAME sign on both legs. The two robots share the convention and the port needs no sign correction. The KScale differences are a 0.0998 rad axis cant where the T1's axes are exactly vertical, which is the whole of the 0.995 gain deficit, and a travel of plus and minus 1.5708 against the T1's plus and minus 1.0. Note that a task space differential term reads WORLD headings and is invariant to this convention regardless; the audit licenses the diagnostic reading of the term back into joint space, not the term itself.

A CAUTION ON THAT DIAGNOSTIC READING, added 2026-08-26 after measurement contradicted the first draft of this entry. The coefficient relating a hip yaw coordinate to a foot heading is NOT a constant of a robot, it is a function of the pose, because the hip yaw is rigidly downstream of the hip pitch and its axis tilts out of vertical with that joint's flexion. On the KScale it is 1.000 at the URDF zero pose, 0.995 at the nominal stance, and 1.524 at a hip pitch of minus 1.0 rad. The general lesson for this workspace is that a foot heading regulated at the JOINT is under priced exactly at the flexions the swing phase passes through, which is when the foot's direction is being decided, so the task space form is preferable on a ground independent of the durability argument, and any quoted joint to heading gain must carry the pose at which it was taken.

## Cluster 19: The emergence of degenerate gaits under a velocity tracking objective, and the remedies adopted (added 2026-08-26 for /ws/plans/QUADRUPED_DEGENERATE_GAIT.md)

THE OCCASION. The quadruped integrated on 2026-08-19 was trained as `quadruped_flat/2026-08-25_10-15-10` and learned to support itself on a reduced set of limbs while continuing to track the commanded base velocity, the run peaking at a mean return of 646 near iteration 5275 and declining to 253 by the iteration 17443 at which it stopped. Cluster 17 established what a quadruped reward budget should contain and this cluster establishes what happens when a budget of that shape nonetheless admits a gait it was not written to permit. The two clusters are complementary rather than overlapping, cluster 17 being a survey of prescriptions and this one a survey of failures, and the distinguishing feature of the present cluster is that its most load bearing entries are not locomotion papers at all but the specification gaming literature, whose formal results explain why a velocity error term cannot by itself select among support patterns.

WHY THE OBJECTIVE ADMITS THE GAIT, THE FORMAL GROUND. Skalse, Howe, Krasheninnikov and Krueger, Defining and Characterizing Reward Hacking, NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2209.13085, define a proxy reward as unhackable with respect to a true objective only where increasing the expected proxy return can never decrease the expected true return, and prove the condition so strong that over the full space of stochastic policies two mutually unhackable rewards require one of them to be constant. The corollary this workspace needs is that a reward built substantially from commanded velocity error is unhackable with respect to gait naturalness only by accident, nothing in the algebra of that error distinguishing a four beat support pattern from a two beat one over the interval it integrates. Krakovna, Uesato, Mikulik, Rahtz, Everitt, Kumar, Kenton, Leike and Legg, Specification gaming, the flip side of AI ingenuity, Google DeepMind blog, `deepmind.google/blog/specification-gaming-the-flip-side-of-ai-ingenuity`, no arXiv or DOI identifier existing for the item, supply the name for the general phenomenon. Pan, Bhatia and Steinhardt, The Effects of Reward Misspecification, ICLR 2022, arXiv:2201.03544, report that a more capable agent exploits a misspecified reward more severely and may undergo a sharp behavioural transition as capacity or training time grows, which is suggestive for a run trained twenty times longer than the Go1 reference, though none of that paper's four environments is a locomotion task and the transfer is an inference rather than a result.

THE SHARPEST PRIMARY ACCOUNT OF COLLAPSE. Reda, Tao and van de Panne, Learning to Locomote, Understanding How Environment Design Matters for Deep Reinforcement Learning, Motion, Interaction and Games 2020, arXiv:2010.04304, ablate the survival bonus over three magnitudes on a simulated character and state outright that values too small or too large lead to local minima corresponding to falling forward and standing still respectively, the large bonus case being exploited by a character that balances and never steps. This is the clearest primary evidence located for a shaping term becoming the term the policy actually optimises, and it bounds the `keep_balance` term of this repository's configurations from both sides rather than only from below.

THE ENERGY TERM AS THE MISSING PREFERENCE OVER GAIT SHAPE. Fu, Kumar, Malik and Pathak, Minimizing Energy Consumption Leads to the Emergence of Gaits in Legged Robots, CoRL 2021, arXiv:2111.01674, report that whether a recognisable walk, trot or gallop emerges at all depends on the presence of an energy minimisation term, their ablation testing inclusion against exclusion rather than sweeping the weight. Read against Skalse, the two results compose into the operative account, the velocity term leaves gait shape unconstrained and the energy term is what supplies a preference over it, so a configuration that prices effort at six per cent of the reference machine's relative strength has correspondingly little to select a gait with. A caution not stated by that paper and recorded here as this workspace's own inference is that the argument does not obviously monotone, since two limbs bearing a static load at a favourable joint angle may cost less than four cycling through swing and stance, so an energy weight raised without measurement could push toward fewer support limbs rather than away from them.

THE DELIBERATE BIPEDALISM LITERATURE, AND WHY IT SHARPENS RATHER THAN DISSOLVES THE PUZZLE. Three works build machinery expressly to make a quadruped walk on two legs. Xiao, Dong, Lam and others, Learning stable bipedal locomotion skills for quadrupedal robots on challenging terrains with automatic fall recovery, npj Robotics 3, 22, 2025, author list not established beyond the first three by the retrieved source, state that the transition is extremely challenging because the robot loses half its ground contacts while its body axis rotates from horizontal to vertical and its centre of mass rises, and require a purpose built centre of mass and centre of pressure estimator feeding a dedicated balance reward. Zhang, Corcodel and Zhao, Bipedalism for Quadrupedal Robots, arXiv:2507.20382, require a risk adaptive distributional formulation to stabilise the same behaviour on a Go2. Peng, Bao, Humphreys, Delfaki, Kanoulas and Zhou, Learning Bipedal Walking on a Quadruped Robot via Adversarial Motion Priors, arXiv:2407.02282, venue not confirmed, require adversarial motion priors over a bipedal reference on the stated ground that the mode remains unverified without such machinery. The inference for this workspace runs opposite to those papers' own purpose. If a clean rear demands that apparatus, a policy arriving at reduced support without any of it has almost certainly not found a rear, which the measurement independently confirms, the trunk of the run under investigation holding between 5.82 and 10.23 degrees of tilt throughout where a thirty degree rear would register a projected gravity magnitude of 0.5 against the 0.178 measured.

A SYNTHESIS ON SYMMETRY, RECORDED BECAUSE NO SURVEYED SOURCE STATES IT. Cluster 9 surveys symmetry augmentation and the mirror loss, and the symmetry group those works enforce is the sagittal reflection, the left limb exchanged for the right. A quadruped supporting itself symmetrically on one pair of limbs, both limbs of the pair treated alike, is invariant under exactly that reflection. The fault axis is fore and aft rather than left and right, so symmetry augmentation of the surveyed form should be expected to improve gait quality without forbidding this collapse, and a configuration relying on it as its sole anti degeneracy device should not assume the case covered. Offered as this workspace's own synthesis of definitions already registered rather than as a claim any cited source makes.

FURTHER MODES AND THEIR REMEDIES. Bellegarda and Ijspeert, CPG-RL, Learning Central Pattern Generators for Quadruped Locomotion, IEEE RA-L 7(4), 12547 to 12554, 2022, arXiv:2211.00458, embed coupled oscillators in the action space so the policy modulates amplitude and frequency rather than joint targets directly, which is the quadruped counterpart of the periodic composition cluster 8 registers for the biped. Lehman, Clune, Misevic and fifty two further authors, The Surprising Creativity of Digital Evolution, Artificial Life 26(2), 274 to 306, 2020, arXiv:1803.03453, catalogue the founding cases of an agent gaming its physics engine rather than its task, including an integrator that granted free energy to rapid small oscillations and a collision model that launched a creature airborne from a corner contact, both traceable to Sims 1994 which cluster 4 already registers for its co-design precedent. Dowdy and Chagas Vaz, Isaac Sim-to-Real, Reinforcement Learning based Locomotion for Quadrupeds, arXiv:2607.18135, is registered here rather than in cluster 17 because its interest is its anti degeneracy machinery rather than its budget, an explicit gait term at 10.0 being the largest single weight in its table and an air time variance penalty at minus 1.0 accompanying it, and its joint position limit weight of minus 100.0 standing an order of magnitude beyond every other figure surveyed. Its table was retrieved as a secondary extraction from the HTML rendering, the source text layer having resisted direct reading, and carries that caution.

A NEGATIVE RESULT, RECORDED HONESTLY. ## Cluster 20: Style requirements as constraints rather than as prices (added 2026-08-28 for the second pass of section 5 of /ws/tron1-rl-isaaclab-cozum/plans/kscale_integration.md)

This cluster exists because the run of 2026-08-26 established that a shaped style penalty can be paid rather than obeyed, and the question it registers is not which reward expresses a style requirement but whether a reward is the right instrument for one at all. It is the successor to Cluster 18, which recorded the term itself, and it should be read with Cluster 19, which already carries the formal account of why a proxy inside a weighted sum under determines behaviour.

The load bearing entry is Chane-Sane, Leziart, Flayols, Stasse, Souères and Mansard, CaT, Constraints as Terminations for Legged Locomotion Reinforcement Learning, 2024, arXiv 2403.18765, verified by direct retrieval of the arXiv abstract on 2026-08-28, which states that any violation of a constraint triggers a probability of terminating potential future rewards the agent could attain, and which reports evaluation on the real Solo quadruped crossing obstacles. The arXiv record does not state a venue and none should be asserted from recall. The mechanism matters here because it removes the exchange rate between a style term and a tracking term rather than adjusting one side of it, since a constraint that can end the episode is not a quantity the policy trades against the return, and the KScale measurements show a policy exploiting exactly such an exchange rate. Kim, Oh, Lee, Choi, Ji, Jung, Youm and Hwangbo, Not Only Rewards But Also Constraints, IEEE Transactions on Robotics, arXiv 2308.12517, already recorded in Cluster 11, is the correct ancestor of the idea and stops short of it, its constraint remaining a cost inside a summed objective.

Three further entries are registered for the second pass. Peng, Guo, Halper, Levine and Fidler, ASE, ACM Transactions on Graphics 41(4), Article 144, SIGGRAPH 2022, arXiv 2205.01906, extends the adversarial motion prior into reusable skill embeddings. Wu, Wang, Ye and Xing, Multi-Gait Learning for Humanoid Robots Using Reinforcement Learning with Selective Adversarial Motion Prior, 2026, arXiv 2604.19102, venue beyond the arXiv listing not established, is registered for one finding in particular, that a style prior strong enough to forbid one behaviour can also forbid behaviours the task legitimately needs, which is the published form of the objection this workspace raised against an ungated heading term. Singh, Benallegue, Morisawa, Cisneros and Kanehiro, Learning Bipedal Walking On Planned Footsteps For Humanoid Robots, 2022, arXiv 2207.12644, grounds the footstep conditioned family, though the retrieved source does not establish whether a planned footstep carries a target heading as well as a position.

Two negative results are registered because they cost search effort and should not be repurchased. No retrieved source reports swing gated regulation of an off axis JOINT angle, the dominant published practice for hip roll and hip yaw being continuous and ungated, and the nearest analogue, Ntagkas, Kiourt and Chatzilygeroudis, PGTT, 2026, arXiv 2510.18348, penalises ground contact during scheduled swing rather than a joint angle, with no ablation against an always on form. No retrieved source reports training a biped with its hip yaw locked and subsequently unlocking it, the nearest mechanism, Liao and colleagues, GPO, 2026, arXiv 2601.20668, growing action magnitude uniformly across every joint rather than releasing one selectively.

One correction to an expectation this workspace held is recorded here rather than left implicit, because Cluster 9 might otherwise be read as offering a remedy. Symmetry enforcement cannot address a differential foot yaw fault, and the reason is algebraic rather than empirical. Writing the two base relative foot headings as e1 and e2, the left right mirror maps e1 to minus e2 and e2 to minus e1, under which the differential mode is INVARIANT and the common mode is ANTI-INVARIANT, so a splay of plus and minus 0.30 rad presents a differential of minus 0.600 both before and after mirroring. A mirror symmetric policy is therefore free to turn both feet inward or both outward, and a mirror loss would constrain only the common mode, which the KScale run measures at essentially zero. This matters practically because the KScale runner at `tron1-rl-isaaclab-cozum/environments/environments/tasks/locomotion/agents/limx_rsl_rl_ppo_cfg.py:317` to `:322` already has symmetry data augmentation active with the mirror loss switched off, so enabling it is a one line change that would appear attractive and would achieve nothing against this fault.

## Cluster 21: The relocation of a penalised behaviour, and the grouping of a reward set (added 2026-09-02 for the third pass of section 5 of /ws/tron1-rl-isaaclab-cozum/plans/kscale_integration.md)

This cluster exists because the runs of 2026-08-31 and 2026-09-01 established something the two earlier passes of that section had no occasion to consider, that a style penalty which is obeyed on the axis it names may be evaded on an axis it does not. Between those two runs the KScale hip yaw excursion fell by 33 per cent while the hip roll excursion rose by 72 per cent and the sum of the two moved by 2.1 per cent, so the policy reallocated its off axis expenditure rather than reducing it. Cluster 20 asked whether a reward is the right instrument for a style requirement, and this cluster asks the prior question of whether the requirement has been stated over the right quantity at all. It should be read with Cluster 19, which carries the formal account of proxy under determination.

The load bearing entry is Wang and Huang, Reward Hacking as Equilibrium under Finite Evaluation, 2026, arXiv 2603.28063, which proves under five stated axioms that an optimised agent under invests in any quality dimension its evaluation does not cover, and derives a computable index predicting the direction and severity of the resulting distortion in advance. The material claim is that substitution following a patch is a structural equilibrium property of finite evaluation rather than a contingent observation, which is exactly the shape of the KScale measurement. The domain is alignment theory and the reported examples concern language model training, so the transfer to a locomotion reward is by mechanism and not by reported result, and the entry is registered on that footing. It extends Skalse and colleagues and Pan and colleagues, already recorded in Cluster 20, in the one direction those two do not go, that the payment a policy makes may be in a currency the objective does not itemise.

The remedial family is the grouping of a reward set rather than the addition of a term to it. Kim, Lee and Park, A Learning Framework for Diverse Legged Robot Locomotion Using Barrier-Based Style Rewards, ICRA, 2025, arXiv 2409.15780, carries two separable contributions. The first is the relaxed logarithmic barrier, which is a logarithm while the constrained quantity remains inside its bound and a quadratic once the bound is crossed, so the price of a violation rises without limit as the limit is approached and yet the gradient survives a crossing. It is the instrument intermediate between the flat price whose exchange rate a policy may pay and the termination of CaT whose signal is sparse, and it is the instrument the KScale plan now proposes. The second is architectural, independent critics processing the barrier rewards and the standard task and regularisation rewards exclusively with advantages normalised before the surrogate, presented as reducing the sensitivity of the outcome to the particular weights chosen. The barrier form is applied there to gait timing, foot clearance, joint position, body height, target velocity, base motion and joint velocity, so foot orientation is not among the constrained quantities and its application to one is an extrapolation.

The architectural entry acquires a particular importance in this workspace because the KScale run of 2026-09-01 performed its environment level approximation by hand without recognising it as such, scaling eleven task and gait shaping terms by a median factor near 0.30 while leaving nineteen posture, style and regularisation terms untouched, and producing the best gait of that sequence. The mechanism cannot have been the overall reward magnitude, since the vendored surrogate is scale free under the advantage normalisation at `rsl_rl/rsl_rl/algorithms/ppo.py:191` with `normalize_advantage_per_mini_batch` disabled, so it was the ratio between the groups. Li, Wu, Liu, Guo and Xue, Experience-Learning Inspired Two-Step Reward Method, 2024, arXiv 2401.12389, supply the temporal rather than the architectural form of the same separation, training first on flat ground under gait related rewards and only then on difficult terrain by adversarial imitation of the resulting experience. The retrieved abstract reports naturalness qualitatively and gives no quantitative measure.

Two entries support the stance width question that the narrow gait of 2026-09-01 raised. Xie, Bai, Shi, Yang, Ge, Zhang and Li, Humanoid Whole-Body Locomotion on Narrow Terrain, 2025, arXiv 2502.17219, extend the zero moment point into a reward and report balance on extremely narrow support from proprioception alone, which establishes the regime as trainable but does not report the trade off between narrowing a stance and rejecting a lateral disturbance, which is the quantity the KScale plan actually needs. Hollman, McDade and Petersen, Normative Spatiotemporal Gait Parameters in Older Adults, Gait and Posture 34(1), 2011, pages 111 to 118, PMID 21531139, report a step width between 7.0 and 9.9 cm against a step length between 54 and 69 cm over 294 adults aged seventy and above. Two cautions attach. The cohort is elderly and older adults walk on a wider base than young adults, so the figure is an upper bound on the human proportion. And step width is measured between successive placements rather than between the two feet at an instant, which is the correct analogue of a stance width parameter and not of an instantaneous frame separation.

Three negative results are registered so that they are not repurchased. No retrieved source reports measuring the redistribution of an off axis excursion from one joint axis to another under a task space penalty. No retrieved source prices a biped stance width against a lateral disturbance rejection margin. And no retrieved source reports a barrier form applied to foot orientation or to hip yaw.

## Cluster 22: Clock free support pattern terms, the touchdown blindness of the air time family, and the published budgets of the Isaac Lab Spot task (added 2026-09-02 for the second round of /ws/plans/QUADRUPED_DEGENERATE_GAIT.md)

THE OCCASION. The quadruped was retrained twice more, as `quadruped_flat/2026-09-01_07-22-37` and `quadruped_flat/2026-09-02_08-53-04`, and the first of those collapsed onto a single diagonal pair in all thirty two of thirty two evaluation environments while remaining fully airborne on 46.7 per cent of steps. Cluster 19 surveys why a velocity objective admits such a gait, and this cluster surveys the terms that would forbid it, together with the structural reason why almost none of the surveyed terms can. The distinguishing finding is negative and is recorded first, because it governs the rest.

THE TOUCHDOWN BLINDNESS OF THE AIR TIME FAMILY, established by reading rather than by report. Isaac Lab's contact sensor exposes two distinct air time quantities, `last_air_time` frozen at the previous touchdown and `current_air_time` running since the last detach, at `IsaacLab/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/sensors/contact_sensor/contact_sensor_data.py:119,128`. Every gait term surveyed across both rounds of that investigation reads the former, this workspace's `feet_air_time` and `feet_air_time_v2` at `rewards.py:196,225`, its `unbalance_feet_air_time` at `rewards.py:522`, and the Isaac Lab Spot task's own `air_time_variance_penalty`. A limb that has stopped touching down triggers no `compute_first_contact`, contributes exactly zero rather than a penalty, and is therefore invisible to all of them, and worse, a term of that family reports its healthiest values precisely when a retraction is total, since only the cycling survivors then register. The consequence for reward design is general rather than specific to this robot, that no touchdown gated construction can price a support pattern collapse, and that a term intended to forbid one must read the running quantity.

THE ISAAC LAB SPOT TASK, the only configuration on disk whose gait terms are its largest, read directly at `IsaacLab/source/isaaclab_tasks/isaaclab_tasks/manager_based/locomotion/velocity/config/spot/`. It is not in the Rudin lineage at all, its `README.md` crediting The AI Institute and Boston Dynamics for the environment specification with no accompanying publication identified, so it is registered here as a software artefact. Two of these are of independent interest, the hip restricted acceleration and velocity penalties being the closest published precedent located for pricing jitter on a proximal axis specifically rather than on the whole articulation, and the standing gated position deviation being a mechanism with no counterpart elsewhere in this survey.

GAITREWARD, AND THE CLAMP THAT DEFEATS IT. Spot's largest single term at weight 10.0 is a clock free quadruped contact synchrony reward pairing `(FL, RR)` against `(FR, RL)`, that is the two diagonals of a trot, rewarding equal air and contact time within a pair and opposition between pairs, reading only the sensor's own timers and committing to no phase clock, gated on body speed exceeding 0.5 metres per second so that standing earns nothing rather than being penalised. This is the most directly relevant published construction located for the pathology of cluster 19, since the retracted pair of the run under investigation is exactly one of its two pairs. It is nonetheless insufficient against that pathology, and the reason is recorded here as this workspace's own analysis of its arithmetic rather than as a claim its authors make. The term clips its squared errors at `max_err**2` before the exponential, so once two feet's timings diverge beyond the configured 0.2 seconds it stops distinguishing a moderate desynchronisation from an unbounded one, and a diagonal pair held aloft together with large and roughly equal air times can score near the synchrony ceiling. This is the same structure that the degenerate gait investigation identifies in this workspace's own air time term, now observed to recur independently in an unrelated construction, and the general lesson is that a clip placed to bound a shaping term also bounds the punishment of the runaway that term exists to prevent.

## Cluster 23: The gait block as the only block that pays for leaving the ground, and the mechanical substrate beneath a style fault (added 2026-09-03 for the fourth pass of section 5 of /ws/tron1-rl-isaaclab-cozum/plans/kscale_integration.md)

THE OCCASION. Three KScale policies were evaluated against one another on 2026-09-03, `kscale_flat/2026-08-28_04-50-51`, `2026-09-02_06-59-31` and `2026-09-02_10-51-29`. Cluster 21 surveyed the relocation of a penalised behaviour between axes and this cluster surveys the exchange of the gait itself for the style, which is a different trade and is settled by a different literature, part locomotion and part human biomechanics.

THE GROUP RATIO FINDING, established by measurement rather than from a source and recorded here because it governs which sources bear. Aggregating the evaluation's weighted term rates by group shows that the two group reweighting of `2026-09-01_06-53-24` did NOT demote the gait shaping block against the task block, their ratio moving only from 1.043 to 0.969. It demoted the gait block against everything else, the style block rising 2.71 fold against it, the regularisation block 1.94 fold and the flat survival bonus 28.7 fold. Those three blocks share the property that each is cheaper to satisfy when the robot does not leave the ground, and the gait block is the only one that pays for leaving it. Velocity tracking meanwhile improved under a threefold cut to its own weight, a raw 0.809 becoming 0.843, which establishes that the task term never distinguished a walk from a shuffle and is the workspace's second confirmation of the position cluster 19 takes from the specification gaming literature.

WILLIAMS AND MARTIN, Gait modification when decreasing double support percentage, Journal of Biomechanics 92, 2019, pages 76 to 83, DOI 10.1016/j.jbiomech.2019.05.028, is the load bearing new entry and it is a human study rather than a robotics one. Sixteen healthy adults walked on a treadmill at a fixed speed under instructions to lengthen and to shorten their double support fraction. Decreasing it produced increased hip flexion, increased knee flexion, increased swing foot height and larger ground reaction force peaks, and attempting to increase it produced changes in the opposite direction. The relevance is that it establishes these as one coordinated pattern rather than as separate quantities, which licenses the double support fraction as the single number summary of the KScale defect and removes the need to posit a separate clearance fault to explain the clearance loss. The measured KScale sequence reproduces the pattern in full, double support 41.1, 51.1 and 64.8 per cent against knee deviation 0.295, 0.290 and 0.169 rad and swing apex clearance 34.8, 27.3 and 29.7 mm. The paper also reports that subjects could reliably decrease the fraction and struggled to increase it, which is the human analogue of the observation that a shuffle is the easy direction of travel for an optimiser.

SPOLJARIC, YASHUAI AND LEE, Variable Stiffness for Robust Locomotion through Reinforcement Learning, 16th IFAC joint symposia of mechatronics and robotics, 2025, arXiv:2502.09436, supplies the position on gain magnitude. It reports, attributing the statement to prior work it surveys rather than measuring it afresh, that a large proportional gain leads to instabilities in training while a low one produces significant tracking errors and leaves the joint behaving as a torque controller, and that small derivative gains produce learning instabilities while large ones prevent tracking the target velocity. Its own baselines use stiffnesses of 20 and 50, the upper figure chosen because instabilities were observed above it. The relevance is that the KScale trained four times with a hip yaw stiffness of 15 against 150 to 200 at every other joint, and the measured consequence is the low gain failure the survey describes, the joint carrying the robot's highest mean speed at 7.3 rad per second at an amplitude of only 0.15 rad while the base yaw rate reached 4.5 to 6.5 rad per second against commands near 0.6 at a command correlation of zero. The paper does not report gait quality as a function of stiffness, which is the gap this workspace's own measurement fills.

WANG, WANG, REN, BEN, HUANG, ZHANG AND PANG, BeamDojo, Learning Agile Humanoid Locomotion on Sparse Footholds, RSS 2025, arXiv:2502.10363, is registered for its dual critic, which separates a dense locomotion objective from a sparse foothold objective so that the two are not summed into one advantage. It is the same instrument cluster 20 registers from Kim, Lee and Park and it is the published answer to the group ratio problem above, since a ratio chosen by hand is exactly what a second critic makes unnecessary. It is recorded as the destination rather than as the next step, the KScale agent configuration sharing a module with four other robots and the algorithm being the vendored `rsl_rl`.

PENG, BAO AND ZHOU, Gait-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning with Multi-Phase Curriculum for Humanoid Locomotion, 2025, arXiv:2505.20619, venue beyond the arXiv listing not established by the retrieved source, introduces gait complexity progressively and expands the command space over several phases, and carries human inspired terms for straight knee stance and coordinated swing. It corroborates the staged schedule that cluster 20 registers from Li and colleagues, and it is the more recent statement of it for a humanoid specifically. Neither source reports the shuffling failure mode directly, so the staged schedule is registered here as a corroborated remedy rather than as a diagnosed one.

A GEOMETRIC RESULT RECORDED FOR REUSE, since no source supplies it and it will recur on any robot with a slender sole. Yawing a foot converts its length into lateral base of support, the extent of a rectangular sole of length L and width W at a yaw of t being L sin t plus W cos t. The KScale sole is 0.2100 m by 0.0846 m, a slenderness of 2.48, so the 7.7 degrees measured in the reference run buys 31.5 per cent of lateral extent at no cost in any term that reads a joint coordinate. Splay is therefore the cheapest lateral support such a robot can buy, which explains why three successive instruments relocated the excursion rather than removing it, and the same arithmetic bounds the remedy, the two toes converging by 27.4 mm of inner gap at that angle against a fifth percentile gap of 23.7 mm in that run.

A NEGATIVE RESULT ON THE MECHANISM. The hypothesis that the splay is a reactive response to falling inward, which would shift the support toward the centre of mass, is not supported at the instantaneous level. The within run Pearson correlation of the symmetric inward splay against lateral velocity, roll rate and lateral projected gravity lies between minus 0.26 and plus 0.17 across the three runs. The splay is a standing postural bias adopted once and held, not a reflex, which is why a price removes it and did, and the run level correlation with lateral centre of pressure excursion is a correlation between two consequences of one lateral demand rather than evidence of a reflex.

## Cluster 25: The bound a scale is not, the placement geometry beneath a stance width price, and the falsification of a gain derived from a driven joint's own torque (added 2026-09-04 for the fifth pass of section 5 of /ws/tron1-rl-isaaclab-cozum/plans/kscale_integration.md)

THE OCCASION. Four KScale runs completed between 2026-09-03 and 2026-09-04, `kscale_flat/2026-09-03_09-27-59`, `2026-09-03_09-49-22`, `2026-09-04_10-15-18` and `2026-09-04_10-40-30`, which vary the actuator gain set, the action scale and the reward weights over a design whose dumped configurations differ from one another in a countable number of lines. Two failed outright and by different mechanisms, one by an entropy collapse following a halved tracking weight and one by an actuation failure at a stiffness this workspace derived itself. Clusters 19 through 23 surveyed the pricing of a degenerate gait and this cluster surveys three questions those left untouched, whether an action scale bounds anything, where a lateral demand belongs if not on a stance width parameter, and what a torque measured at a position controlled joint is a measurement of.

CHOU, MATURANA AND SCHERER, Improving Stochastic Policy Gradients in Continuous Control with Deep Reinforcement Learning using the Beta Distribution, ICML, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research volume 70, 2017, is the load bearing new entry and it settles the first question. The paper's subject is the bias a Gaussian policy incurs when the admissible actions are bounded and the sampled action must be clipped or squashed, and it proposes the Beta distribution, whose support is the interval itself, as the bias free alternative, reporting faster convergence and higher return under both TRPO and ACER. The relevance to this workspace is the contrapositive. Where the action is NOT bounded, as in the Isaac Lab joint position action with `clip` unset, the Gaussian's unbounded support is exactly what allows the policy to hold a commanded joint excursion invariant while the scale that multiplies it is reduced, which is the measured result, the ninety fifth percentile commanded hip yaw deviation standing at 0.91 rad at a scale of 0.4 and 0.95 rad at 0.25 on otherwise identical configurations. An action scale is a reparameterisation and not a constraint, and a workspace wanting a constraint must impose a clip, a joint limit or a termination.

HUANG, XU, WANG, GAO AND ZHANG, Traversing Narrow Paths, A Two-Stage Reinforcement Learning Framework for Robust and Safe Humanoid Walking, 2025, arXiv:2508.20661, venue beyond the arXiv listing not established by the retrieved source, bears on the second. It couples a template based foothold planner with a learned foothold tracker in a first stage and a perception aided foothold modifier in a second, under a curriculum from flat ground to a 0.2 m beam, and it shapes the foothold's yaw angle directly while pricing the foothold's lateral offset through a Gaussian rather than a hinge. The retrieved abstract does not state the mathematical form of either term and none is asserted here. The relevance is that the published treatment of foot placement on narrow support is a placement geometry attached to a planner's target, whereas the KScale configuration expresses the same requirement as an unsigned hinge on the separation between two foot frames, and it is that hinge which the workspace has measured to cause the pigeon toed splay it was accompanied by terms intended to prevent. Taken with Xie and colleagues in cluster 23, whose zero moment point reward dispenses with a width parameter altogether, the two establish that a stance width floor is a proxy the recent literature has largely stopped using.

TANAKA, ZHU, WANG, LIU AND HONG, Mechanical Intelligence-Aware Curriculum Reinforcement Learning for Humanoids with Parallel Actuation, IEEE-RAS International Conference on Humanoid Robots, 2025, arXiv:2507.00273, supports the third and the most general finding of the pass. Its argument is that the mechanical properties a mechanism embeds, the nonlinearities of differential pulleys and four and five bar linkages, must be simulated natively rather than approximated by a serial chain, and that preserving them through training yields better surface generalisation and zero shot transfer than a controller trained against the approximation. The relevance is by extension rather than by subject, this robot carrying no closed chain. What the KScale sequence found is the same proposition at the level of the gain set, that a mechanical property was doing the work of a controller, the yawed slender sole supplying the lateral base of support that a 20 Nm per radian ankle roll could not, and that a gain change removing that property without supplying its replacement produced an immediate and total failure, minus 26.21 per second on the lateral rate penalty and minus 21.79 on the orientation penalty against minus 3.45 and minus 0.45 in the run it was derived from. The paper is registered as the nearest published statement of the principle and not as a source for the measurement.

A METHODOLOGICAL RESULT RECORDED FOR REUSE, since no source states it and it will recur wherever a gain is sized from logged torque. A torque recorded at a position controlled joint is the sum of a disturbance the joint meets and the spring's own response to the command it is tracking, and only the first is a quantity a stiffness may be sized against. The two are separated by reconstructing the commanded deviation from the applied torque and the joint velocity through the control law, and where that reconstruction returns an excursion comparable to the action scale the recorded torque is the policy driving the joint rather than the world disturbing it. On this robot the hip yaw at a stiffness of 15 returned a commanded deviation whose ninety fifth percentile was 1.28 rad against an actual deviation of 0.77 rad, so a stiffness derived by dividing its ninety ninth percentile torque by an allowed deflection was derived from the wrong quantity, and the run that tested it never stood.

A NEGATIVE RESULT ON A REMEDY THIS WORKSPACE PROPOSED TO ITSELF. The reduction of the action scale was adopted as the single parameter acting on all four saturated joints at once, on the argument that halving the scale halves the position error everywhere. It does not, for the reason the Chou entry gives, and the measurement is direct, the ankle roll mechanical stop residency standing at 0.669 and 0.667 at an action scale of 0.4 and rising to 0.765 and 0.774 at 0.25 on otherwise identical configurations. What the reduction did deliver, and delivered decisively, is the removal of the sixfold delay in gait emergence that the identified stance servos impose, mean episode length at iteration 1000 rising from 31 steps to 317 against the reference run's 235. A parameter may be right for a reason other than the one it was adopted for, and the record should carry both.

## Cluster 26: The stability bound of an explicitly integrated servo, and the identification of an actuator from the name of the joint it drives (added 2026-09-08 for the gain recalculation of /ws/tron1-rl-isaaclab-cozum/context/KScale.md sections 5, 11 and 12)

THE OCCASION. The KScale run `kscale_flat/2026-09-07_11-08-18`, which carried the stance derived stiffnesses of `KScale.md` section 12 in full for the first time and never stood, its policy still exploring at iteration 9915 against a `base_contact` termination rate of 1.0000 and a mean episode length of 29 steps. The stiffnesses were not what failed, the run halving the lateral orientation penalty of the comparable `2026-09-04_10-40-30`, and the cause proved to be a property of the substrate rather than of the robot or of the objective.

NO NEW PUBLISHED WORK IS REGISTERED BY THIS CLUSTER and the omission is deliberate rather than an oversight. The result below is a numerical analysis of a discrete update that this workspace performed against its own configuration, and the two sources it leans on are already registered, Rudin and colleagues in cluster 15 for the proportional derivative action interface and the NVIDIA joint gain tutorial for the damping ratio band. Nothing was cited from recall and no author list, venue or year has been asserted that a retrieved source did not carry.

A METHODOLOGICAL RESULT RECORDED FOR REUSE, since it applies to every robot in this workspace whose actuator model is explicit and it decided four gains of six on this one. An actuator that hands the physics engine a torque rather than a position target is integrated explicitly, and its damping term is then conditionally stable rather than unconditionally so. Writing the semi implicit update the engine performs gives a companion matrix whose determinant is `1 - D dt / I` and whose trace is `2 - dt^2 K / I - D dt / I`, so the mode grows wherever the spectral radius exceeds unity, which the determinant alone bounds by `D dt / I < 2`. The inertia in that expression is the SWING inertia and not the stance inertia, since the bound must hold at the moment the foot leaves the ground, and on a robot whose stance to swing inertia ratio reaches 937 at the ankle a damping sized correctly for the ground is three orders of magnitude past the bound in the air. The practical rule is that the stance inertia states how much damping a joint wants and the swing inertia states how much it may be given, and the second is a bound where the first is a preference.

The distinction between an explicit and an implicit actuator is therefore load bearing and is not visible in a configuration file. Isaac Lab's `ImplicitActuator` delegates the proportional derivative law to the PhysX joint drive, which is unconditionally stable and to which none of the above applies. A custom actuator deriving from `IdealPDActuator`, as this workspace's `IdentifiedActuator` does, computes the law in Python and applies the result as an effort, and is subject to the bound in full. Any gain derivation performed in this workspace must therefore state which of the two it assumes.

A CORROBORATION FROM AN INDEPENDENT SOURCE ALREADY IN THE REGISTER. Section 11 of [kscale-opensource.md](kscale-opensource.md), recorded in cluster 24, derives the identical dimensionless number for the K-Scale Labs K-Bot in MuJoCo, states the same bound of two, and identifies that robot's wrist as the binding joint at 1.68 of the bound under the proportional and derivative scale of 2.0 that the K-Scale training script's own inline comment describes as unstable in edge cases. Two robots, two simulators and two derivations performed for different reasons arrive at the same criterion, and the published stack appears to have located the boundary empirically without naming it.

A SECOND METHODOLOGICAL RESULT, on the provenance of an actuator specification. The joint names of a robot description may themselves carry the actuator identification, and where they do the identification is checkable rather than speculative. Every actuated joint of the KScale sole footed biped carries a two digit suffix, and the K-Bot of cluster 24 names its own joints by the same rule under a metadata file that assigns the RobStride class by that suffix without exception. Reading this robot's suffixes against that table established that its two `_04` joints had been configured at the `_03` peak torque and were under provisioned by a factor of two, and that its two ankles had been configured at 10 rad per second against a declared class maximum of 37.699, a limit that the four quadrant torque speed curve of the `DCMotor` base class turned into an available torque of 0.69 Nm at the ninety fifth percentile speed those joints were measured to reach. Neither error was visible in the URDF, which carries a uniform export placeholder of 10 Nm and 10 rad per second at every joint, and neither would have been found without the comparison.

## Cluster 27: Gait emergence without a schedule, the clock free alternatives to a phase reference, and the absence of a published account of the impact penalty's degenerate solution (added 2026-09-10 for section 3.4 of /ws/plans/QUADRUPED_DEGENERATE_GAIT.md)

This cluster was opened by a question the earlier gait clusters do not answer, namely which published mechanism is capable of charging a limb that has left the support pattern, and what may be used in place of a phase clock when a coordinated gait is wanted without committing to a contact schedule.

The strongest positive result is Fu, Kumar, Malik and Pathak, who obtain walk, trot and bound and the transitions between them from a reward containing the forward velocity, a survival bonus and the negative instantaneous mechanical power at a weight of 0.04, with no gait term, no foot slip term, no joint speed term and no action regularisation whatever, arXiv:2111.01674. The relevance is that the gait is selected by the energetic objective and by nothing else, which makes it the one surveyed configuration whose reward set is smaller than this workspace's rather than larger, and it is the basis of phase 7 of the quadruped plan. No training code is public, only the project website, which was established by fetching it.

Owaki and Ishiguro reach the same coordination in hardware without learning, a central pattern generator driven by local load sensing alone producing spontaneous walk to trot to gallop transitions as a single speed parameter rises, Scientific Reports 7, 277, DOI 10.1038/s41598-017-00348-9. The relevance is that the coordination is recoverable from force feedback, so a clock is not a precondition for a gait. Humphreys, Li, Wan, Gao and Zhou derive the transition points analytically from the Froude number, IEEE RA-L 8(10), 6131 to 6138, and the retrieval returned two conflicting DOIs, 10.1109/LRA.2023.3302214 and 10.1109/LRA.2023.3300249, which is recorded rather than resolved. Humphreys and Zhou carry that criterion into a learned controller in Nature Machine Intelligence, arXiv:2412.09440, whose reward specification the retrieved source does not state. Yu, Acero, Atanassov, Yang, Havoutis, Kanoulas and Li take the opposing position, learning which quantity should trigger a switch rather than assuming the Froude number, Frontiers in Robotics and AI 13, 1697159, DOI 10.3389/frobt.2026.1697159, also arXiv:2502.06676. Cooke and Fisher extend the emergent set to pace, bound, half bound and pronk without motion references, Advanced Robotics, DOI 10.1080/01691864.2025.2587837, the publisher having refused the automated request for the reward specification.

Bellegarda, Shafiee and Ijspeert span nine gaits with one policy through central pattern generator coupling biases, the policy observing the oscillator phase and amplitude but never the coupling matrix, arXiv:2411.04787. Its relevance is a stated one, that no style reward is needed to enforce any particular gait, so gait fidelity is bought by observation design rather than by reward design, and it is the sharpest available alternative to the phase clock of arXiv:2212.03238 already recorded in cluster 22. The paper reports its own limitation, that the parameterisation is restrictive for combinations of high linear and angular velocity.

Watanabe, Miki, Shi, Kadokawa, Bjelonic, Kawaharazuka, Cramariuc and Hutter penalise foot contact velocity for quiet walking, raising the weight from 5.0 to 25.0 across a curriculum, ICRA 2025, arXiv:2502.10983. ## Cluster 28: The Newton engine, its solver family, and the published evidence for GPU physics throughput (added 2026-09-11 for /ws/context/isaaclab_newton.md)

This cluster was opened by the question of what replaces PhysX when the physics backend becomes a selectable property of a run rather than a property of the codebase, and it differs in kind from every cluster above it, because its sources are engineering publication and vendor measurement rather than peer reviewed result. That difference governs how its entries may be used. A solver's documented capability is citable as fact, a vendor's throughput figure is citable only as a vendor claim, and the absence of a comparison is itself a finding to be recorded rather than a gap to be filled by inference.

The engine itself is established by three sources that must be read together. The Linux Foundation announced the contribution of Newton by Disney Research, Google DeepMind and NVIDIA on 2025-09-29, https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/linux-foundation-announces-contribution-of-newton-by-disney-research-google-deepmind-and-nvidia-to-accelerate-open-robot-learning, which establishes the governance and the Apache 2.0 licence. The source repository, https://github.com/newton-physics/newton, establishes the dependency floor at Python 3.10 and a CUDA 12 driver at version 545, states that MuJoCo Warp is the primary backend, and records that the older `warp.sim` module is deprecated in Newton's favour. The documentation at https://newton-physics.github.io/newton/stable/ establishes the data model, a `ModelBuilder` producing a `Model` of bodies, joints, shapes and static arrays, a `State` of evolving positions and velocities, a `Control` of joint targets and forces and an optional `Contacts` set, and lists eight solver backends, XPBD, VBD, MuJoCo, Featherstone, SemiImplicit, Kamino, ImplicitMPM and Style3D. The version read was 1.5.2, against the 1.6.0rc1 that Isaac Lab's `develop` branch pins, so the documentation channel trails the integration and a claim taken from it must be checked against the integration before it is relied upon.

The MuJoCo Warp reference, https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/mjwarp/index.html, supplies the two facts on which the co-optimisation argument of that document turns. The first is that most model fields carry a leading batch dimension of one so that a single compiled model is shared across worlds, with selected fields expanded per world through the `batch_sizes` argument of `put_model`, which is the mechanism that replaces per environment articulation spawning. The second is the asymmetry of the capacity bounds, quoted exactly, that it is possible for the number of contacts per world to exceed `nconmax` if the total number of contacts for all worlds does not exceed `nworld` multiplied by `nconmax`, however the number of constraints per world is strictly limited by `njmax`. The same source records that MuJoCo Warp supports most of the main simulation features of MuJoCo with a few exceptions and raises rather than degrades when asked to accept an unsupported field, which is the desirable failure mode and which makes asset validation a precondition rather than a diagnostic. This entry corroborates and extends the reading already recorded in cluster 15's neighbourhood at [mjlab.md](mjlab.md), whose section on per world model fields was derived from the same reference.

The throughput evidence is thin and must be quoted with its scope attached. Frey, a maintainer, announced MuJoCo Warp in google-deepmind/mujoco discussion 2511 on 2025-03-18, https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/discussions/2511, stating that simulations of more complex scenes such as manipulation tasks run more than one hundred times faster in MuJoCo Warp than in MuJoCo XLA, without naming the hardware. NVIDIA quantified the same comparison on 2025-09-29 in Train a Quadruped Locomotion Policy and Simulate Cloth Manipulation with NVIDIA Isaac Lab and Newton, https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/train-a-quadruped-locomotion-policy-and-simulate-cloth-manipulation-with-nvidia-isaac-lab-and-newton/, reporting up to one hundred and fifty two times faster for locomotion and three hundred and thirteen times for manipulation than MuJoCo XLA on a GeForce RTX 4090, an RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell being a further forty four percent faster, and a cloth demonstration at approximately thirty frames per second with more than three hundred times the performance of GPU-IPC. The relevance of all of these is narrow, since MuJoCo XLA is not this workspace's incumbent. That blog carries the single figure that compares the two engines which matter, up to sixty five percent faster in-hand dexterous manipulation with MuJoCo Warp against PhysX, and in-hand manipulation is a contact dense low body count regime that does not transfer to legged locomotion at four thousand environments.

The cluster closes, as cluster 27 did, with a load bearing negative result. A directed search for a published, methodologically stated comparison of steps per second between PhysX and Newton MuJoCo Warp for legged locomotion at a fixed environment count returned nothing. The Isaac Lab performance dashboard, https://nvidia.github.io/omniperf/, and the mjlab nightly benchmarks at four thousand and ninety six parallel environments on an RTX 5090, https://mujocolab.github.io/mjlab/nightly/, both exist and both render their figures through client side script, so neither yielded a retrievable number and neither carries a PhysX comparison. The Isaac Lab paper itself, Mittal and one hundred and four co-authors, Isaac Lab, A GPU-Accelerated Simulation Framework for Multi-Modal Robot Learning, arXiv:2511.04831, 2025, describes the Newton integration as upcoming rather than measured, which dates the paper behind the `develop` branch and confirms that no first party measurement of the integrated backend had been published at the time of writing. Any throughput claim about this workspace's own tasks must therefore come from this workspace's own paired benchmark and from nowhere else, and that measurement is staged as gate five of [isaaclab_newton.md](isaaclab_newton.md).

### Addition to cluster 28, 2026-09-14, the Newton world model and its bearing on design co-optimisation

The rewrite of section 10.13 of [isaaclab_newton.md](isaaclab_newton.md) required three sources the cluster did not carry, and it overturned a conclusion the cluster's first pass had supported, so the correction is recorded here rather than by editing the paragraphs above.

The Newton worlds concept page, https://newton-physics.github.io/newton/stable/concepts/worlds.html, is the decisive entry. It states that multiple independent simulations, referred to as worlds, live within a single Model, that model arrays remain flat across all worlds rather than being separated per world, and that Newton stores entities sequentially alongside parallel world assignment arrays such as body_world, a layout chosen so that GPU operations parallelise across entire entity sets without world awareness. It names three construction routes, begin_world and end_world scoping, add_world incorporating a pre-built ModelBuilder as a complete world, and replicate producing N identical copies. Only the third requires the worlds to agree. The ModelBuilder reference, https://newton-physics.github.io/newton/stable/api/_generated/newton.ModelBuilder.html, supplies the matching caution, that a replicated source acts as a template whose entries may be shared across copies so that mutating one in place writes through to every copy and is unsupported. The release notes, https://github.com/newton-physics/newton/releases, date per world assets to v1.5.0 and v1.6.0 to 2026-09-10, against the 1.6.0rc1 that Isaac Lab pins.

Two API pages carry the runtime half. THE CORRECTION. The first pass of this cluster, and section 10.13 as first written, reasoned from the absence of a Newton respawn mechanism to the absence of runtime morphology variation, and recommended holding the co-optimisation work stream on PhysX. The two tier respawn and prototype edit architecture the workspace built on PhysX exists to defeat USD instancing, which is a composition constraint of Isaac Sim and not a physics constraint, and it has no analogue and no purpose under Newton. The reading of [mjlab.md](mjlab.md) that this cluster corroborated remains correct for mjlab, which drives MuJoCo Warp directly and therefore must expand model fields per world explicitly, but it does not carry to Newton, whose model is per world flat before any solver sees it. The practical consequence is that Newton is the better substrate for design co-optimisation rather than the worse one, and the open questions are about propagation through SolverMuJoCo rather than about representation in Newton.

## Cluster 30: Bang-bang control as an expected solution rather than a fault, the three published families of remedy, and the postural literature that explains why a hip strategy silences an ankle (added 2026-09-23 for sections 14 through 17 of /ws/tron1-rl-isaaclab-cozum/plans/kscale_gait_shaping.md)

THE OCCASION. Three KScale runs completed between 2026-09-21 and 2026-09-22, `kscale_flat/2026-09-21_06-45-40` carrying one action scale of 0.4 at every joint, `2026-09-22_07-18-05` adding a per joint action scale, and `2026-09-22_07-40-43` adding a hip lateral sway reward on top of it. The first two drive the ankle roll from one mechanical stop to the other 2.031 times per second against a stride cadence near 2.35, under a commanded position target that lies 0.155 radians outside the joint's travel, and the third stops the reversal altogether at 0.002 per second. Clusters 19 through 23 surveyed the pricing of a degenerate gait and cluster 25 established that an action scale is a reparameterisation rather than a bound. This cluster surveys the question those leave open, namely what the published record says about a policy that prefers the extremes of its action range, and what it offers in place of the reward weights this workspace has been tuning.

SEYDE, GILITSCHENSKI, SCHWARTING, STELLATO, RIEDMILLER, WULFMEIER AND RUS, Is Bang-Bang Control All You Need? Solving Continuous Control with Bernoulli Policies, Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 34, 2021, arXiv:2111.02552, is the load bearing entry and it reframes the problem. Its subject is what the abstract calls the colloquially known phenomenon that trained agents often prefer actions at the boundaries of the action space, it draws theoretical connections to the emergence of bang-bang behaviour in optimal control where the behaviour is provably optimal for minimum time problems, and it replaces the Gaussian policy with a Bernoulli distribution over the two extremes of each dimension, reporting state of the art performance on several continuous control benchmarks. Two features of the paper bear on this workspace beyond the headline. The authors disentangle exploration from the solution by repeating the analysis under imitation learning, which forecloses the reading that boundary preference is merely an artefact of the exploration noise, and they state the contrast that governs a robotics application directly, that the benchmark result stands in contrast to robotic hardware where energy and maintenance cost affect controller choices. The relevance is that a workspace measuring a latched joint should stop asking why the policy chose the stop and start asking what in its objective should have stopped it, and the measurement that answers this is recorded below.

THREE FAMILIES OF REMEDY ARE REGISTERED AND THEY DIFFER IN WHAT THEY ACT UPON. The first prices the output. MYSORE, MABSOUT, MANCUSO AND SAENKO, Regularizing Action Policies for Smooth Control with Reinforcement Learning, IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation, 2021, arXiv:2012.06644, propose a temporal term penalising the difference between actions on consecutive states and a spatial term penalising the difference between actions on nearby states, and report that on a quadrotor the resulting smoothness improvement reduced power consumption by almost eighty per cent. Their diagnosis is the general one, that reinforcement learning algorithms do not typically optimise for smoothness and that the Markov property assumes independence of state action pairs, so oscillation is the default rather than the exception. HA, LEE, VAN DE PANNE, XIE, YU AND KHADIV, Learning-based legged locomotion, state of the art and future perspectives, International Journal of Robotics Research, 2025, DOI 10.1177/02783649241312698, arXiv:2406.01152, records the same two terms as standard practice in this field, the action rate and the second difference of the action written as the norm of a_t minus twice a_{t-1} plus a_{t-2}, which is the form this workspace's `ActionSmoothnessPenalty` implements, and states the family's standing difficulty plainly, that maintaining a good balance between task rewards such as velocity tracking and regularisation rewards makes the reward shaping problem a laborious task.

The second family constrains the policy class rather than pricing its output. FUJITA AND MAEDA, Clipped Action Policy Gradient, International Conference on Machine Learning, 2018, arXiv:1802.07564, observe that out of bound actions are clipped before execution while the policy is optimised as though they were not, and propose an estimator that accounts for the clipping, which they report is unbiased and achieves lower variance than the conventional estimator that ignores action bounds. It is the estimator side companion to CHOU, MATURANA AND SCHERER in cluster 25, which addresses the same mismatch by changing the distribution. SHIN, CHA, KIM, CHA AND PARK, Spectral Normalization for Lipschitz-Constrained Policies on Learning Humanoid Locomotion, 2025, arXiv:2504.08246, venue beyond the arXiv listing not established by the retrieved source, act on the network instead, arguing that policies develop abrupt high frequency torque changes which are infeasible for real actuators with finite bandwidth, that the conventional answer of regularisation rewards on joint velocities, accelerations and energy requires extensive hyperparameter tuning, and that constraining the spectral norm of the network weights limits high frequency policy fluctuation while enabling more efficient parallel training than a gradient penalty achieves. RAFFIN, KOBER AND STULP, Smooth Exploration for Robotic Reinforcement Learning, Proceedings of the 5th Conference on Robot Learning, PMLR volume 164, pages 1634 to 1644, 2022, locate the problem in the exploration noise rather than in the policy mean, reporting that unstructured step based exploration leads to jerky motion patterns on real robots and that resampling state dependent noise at an interval enables a compromise between performance and smoothness sufficient to train directly on hardware.

The third family declares the requirement a constraint rather than a price, and it is the one this workspace should reach for. KIM, OH, LEE, CHOI, JI, JUNG, YOUM AND HWANGBO, already registered as entry four of the KScale plan's bibliography and recorded here for the first time, argue that a reward only formulation requires more than ten reward terms and a tuning process repeated several times, and that a constrained Markov decision process solved by a modified interior point policy optimisation reaches the same behaviour with three reward terms and a single tuned coefficient. Three of their six probabilistic constraints are joint position, joint velocity and joint torque, which are exactly the three quantities the measurement below finds unpriced on this robot, and their reported hardware violation rates are 0.007, 0.001 and 0.002 against a threshold of 0.025. The property that should decide the matter for a workspace running four robots against one shared mdp package is the transfer result, their constrained controller moving from Raibo to Anymal C without retuning the torque coefficient where the reward only controller degraded significantly. CHANE-SANE, LEZIART, FLAYOLS, STASSE, SOUÈRES AND MANSARD, already registered as entry three of the same bibliography, offer the cheaper route, each constraint becoming a probability of terminating future reward under what they describe as a minimal modification to an off the shelf algorithm such as proximal policy optimisation.

HONG AND PARK, Biomechanical optimization and reinforcement learning provide insight into transition from ankle to hip strategy in human postural control, Scientific Reports 15, article 13640, 2025, DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-97637-5, supplies the mechanism behind the one arm that worked, and it was found after the arm succeeded rather than before, which is recorded so that it is not mistaken for the arm's justification. They report that a centre of pressure constraint reproduces the saturated ankle torque observed in humans, that the ankle torque plateau corresponds to a stabilisation of ankle joint motion while the flat foot constraint is maintained, and that as the perturbation grows the model shows progressively reduced ankle feedback gains coupled with increased hip involvement, the transition occurring in their words well before reaching the ankle torque limit. The relevance is that the saturation is a geometric consequence of the support polygon rather than an actuator deficit, which is the same conclusion this workspace reached from its own travel measurement, and that supplying a hip strategy to a machine that had only an ankle strategy should be expected to silence the ankle, which is what the lateral sway reward did.

KIM, JORGENSEN, LEE, AHN, LUO AND SENTIS, Dynamic locomotion for passive-ankle biped robots and humanoids using whole-body locomotion control, International Journal of Robotics Research, 2020, DOI 10.1177/0278364920918014, arXiv:1901.08100, volume, issue and page range not established by the retrieved source since the publisher refused the automated request, bounds the fixed joint proposal. They demonstrate unsupported dynamic locomotion on two passive ankle bipeds, Mercury and DRACO, under a whole body locomotion controller whose contact constraint relaxation reduces jerk at foot contact switching, and the surveyed position is that passive ankle bipeds carry fewer degrees of freedom, lower mechanical complexity and lighter lower legs at the cost of a small horizontal footprint. The relevance is bounded and must be stated as such, that work removing the ankle entirely under a model based controller where this workspace proposes removing one ankle degree of freedom from a learned one, so it establishes feasibility in principle rather than supplying a method. What it does establish, and what no other surveyed source does, is that lateral balance on a biped does not require an actuated ankle roll.

A MEASUREMENT RECORDED FOR REUSE, since it is the quantity every entry above is silent on and since it decides which family applies. On this robot the four energy terms together, joint torque, joint acceleration, joint velocity and joint torque rate, are 1.04 per cent of the negative reward budget and 0.16 per cent of the positive. The run that tripled its mechanical power from 109 watts to 395 paid 0.4276 per second for it and collected 1.9175 per second more on velocity tracking alone, an exchange rate of 4.48 to one in favour of the expensive gait, and it led the reference run on mean reward, on episode length and on termination rate while doing so. The general statement is that the bang-bang preference [16] is not a pathology to be diagnosed where the regularisation budget is this small, because an objective in which tripling the power costs 0.68 per cent of the return is very close to the unregularised objective in which extremes are expected, and the first question to ask of any such measurement is the ratio above rather than the behaviour itself.

TWO NEGATIVE RESULTS, recorded in the manner cluster 27 adopts. A directed search returned no published account of a learned locomotion policy holding a joint against a mechanical stop by commanding a position target permanently outside the joint's travel. The nearest statements are the boundary preference of Seyde and colleagues, which concerns the action space rather than the joint space, and the clipping literature of Chou and of Fujita, which concerns an action space that is bounded where this one is not, and the distinction is mechanical rather than semantic, a bang-bang controller switching between two admissible extremes whereas this policy names one inadmissible target and lets the mechanism absorb the difference, which consumes actuator effort continuously rather than at the switches. And no surveyed source reports the effect of clipping the PROCESSED action, meaning the commanded joint target in radians after the scale and the per environment offset have been applied, as distinct from clipping the raw action, which is the intervention this workspace has staged as arm 14. Both observations are to be treated as unreported, and any future document wishing to cite a precedent must find one rather than assume it exists.

## Cluster 31: The usable range of a regularisation weight, the phase asymmetry of a locomotion policy, and the published treatment of an actuator envelope as a constraint rather than a price (added 2026-09-28 for section 16 of /ws/tron1-rl-isaaclab-cozum/plans/kscale_gait_shaping.md)

THE OCCASION. Three KScale runs completed between 2026-09-24 and 2026-09-25 carried the energy budget of cluster 30 raised by fifty, by one hundred, and by fifty with a mechanical power term added, and the second and third added a joint deviation penalty on the ankle roll. A fourth run of the same week raised the four weights by five hundred. Four findings prompted this survey and each is a question cluster 30 left open. The escalation has a cliff, the five hundred fold run collapsing to an eighteen step episode at a unit termination rate while the fifty and one hundred fold runs both improved on their predecessor. The ankle roll deviation penalty moved the swing joint from one mechanical stop to the other rather than off a stop, halving its deviation while raising its envelope saturation from forty six to ninety eight per cent and its mean absolute torque from 8.5 to 16.1 newton metres of a seventeen newton metre ceiling. The swing hip yaw excursion, which the pigeon toe was attributed to, is COMMANDED rather than a tracking failure, the target recovered by inverting the control law standing at 0.170 to 0.199 radians from the nominal against an achieved 0.177 to 0.206, a ratio of 0.94 to 0.99 at 1.05 newton metres of a forty two newton metre ceiling. And every joint of this robot behaves differently in swing than in stance by margins that no single gain can serve, the ankle roll running at 1.17 radians per second in stance and 0.013 in swing while its saturation runs the other way at 0.30 per cent against 99.4.

THE FIRST QUESTION, WHETHER A LOCOMOTION POLICY SHOULD CARRY DIFFERENT GAINS IN SWING AND IN STANCE, and the surveyed answer is that the published route is to let the policy choose the gains rather than to schedule them. BOGDANOVIC, KHADIV AND RIGHETTI, Learning variable impedance control for contact sensitive tasks, IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters volume 5 issue 4, 2020, pages 6129 to 6136, arXiv:1907.07500, is the load bearing entry. They observe that policies directly outputting joint torques have difficulty robustly solving problems involving contact interactions without given structure in the action space, propose a policy whose output is an impedance and a desired position in joint space rather than a position alone, and report on a hopper with intermittent contacts and a manipulator wiping a table that the variable impedance action space outperforms both torque and position control on learning rate and on robustness to environment uncertainty. The relevance to a machine whose ankle is saturated in swing and quiet in stance is direct, the hopper being the surveyed system closest to a leg that alternates between free flight and load.

TOMMASELLI, SEGRETO, NEGRI, GODOY AND BECKER, Mind the Phase, Effective Rank and Representation Health in Legged Locomotion, 10th Conference on Robot Learning, 2026, arXiv:2609.06958, establishes that the phase asymmetry is present in the policy itself and not only in the mechanics. They analyse the effective rank of the policy Jacobian conditioned on gait phase and report that layer normalisation and residual connections allocate roughly two more dimensions of effective rank to swing than to stance, a disparity they find absent in a plain multilayer perceptron, and they use the measurement to reduce joint jitter by approximately three times on a Boston Dynamics Spot. Two things follow for this workspace. A policy already devotes unequal representational capacity to the two phases, so a controller that treats them identically is the odd part of the stack rather than the natural one. And effective rank conditioned on phase is a training time instrument that predicts deployment smoothness, which is a diagnostic this workspace does not presently compute and which costs no extra rollout.

BRONARS, PARK AND AGRAWAL, Tune to Learn, How Controller Gains Shape Robot Policy Learning, 2026, arXiv:2604.02523, venue beyond the arXiv listing not established by the retrieved source, is the caution that attaches to any gain change made for a learned policy. They argue that gains should be selected for what they call learnability, how amenable a gain setting is to the learning algorithm in use, rather than for the desired task stiffness, and report that reinforcement learning can succeed across all gain regimes provided the hyperparameters are adjusted with them while behaviour cloning prefers compliant and overdamped settings and sim to real transfer degrades under stiff and overdamped ones. Their summary position, that optimal gain selection depends not on the desired task behaviour but on the learning paradigm employed, is the reason a gain arm must be read against its own training record rather than against a stability calculation alone, and it bears directly on a workspace whose gain derivations in `KScale.md` are conducted entirely in the frequency domain.

THE SECOND QUESTION, WHETHER AN ACTUATOR ENVELOPE SHOULD BE PRICED OR IMPOSED, and here the published record is unambiguous and this workspace is on the wrong side of it. SHIN, SONG, JI AND PARK, Actuator-Constrained Reinforcement Learning for High-Speed Quadrupedal Locomotion, 2023, arXiv:2312.17507, venue beyond the arXiv listing not established by the retrieved source, incorporate the motor operating region, meaning the feasible torque and speed envelope, as a CONSTRAINT during policy training so that the network avoids infeasible regions during sampling, rather than penalising the torque after the fact. They add a gait reward that distributes motor torque evenly across the legs specifically to remove the bottleneck an individual saturating motor creates, and report the 45 kilogram KAIST Hound reaching 6.5 metres per second. TAO, CHENG, LAN, YUAN, JIN AND WANG, Extending the Speed Limit of Quadrupedal Locomotion via Refined Actuator Modeling and Adaptive Command Scheduling, IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters, 2026, arXiv:2609.13289, take the same position from the modelling side, arguing that methods neglecting actuator nonlinearities and physical constraints during training suffer a significant sim to real gap under dynamic motion, and building a model that captures high speed voltage coupling and magnetic saturation so that the torque and speed envelope is represented accurately enough that the policy cannot exploit a non physical region. They report the 36.5 kilogram BlackPanther2 at 13.2 metres per second on a treadmill and 11.65 outdoors. The bearing on this workspace is that both treat the envelope as the boundary of the admissible set, where every KScale run to date has treated it as a shape the clip inside `DCMotor` applies silently while the reward set prices only the resulting torque, and cluster 30's constraint family is therefore the correct home for the ankle saturation rather than a larger energy weight.

THE THIRD QUESTION, WHETHER AN ANKLE ROLL IS NEEDED AT ALL, extends the bound cluster 30 took from KIM, JORGENSEN, LEE, AHN, LUO AND SENTIS with a design datum from the same work that cluster did not record. Their DRACO carries five actuators per limb, being hip yaw, hip roll, hip pitch, knee pitch and ankle pitch, with a soft joint stiffness policy applied to the ankle pitch to emulate a passive joint, so the machine has NO ankle roll axis whatever and walks without one. XIA, LI, LEE, SCUTARI AND CHEN, The Duke Humanoid, Design and Control For Energy Efficient Bipedal Locomotion Using Passive Dynamics, 2024, arXiv:2409.19795, venue beyond the arXiv listing not established by the retrieved source, is the second datum and the more recent, a ten degree of freedom humanoid carrying five per leg with hip rotation, hip flexion and extension, hip abduction and adduction, knee flexion and extension, and ankle plantarflexion and dorsiflexion, again with no ankle roll, under a learned policy that leverages passive dynamics and reports a fifty per cent reduction in cost of transport in simulation and thirty one per cent on hardware. Two independently designed bipeds, one model based and one learned, therefore omit the axis this workspace is spending ninety five per cent of an actuator's ceiling to hold against a stop.

A MEASUREMENT RECORDED FOR REUSE, THE USABLE RANGE OF A REGULARISATION WEIGHT, since cluster 30 measured the group at 1.04 per cent of the negative budget and said nothing about how far it may be raised. On this robot the four energy weights were run at one, ten, fifty, one hundred and five hundred times that baseline, against a fixed reward set otherwise. At ten the mean reward rose from 887.6 to 955.4 with the velocity tracking reward unmoved at 35.2, the base contact termination rate falling from 0.0771 to 0.0526 and the policy noise standard deviation from 0.761 to 0.584. At fifty and at one hundred the mean reward held near 953, the tracking reward rose slightly to 35.8 and 36.1, and the group reached 12.6 and 20.7 per cent of the negative budget. At five hundred the run collapsed, the mean reward standing at minus 66.8, the episode length at 18 steps of a 2000 step limit and the base contact termination rate at exactly 1.0000, the machine falling on every episode. The usable range is therefore two decades wide with a cliff immediately beyond it, and the general statement for reuse is that a regularisation group at one per cent of the negative budget may be raised by two orders of magnitude before the task is destroyed, that the improvement is concentrated in the first order of magnitude, and that the cliff is not announced by a gradual decline in any of the logged quantities at the preceding rung.

A SECOND MEASUREMENT, THE COST OF PRICING A JOINT WHOSE POSITION THE GROUND SETS. A joint deviation penalty of minus 1.0 was added to the ankle roll, whose position in stance is set by the ground reaction moment and whose position in swing was already at a mechanical stop. The penalty halved the swing deviation from 0.33 to 0.18 radians, and it did so by moving the joint from the stop far from its nominal to the stop near it rather than off a stop at all, the stop residency in swing standing at 89.8 per cent before and 99.8 after. Over the same change the envelope saturation in swing rose from 46 to 98 per cent, the mean absolute torque from 8.5 to 16.1 newton metres of a seventeen newton metre ceiling, and the fraction of samples on which the actuator torque OPPOSES the deviation, which is the actuator pulling the joint toward its nominal, fell from 9.4 per cent to 0.7. The term was therefore paid rather than avoided, at a term value of minus 0.354 to minus 0.409 per second, and the behaviour it bought is the opposite of the one it names. The general statement for reuse is that a deviation penalty on a joint whose position is determined by contact does not move the joint, it moves the commanded target, and where the commanded target is unbounded the policy satisfies the letter of the penalty by selecting whichever inadmissible target lies nearer the nominal and pressing into it harder.

TWO NEGATIVE RESULTS. A directed search returned no published account of a joint deviation penalty applied to a joint whose position is set by contact rather than by the command, and no treatment of what such a penalty does to the actuator effort. The searches reached the general reward shaping literature on penalty terms and on constraint handling and nothing specific to the contact case, so the measurement above is recorded as the workspace's own and any future document must find a precedent rather than assume one. And no surveyed source prescribes an explicit swing and stance gain SCHEDULE for a learned locomotion policy. The nearest are the learned variable impedance family above, in which the gains are an output of the policy and no schedule is written, and the phase conditioned representation analysis of Tommaselli and colleagues, which measures a phase asymmetry in the network rather than prescribing one in the controller. A workspace proposing a hand written schedule is therefore proposing something the survey does not support, and the learned variable impedance action space is the better supported alternative to put beside it.

## Cluster 32: Bounding an action rather than pricing it, the vanishing gradient a squashing function buys, and the property that makes a penalty weight calculable (added 2026-09-30 for sections 19.3 and 21.28 of /ws/tron1-rl-isaaclab-cozum/plans/kscale_gait_shaping.md)

THE OCCASION. A commanded target clip was applied to the KScale biped as `kscale_flat/2026-09-29_06-15-58` and it cured the fault three earlier sequences had chased, the ankle roll saturation falling from 60.17 per cent to 0.06 and its sustained actuator demand from 13.0 newton metres of a 17 newton metre ceiling to 3.5. It also relocated that fault, the hip roll standing at its mechanical stop for 45.9 per cent of every episode under a raw action of NINETEEN standard deviations against 0.54 in the run before it. A clip bounds the commanded target and leaves the action that produces it unbounded, so an unbounded Gaussian answers a bound it cannot reach by commanding further past it, and the quantity a penalty would price accordingly grew by a factor of 51000 over training. Cluster 30 surveyed the bang-bang preference and cluster 31 the usable range of a regularisation weight. This cluster surveys what the record says about bounding the action itself.

THE MISMATCH IS NAMED AND THE SQUASHING REMEDY IS NAMED WITH IT. LIN, Beyond Distributions, Geometric Action Control for Continuous Reinforcement Learning, 2026, arXiv:2511.08234, venue beyond the arXiv listing not established by the retrieved source, states the general position, that Gaussian policies have dominated continuous control yet suffer from a fundamental mismatch, their unbounded support requiring ad hoc squashing functions that distort the geometry of bounded action spaces, and proposes Geometric Action Control, which decomposes action generation into a direction vector and a learnable concentration and reduces the parameter count from 2d to d plus 1. The entry is registered for its statement of the problem rather than for its remedy, which is further from this workspace's stack than the two below.

TWO RECENT WORKS TREAT THE DISTORTION DIRECTLY AND BOTH ARE IN THIS WORKSPACE'S DOMAIN. LIAO, LI, YANG, CHANG, FAN, WANG, SHI, CAO, WU AND SARTORETTI, GPO, Growing Policy Optimization for Legged Robot Locomotion and Whole-Body Control, 2026, arXiv:2601.20668, venue beyond the arXiv listing not established by the retrieved source, apply a time varying action transformation that restricts the effective action space early in training and progressively expands it, reporting on quadruped and hexapod hardware. Their load bearing claim for this workspace is the theoretical one, that the transformation PRESERVES THE PPO UPDATE RULE and introduces only bounded, vanishing gradient distortion. Their motivation is also worth recording, that existing methods work well for position based control with environment specific heuristics and are less effective for torque based control, where exploring the action space and obtaining informative gradients is significantly harder. LU, DUN, ZHOU, TAO, DUAN, LI AND LI, FastDSAC, Enhancing Policy Plasticity via Constrained Exploration for Scalable Humanoid Locomotion, 2026, arXiv:2606.31691, venue beyond the arXiv listing not established by the retrieved source, take the other fork on a humanoid, approximating the learned policy by a TRUNCATED Gaussian that excludes out of distribution actions while retaining exploration stochasticity, with adaptive variance regulation as an implicit regulariser against the plasticity loss that aggressive gradient updates cause under high throughput parallel sampling. The two agree on the problem and differ on whether to bound the transformation or the distribution, which is the same fork cluster 30 records between pricing an output and constraining a policy class.

A READING OF THIS WORKSPACE'S OWN ALGORITHM, recorded because it makes the transformation far cheaper here than the survey implies and because it was established from the source rather than from any paper. `PPO.update` forms its ratio as the exponential of the new log probability minus the old at `/ws/rsl_rl/rsl_rl/algorithms/ppo.py:295`, and both are evaluated at the SAME stored action, so a deterministic transformation of that action contributes an identical change of variables term to each and the two cancel exactly in the difference. The surrogate objective is therefore unchanged whether the Jacobian correction is applied or omitted, provided it is done consistently, which is the mechanism behind the claim of Liao and colleagues and which no surveyed source states in this form. What does not cancel is the entropy bonus at `ppo.py:313`, which continues to measure the pre squash Gaussian and is an upper bound on the entropy of the action the environment receives. A second consequence follows, that the transformation need not touch the actor at all, since applying it inside the environment's action term leaves the log probability, the entropy, the rollout storage and any symmetry augmentation untouched.

THE VANISHING GRADIENT IS REAL AND ITS ARITHMETIC IS SIMPLE, so it is recorded here rather than left to a citation. Where the action is a hyperbolic tangent of the network's output, the derivative of the transformation is one minus the square of that tangent, which is unity at the origin, 0.420 at a pre activation of one, 0.0707 at two and 0.00987 at three. A policy that wants the extreme of its range must drive the pre activation far out and the gradient falls away exactly as it does so. The remedy is not to avoid the transformation but to SIZE it, choosing the scale so that the range the task actually needs is reached at a pre activation near one rather than near three. On the KScale, sizing the scale as the admitted deviation divided by the hyperbolic tangent of one places the working gait's ninety fifth percentile commanded departure at a pre activation between 0.335 and 0.708, where the derivative stands between 0.629 and 0.896 of its peak, so there is no vanishing gradient at the operating point. The general rule for reuse is that a squashing function is safe wherever the scale exceeds the needed range by at least the reciprocal of the hyperbolic tangent of the pre activation one is willing to operate at, being a factor of 1.313 at a pre activation of one.

A MEASUREMENT RECORDED FOR REUSE, AND IT IS THE REASON TO PREFER A BOUND OVER A PRICE. Cluster 31 records that a regularisation weight has a usable range of two decades with a cliff beyond it, and cluster 30's measurement assumes the quantity being priced is roughly stationary. Neither holds for a quantity the policy discovers. The KScale's clip excess grows from 0.000595 radians squared per step at iteration 193 to 30.380 at iteration 10000, a factor of 51059, so a quadratic penalty upon it spans ten orders of magnitude and no fixed coefficient is right at both ends. Bounding the action removes the difficulty rather than managing it, as the adaptive budget of cluster 31 would. Where the action is bounded at plus and minus a scale and the clip stands inside that scale, the excess cannot exceed the difference between the two, which is a constant of the configuration. On this robot that constant is 0.2846 radians squared per step against the 30.380 measured unbounded, a reduction of the WORST CASE by a factor of 107, and the span a fixed weight must cover falls from 51059 to one to 5.7 to one. The general statement is that a penalty on a quantity the policy can grow without limit is not a tuning problem but a specification error, and the repair is to bound the quantity rather than to search for the coefficient.

TWO NEGATIVE RESULTS. No surveyed source reports a rule for SIZING a squashing scale against the range a task needs, the literature treating the transformation as given and the range as the environment's. The rule above is this workspace's own. And a figure encountered in a search summary, that actions sit in the tanh saturated region on roughly forty per cent of SAC training steps on HalfCheetah, could NOT be traced to a retrievable source and is therefore recorded as unverified and must not be cited until it is.

THREE CORRECTIONS RECORDED ON READING THE FULL TEXTS, dated 2026-10-01. First, Belmonte-Baeza and colleagues, entered above, optimise the design with a genetic algorithm using the meta-learned policy as evaluator, not with a bandit, so the first draft of the report, which placed them under Bayesian optimisation, was wrong and the expanded report corrects it.
