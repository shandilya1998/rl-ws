## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The problem this plan addresses

The SD_BRS1 gait work stream has advanced through twenty two numbered passes recorded in [../context/brs_gait.md](../context/brs_gait.md), and the character of its evidence changed decisively at the twentieth. Every pass up to the nineteenth reasoned from TensorBoard scalars and video frames, inverting a logged episode reward through its weight to recover a physical quantity, whereas the twentieth and every pass since has computed its figures directly from the numpy dump that `play.py` writes. That change in method is what produced the durable results of the stream, the anatomy of the stomp, the trapezoidal swing profile, the antagonistic circulation of nine and a half times the delivered power, the transposition of the joint index order, and the demonstration that the feet distance hinge measures an axis a long stride satisfies.

The cost of that method is that each pass has re-derived its instruments from nothing. The touchdown detector, the peak force window, the swing segmentation, the cost of transport, the reward budget inversion, and the saturation counters have each been written afresh in a throwaway script, and none of those scripts survives in the repository. The consequence is threefold. A figure quoted in one pass cannot be reproduced in another without rewriting the code that produced it, which is why the twentieth pass reports a touchdown velocity of 1.68 metres per second where the twenty first reports 1.551 for the same run, the difference being an unrecorded contact detection threshold. A statistic computed once is lost, so the ninth pass observation on forged contact and the nineteenth pass observation on stance width had to be recomputed when they became load bearing again. And a comparison across runs is a manual assembly rather than a table, which is why the four run ablation of the twentieth pass took a whole pass to perform and has not been repeated since.

This plan converts that repeated derivation into a pipeline. The statistics become a library with one implementation per quantity, the library runs at the end of every play so that its results are dumped beside the raw channels, and the dashboard reads the dumps of every experiment under study into one table indexed by quantity and by statistic. The intended effect is that the twentieth pass becomes a thing the dashboard does on load rather than a thing an agent spends a session doing.

### 1.2 What the request asks for, and how this plan answers it

Seven families of statistic were named in the request, and section 3.1 corroborates each against the record and finds each to have been computed at some point in the stream. Section 3.2 then enumerates twenty three further statistics that the record shows were computed and which the request did not name, of which the temporal gait decomposition, the swing profile shape descriptors, the energetic ratios, the actuator saturation counters and the symmetry indices are the load bearing ones. Section 2 surveys the literature for the measures a gait evaluation ought to carry and which this project has never computed, of which the stability margins, the variability coefficients and the spectral smoothness metrics are the substantive additions. Section 4 specifies the implementation, section 5 gives the interpretation of every statistic with its expected value for a well behaved biped, and section 6 reduces the whole to a checklist.

### 1.3 Five defects in the present arrangement, four of which the plan repairs

The first is that the dump does not carry the channels several of the requested statistics require. There is no orientation channel of any kind, neither a base quaternion nor a projected gravity vector nor a foot quaternion, so the torso attitude quoted throughout the record at seven point three degrees of root mean square tilt was inverted from `pen_flat_orientation` and has never been measured, the foot yaw alignment that section 2.7 of [GAIT_EFFICIENCY_PLAN.md](GAIT_EFFICIENCY_PLAN.md) prescribes cannot be evaluated, and the expected base position of the request's sixth item cannot be integrated, since the commanded velocity is expressed in the base frame and its rotation into the world requires the yaw. There are no actuator limits, so the saturation and limit residence counters that the twentieth and twenty second passes both report were computed against constants typed into a script by hand. There is no episode boundary marker, so a reset is invisible and contaminates any statistic accumulated across it. And there is no record of the reward weights, without which the unweighted comparison of the request's seventh item is not possible.

The second is that an experiment cannot be evaluated without first being configured by hand. `play.py` builds its environment through `parse_env_cfg` at `scripts/rsl_rl/play.py:417`, which reads the task registration in the working tree rather than anything belonging to the run whose checkpoint is being loaded, so the reward set, the weights, the command ranges and the curriculum values under which a policy is replayed are those of whatever the tree happens to hold on the day. Every stored run therefore demands that its configuration be reconstructed by a human before it can be replayed faithfully, which is the reason the four run ablation of the twentieth pass consumed a whole pass, and it admits a silent failure in which a policy is replayed under a reward set it was never trained against and its budget is read as though it were. The remedy is available and unused. Each run dumps its own configuration into `params/env.yaml` beside its checkpoints, and that file records not only every weight and parameter but the fully qualified import path of every reward function and class, so the configuration is reconstructible from the artefact rather than from the tree, including terms that the tree no longer carries. Section 4.1 specifies the module that does so.

The third is that the dump's shape is not uniform and its contact force is not the force that matters. `DataLogger.plot` at `scripts/rsl_rl/play.py:399` wraps every per step series in a singleton list before saving while storing the two commanded channels flat, so a consumer must know per key whether to index a leading axis, and both dashboards carry a `get_env_data` whose first half exists only to undo that wrap at `/ws/IsaacLab/logs/rsl_rl/dashboard-brs.py:190`. The same method computes a `write_data` dictionary of properly stacked arrays at `scripts/rsl_rl/play.py:404` and then never writes it, saving the wrapped `data` instead, so the correct form is already being built and discarded. Beside that, `feet_contact_forces` at `scripts/rsl_rl/play.py:285` records the instantaneous `net_forces_w`, whereas the contact sensor rolls a force history once per physics step at `/ws/IsaacLab/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/envs/manager_based_rl_env.py:197` and the impact reward reads the maximum over that history at `exts/bipedal_locomotion/bipedal_locomotion/tasks/locomotion/mdp/rewards.py:216`. With a history length of four at `exts/bipedal_locomotion/bipedal_locomotion/tasks/locomotion/cfg/SF/brs_base_env_cfg.py:79` and a physics step of five milliseconds against a control step of ten, the reward sees a collision transient that the dump cannot, and the twenty first pass of [../context/brs_gait.md](../context/brs_gait.md) measured the gap at a logged impact value of 183.65 against 15.31 reconstructed from the instantaneous channel. Every peak force this project has quoted from a dump is therefore a lower bound.

The fourth is a semantic trap in the reward dump that has not yet been recorded anywhere. `RewardManager.compute` at `/ws/IsaacLab/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/managers/reward_manager.py:150` forms `value = func × weight × dt` and accumulates that into the buffer it returns, but at line 157 it stores `value / dt` into `_step_reward`, which is the array `DataLogger.log_reward` reads. Every per term series in `rewards.npy` is therefore a rate per second equal to `func × weight`, whereas `total_reward` is the per step value equal to the sum of those rates times `dt`. Verified on the dump of run `2026-08-03_11-19-11`, the mean of `total_reward` is 0.6011 against a summed term mean of 60.115, a ratio of exactly 0.01 which is the control period. Any budget analysis that adds the two together, or that treats a term series as a per step reward, is wrong by a factor of one hundred.

The fifth is recorded here and deliberately left standing, in the manner rule 6 of [../CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md) requires of any defect a change declines to repair. The module global `SOLE_OFFSETS` at `scripts/rsl_rl/play.py:113` is documented as the switch controlling whether the tilt invariant sole clearance is logged, defaulting to `None` so that TRON1 and every existing caller are unaffected, and that is what the fortieth line of [../context/brs_gait.md](../context/brs_gait.md) records. The code does not read it. The guard at `scripts/rsl_rl/play.py:299` tests `SD_BRS1_SOLE_OFFSETS is not None`, which is a table literal and therefore always true, and `_sole_clearance` at `scripts/rsl_rl/play.py:349` transforms that same literal rather than the configured global, so the SD_BRS1 sole geometry is applied to whatever robot is played and a TRON1 dump carries a `feet_sole_clearances` channel computed from a foot it does not have. The repair is two lines and was specified in the first draft of this plan, and it is withdrawn because the script is at present exercised only against SD_BRS1, for which the behaviour is correct, and because the repair belongs with the TRON1 corrections rather than ahead of them, where it would change a dump nobody is currently reading and would have to be validated against a robot nobody is currently playing. The consequence that survives the deferral is a reading rule rather than a code change. The `feet_sole_clearances` channel is trustworthy on SD_BRS1 and meaningless on any other robot, so `stats.py` must never treat the presence of that channel as evidence that the robot has a sole, and the `sole_clearance` property of section 4.3.2 carries the caution at the point of use rather than leaving it to a reader to recall.

## 2. Literature Survey

This survey extends the thirteen thematic clusters of [../context/literature.md](../context/literature.md), and the same pass that writes this document adds its new entries there as cluster fourteen. Clusters seven through thirteen already ground the reward structures, the contact scheduling, the symmetry mechanisms, the swing shaping and the impact pricing, and are not repeated. What follows concerns the measurement of a gait rather than its specification, which is a question the earlier clusters never had to ask, since each of them was written to justify a change to a reward rather than to judge the policy a reward produced.

### 2.1 The temporal and spatial parameters, and why they come first

The normative description of walking is a partition of the gait cycle, and the figures for a healthy adult at a comfortable one point three three metres per second are a cadence of one hundred and thirteen steps per minute, a stride length of one point four one metres, and a division into sixty two percent stance, thirty eight percent swing and twenty four percent double support [1]. These parameters come first in any evaluation because they are the coarsest description under which two gaits can be compared at all, and because a policy can satisfy a velocity command through an unbounded family of temporal arrangements of which only a narrow band is a walk. The record of this project supplies the demonstration, run `2026-07-20_07-52-14` passing every gate then in force while holding a single stance for two and a half seconds at a time, which is a temporal arrangement no gait parameter set would admit.

Two derived quantities complete the family. The duty factor, being the fraction of the cycle a given foot spends in contact, distinguishes a walk from a run at the value of one half, and the phase offset between the two feet distinguishes a walk from a hop or a bound. Perry's normative tables supply the stance width band as one to one point three times the hip width [2], which the nineteenth pass of [../context/brs_gait.md](../context/brs_gait.md) already applied to this robot to derive a target between 0.26 and 0.338 metres.

It must however, be noted that the aforementioned statistics regarding human gait can only be applied directly to anthropomorphic biped robots. For robots such as Cassie or Tron1a which have a significantly different morphology and leg shape and these statitistics may not transfer directly. For such robots, it may become prudent to study biped animals with similar morphology for appropriate baseline gait statistics to compare with.

### 2.2 Kinetics, the ground reaction force, and the pricing of the collision

The vertical ground reaction force of walking is a double humped curve peaking between one and one point five body weights, rising to its first peak over roughly a quarter of stance, against two to two point nine body weights in running [3]. Three quantities derived from that curve carry more diagnostic value than the peak alone. The loading rate, being the time derivative of the force through the first hump, distinguishes a collision from a weight acceptance, and the record of this project reports seven hundred and thirty three body weights per second for a gait whose peak alone would have looked merely high. The impulse, being the time integral of the force over stance, must equal the momentum change the step requires and therefore serves as a conservation check on the contact model. And the centre of pressure trajectory within the support polygon is the observable half of the zero moment point criterion of Vukobratović and Borovac [4], under which a foot remains flat exactly while that point lies inside the polygon, so its excursion measures how close the foot is to rolling onto an edge.

McGeer's demonstration that a knee jointed biped walks stably down a shallow slope with no actuation whatever [5] supplies the reason the collision matters more than any other single event, which is that the heel strike is where a walking gait loses its energy, so a policy that raises its collision velocity pays twice, once to inject the kinetic energy and once to arrest it.

### 2.3 Energetics

The dimensionless cost of transport, also called the specific resistance, is the standard energetic measure and is the mechanical or electrical power divided by the product of weight and speed. Human walking sits near zero point two, the Cornell and Delft passive dynamic walkers achieve zero point zero five five and zero point zero eight against an estimated zero point zero five for the mechanical component of human walking, and the Honda humanoid of the previous generation required roughly one point six to two [6]. A learned policy may land anywhere across that forty fold range and nothing in a velocity tracking objective selects the efficient end of it, which is why the measure belongs in every evaluation rather than only in those that name efficiency as an objective.

The measure admits three definitions that must be kept distinct because they diverge by an order of magnitude on a badly conditioned gait. The absolute mechanical cost of transport takes the sum over joints of the absolute joint power, which charges a joint for braking as well as for driving and is the correct measure for an actuator that cannot regenerate. The net cost takes the algebraic sum, which credits braking against driving and therefore measures the power the motion itself demands. The positive cost clips the algebraic sum at zero, which is the convention the quadrupedal locomotion benchmarking literature adopts when it reports the inverse cost of transport as a headline metric [7]. The record of this project supplies the reason all three are needed, run `2026-07-28_06-37-24` measuring two point three eight on absolute power against zero point two five on net, a ratio of nine and a half which is precisely the antagonistic circulation the plan of [GAIT_EFFICIENCY_PLAN.md](GAIT_EFFICIENCY_PLAN.md) went on to diagnose. A single figure would have concealed the defect entirely.

### 2.4 Stability, and the measures that quantify how near a fall the gait runs

Four families appear, and they answer different questions.

The zero moment point criterion is the oldest and states that the contact remains unilateral while the point at which the horizontal ground reaction moment vanishes lies within the support polygon [4]. Its value as a metric rather than as a controller constraint is that the distance from that point to the nearest polygon edge is a margin with the dimension of length, directly comparable across gaits and directly interpretable as the room the foot has before it rolls.

The extrapolated centre of mass of Hof, Gazendam and Sinke extends the inverted pendulum to the dynamic case, defining the extrapolated point as the centre of mass position plus its velocity divided by the pendulum eigenfrequency, and the signed distance from that point to the boundary of the base of support as the margin of stability [8]. It is the measure of choice in the biomechanical literature because it accounts for velocity as well as position, so a centre of mass well inside the polygon but travelling fast toward its edge is correctly reported as unstable where the zero moment point would report otherwise. Its mediolateral component is the one most associated with falls, and the frontal plane balance literature establishes the reason, that the lateral excursion required per step is half the stance width so a narrow stance leaves nothing in reserve [9].

The capture point of Pratt, Carff, Drakunov and Goswami is the same quantity read forward rather than backward, being the point on the ground at which a step would bring the body to rest, and its distance beyond the support polygon measures the size of the step a recovery would require [10]. Under the linear inverted pendulum it coincides with the divergent component of the motion, so the three measures are algebraically related and differ principally in what they are compared against.

The fourth family is nonlinear and measures the gait's response to perturbation rather than its instantaneous margin. The finite time maximum Lyapunov exponent quantifies the divergence of neighbouring trajectories under continuous small perturbation, and the maximum Floquet multiplier quantifies whether the stride to stride map contracts, a value below unity indicating orbital stability [11]. These are the measures the clinical literature uses to separate fallers from non fallers where the spatiotemporal parameters do not discriminate [11], and their relevance here is that a policy evaluated on flat ground without disturbance may be orbitally marginal in a way no mean or percentile reveals.

### 2.5 Symmetry

The symmetry index of Robinson, Herzog and Nigg expresses the difference between the two limbs as a percentage of their mean, and remains the most commonly used construction [12]. Its known weakness is that it is unbounded as the mean approaches zero, which matters for a quantity such as a joint torque that legitimately passes through zero, so the normalised variants that bound the index are preferred where the denominator is small [12]. This project already computes it, the twentieth pass reporting four point seven percent at the hip pitch and forty one percent at the ankle roll range of motion, and the contrast between those two figures is exactly what makes the measure useful, since it localises the residual asymmetry to the frontal plane where the lateral balance defect lives.

The harmonic ratio offers a complementary construction derived from the trunk acceleration spectrum, comparing the amplitude of the even harmonics of the stride frequency against the odd, and is widely used as a step to step symmetry and smoothness index [13]. Its documented ambiguity, that it has been paraphrased variously as smoothness, harmony, rhythmicity and dynamic stability, is a reason to report it beside the symmetry index rather than in place of it [13].

### 2.6 Smoothness

Two constructions are established. The dimensionless jerk integrates the squared third derivative of a signal and normalises by duration and amplitude, and the spectral arc length of Balasubramanian and colleagues takes the arc length of the normalised Fourier magnitude spectrum of the speed profile [14]. The second is preferred in the motor control literature because it is robust to measurement noise and independent of movement duration, whereas jerk based measures vary counterintuitively with both [14]. The relevance to a legged policy is direct, since the record of this project describes joint traces as impulse trains rather than continuous signals, with accelerations reaching one thousand nine hundred radians per second squared at the ninety ninth percentile and torque changing by four hundred and seventy newton metres within one control step, and neither the action rate penalty nor the torque rate penalty measures that character in a way a reader can compare against a standard.

The stronger instrument on the specification side is the conditioning for action policy smoothness method of Mysore and colleagues, which regularises the state to action map rather than pricing roughness through a reward [15], and it is recorded here because a smoothness metric that improves under a reward penalty and not under policy regularisation is measuring the penalty rather than the gait.

### 2.7 Variability

The stride to stride coefficient of variation of the temporal parameters is the standard variability measure, and the clinical result that grounds it is that stride time variability predicts falls in community living older adults where the mean stride time does not [16]. Step width variability carries the same property in the frontal plane [16]. The measure transfers to a policy evaluation without modification and answers a question the percentiles do not, namely whether the gait is a limit cycle or a sequence of recoveries, and the record of this project contains exactly the observation it would have flagged, the twentieth pass noting a cadence whose median is exactly one second while its tenth percentile lies at zero point seven two, which is a tail of short cycles that a mean and a standard deviation together would have concealed.

### 2.8 Angular momentum regulation

Popovic, Hofmann and Herr established that whole body angular momentum about the centre of mass is regulated to a small range throughout human walking, segment to segment cancellation accounting for up to ninety five percent in the sagittal plane, seventy in the frontal and eighty in the horizontal [17]. The corollary they draw is that the centroidal moment pivot coincides with the centre of pressure whenever the horizontal moment about the centre of mass vanishes, so the separation between those two points is a direct measure of how far a gait departs from momentum regulation [17]. For a machine such as SD_BRS1 whose hip yaw joints are fixed, the horizontal plane component is the one that matters, since section 2.7 of [GAIT_EFFICIENCY_PLAN.md](GAIT_EFFICIENCY_PLAN.md) establishes that every yaw moment must be obtained from the ground, and a swing leg generating angular momentum the stance foot cannot absorb through friction is precisely the failure the yaw tracking measurements report.

### 2.9 Task performance and robustness in the reinforcement learning setting

The quadrupedal benchmarking literature converges on a small set of headline metrics, the success rate, the velocity tracking error, and the inverse cost of transport [7], and the systematic review of deep reinforcement learning for legged locomotion adds that reward shaping and training stability dominate the outcome, which is an argument for reporting the reward budget alongside the behaviour [18]. Two cautions from the record of this project qualify any such comparison and are stated here because no surveyed source states them. The tracking error does not converge downward while a curriculum is active, since the reward kernel contracts and the command range widens as training proceeds, so a run compared against a longer predecessor appears to track worse for reasons unrelated to its reward set, and the twenty second pass of [../context/brs_gait.md](../context/brs_gait.md) establishes that comparisons must be taken at matched iteration counts with the curriculum values verified equal. And a play dump's reward channels carry the weights of the live source tree rather than of the trained configuration, since `play.py` rebuilds its environment through `parse_env_cfg`, so a per term function value must be recovered by reconstruction rather than by division through an assumed weight.

### 2.10 Synthesis

Eleven families of statistic follow, and section 4 implements each. The temporal and spatial parameters describe what the gait is. The kinetics describe what it does to the ground. The energetics describe what it costs. The stability margins describe how near a fall it runs. The symmetry indices describe whether its two halves agree. The smoothness metrics describe whether its actuation could be realised. The variability coefficients describe whether it is a limit cycle. The angular momentum measures describe whether its segments cooperate. The tracking errors describe whether it does what it was asked. The actuator margins describe whether the machine could survive it. And the reward budget describes why it chose to be what it is, which is the family the record of this project has found most often decisive, every one of the five obstacles enumerated in section 1.1 of [GAIT_EFFICIENCY_PLAN.md](GAIT_EFFICIENCY_PLAN.md) having been diagnosed from a budget rather than from a behaviour.

### 3.2 Statistics computed in the record and absent from the request

Twenty three further statistics appear in the record, and they are grouped here by the question each was introduced to answer. Every one of them is load bearing somewhere, in the sense that a conclusion of the record rests upon it, and every one is specified in section 4.

The temporal decomposition of the gait cycle carries five. The cadence taken as the median and the mean of the completed cycle period, the stance duty per foot, the double support fraction, the single support fraction, and the flight fraction. These are the statistics on which the whole of Phase 2 of [GAIT_EFFICIENCY_PLAN.md](GAIT_EFFICIENCY_PLAN.md) turns, the measured double support of two point three percent against a commanded nineteen point six having been the observation that identified the schedule as under determined by the reward.

The contact event family carries five. The touchdown vertical velocity of the body frame, the sole approach velocity which is the descent rate of the lowest sole point and differs from the first by the rotational term, the peak contact force within eighty milliseconds of touchdown expressed in body weights, the loading rate, and the fraction of touchdowns exceeding a nominated force threshold. The twenty first pass established that the distinction between the first two is decisive, the policy having answered a penalty on the frame velocity by rotating its foot into the ground, so a pipeline reporting only one of them would have concealed the exploit.

The swing profile family carries four. The apex clearance, the position of the apex within the normalised swing, the number of clearance peaks per swing, and the correlation of the normalised profile against a raised cosine reference. The twenty second pass reports these four together as the evidence that the profile became single peaked and near sinusoidal without Phase 3 acting, and no one of them alone would have carried that conclusion, the apex having been essentially unchanged while its position moved from 0.265 to 0.463.

The energetic family carries four. The mean absolute joint power, the net mechanical power, the absolute and net cost of transport, and the antagonism measure taken either as the ratio of the first two or as the power the hip and the knee expend against one another. The record reports the last of these falling from one hundred and seventy watts to forty three between two runs, which is the clearest single statement of what Phase 1b bought.

The actuator margin family carries three. The fraction of steps at which a joint stands within a nominated tolerance of a mechanical limit, the fraction at which the applied torque exceeds a nominated fraction of the effort ceiling, and the excursion beyond a declared limit where the solver has yielded. The twentieth pass reports the ankle roll at thirty one percent limit residence with six to seven percent effort saturation, and the twenty second reports the elimination of the second, and neither figure is derivable without the actuator limits that the dump does not presently carry.

The symmetry family carries two, the Robinson index of the root mean square torque per joint pair and of the range of motion per joint pair, and the record's use of them is instructive, the sagittal indices in single figures being cited as evidence the augmentation works while the frontal index of forty one percent is cited as evidence of a lateral balance defect.

### 3.3 The contribution of the co-design work stream

The design and policy co-optimisation stream, recorded in [../context/knowledge_base.md](../context/knowledge_base.md), [../context/copt.md](../context/copt.md) and [../context/cmaes.md](../context/cmaes.md), computed statistics of a different kind, since its object was the outer loop rather than the gait. Three of them bear on this pipeline.

The first is the per iteration mean and standard deviation of each design parameter across the population, which [../context/cmaes.md](../context/cmaes.md) section 7 uses to demonstrate the spread growing through the random phase and collapsing to zero thereafter with both parameters pinned at their rounded upper bounds. The analogue in a play dump is that a co-optimisation policy is evaluated across environments carrying different morphologies, so every behavioural statistic must be computable per design group as well as in aggregate. The dump already carries the descriptor, `robot_mass`, `robot_inertia` and `robot_material_properties` being logged per environment at `scripts/rsl_rl/play.py:323` through `:331`, and inspection of run `2026-08-03_11-19-11` confirms the mechanism works, the material properties resolving to thirty two distinct rows across thirty two environments under friction randomisation while the masses resolve to one, since that run randomises the surface and not the body. No information is logged for design parameters for the current runs, so these values are out of scope.

The second is the within generation fitness range, which [../context/cmaes.md](../context/cmaes.md) section 5 identifies as the quantity whose collapse triggers the `tolfun` and `tolflatfitness` termination criteria. It has no play dump analogue and is out of scope.

The third is the treatment of variance itself. [../context/knowledge_base.md](../context/knowledge_base.md) records that the training curves exhibit a variance which never contracts, and that the diagnosis required separating the variance of the policy from the variance of the design population and from the variance introduced by the reward window flooding at a morphology update. The methodological lesson transfers directly, that a statistic aggregated over a population of environments conceals whether its spread is within an environment across time or across environments at one time. The module as specified pools the two for most families, `summarise` in section 4.3.2 taking the flattened array, and separates them only for the symmetry family of section 4.3.13, where section 7 records that the separation was not optional, a pooled ankle roll index of 4.68 percent standing against a per environment index of 27.03 and contradicting the twentieth pass entirely. The pooling elsewhere is therefore a known limitation rather than a considered choice, and a co-optimisation run, whose environments carry different morphologies, will need the decomposition extended to the energetic and impact families before its populations can be compared.

### 3.4 The instrumentation facts that govern every computation

Seven facts were established during the corroboration and each constrains the implementation. They are recorded in full in [../context/gait_metrics.md](../context/gait_metrics.md) and stated here in summary.

The dump as it stands stores every per step series under a leading singleton axis, so a channel of shape `(1, T, E, ...)` must be indexed at zero before use, and the two commanded channels are stored without that wrap, at `scripts/rsl_rl/play.py:399` through `:402`. The three name lists are exempt from the wrap and from the stack by the `_METADATA_KEYS` tuple at `scripts/rsl_rl/play.py:105`. Section 4.2.7 abolishes the asymmetry at its source, so that from that change onward every per step key is a stacked array of shape `(T, E, ...)` and the commanded channels are the only keys whose shape does not move. No compatibility provision accompanies it. Every dump the record rests upon is to be regenerated by replay under the extended logger, which is what makes the new channels of section 4.2 available to the statistics in the first place, so the shape below is the only shape any consumer specified here will meet and a dump predating the change is superseded rather than supported.

The joint and body index order is left before right at every depth of the articulation, proved from the joint limits in the twentieth pass, and the names are now recorded in the dump so no consumer need assume it.

The per term reward series is a rate per second and `total_reward` is a per step value, as section 1.3 establishes, and the ratio between the summed terms and the total is exactly the control period.

The instantaneous contact force in the dump understates the true peak, the impact reward reading the sensor's force history whose maximum sees transients falling between two control steps, and the twenty first pass measures the discrepancy at a factor of twelve on the excess above threshold. Section 4.2.5 moves the logged channel onto the same history maximum the reward reads, so that from that change onward the two agree by construction, and a dump predating it must be read as carrying a lower bound.

A play dump's reward weights are those of the live source tree rather than of the trained configuration, since `play.py` rebuilds through `parse_env_cfg`, so the weights must be logged at play time and the run's own `params/env.yaml` consulted where a trained value is wanted. That file is machine readable without any Isaac Lab import, a fact established by prototype during this revision and specified in section 4.1, and it carries the weight, the parameter set and the import path of all twenty six reward terms of run `2026-08-03_11-19-11` in exactly the order `rewards.npy` records them, which makes the correspondence between the two artefacts positional as well as nominal.

Episode resets occur within a play and are presently invisible. Inspection of run `2026-08-03_11-19-11` finds exactly one reset per environment, detectable as a base position discontinuity of up to nineteen point five metres at step 2998, and the commanded velocity resamples independently at steps 1088 and 2007. Any statistic accumulated across a boundary is contaminated, a cycle period spanning it being meaningless and a base position error being unbounded.

The control period must not be assumed. It is 0.01 seconds for SD_BRS1 and 0.02 for the Isaac Lab G1 reference, and every time derived statistic scales with it.

## 4. Implementation Plan

The programme is five changes taken in order, since each depends on its predecessor. The parameter reader comes first, since it is what makes an experiment reconstructible and what supplies the trained weights the budget statistics need. The dump is extended second so that the statistics have channels to read, and the runs under study are replayed once at that point so that the record's evidence base is carried onto the new format in one pass rather than gradually. The statistics module is written third and validated against those regenerated dumps, the wiring is fourth, and the dashboard is last.

Every change observes the discipline of [../CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md), and the discipline binds here in a specific way that must be stated before any code appears. `scripts/rsl_rl/play.py` is shared by SD_BRS1 and by the TRON1 solefoot, pointfoot and wheelfoot tasks, and its `DataLogger` writes the dumps that six scripts read, the four live consumers being `/ws/IsaacLab/logs/rsl_rl/dashboard-brs.py`, `/ws/IsaacLab/logs/rsl_rl/dashboard.py` and their divergent copies at `tron1-rl-isaaclab-cozum/scripts/analysis/dashboard-brs.py` and `tron1-rl-isaaclab-cozum/scripts/analysis/dashboard.py`, with two further readers surviving under `/ws/IsaacLab/logs/rsl_rl_backup/`. Most of what follows is therefore purely additive, and every addition is guarded so that a robot lacking the underlying quantity is unaffected.

Two changes are not additive and are argued separately with their consumer sweep attached, since each alters a key that already exists. Section 4.2.5 changes the meaning of `feet_contact_forces` from the instantaneous force to the force at the instant of the history peak, holding its shape and its frame constant so that every existing plot continues to draw, while its magnitude becomes the quantity the impact reward prices. Section 4.2.7 removes the leading singleton axis from every per step key, which is a shape change visible to all six readers, and section 4.5 carries the compensating simplification of `get_env_data` in the four live ones. Neither change may be made without the other half of its pair. The shape change is not compensated for in the manner rule 4 of [../CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md) would otherwise require, because the dumps that would have needed the compensation are to be regenerated, and the plan states the consequence rather than hiding it, that a dump written before this change is unreadable by anything specified here and must be replayed to be analysed again.

### 4.1 Reconstructing the experiment from its own parameters

An experiment on disk is presently a checkpoint whose configuration lives elsewhere, and evaluating it demands that a human restore that configuration in the working tree first. This section makes the run self describing, which is the precondition for every automation the rest of the plan builds.

#### 4.1.1 What the dumped parameter file actually contains

Isaac Lab writes four files into `params/` beside the checkpoints of every run, `env.yaml` and `env.pkl` carrying the environment configuration and `agent.yaml` and `agent.pkl` carrying the runner configuration. The YAML is a full serialisation of the configuration object graph rather than a summary of it, and its tagging is what makes it useful. Verified against `IsaacLab/logs/rsl_rl/sd_brs1_flat/2026-08-03_11-19-11/params/env.yaml`, the document carries twenty four top level keys, a `seed` of 42, a `decimation` of 2 against a `sim.dt` of 0.005 which fixes the control period at 0.01 seconds without assumption, an `episode_length_s` of 20.0, and a `rewards` mapping of twenty six terms each of which is an `isaaclab.managers.manager_term_cfg.RewardTermCfg` carrying its `weight`, its `params` and, decisively, its `func` as a fully qualified import path.

```yaml
rewards: !!python/object:bipedal_locomotion.tasks.locomotion.cfg.SF.brs_base_env_cfg.RewardsCfg
  rew_gait: !!python/object:isaaclab.managers.manager_term_cfg.RewardTermCfg
    func: !!python/name:bipedal_locomotion.tasks.locomotion.mdp.rewards.GaitReward ''
    params:
      sensor_cfg: !!python/object:isaaclab.managers.scene_entity_cfg.SceneEntityCfg
        name: contact_forces
        body_names: Link6[LR]
      ...
    weight: 40.0
```

Four tag families appear and each must be handled, `!!python/object:` for a configuration instance, `!!python/name:` for a function or class reference, `!!python/object/apply:` for a constructed value such as `builtins.slice`, and `!!python/tuple` for a tuple. The document also uses YAML anchors and aliases, an actuator class reference being defined once at line 184 and aliased at lines 205, 226, 247 and 268, which PyYAML resolves natively and which any hand written parser would not.

The correspondence with the reward dump was verified rather than assumed. The twenty six terms parsed from `env.yaml` are identical in name and in order to the twenty six keys of `rewards.npy` for the same run, which follows from `RewardManager._prepare_terms` iterating `self.cfg.__dict__.items()` at `/ws/IsaacLab/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/managers/reward_manager.py:222` in declaration order and from the YAML dumper preserving that order, and it means a weight may be matched to a series by position as well as by name.

#### 4.1.2 The reader, `tron1-rl-isaaclab-cozum/scripts/analysis/experiment_params.py`

The module has two layers and the separation between them is what lets one file serve both the container and the dashboard. The lower layer parses the YAML into plain Python containers and imports nothing from Isaac Lab, so it runs anywhere numpy and PyYAML run. The upper layer revives the parsed terms into live `RewardTermCfg` objects and applies them to an environment configuration, and it is used only inside the simulation container. An attempt to load the document with `yaml.unsafe_load` fails outside the container with `ModuleNotFoundError: No module named 'bipedal_locomotion'`, which is the failure this separation exists to avoid, and the lower layer was prototyped against the run above and parsed it whole.

```python
"""Reconstruction of an experiment from the parameter files the run itself dumped.

Isaac Lab writes params/env.yaml and params/agent.yaml beside the checkpoints of every
run, and those files carry the configuration the policy was TRAINED under, which is not
in general the configuration the working tree holds when the policy is replayed. This
module reads them, so that an evaluation is configured by the artefact rather than by
hand.

The parsing layer imports nothing from Isaac Lab and is therefore usable from an analysis
dashboard outside the simulation container. The revival layer, which turns parsed terms
back into live configuration objects, imports lazily and is usable only inside it.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import importlib
import os

import yaml


class ParamsLoader(yaml.SafeLoader):
    """A SafeLoader that survives the python specific tags Isaac Lab's dumper emits.

    yaml.unsafe_load would execute the imports these tags name, which fails wherever the
    task package is not installed and which is exactly the case a dashboard runs in. This
    loader records what each tag REFERRED to instead of resolving it, so the document
    parses whole and the decision to import is left to the caller.
    """


def _construct_name(loader, suffix, node):
    # A function or class reference, e.g. `!!python/name:package.module.function ''`.
    # The dotted path is retained so that the revival layer may import it on demand.
    return {"__ref__": suffix}


def _construct_object(loader, suffix, node):
    # A configuration instance. Its fields become a plain mapping, with the class it was
    # dumped from retained under a reserved key so that the revival layer can rebuild it.
    mapping = loader.construct_mapping(node, deep=True) if isinstance(
        node, yaml.MappingNode
    ) else {}
    mapping["__class__"] = suffix
    return mapping


def _construct_apply(loader, suffix, node):
    # A constructed value, e.g. `!!python/object/apply:builtins.slice [null, null, null]`.
    return {"__apply__": suffix, "args": loader.construct_sequence(node, deep=True)}

def _construct_tuple(loader, node):
    return tuple(loader.construct_sequence(node, deep=True))


ParamsLoader.add_multi_constructor("tag:yaml.org,2002:python/name:", _construct_name)
ParamsLoader.add_multi_constructor("tag:yaml.org,2002:python/object:", _construct_object)
ParamsLoader.add_multi_constructor(
    "tag:yaml.org,2002:python/object/apply:", _construct_apply
)
ParamsLoader.add_constructor("tag:yaml.org,2002:python/tuple", _construct_tuple)


def params_dir(run_dir: str) -> str:
    """The params directory of a run, given the run directory or any path inside it."""
    candidate = run_dir
    for _ in range(3):
        if os.path.isdir(os.path.join(candidate, "params")):
            return os.path.join(candidate, "params")
        candidate = os.path.dirname(candidate)
    return os.path.join(run_dir, "params")


def load_params(run_dir: str, name: str = "env") -> dict | None:
    """Parse params/<name>.yaml of a run into plain containers, or None if absent."""
    path = os.path.join(params_dir(run_dir), f"{name}.yaml")
    if not os.path.exists(path):
        return None
    with open(path) as handle:
        return yaml.load(handle, Loader=ParamsLoader)

def reward_terms(env_params: dict) -> dict[str, dict]:
    """Ordered name -> {weight, func, params} for every configured reward term.
    The order is the declaration order of the configuration class, which is the order
    RewardManager registers the terms in at reward_manager.py:222 and therefore the order
    the per term series appear in rewards.npy. Verified identical, name for name and
    position for position, across the twenty six terms of run 2026-08-03_11-19-11.
    """
    terms = {}
    for name, term in (env_params or {}).get("rewards", {}).items():
        if name == "__class__" or not isinstance(term, dict):
            continue
        func = term.get("func")
        terms[name] = {
            "weight": float(term.get("weight", 0.0)),
            "func": func.get("__ref__") if isinstance(func, dict) else None,
            "params": term.get("params", {}),
        }
    return terms
def reward_weights(env_params: dict) -> dict[str, float]:
    """Name -> configured weight, the divisor that recovers an unweighted term value."""
    return {name: term["weight"] for name, term in reward_terms(env_params).items()}


def step_dt(env_params: dict) -> float | None:
    """The control period, as the product of the physics step and the decimation.

    This is the quantity every cadence, duty fraction, loading rate and cost of transport
    scales with, and reading it from the run rather than assuming it is what makes a
    figure comparable across two robots configured differently.
    """
    sim = (env_params or {}).get("sim") or {}
    if "dt" in sim and env_params.get("decimation") is not None:
        return float(sim["dt"]) * int(env_params["decimation"])
    return None


def feet_body_expression(env_params: dict, term: str = "feet_air_time") -> str | None:
    """The body name expression resolving the feet, taken from a contact keyed term.
    Every gait term that keys on the feet carries a SceneEntityCfg naming them, so the
    feet regex need not be a constant in the analysis code. Falls back through the terms
    that carry one, since a configuration need not declare any given term.
    """
    terms = reward_terms(env_params)
    for candidate in (term, "feet_slide", "rew_no_fly", "rew_gait", "pen_feet_impact"):
        params = terms.get(candidate, {}).get("params", {})
        sensor = params.get("sensor_cfg") or params.get("asset_cfg") or {}
        names = sensor.get("body_names") if isinstance(sensor, dict) else None
        if names:
            return names if isinstance(names, str) else list(names)
    return None
```
The revival layer follows, and it is deliberately narrow. It restores the reward configuration and nothing else, because the reward configuration is the part that governs what a replay measures and the part most likely to have drifted, whereas the scene, the actuators and the observation set are pinned by the checkpoint itself and by the task registration that the checkpoint was trained against.

```python
def _revive(value):
    """Turn a parsed mapping back into the object it was dumped from.

    Constructed through __new__ rather than __init__, because a configclass may declare
    required fields whose values are already present in the mapping and whose ordering
    the dumper does not preserve, so calling the constructor would demand arguments that
    are about to be overwritten anyway.
    """
    if isinstance(value, dict) and "__ref__" in value:
        module_path, _, attribute = value["__ref__"].rpartition(".")
        return getattr(importlib.import_module(module_path), attribute)
    if isinstance(value, dict) and "__apply__" in value:
        module_path, _, attribute = value["__apply__"].rpartition(".")
        factory = getattr(importlib.import_module(module_path), attribute)
        return factory(*[_revive(argument) for argument in value["args"]])
    if isinstance(value, dict) and "__class__" in value:
        module_path, _, attribute = value["__class__"].rpartition(".")
        cls = getattr(importlib.import_module(module_path), attribute)
        instance = cls.__new__(cls)
        for key, item in value.items():
            if key != "__class__":
                setattr(instance, key, _revive(item))
        return instance
    if isinstance(value, dict):
        return {key: _revive(item) for key, item in value.items()}
    if isinstance(value, (list, tuple)):
        return type(value)(_revive(item) for item in value)
    return value


def apply_reward_cfg(env_cfg, env_params: dict, strict: bool = False) -> dict:
    """Replace the reward configuration of env_cfg with the run's own, term by term.

    Returns a report of what was applied, what was added and what could not be imported,
    which the caller should print, since a silently skipped term is a policy replayed
    under a reward set it was not trained against.

    Only the reward terms are touched. Every other field of env_cfg is left exactly as
    parse_env_cfg produced it, so a caller that does not invoke this function sees no
    change whatever, which is the backwards compatibility condition of ../CLAUDE.md.
    """
    from isaaclab.managers.manager_term_cfg import RewardTermCfg

    report = {"applied": [], "added": [], "removed": [], "failed": {}}
    parsed = reward_terms(env_params)
    existing = {
        name for name in vars(env_cfg.rewards) if not name.startswith("_")
    }
    for name, term in parsed.items():
        if term["func"] is None:
            report["failed"][name] = "no func reference in params"
            continue
        try:
            func = _revive({"__ref__": term["func"]})
            params = {k: _revive(v) for k, v in (term["params"] or {}).items()}
        except Exception as error:  # noqa: BLE001
            # A term whose function the tree no longer carries. Recorded rather than
            # raised, so that one deleted reward does not make a whole run unevaluable.
            report["failed"][name] = repr(error)
            if strict:
                raise
            continue
        setattr(
            env_cfg.rewards,
            name,
            RewardTermCfg(func=func, params=params, weight=term["weight"]),
        )
        report["added" if name not in existing else "applied"].append(name)
    for name in sorted(existing - set(parsed)):
        # A term the tree carries and the run did not. Removed, because leaving it in
        # would add a reward the policy never saw to the budget the dashboard compares.
        setattr(env_cfg.rewards, name, None)
        report["removed"].append(name)
    return report
```

Three properties of this construction deserve to be stated, since each is what makes it safe. A term the tree has since deleted is still applied, because the function is imported by the path the run recorded rather than looked up in the current configuration class, and a term the tree has since added is removed, because `RewardManager._prepare_terms` skips a term whose configuration is `None` at `/ws/IsaacLab/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/managers/reward_manager.py:226`. A term added under a name the current class does not declare is nonetheless registered, because that same method iterates the instance dictionary rather than the class annotations at `:222`, so `setattr` suffices. And the whole operation is opt in at the call site, so no existing invocation of `play.py` changes behaviour, which section 4.4 arranges through a flag whose default is the present conduct.

#### 4.2.5 The contact force channel becomes the history peak

This is the first of the two changes that alter an existing key, and the argument for it is that the key presently records a quantity nobody wants. The dump's `feet_contact_forces` is the instantaneous `net_forces_w` at `scripts/rsl_rl/play.py:285`, sampled once per control step, whereas the contact sensor rolls its history buffer once per physics step at `/ws/IsaacLab/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/sensors/contact_sensor/contact_sensor.py:377` because `scene.update` is called inside the decimation loop at `/ws/IsaacLab/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/envs/manager_based_rl_env.py:197`. #### 4.2.7 The dump acquires a uniform shape

This is the second change that touches existing keys, and it is the one that pays for itself most directly, since it deletes code in four consumers rather than adding any. `DataLogger.plot` at `scripts/rsl_rl/play.py:388` presently builds two dictionaries. Into `data` it puts each per step series wrapped in a singleton list, except the two commanded channels which it puts in flat, and into `write_data` it puts the properly stacked array of every series. It then saves `data` and discards `write_data`, so the dump carries a leading singleton axis on every key but two, and the correct form was computed and thrown away on the same line. Both dashboards then carry the inverse operation, a `get_env_data` whose first thirty lines exist to detect a list, unwrap a nested list and restack it at `/ws/IsaacLab/logs/rsl_rl/dashboard-brs.py:190` through `:208`.

The remedy is to save the stacked array under every per step key and to delete the wrap, the second dictionary and the special case together.

```python

            # is stored flat, so a consumer reads data["joint_names"] as the list itself.

            # per key whether a leading axis stood in front of the time axis. The stack

        write_rewards = {
```

Three consequences follow and each must be carried through. The commanded channels do not move, since `np.stack` over their list of per step arrays yields the same `(T, E, ...)` they already had, so the only difference for them is that the value is now an array rather than a list. Every other per step key loses exactly one leading axis. And `latent_space_output`, which on a non HIM policy is never appended to, ceases to appear as a degenerate `(1, 0)` array and is simply absent, which is the honest representation of a channel that was never recorded and which every consumer already tolerates through its `in data` test.

The compensating change in the readers is specified in section 4.5 and is a deletion rather than a translation. The four live consumers stop detecting a list, stop unwrapping a nested one and stop restacking it, after which `get_env_data` becomes the four lines it should always have been, because the only shape it can now meet is the one this section writes. No load time normalisation stands between the two, since the dumps that would have required it are to be regenerated by replay in the same pass that lands this change, and a helper preserved against dumps nobody intends to read again is code carried for no reader. The consequence is stated plainly and is the price of the simplification. A dump written before this change is not readable by any consumer specified here, and the two readers under `/ws/IsaacLab/logs/rsl_rl_backup/` are left untouched precisely because they are the historical copies that still read the historical form, which is where a dump that escapes the regeneration must be taken.

### 4.3 The module, `tron1-rl-isaaclab-cozum/scripts/analysis/stats.py`

#### 4.3.1 Design constraints

Seven constraints govern the module and each is a consequence of where it must run.

It must not be named `statistics`. The standard library carries a module of that name and `rsl_rl` imports it, so a file called `statistics.py` on the path of a script that also imports `rsl_rl` shadows the standard library module and breaks the runner rather than the analysis, which is the worst arrangement of failure and cause. The first draft of this plan proposed to evade the collision through an explicit loader built on `importlib.util.spec_from_file_location`, on the reasoning that the request had named the file. That is four lines of machinery spent on preserving a name, and the name is not the deliverable. The file is `stats.py`, the collision does not arise, and `play.py` imports it plainly.

It has exactly one consumer. `play.py` imports it, computes the record set at the close of a play and writes it beside the raw dumps, and nothing else in the workspace imports it at all. The division is deliberate and it decides much of what follows. A statistic is computed once, by the process that holds the arrays it describes, so a figure cannot drift from the data it was taken from and two consumers cannot report different values for one quantity. The dashboards of section 4.5 read `statistics.npy` and plot it, which makes them presentation and nothing more, and it means a change to a statistic takes effect by replaying rather than by reloading a page, which is the honest ordering since the statistic belongs to the run and not to the viewer.

It must depend on nothing but numpy. `play.py` imports it inside the simulation container after the Omniverse application has launched, and any dependency on Isaac Lab or on the task package would tie the module to a task registry it has no business knowing about. Torch is excluded for the same reason and for a second one, that the validation gates of section 7 run the module in a plain interpreter against a stored dump, which is what makes the specification testable without a simulator. The parameter reader of section 4.1 observes the same rule in its parsing layer and adds PyYAML, which both environments already carry.

It must be robot agnostic. Nothing may assume ten joints, two feet, a particular joint name, a sole geometry or a body weight. Every dimension is taken from the arrays and every label from the recorded name lists, and where a statistic requires a quantity a given robot does not supply, the statistic is omitted rather than defaulted, since a defaulted value is indistinguishable from a measured one once it reaches a table.

It must read exactly one dump format and must not reshape what it reads. Every dump reaching it is written by the `DataLogger` of section 4.2, so every per step key is a stacked array of shape `(T, E, ...)` and the module carries no branch that asks which era a dump belongs to. Tolerance of absence remains and is a different property, since a robot without a sole supplies no clearance channel and a task without a clock supplies no gait command, so a channel the dump does not carry resolves to `None` and the statistics depending on it are omitted and reported as omitted rather than defaulted or raised over. `rewards.npy` is exempt from that tolerance, being a mandatory member of a dump rather than a robot dependent channel, and its absence is an error.

It must be episode aware. Statistics that accumulate across time are computed within episode segments, and the segmentation falls back to a base position discontinuity heuristic where the `episode_dones` channel is absent.

It must produce a flat record set rather than a nested structure, since the destination is a two index table and a nested dictionary would have to be flattened by every consumer independently.

#### 4.3.2 The container and the generic helpers

```python
"""Statistics for legged locomotion play dumps.

Computes the descriptive and derived statistics by which a legged locomotion policy is
evaluated and compared against another, from the numpy dump that scripts/rsl_rl/play.py
writes. Pure numpy, with no dependency on Isaac Lab, on torch or on the task package, so
that it runs inside the simulation container and equally in a plain interpreter against a
stored dump, which is what makes it testable without a simulator.

Imported by scripts/rsl_rl/play.py and by nothing else. The record set is computed once,
at the close of a play, by the process that holds the arrays it describes, and written to
statistics.npy beside dump.npy and rewards.npy. Every downstream consumer reads that file
and none recomputes from it, so a figure cannot drift from the data it was taken from.

The output is a flat list of records, each carrying a group, a quantity, a statistic, a
value and a unit, which pivots directly into a table indexed by quantity and statistic.

Provenance. Every reference implementation here was validated against the play dumps of
sd_brs1_flat/2026-07-28_06-37-24 and sd_brs1_flat/2026-08-03_11-19-11 and reproduces the
figures recorded in the twentieth, twenty first and twenty second passes of
/ws/context/brs_gait.md. The validated correspondences are tabulated in
/ws/context/gait_metrics.md and are the regression gate for any change here.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from dataclasses import dataclass, field

import numpy as np

# Percentiles reported for every scalar summary. The request names these six and the
# record uses the 99th throughout, the traces being impulse trains whose mean understates
# the demand by an order of magnitude, so the upper tail is where the information is.
PERCENTILES = (5, 25, 50, 75, 90, 99)

# Force in newtons above which a body counts as in contact. The record establishes that
# this constant is not incidental. The twentieth pass used a different value and reported
# a touchdown velocity of 1.68 m/s where the twenty first, at 1.0 N, reports 1.551 for the
# same run. Any figure quoted from this module must therefore quote the threshold with it.
CONTACT_FORCE_THRESHOLD = 1.0

# Window, in seconds, over which the peak contact force following a touchdown is taken.
# 0.08 s is the value the twenty first and twenty second passes used and is long enough to
# span the impact transient while excluding the steady stance load that follows it.
IMPACT_WINDOW_S = 0.08

# Tolerance, in radians, within which a joint counts as standing at a mechanical limit,
# and the fraction of the effort ceiling above which an actuator counts as saturated.
# Both are the values of the twentieth pass per joint table.
LIMIT_TOLERANCE_RAD = 0.02
EFFORT_SATURATION_FRACTION = 0.98

GRAVITY = 9.81


@dataclass
class GaitData:
    """A play dump, resolved into named arrays.

    Every per step channel has shape (T, E, ...) where T is the number of control steps
    and E the number of environments, which is the shape the dump itself carries, so
    nothing here reshapes or reindexes what it reads. Optional channels are None where the
    robot or the task does not supply them, a sole clearance on a point footed robot or a
    gait command on a task with no clock, and every consumer must test for that rather
    than assume presence.
    """

    dt: float
    joint_names: list[str]
    body_names: list[str]
    feet_names: list[str]

    joint_positions: np.ndarray          # (T, E, J)
    joint_velocities: np.ndarray         # (T, E, J)
    joint_torques: np.ndarray            # (T, E, J)
    joint_accelerations: np.ndarray      # (T, E, J)
    joint_powers: np.ndarray             # (T, E, J)

    base_com_position: np.ndarray        # (T, E, 3), world frame
    base_linear_velocity: np.ndarray     # (T, E, 3), base frame
    base_angular_velocity: np.ndarray    # (T, E, 3), base frame
    commanded_linear_velocity: np.ndarray   # (T, E, 2), base frame
    commanded_angular_velocity: np.ndarray  # (T, E), yaw rate

    feet_contact_forces: np.ndarray      # (T, E, F, 3), world frame
    feet_velocities: np.ndarray          # (T, E, F, 3), world frame
    feet_frame_heights: np.ndarray       # (T, E, F), world frame

    body_mass: float                     # total, kilograms

    # Optional, absent where the robot, the task or the environment supplies no such
    # quantity, a sole on a point foot, a clock on a task without one, a termination
    # manager on an environment that carries none.
    base_quaternion: np.ndarray | None = None            # (T, E, 4), wxyz
    base_projected_gravity: np.ndarray | None = None     # (T, E, 3), base frame
    feet_quaternions: np.ndarray | None = None           # (T, E, F, 4), wxyz
    feet_sole_clearances: np.ndarray | None = None       # (T, E, F)
    feet_distance: np.ndarray | None = None              # (T, E, 3), signed, foot0 - foot1
    gait_command: np.ndarray | None = None               # (T, E, 4), freq/offset/dur/height
    episode_dones: np.ndarray | None = None              # (T, E), bool
    episode_terminated: np.ndarray | None = None         # (T, E), bool
    episode_time_outs: np.ndarray | None = None          # (T, E), bool

    joint_effort_limits: np.ndarray | None = None        # (J,)
    joint_velocity_limits: np.ndarray | None = None      # (J,)
    joint_position_limits: np.ndarray | None = None      # (J, 2)
    joint_soft_position_limits: np.ndarray | None = None # (J, 2)
    default_joint_pos: np.ndarray | None = None          # (J,)

    # Depth of the contact sensor's force history, in physics steps, from which the
    # provenance of feet_contact_forces follows. A value above zero means the channel
    # carries the force at the history peak, which is the quantity the impact reward
    # prices.
    contact_force_history_length: int = 0

    rewards: dict[str, np.ndarray] = field(default_factory=dict)   # name -> (T, E) RATE
    reward_weights: dict[str, float] = field(default_factory=dict)

    @property
    def num_steps(self) -> int:
        return self.joint_positions.shape[0]

    @property
    def num_envs(self) -> int:
        return self.joint_positions.shape[1]

    @property
    def num_joints(self) -> int:
        return self.joint_positions.shape[2]

    @property
    def num_feet(self) -> int:
        return self.feet_frame_heights.shape[2]

    @property
    def body_weight(self) -> float:
        """Weight in newtons, the denominator of every force normalisation."""
        return self.body_mass * GRAVITY

    @property
    def sole_clearance(self) -> np.ndarray:

        The distinction is not cosmetic. The twenty first pass established that on a sole
        footed robot the frame height overestimates the true sole clearance by 23.5 mm on
        average and 74.4 mm at the 95th percentile, so a gate or a percentile computed on
        the frame is a different quantity. Where only the frame is available the caller is
        told so by `has_sole_clearance`, and every dependent record is labelled accordingly.

        CAUTION, recorded because the defect it guards against is deliberately left
        standing. play.py applies the SD_BRS1 sole table to whatever robot is played, the
        guard at play.py:299 testing a table literal rather than the configured global, so
        the PRESENCE of this channel is not evidence that the robot has a sole. Trust it
        on SD_BRS1 and read it as the frame height on anything else. See section 1.3.
        """
        if self.feet_sole_clearances is not None:
            return self.feet_sole_clearances
        return self.feet_frame_heights

    @property
    def has_sole_clearance(self) -> bool:
        return self.feet_sole_clearances is not None

    @property
    def has_peak_contact_force(self) -> bool:
        """Whether feet_contact_forces carries the history peak or the instantaneous force."""
        return self.contact_force_history_length > 0
```

The loader reads every channel exactly as the dump stores it. Section 4.2.7 having made every per step key a stacked array of shape `(T, E, ...)`, there is no leading axis to strip, no key that is special, and no branch inside the loader that a reader must hold in mind while reading a statistic that depends on it. The single concession to absence is that a key the dump does not carry, or carries empty, resolves to `None`, so a dependent statistic is omitted rather than computed over a zero length array. The reward dump is not among the things that may be absent. It accompanies every `dump.npy` by the construction of section 4.2.4, the whole budget family depends upon it, and a `GaitData` built without it would report the absence of a family that is in fact present on disk, so the loader requires it and says so by raising.

```python
def from_dump(dump: dict, rewards: dict, dt: float | None = None) -> GaitData:
    """Build a GaitData from a loaded dump.npy and its loaded rewards.npy.

    Args:
        dump: The dictionary saved as dump.npy, every per step key an array (T, E, ...).
        rewards: The dictionary saved as rewards.npy, every term an array (T, E) of
            RATES per second, carrying the logged weights under "_weights". MANDATORY.
            Every play writes this file beside dump.npy, so a caller that cannot supply
            it is holding an incomplete dump and must be told rather than accommodated.
        dt: Control period, overriding the one the dump records. Supply it from the run's
            own params/env.yaml through experiment_params.step_dt, since assuming 0.01 is
            wrong for half the tasks here.

    Raises:
        ValueError: If rewards is None, which means rewards.npy was missing or unread.
    """
    if rewards is None:
        raise ValueError(
            "rewards.npy is a mandatory member of a play dump and was not supplied. "
            "Every play writes it beside dump.npy, so its absence is a broken dump "
            "rather than an optional channel, and the reward budget statistics cannot "
            "be computed without it."
        )

    def series(key: str) -> np.ndarray | None:
        """A channel as stored, cast to float64, or None where the dump lacks it."""
        value = dump.get(key)
        if value is None:
            return None
        array = np.asarray(value, dtype=np.float64)
        return array if array.size else None

    def names(key: str) -> list[str]:
        return [str(n) for n in np.asarray(dump.get(key, [])).ravel()]

    masses = series("body_masses")
    if masses is None:
        # Fall back to the per step morphology channel, which every dump carries. Its
        # shape is (T, E, B), so the first step of the first environment is the row of
        # per body masses, and their sum is the articulation mass.
        per_step = series("robot_mass")
        masses = per_step[0, 0] if per_step is not None else None

    # Reserved keys are namespaced with a leading underscore and are not term series.

        if not k.startswith("_")
    }

    return GaitData(
        dt=float(dt if dt is not None else dump.get("step_dt", 0.01)),
        joint_names=names("joint_names"),
        body_names=names("body_names"),
        feet_names=names("feet_names"),
        joint_positions=series("joint_positions"),
        joint_velocities=series("joint_velocities"),
        joint_torques=series("joint_torques"),
        joint_accelerations=series("joint_accelerations"),

        base_com_position=series("base_com_position"),
        base_linear_velocity=series("base_linear_velocity"),
        base_angular_velocity=series("base_angular_velocity"),
```

The record and the summary helper are the two constructions on which every family below rests, and their uniformity is what makes the resulting table readable, since a reader who has learned to read one row has learned to read all of them.

```python
def record(group: str, quantity: str, statistic: str, value, unit: str = "") -> dict:
    """One cell of the output table."""
    return {
        "group": group,
        "quantity": quantity,
        "statistic": statistic,
        "value": float(value) if np.isscalar(value) or np.ndim(value) == 0 else np.nan,
        "unit": unit,
    }


def summarise(group: str, quantity: str, values, unit: str = "",
              percentiles=PERCENTILES, absolute: bool = False) -> list[dict]:
    """The uniform descriptive set applied to every scalar quantity.

    Mean, standard deviation, median, the six percentiles, the minimum, the maximum and
    the root mean square. The last is included because the record quotes it throughout for
    torque and for tracking error, and because for a zero mean oscillation it is the only
    one of the three central measures that does not vanish. `absolute` takes the magnitude
    first, which is the correct reading for a signed quantity whose sign alternates by
    construction, a joint torque or a foot velocity, where the mean of the signed series
    reports the drift and not the demand.
    """
    values = np.asarray(values, dtype=np.float64)
    values = values[np.isfinite(values)]
    if values.size == 0:
        return [record(group, quantity, "count", 0, "")]
    if absolute:
        values = np.abs(values)
    out = [
        record(group, quantity, "mean", np.mean(values), unit),
        record(group, quantity, "std", np.std(values), unit),
        record(group, quantity, "median", np.median(values), unit),
    ]
    for p in percentiles:
        out.append(record(group, quantity, f"p{p}", np.percentile(values, p), unit))
    out += [
        record(group, quantity, "min", np.min(values), unit),
        record(group, quantity, "max", np.max(values), unit),
        record(group, quantity, "rms", np.sqrt(np.mean(np.square(values))), unit),
        record(group, quantity, "count", values.size, ""),
    ]
    return out


def _correlation(a, b) -> float:
    """Pearson correlation, returning nan rather than raising on a degenerate input."""
    a, b = np.asarray(a, dtype=np.float64).ravel(), np.asarray(b, dtype=np.float64).ravel()
    mask = np.isfinite(a) & np.isfinite(b)
    if mask.sum() < 2 or np.std(a[mask]) == 0 or np.std(b[mask]) == 0:
        return float("nan")
    return float(np.corrcoef(a[mask], b[mask])[0, 1])


def _yaw_from_quaternion(quat: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
    """Yaw angle from a wxyz quaternion array of shape (..., 4)."""
    w, x, y, z = quat[..., 0], quat[..., 1], quat[..., 2], quat[..., 3]
    return np.arctan2(2.0 * (w * z + x * y), 1.0 - 2.0 * (y * y + z * z))


def episode_segments(data: GaitData) -> list[tuple[int, int, int]]:
    """Contiguous (env, start, stop) segments free of any reset.

    A reset teleports the base, resamples the command and clears every contact
    accumulator, so a cycle period or a position error spanning one is meaningless.
    Where the dump carries `episode_dones` the boundaries are exact, which is every dump
    an environment with a termination manager produces. Where it does not, the getattr
    guard of section 4.2.3 having found no such manager, they are inferred from a base
    position discontinuity, a threshold of 0.2 m being far above the 0.03 m a single
    control step can produce at any plausible speed and far below the 19.5 m observed at
    a real reset.
    """
    T, E = data.num_steps, data.num_envs
    if data.episode_dones is not None:
        boundaries = np.asarray(data.episode_dones, dtype=bool)
    else:
        jump = np.linalg.norm(np.diff(data.base_com_position, axis=0), axis=-1)
        boundaries = np.zeros((T, E), dtype=bool)
        boundaries[1:] = jump > 0.2
    segments = []
    for e in range(E):
        edges = [0] + (np.flatnonzero(boundaries[:, e]) + 1).tolist() + [T]
        for start, stop in zip(edges[:-1], edges[1:]):
            if stop - start >= 2:
                segments.append((e, start, stop))
    return segments
```

#### 4.3.3 The contact primitive, on which six families depend

Every temporal, kinetic and swing statistic reduces to one boolean array and the events derived from it, so the primitive is defined once and shared.

```python
def contact_state(data: GaitData, threshold: float = CONTACT_FORCE_THRESHOLD) -> np.ndarray:
    """Per foot contact indicator of shape (T, E, F)."""
    return np.linalg.norm(data.feet_contact_forces, axis=-1) > threshold


def touchdown_events(contact: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
    """Indices (t, e, f) of every rising edge, t being the first step in contact."""
    rising = contact[1:] & ~contact[:-1]
    idx = np.argwhere(rising)
    idx[:, 0] += 1
    return idx


def swing_segments(contact: np.ndarray) -> list[tuple[int, int, int, int]]:
    """Complete airborne intervals as (env, foot, start, stop), half open in stop.

    Only intervals bounded by a lift off and a touchdown are returned, so a foot airborne
    at the first or the last step of the dump contributes nothing, which is correct
    because its apex and its duration are both unobserved.
    """
    out = []
    T, E, F = contact.shape
    for e in range(E):
        for f in range(F):
            air = ~contact[:, e, f]
            starts = np.flatnonzero((~air[:-1]) & air[1:]) + 1
            stops = np.flatnonzero(air[:-1] & (~air[1:])) + 1
            for start in starts:
                later = stops[stops > start]
                if later.size:
                    out.append((e, f, int(start), int(later[0])))
    return out
```

#### 4.3.4 Temporal gait statistics

```python
def temporal_statistics(data: GaitData) -> list[dict]:
    """Cadence, duty factor, support phase occupancy and their variability.

    These are the coarsest description under which two gaits compare at all, and the
    record supplies the demonstration that they are not optional.
    """
    group = "temporal"
    contact = contact_state(data)
    n_in_contact = contact.sum(axis=-1)
    out = [
        record(group, "double support", "fraction",
               100.0 * np.mean(n_in_contact == 2), "pct"),
        record(group, "single support", "fraction",
               100.0 * np.mean(n_in_contact == 1), "pct"),
        record(group, "flight", "fraction",
               100.0 * np.mean(n_in_contact == 0), "pct"),
    ]
    # Duty factor per foot, the fraction of the cycle a given foot is loaded. A walk sits
    # above one half by construction and a run below it, so the value separates the two
    # gait classes without reference to speed.
    for f, name in enumerate(data.feet_names or range(data.num_feet)):
        out.append(record(group, f"stance duty {name}", "fraction",
                          100.0 * np.mean(contact[:, :, f]), "pct"))

    # Cycle period, taken between successive touchdowns of the SAME foot and restricted to
    # intervals free of a reset. The median and the mean must both be reported. The
    # twentieth pass records a median of exactly 1.000 s against a mean of 0.929, the
    # difference being a tail of short cycles whose tenth percentile lies at 0.720, which
    # is the signature of a stumble or of a contact breaking and re-forming within one
    # phase, and neither statistic alone reveals it.
    valid = np.zeros((data.num_steps, data.num_envs), dtype=bool)
    for e, start, stop in episode_segments(data):
        valid[start:stop, e] = True
    periods, stance_times, swing_times = [], [], []
    for e in range(data.num_envs):
        for f in range(data.num_feet):
            td = np.flatnonzero(contact[1:, e, f] & ~contact[:-1, e, f]) + 1
            td = td[valid[td, e]]
            if td.size > 1:
                gaps = np.diff(td) * data.dt
                # Drop any interval spanning a reset.
                keep = [
                    i for i, (a, b) in enumerate(zip(td[:-1], td[1:]))
                    if valid[a:b, e].all()
                ]
                periods.extend(gaps[keep].tolist())
    for e, f, start, stop in swing_segments(contact):
        if valid[start:stop, e].all():
            swing_times.append((stop - start) * data.dt)
    out += summarise(group, "cycle period", periods, "s")
    out += summarise(group, "swing duration", swing_times, "s")
    if periods:
        periods_arr = np.asarray(periods)
        out.append(record(group, "cadence", "steps per second",
                          2.0 / np.median(periods_arr) if np.median(periods_arr) else np.nan,
                          "1/s"))
        # Stride time variability. The clinical result grounding it is that this quantity
        # predicts falls where the mean stride time does not [16], and it answers a
        # question no percentile answers, whether the gait is a limit cycle or a sequence
        # of recoveries.
        out.append(record(group, "cycle period", "coefficient of variation",
                          100.0 * np.std(periods_arr) / np.mean(periods_arr), "pct"))

    # Commanded schedule, where a gait clock exists. The identity is exact for an anti
    # phase offset of one half, the stance duty per foot equalling the duration and the
    # double support fraction equalling 2 * duration - 1, which the twenty first pass
    # established and which corrects the 19.6 percent of the twentieth pass to 20.0.
    if data.gait_command is not None and data.gait_command.shape[-1] >= 3:
        duration = float(np.mean(data.gait_command[..., 2]))
        offset = float(np.mean(data.gait_command[..., 1]))
        out.append(record(group, "commanded stance duty", "value", 100.0 * duration, "pct"))
        if abs(offset - 0.5) < 1e-6:
            out.append(record(group, "commanded double support", "value",
                              100.0 * (2.0 * duration - 1.0), "pct"))
        out.append(record(group, "commanded frequency", "value",
                          float(np.mean(data.gait_command[..., 0])), "Hz"))
    return out
```

#### 4.3.5 Contact event and impact statistics

```python
def contact_event_statistics(data: GaitData) -> list[dict]:
    """Touchdown velocities, peak forces, loading rates and impulses.

    Two velocity measures must both be reported and must not be confused. The frame
    vertical velocity is what the superseded foot_landing_vel charged, and the sole
    approach velocity is what the collision is actually governed by, the two differing by
    the rotational term.

    The force channel is the per step maximum over the contact sensor's history, which is
    the quantity the impact reward prices, wherever the sensor declares a history. Where
    it declares none the sensor falls back to the instantaneous force and the channel
    reduces to what it was before section 4.2.5. The two are not interchangeable, the
    latter understating the former by a factor the twenty first pass measured at twelve,
    so the provenance is reported beside the figures through the history depth rather than
    left to be inferred.
    """
    group = "impact"
    contact = contact_state(data)
    events = touchdown_events(contact)
    window = max(1, int(round(IMPACT_WINDOW_S / data.dt)))
    force_norm = np.linalg.norm(data.feet_contact_forces, axis=-1)
    clearance = data.sole_clearance
    descent = -(clearance[1:] - clearance[:-1]) / data.dt   # positive downward

    frame_vz, sole_vz, peaks, rates, impulses = [], [], [], [], []
    for t, e, f in events:
        frame_vz.append(abs(data.feet_velocities[t - 1, e, f, 2]))
        sole_vz.append(descent[t - 1, e, f])
        segment = force_norm[t:t + window, e, f]
        if segment.size:
            peaks.append(segment.max())
            # Loading rate, the rise of the force through the first hump divided by the
            # time it took. A collision and a weight acceptance may reach the same peak
            # and are told apart only here, the record measuring 733 body weights per
            # second for a gait whose peak alone read merely high.
            rise = int(np.argmax(segment))
            if rise > 0:
                rates.append(segment.max() / (rise * data.dt))
        # Impulse over the following stance, the time integral of the normal force, which
        # must equal the momentum change the step requires and therefore serves as a
        # conservation check on the contact model. CAVEAT, from section 4.2.5 onward the
        # summand is the per step MAXIMUM over the force history rather than a sample of
        # it, so this over-reads the true impulse wherever the force varies within a
        # control step, most of all through the collision itself. It remains valid as a
        # comparison between two runs measured the same way and as a bound above, and it
        # is not a substitute for the momentum balance a dedicated instantaneous channel
        # would permit. Recorded rather than repaired, since restoring the instantaneous
        # force would mean a second channel of the same size for one statistic.
        stance_end = t
        while stance_end < contact.shape[0] and contact[stance_end, e, f]:
            stance_end += 1
        impulses.append(force_norm[t:stance_end, e, f].sum() * data.dt)

    bw = data.body_weight
    out = summarise(group, "touchdown frame vertical speed", frame_vz, "m/s")
    out += summarise(group, "touchdown sole approach speed", sole_vz, "m/s")
    out += summarise(group, "peak contact force", np.asarray(peaks) / bw, "BW")
    out += summarise(group, "loading rate", np.asarray(rates) / bw, "BW/s")
    out += summarise(group, "stance impulse", np.asarray(impulses) / bw, "BW·s")
    if frame_vz and sole_vz:
        # The ratio itself, since it is the signature of the rotational substitution.
        out.append(record(group, "sole to frame speed ratio", "mean",
                          np.mean(sole_vz) / np.mean(np.abs(frame_vz)), ""))
    out.append(record(group, "touchdowns", "count", len(events), ""))
    # The provenance of every force figure above, stated as a record so that it travels
    # with the numbers into the comparison table rather than living in a footnote.
    out.append(record(group, "contact force history depth", "steps",
                      data.contact_force_history_length, "physics steps"))
    return out
```

#### 4.3.6 Swing profile statistics

```python
def swing_profile_statistics(data: GaitData, samples: int = 21) -> list[dict]:
    """Apex, apex position, peak count and correlation against a raised cosine."""
    group = "swing"
    contact = contact_state(data)
    clearance = data.sole_clearance
    phi = np.linspace(0.0, 1.0, samples)
    reference = np.sin(np.pi * phi) ** 2

    apex, apex_pos, peaks, corr, profiles = [], [], [], [], []
    for e, f, start, stop in swing_segments(contact):
        profile = clearance[start:stop, e, f]
        if profile.size < 6:
            continue
        apex.append(profile.max())
        apex_pos.append(np.argmax(profile) / (profile.size - 1))
        interior = profile[1:-1]
        n_peaks = int(((interior > profile[:-2]) & (interior >= profile[2:])).sum())
        peaks.append(max(n_peaks, 1))
        resampled = np.interp(phi, np.linspace(0.0, 1.0, profile.size), profile)
        profiles.append(resampled)
        corr.append(_correlation(resampled, reference))

    out = summarise(group, "swing apex clearance", apex, "m")
    out += summarise(group, "swing apex position", apex_pos, "fraction")
    out += summarise(group, "clearance peaks per swing", peaks, "count")
    out += summarise(group, "raised cosine correlation", corr, "")
    if profiles:
        mean_profile = np.mean(profiles, axis=0)
        # The plateau residue a set point kernel cannot remove, which is what sizes the
        # width of a reference tracking kernel. The twenty second pass measures the foot
        # spending 65.6 percent of its swing within one standard deviation of the set
        # point where the reference spends 46.0.
        out.append(record(group, "swing profile", "fraction above 90 pct of apex",
                          100.0 * np.mean(mean_profile >= 0.9 * mean_profile.max()), "pct"))
        scale = mean_profile.max() if mean_profile.max() > 0 else 1.0
        out.append(record(group, "swing profile", "rms error against raised cosine",
                          float(np.sqrt(np.mean((mean_profile - scale * reference) ** 2))),
                          "m"))
    out.append(record(group, "clearance source",
                      "is true sole" if data.has_sole_clearance else "is frame proxy",
                      1, ""))
    return out
```

#### 4.3.7 Joint statistics and actuator margins

```python
def joint_statistics(data: GaitData) -> list[dict]:
    """Per joint and aggregate moments, percentiles, peaks and margins.

    Reported both per joint and in aggregate, because the two answer different questions.
    The aggregate says what the machine costs and the per joint says which actuator pays.
    The percentiles are taken on the MAGNITUDE, because every one of these
    quantities alternates in sign by construction, so the mean of the signed series
    reports the drift and the mean of the magnitude reports the demand.
    """
    group = "joints"
    channels = (
        ("joint velocity", data.joint_velocities, "rad/s", data.joint_velocity_limits),
        ("joint torque", data.joint_torques, "N·m", data.joint_effort_limits),
        ("joint power", data.joint_powers, "W", None),
        ("joint acceleration", data.joint_accelerations, "rad/s^2", None),
        ("joint position", data.joint_positions, "rad", None),
    )
    names = data.joint_names or [f"joint {j}" for j in range(data.num_joints)]
    out = []
    for label, series, unit, ceiling in channels:
        if series is None:
            continue
        # Aggregate over every joint and every step.
        out += summarise(group, f"{label}, all joints", series, unit, absolute=True)
        # The peak of the per step sum, which is the instantaneous whole body demand and
        # is not recoverable from the per joint percentiles, those being taken
        # independently and therefore never coincident.
        out += summarise(group, f"{label}, whole body sum",
                         np.abs(series).sum(axis=-1), unit)
        for j, name in enumerate(names):
            column = series[:, :, j]
            out.append(record(group, f"{label} {name}", "mean absolute",
                              np.mean(np.abs(column)), unit))
            out.append(record(group, f"{label} {name}", "std", np.std(column), unit))
            out.append(record(group, f"{label} {name}", "median absolute",
                              np.median(np.abs(column)), unit))
            for p in PERCENTILES:
                out.append(record(group, f"{label} {name}", f"p{p} absolute",
                                  np.percentile(np.abs(column), p), unit))
            out.append(record(group, f"{label} {name}", "peak", np.max(np.abs(column)), unit))
            out.append(record(group, f"{label} {name}", "range",
                              np.max(column) - np.min(column), unit))
            if ceiling is not None and j < len(ceiling) and ceiling[j] > 0:
                # Saturation. An absolute penalty cannot see this, since the same torque
                # is unremarkable at a hip and saturating at an ankle, which is the
                # argument for Booster Gym's torque tiredness term [19].
                out.append(record(
                    group, f"{label} {name}", "p99 as fraction of ceiling",
                    100.0 * np.percentile(np.abs(column), 99) / ceiling[j], "pct"))
                out.append(record(
                    group, f"{label} {name}", "fraction above 98 pct of ceiling",
                    100.0 * np.mean(np.abs(column) > EFFORT_SATURATION_FRACTION * ceiling[j]),
                    "pct"))
                out.append(record(
                    group, f"{label} {name}", "fraction exceeding ceiling",
                    100.0 * np.mean(np.abs(column) > ceiling[j]), "pct"))

    # Limit residence. A policy that discovers a mechanical stop is a cheap source of
    # support will lean on it, and the twentieth pass finds the knee within 0.02 rad of a
    # limit for 37 percent of steps with a distribution so bimodal that only 15 percent
    # falls in the intermediate band. The stop is not a joint being controlled, it is a
    # joint being switched between two constraints.
    if data.joint_position_limits is not None:
        lower, upper = data.joint_position_limits[:, 0], data.joint_position_limits[:, 1]
        for j, name in enumerate(names):
            column = data.joint_positions[:, :, j]
            at_limit = (
                (column - lower[j] < LIMIT_TOLERANCE_RAD)
                | (upper[j] - column < LIMIT_TOLERANCE_RAD)
            )
            out.append(record(group, f"joint position {name}", "fraction at limit",
                              100.0 * np.mean(at_limit), "pct"))
            out.append(record(group, f"joint position {name}", "fraction beyond limit",
                              100.0 * np.mean((column < lower[j]) | (column > upper[j])),
                              "pct"))
    return out
```

The excursion beyond a declared hard limit deserves its own note, since it is not a policy behaviour but a solver artefact. The twentieth pass records the ankle pitch reaching 0.619 radians against a declared limit of 0.454 and both ankles exceeding their 25 radian per second velocity ceilings, and attributes it to the position solver yielding at a `solver_position_iteration_count` of two under loads of that size. A nonzero value in the fraction exceeding a ceiling is therefore a signal about the simulation rather than about the gait, and it belongs in the table for exactly that reason.

#### 4.3.8 Foot statistics

```python
def foot_statistics(data: GaitData) -> list[dict]:
    """Per foot forces, velocities, clearances and orientations, in stance and in swing.

    Every quantity is reported three ways, over all steps, over the steps in contact and
    over the steps airborne, because the aggregate conceals the distinction that matters.
    A horizontal foot speed of 3.40 m/s at the 99th percentile is a swing property and a
    horizontal foot speed of 0.158 m/s is a stance property, and the first is the tanh
    factor of the clearance reward being farmed while the second is the slide penalty.
    """
    group = "feet"
    contact = contact_state(data)
    names = data.feet_names or [f"foot {f}" for f in range(data.num_feet)]
    force = data.feet_contact_forces
    velocity = data.feet_velocities
    bw = data.body_weight
    out = []
    for f, name in enumerate(names):
        in_contact = contact[:, :, f]
        airborne = ~in_contact
        channels = (
            ("contact force magnitude", np.linalg.norm(force[:, :, f], axis=-1) / bw, "BW"),
            ("contact force vertical", force[:, :, f, 2] / bw, "BW"),
            ("contact force horizontal",
             np.linalg.norm(force[:, :, f, :2], axis=-1) / bw, "BW"),
            ("contact force x", force[:, :, f, 0] / bw, "BW"),
            ("contact force y", force[:, :, f, 1] / bw, "BW"),
            ("foot speed horizontal",
             np.linalg.norm(velocity[:, :, f, :2], axis=-1), "m/s"),
            ("foot velocity vertical", velocity[:, :, f, 2], "m/s"),
            ("foot velocity x", velocity[:, :, f, 0], "m/s"),
            ("foot velocity y", velocity[:, :, f, 1], "m/s"),
            ("frame height", data.feet_frame_heights[:, :, f], "m"),
            ("sole clearance", data.sole_clearance[:, :, f], "m"),
        )
        for label, series, unit in channels:
            out += summarise(group, f"{label} {name}", series, unit)
            out += summarise(group, f"{label} {name}, in stance", series[in_contact], unit)
            out += summarise(group, f"{label} {name}, in swing", series[airborne], unit)
        # Slip, the horizontal distance a loaded foot travels, which is the quantity
        # feet_slide prices and which is also the stance pivot a flat footed biped needs
        # in order to turn, so it must not be driven to zero.
        slip = np.linalg.norm(velocity[:, :, f, :2], axis=-1) * in_contact * data.dt
        out.append(record(group, f"stance slip distance {name}", "mean per stance step",
                          np.sum(slip) / max(in_contact.sum(), 1), "m"))
        # The frame to sole discrepancy, which is the tilt the frame height cannot
        # distinguish from a lift, and which the twenty first pass measured at 23.5 mm.
        if data.has_sole_clearance:
            out += summarise(group, f"frame minus sole clearance {name}",
                             data.feet_frame_heights[:, :, f]
                             - data.feet_sole_clearances[:, :, f], "m")
        # Forged contact, a foot reporting force while its sole is demonstrably clear of
        # the ground. This is the ninth pass signature, and it was caused by a shank into
        # foot interpenetration in the mesh asset which silenced every contact keyed term
        # in the reward set for eight passes before it was found.
        if data.has_sole_clearance:
            forged = in_contact & (data.feet_sole_clearances[:, :, f] > 0.01)
            out.append(record(group, f"forged contact {name}", "fraction",
                              100.0 * np.mean(forged), "pct"))
        if data.feet_quaternions is not None:
            quat = data.feet_quaternions[:, :, f]
            w, x, y, z = quat[..., 0], quat[..., 1], quat[..., 2], quat[..., 3]
            roll = np.arctan2(2 * (w * x + y * z), 1 - 2 * (x * x + y * y))
            pitch = np.arcsin(np.clip(2 * (w * y - z * x), -1.0, 1.0))
            out += summarise(group, f"foot roll {name}", roll, "rad", absolute=True)
            out += summarise(group, f"foot pitch {name}", pitch, "rad", absolute=True)
            out += summarise(group, f"foot roll {name}, in stance", roll[in_contact],
                             "rad", absolute=True)
            if data.base_quaternion is not None:
                # Foot yaw alignment, the term Booster Gym carries at twice its linear
                # tracking weight and which is absent from the SD_BRS1 set [19].
                misalignment = np.arctan2(
                    np.sin(_yaw_from_quaternion(quat) - _yaw_from_quaternion(data.base_quaternion)),
                    np.cos(_yaw_from_quaternion(quat) - _yaw_from_quaternion(data.base_quaternion)),
                )
                out += summarise(group, f"foot yaw misalignment {name}", misalignment,
                                 "rad", absolute=True)
    return out
```

```python
def feet_distance_statistics(data: GaitData) -> list[dict]:
    """Signed per axis separation, its norms, and the correlations among them.

    The decisive statistic here is the LATERAL component alone and not the planar norm.
    The twentieth pass established that a hinge on the planar separation is satisfied by a
    long stride while the lateral separation is exactly zero, the feet crossing the
    midline beneath it, and the twenty second measured the substitution directly, a
    lateral mean of 0.206 m against a planar mean of 0.316 with the hinge reporting
    compliance at a 0.25 m threshold. Any configuration adopting such a term must confirm
    which axis it measures before trusting it to regulate stance width.
    """
    group = "stance"
    if data.feet_distance is None:
        return []
    separation = data.feet_distance
    fore_aft, lateral, vertical = separation[..., 0], separation[..., 1], separation[..., 2]
    planar = np.linalg.norm(separation[..., :2], axis=-1)
    total = np.linalg.norm(separation, axis=-1)
    out = []
    for label, series in (
        ("fore and aft separation", fore_aft),
        ("lateral separation", lateral),
        ("vertical separation", vertical),
    ):
        out += summarise(group, label, series, "m")
        out += summarise(group, f"{label} magnitude", series, "m", absolute=True)
    out += summarise(group, "planar separation", planar, "m")
    out += summarise(group, "total separation", total, "m")
    # The pairwise correlations, which say whether the axes trade against one another.
    # A strongly negative correlation between the lateral and the fore and aft magnitudes
    # is the substitution above, made visible without any threshold being assumed.
    pairs = (
        ("fore and aft against lateral", fore_aft, lateral),
        ("fore and aft against vertical", fore_aft, vertical),
        ("lateral against vertical", lateral, vertical),
        ("planar against vertical", planar, vertical),
        ("lateral magnitude against fore and aft magnitude",
         np.abs(lateral), np.abs(fore_aft)),
    )
    for label, a, b in pairs:
        out.append(record(group, f"separation correlation, {label}", "pearson",
                          _correlation(a, b), ""))
    return out
```

#### 4.3.10 Command tracking and odometry

```python
def tracking_statistics(data: GaitData) -> list[dict]:
    """Velocity tracking errors, correlations, and the components that should vanish.

    Six components are reported. The three commanded ones carry an error and a
    correlation, and the three uncommanded ones, the lateral linear velocity and the roll
    and pitch rates, carry a magnitude, since for those the ideal is zero and an error
    against a zero command is the magnitude itself.

    Two cautions from the record govern the reading. The yaw command is a heading
    controller output rather than a sample where `heading_command` is true, so it saturates
    at the range limit while the error persists and a robot that cannot turn accumulates a
    command it still cannot follow. And the tracking error does not converge downward while
    a curriculum is active, the reward kernel contracting and the command range widening as
    training proceeds, so runs must be compared at matched iteration counts.
    """
    group = "tracking"
    measured_lin = data.base_linear_velocity
    measured_ang = data.base_angular_velocity
    commanded_lin = data.commanded_linear_velocity
    commanded_ang = np.asarray(data.commanded_angular_velocity)
    if commanded_ang.ndim == 3:
        commanded_ang = commanded_ang[..., 0]

    out = []
    axes = (("x", 0), ("y", 1))
    for label, axis in axes:
        error = measured_lin[..., axis] - commanded_lin[..., axis]
        out += summarise(group, f"linear velocity error {label}", error, "m/s")
        out += summarise(group, f"linear velocity error {label} magnitude", error,
                         "m/s", absolute=True)
        out.append(record(group, f"linear velocity {label}", "command correlation",
                          _correlation(measured_lin[..., axis], commanded_lin[..., axis]), ""))
        out.append(record(group, f"linear velocity {label}", "commanded magnitude mean",
                          np.mean(np.abs(commanded_lin[..., axis])), "m/s"))
        out.append(record(group, f"linear velocity {label}", "achieved magnitude mean",
                          np.mean(np.abs(measured_lin[..., axis])), "m/s"))
    planar_error = np.linalg.norm(measured_lin[..., :2] - commanded_lin[..., :2], axis=-1)
    out += summarise(group, "planar velocity error", planar_error, "m/s")

    yaw_error = measured_ang[..., 2] - commanded_ang
    out += summarise(group, "yaw rate error", yaw_error, "rad/s")
    out.append(record(group, "yaw rate", "command correlation",
                      _correlation(measured_ang[..., 2], commanded_ang), ""))
    out.append(record(group, "yaw rate", "commanded magnitude mean",
                      np.mean(np.abs(commanded_ang)), "rad/s"))
    out.append(record(group, "yaw rate", "achieved magnitude mean",
                      np.mean(np.abs(measured_ang[..., 2])), "rad/s"))
    # The three that should be zero. Reporting them as magnitudes rather than as errors
    # is deliberate, since an error against a zero command IS the magnitude and naming it
    # an error invites a reader to look for a command that does not exist.
    for label, series, unit in (
        ("lateral linear velocity", measured_lin[..., 1], "m/s"),
        ("vertical linear velocity", measured_lin[..., 2], "m/s"),
        ("roll rate", measured_ang[..., 0], "rad/s"),
        ("pitch rate", measured_ang[..., 1], "rad/s"),
    ):
        out += summarise(group, f"{label} magnitude", series, unit, absolute=True)
    return out


def odometry_statistics(data: GaitData) -> list[dict]:
    """Error between the base position a commanded trajectory implies and the measured one.

    The integration is performed within an episode segment and reset at every boundary,
    because a reset teleports the base and an error accumulated across one is unbounded.
    The error is reported both in absolute metres and normalised by the distance the
    command asked for, the second being the comparable figure across runs whose command
    distributions differ.
    """
    group = "odometry"
    if data.base_quaternion is None:
        return [record(group, "expected base position error",
                       "requires base_quaternion, absent", np.nan, "m")]
    yaw = _yaw_from_quaternion(data.base_quaternion)
    errors, lateral_errors, along_errors, commanded_distance = [], [], [], []
    for e, start, stop in episode_segments(data):
        cos, sin = np.cos(yaw[start:stop, e]), np.sin(yaw[start:stop, e])
        vx, vy = data.commanded_linear_velocity[start:stop, e, 0], \
                 data.commanded_linear_velocity[start:stop, e, 1]
        # Rotate the body frame command into the world frame at the measured heading.
        world_vx, world_vy = cos * vx - sin * vy, sin * vx + cos * vy
        expected = np.cumsum(np.stack([world_vx, world_vy], axis=-1), axis=0) * data.dt
        measured = (data.base_com_position[start:stop, e, :2]
                    - data.base_com_position[start, e, :2])
        delta = measured - expected
        errors.extend(np.linalg.norm(delta, axis=-1).tolist())
        # Decomposed along and across the commanded heading, since a lag along the
        # direction of travel is a speed deficit while a drift across it is a heading
        # failure, and the two call for different remedies.
        norm = np.linalg.norm(expected, axis=-1)
        norm[norm == 0] = 1.0
        unit = expected / norm[:, None]
        along_errors.extend((delta * unit).sum(axis=-1).tolist())
        lateral_errors.extend((delta[:, 0] * -unit[:, 1] + delta[:, 1] * unit[:, 0]).tolist())
        commanded_distance.append(float(np.linalg.norm(expected[-1])))
    out = summarise(group, "expected base position error", errors, "m")
    out += summarise(group, "position error along command", along_errors, "m")
    out += summarise(group, "position error across command", lateral_errors, "m")
    if commanded_distance and np.sum(commanded_distance) > 0:
        out.append(record(group, "expected base position error",
                          "as fraction of commanded distance",
                          100.0 * np.mean(errors) / np.mean(commanded_distance), "pct"))
    out += summarise(group, "base height", data.base_com_position[..., 2], "m")
    return out
```

```python
def energetic_statistics(data: GaitData) -> list[dict]:
    """Power, cost of transport in three conventions, and the antagonism ratio.

    The three conventions diverge by an order of magnitude on a badly conditioned gait and
    must all be reported. 
    """
    group = "energetics"
    power = data.joint_powers
    absolute = np.abs(power).sum(axis=-1)
    net = power.sum(axis=-1)
    positive = np.clip(net, 0.0, None)
    speed = float(np.mean(np.linalg.norm(data.base_linear_velocity[..., :2], axis=-1)))
    denominator = data.body_weight * speed if speed > 0 else np.nan

    out = summarise(group, "absolute joint power", absolute, "W")
    out += summarise(group, "net joint power", net, "W")
    out.append(record(group, "cost of transport, absolute", "value",
                      np.mean(absolute) / denominator, ""))
    out.append(record(group, "cost of transport, net", "value",
                      np.mean(np.abs(net)) / denominator, ""))
    out.append(record(group, "cost of transport, positive", "value",
                      np.mean(positive) / denominator, ""))
    out.append(record(group, "antagonism ratio", "absolute over net",
                      np.mean(absolute) / max(np.mean(np.abs(net)), 1e-9), ""))
    out.append(record(group, "mean forward speed", "value", speed, "m/s"))
    # Pairwise opposition, which localises the antagonism to a joint pair. The twenty
    # second pass reports the hip pitch and the knee expending 170 W against one another
    # falling to 43, which is the clearest single statement of what Phase 1b bought, and
    # it is invisible in any aggregate.
    names = data.joint_names or [f"joint {j}" for j in range(data.num_joints)]
    for a in range(data.num_joints):
        for b in range(a + 1, data.num_joints):
            pa, pb = power[:, :, a], power[:, :, b]
            opposed = np.sign(pa) != np.sign(pb)
            if opposed.mean() < 0.4:
                continue
            out.append(record(group, f"opposed power, {names[a]} against {names[b]}",
                              "mean", np.mean(np.minimum(np.abs(pa), np.abs(pb)) * opposed),
                              "W"))
            out.append(record(group, f"opposed power, {names[a]} against {names[b]}",
                              "fraction of steps opposed", 100.0 * opposed.mean(), "pct"))
    # Dimensionless speed, which is what makes a cadence comparable across leg lengths.
    # The Froude number is v^2 / (g L), and human walking transitions to running near 0.5.
    leg_length = float(np.mean(data.base_com_position[..., 2]))
    if leg_length > 0:
        out.append(record(group, "Froude number", "value",
                          speed ** 2 / (GRAVITY * leg_length), ""))
    return out
```

```python
def posture_statistics(data: GaitData) -> list[dict]:
    """Torso attitude, and the three stability margins the literature prescribes."""
    group = "posture"
    out = []
    if data.base_projected_gravity is not None:

        # is exactly the quantity flat_orientation_l2 squares.
        tilt = np.arcsin(np.clip(

        out += summarise(group, "torso tilt from vertical", np.degrees(tilt), "deg")

    if data.base_quaternion is not None:
        quat = data.base_quaternion
        w, x, y, z = quat[..., 0], quat[..., 1], quat[..., 2], quat[..., 3]
        roll = np.arctan2(2 * (w * x + y * z), 1 - 2 * (x * x + y * y))
        pitch = np.arcsin(np.clip(2 * (w * y - z * x), -1.0, 1.0))
        out += summarise(group, "torso roll", np.degrees(roll), "deg", absolute=True)
        out += summarise(group, "torso pitch", np.degrees(pitch), "deg", absolute=True)
    out += summarise(group, "base height", data.base_com_position[..., 2], "m")
    out += summarise(group, "base vertical velocity", data.base_linear_velocity[..., 2],

    # Vertical centre of mass excursion across a stride, the human figure being about

    excursions = []
    for e, start, stop in episode_segments(data):
        column = data.base_com_position[start:stop, e, 2]
        if column.size > 10:

    out += summarise(group, "base height excursion per episode", excursions, "m")

def stability_statistics(data: GaitData) -> list[dict]:
    """Margin of stability, capture point excursion, and centre of pressure.

    The margin of stability of Hof, Gazendam and Sinke is the signed distance from the
    extrapolated centre of mass, being the position plus the velocity divided by the
    pendulum eigenfrequency, to the boundary of the base of support [8]. Its mediolateral
    component is the one associated with falls, and the frontal plane result that grounds
    it is that the lateral excursion required per step is half the stance width, so a
    narrow stance leaves nothing in reserve [9]. A negative value means a step is required
    to avoid a fall, which is normal during walking and pathological during standing.

    The base of support is approximated by the convex hull of the loaded feet positions,
    which for a two footed robot in single stance degenerates to a point and in double
    stance to a segment. That approximation understates the true polygon by the sole
    dimensions, which are not recorded in the dump, so the margin computed here is a lower
    bound and is labelled as such. A future extension carrying the sole offsets would
    close it.
    """
    group = "stability"
    contact = contact_state(data)
    height = np.mean(data.base_com_position[..., 2])
    omega = np.sqrt(GRAVITY / height) if height > 0 else np.nan
    # The base frame planar velocity rotated into the world frame, which requires the yaw.
    if data.base_quaternion is None:
        return [record(group, "margin of stability",
                       "requires base_quaternion, absent", np.nan, "m")]
    yaw = _yaw_from_quaternion(data.base_quaternion)
    cos, sin = np.cos(yaw), np.sin(yaw)
    vx_b, vy_b = data.base_linear_velocity[..., 0], data.base_linear_velocity[..., 1]
    vx_w, vy_w = cos * vx_b - sin * vy_b, sin * vx_b + cos * vy_b
    com = data.base_com_position[..., :2]
    # The extrapolated centre of mass, and the capture point, which under the linear
    # inverted pendulum are the same construction read backward and forward [8, 10].
    xcom = com + np.stack([vx_w, vy_w], axis=-1) / omega

    # Feet positions are not logged directly, but the signed separation and the contact
    # state together locate the support. Where the separation is absent the margin is
    # reported against the loaded foot count alone.
    margins_lateral, margins_fore_aft = [], []
    if data.feet_distance is not None:
        # Midpoint of the loaded feet as the support centroid, reconstructed from the
        # base position and the recorded separation, which is foot0 minus foot1.
        half = data.feet_distance[..., :2] * 0.5
        loaded = contact.sum(axis=-1)
        support = np.where(
            (loaded == 2)[..., None], com,
            np.where(contact[..., 0:1], com + half, com - half),
        )
        delta = xcom - support
        margins_fore_aft.extend(delta[..., 0].ravel().tolist())
        margins_lateral.extend(delta[..., 1].ravel().tolist())
    out = summarise(group, "extrapolated com offset, fore and aft", margins_fore_aft, "m")
    out += summarise(group, "extrapolated com offset, lateral", margins_lateral, "m")
    out.append(record(group, "pendulum eigenfrequency", "value", omega, "1/s"))
    # Centre of pressure under the loaded feet, the observable half of the zero moment
    # point criterion [4], computed as the force weighted mean of the loaded foot
    # positions. Its excursion measures how near the foot is to rolling onto an edge.
    force_z = np.clip(data.feet_contact_forces[..., 2], 0.0, None)
    total = force_z.sum(axis=-1)
    if data.feet_distance is not None:
        weights = np.divide(force_z, total[..., None], out=np.zeros_like(force_z),
                            where=total[..., None] > 0)
        cop_offset = (weights[..., 0] - weights[..., 1])[..., None] * half
        out += summarise(group, "centre of pressure offset, lateral",
                         cop_offset[..., 1], "m")
        out += summarise(group, "centre of pressure offset, fore and aft",
                         cop_offset[..., 0], "m")
    out.append(record(group, "base of support", "is a lower bound, sole extent unknown",
                      1, ""))
    return out
```

#### 4.3.13 Symmetry, smoothness and variability

```python
def _robinson_index(left: float, right: float) -> float:
    """Symmetry index of Robinson, Herzog and Nigg [12], as a percentage.

    Unbounded as the mean approaches zero, which is why it is applied here to magnitudes
    such as a root mean square torque or a range of motion rather than to a signed series.
    """
    denominator = 0.5 * (abs(left) + abs(right))
    if denominator == 0:
        return float("nan")
    return 100.0 * (left - right) / denominator


def symmetry_statistics(data: GaitData) -> list[dict]:
    """Left against right, on the quantities the record has found discriminating.

    Pairing is by name, a joint whose name differs from another's only in a trailing L or
    R being taken as its partner, which generalises across robots without a hardcoded map
    and degrades to reporting nothing where the naming convention differs. The record's
    use of these indices is instructive, the sagittal figures in single digits being cited
    as evidence the symmetry augmentation works while the frontal figure of 41 percent is
    cited as evidence of a lateral balance defect, so the value of the family lies in
    localising an asymmetry rather than in scoring it.
    """
    group = "symmetry"
    names = data.joint_names or []
    pairs = []
    for i, name in enumerate(names):
        if name.endswith("L"):
            partner = name[:-1] + "R"
            if partner in names:
                pairs.append((name[:-1], i, names.index(partner)))
    out = []
    for label, li, ri in pairs:
        for quantity, series in (
            ("torque", data.joint_torques),
            ("velocity", data.joint_velocities),
            ("power", data.joint_powers),
        ):
            # PER ENVIRONMENT, then averaged as a magnitude. Pooling the environments
            # before taking the index cancels the asymmetry, because the left and the
            # right pooled statistics each take the union over environments whose
            # asymmetries point in opposite directions. Measured on run
            # 2026-07-28_06-37-24, the ankle roll range of motion index reads 4.68
            # percent pooled, minus 5.17 as a signed per environment mean, and 13.4 as a
            # mean magnitude, against 14.09 in environment zero alone which is the panel
            # the plots display. The pooled figure is the one that misleads.
            per_env = [
                _robinson_index(
                    float(np.sqrt(np.mean(series[:, e, li] ** 2))),
                    float(np.sqrt(np.mean(series[:, e, ri] ** 2))),
                )
                for e in range(data.num_envs)
            ]
            per_env = np.asarray(per_env, dtype=np.float64)
            per_env = per_env[np.isfinite(per_env)]
            if per_env.size:
                out.append(record(group, f"{quantity} rms symmetry index {label}",
                                  "mean magnitude", np.mean(np.abs(per_env)), "pct"))
                out.append(record(group, f"{quantity} rms symmetry index {label}",
                                  "signed mean", np.mean(per_env), "pct"))
                out.append(record(group, f"{quantity} rms symmetry index {label}",
                                  "p99 magnitude", np.percentile(np.abs(per_env), 99), "pct"))
        # np.ptp rather than the ndarray method, the latter having been removed in
        # numpy 2.0 and the analysis environment carrying that version.
        rom = np.asarray([
            _robinson_index(float(np.ptp(data.joint_positions[:, e, li])),
                            float(np.ptp(data.joint_positions[:, e, ri])))
            for e in range(data.num_envs)
        ], dtype=np.float64)
        rom = rom[np.isfinite(rom)]
        if rom.size:
            out.append(record(group, f"range of motion symmetry index {label}",
                              "mean magnitude", np.mean(np.abs(rom)), "pct"))
            out.append(record(group, f"range of motion symmetry index {label}",
                              "signed mean", np.mean(rom), "pct"))
    # Per foot temporal and kinetic symmetry, which is where a limp shows first.
    contact = contact_state(data)
    if data.num_feet == 2:
        duties = [float(np.mean(contact[:, :, f])) for f in range(2)]
        out.append(record(group, "stance duty symmetry index", "value",
                          _robinson_index(*duties), "pct"))
        swings = [[] for _ in range(2)]
        for e, f, start, stop in swing_segments(contact):
            swings[f].append((stop - start) * data.dt)
        if all(swings):
            out.append(record(group, "swing duration symmetry index", "value",
                              _robinson_index(np.mean(swings[0]), np.mean(swings[1])), "pct"))
        peaks = [float(np.percentile(
            np.linalg.norm(data.feet_contact_forces[:, :, f], axis=-1), 99))
            for f in range(2)]
        out.append(record(group, "peak contact force symmetry index", "value",
                          _robinson_index(*peaks), "pct"))
    return out


def _spectral_arc_length(signal: np.ndarray, dt: float,
                         cutoff: float = 20.0, amplitude_threshold: float = 0.05) -> float:
    """Spectral arc length of Balasubramanian and colleagues [14].

    The arc length of the normalised Fourier magnitude spectrum of a speed profile, taken
    over the band below a cutoff and above an amplitude threshold. Returned negated by
    convention, so that a value nearer zero is smoother and a large negative value is
    rough. Preferred over a jerk based measure because it is robust to measurement noise
    and independent of duration, whereas jerk measures vary counterintuitively with both.
    """
    signal = np.asarray(signal, dtype=np.float64).ravel()
    if signal.size < 16 or np.allclose(signal, signal[0]):
        return float("nan")
    n = int(2 ** np.ceil(np.log2(signal.size)) * 4)
    spectrum = np.abs(np.fft.rfft(signal, n=n))
    frequency = np.fft.rfftfreq(n, d=dt)
    spectrum = spectrum / max(spectrum.max(), 1e-12)
    band = frequency <= cutoff
    spectrum, frequency = spectrum[band], frequency[band]
    keep = np.flatnonzero(spectrum >= amplitude_threshold)
    if keep.size < 2:
        return float("nan")
    spectrum = spectrum[keep[0]:keep[-1] + 1]
    frequency = frequency[keep[0]:keep[-1] + 1]
    df = (frequency[-1] - frequency[0]) or 1.0
    return float(-np.sum(np.sqrt((np.diff(frequency) / df) ** 2 + np.diff(spectrum) ** 2)))


def smoothness_statistics(data: GaitData) -> list[dict]:
    """Jerk, first differences, and the spectral arc length of the principal signals.

    The record describes the joint traces as impulse trains rather than continuous
    signals, with accelerations reaching 1900 rad/s^2 at the 99th percentile and torque
    changing by 470 N·m within one control step, and neither the action rate penalty nor
    the torque rate penalty measures that character against any standard a reader can
    compare. These do. A caution belongs with them, that a smoothness metric which
    improves under a reward penalty and not under policy space regularisation [15] is
    measuring the penalty rather than the gait.
    """
    group = "smoothness"
    out = []
    torque_rate = np.diff(data.joint_torques, axis=0) / data.dt
    out += summarise(group, "torque rate, all joints", torque_rate, "N·m/s", absolute=True)
    out += summarise(group, "torque first difference, all joints",
                     np.diff(data.joint_torques, axis=0), "N·m", absolute=True)
    jerk = np.diff(data.joint_accelerations, axis=0) / data.dt
    out += summarise(group, "joint jerk, all joints", jerk, "rad/s^3", absolute=True)
    # Spectral arc length of the base speed profile, computed within each episode segment
    # so that the reset discontinuity does not enter the spectrum as a broadband impulse.
    speed = np.linalg.norm(data.base_linear_velocity[..., :2], axis=-1)
    sparcs = []
    for e, start, stop in episode_segments(data):
        segment = speed[start:stop, e]
        value = _spectral_arc_length(segment, data.dt)
        if np.isfinite(value):
            sparcs.append(value)
    out += summarise(group, "spectral arc length of base speed", sparcs, "")
    names = data.joint_names or [f"joint {j}" for j in range(data.num_joints)]
    for j, name in enumerate(names):
        values = []
        for e, start, stop in episode_segments(data):
            value = _spectral_arc_length(data.joint_velocities[start:stop, e, j], data.dt)
            if np.isfinite(value):
                values.append(value)
        if values:
            out.append(record(group, f"spectral arc length {name}", "mean",
                              float(np.mean(values)), ""))
    return out


def variability_statistics(data: GaitData) -> list[dict]:
    """Stride to stride coefficients of variation, and the termination record.

    Variability answers a question the percentiles do not, whether the gait is a limit
    cycle or a sequence of recoveries [16], and the record contains exactly the
    observation it would have flagged, a cadence whose median is exactly one second while
    its tenth percentile lies at 0.720.
    """
    group = "variability"
    contact = contact_state(data)
    out = []
    swings = [[] for _ in range(data.num_feet)]
    for e, f, start, stop in swing_segments(contact):
        swings[f].append((stop - start) * data.dt)
    for f, values in enumerate(swings):
        if len(values) > 2:
            name = data.feet_names[f] if data.feet_names else f"foot {f}"
            out.append(record(group, f"swing duration {name}", "coefficient of variation",
                              100.0 * np.std(values) / max(np.mean(values), 1e-9), "pct"))
    if data.feet_distance is not None:
        lateral = np.abs(data.feet_distance[..., 1])
        out.append(record(group, "step width", "coefficient of variation",
                          100.0 * np.std(lateral) / max(np.mean(lateral), 1e-9), "pct"))
    # Episode outcomes. A policy compared on its behaviour alone is compared on the
    # episodes it survived, which is a selection effect, so the survival record belongs
    # beside every behavioural statistic. The twenty second pass records low height
    # terminations nearly doubling while every impact statistic improved.
    if data.episode_dones is not None:
        out.append(record(group, "episode ends", "count",
                          float(np.sum(data.episode_dones)), ""))
        if data.episode_terminated is not None:
            total = max(float(np.sum(data.episode_dones)), 1.0)
            out.append(record(group, "episode ends", "fraction terminated early",
                              100.0 * float(np.sum(data.episode_terminated)) / total, "pct"))
            out.append(record(group, "episode ends", "fraction timed out",
                              100.0 * float(np.sum(data.episode_time_outs)) / total, "pct"))
        lengths = [(stop - start) * data.dt for _, start, stop in episode_segments(data)]
        out += summarise(group, "episode duration", lengths, "s")
    return out
```

#### 4.3.14 The reward budget

```python
def reward_budget_statistics(data: GaitData) -> list[dict]:
    """Shares of the positive and negative budgets, moments, and unweighted values.

    THE UNIT TRAP. Every per term series in rewards.npy is a RATE PER SECOND equal to
    func x weight, because RewardManager.compute divides by dt before storing into
    _step_reward at reward_manager.py:157, whereas `total_reward` is the PER STEP value
    equal to the sum of those rates times dt. Verified on run 2026-08-03_11-19-11, the
    mean total is 0.6011 against a summed term mean of 60.115, a ratio of exactly 0.01.
    A budget that adds the two is wrong by a factor of one over the control period.

    The unweighted value, func = rate / weight, is the quantity that makes two runs with
    different weights comparable, and it is the quantity the record inverts by hand in
    every pass from the fifth onward. It requires the weight, and the weight must be the
    one the replay APPLIED rather than one assumed, because play.py has historically
    rebuilt its environment from the live source tree through parse_env_cfg so the tree's
    weight and the trained weight may differ, as they did for run 2026-07-31_10-21-10.
    The weight is therefore logged into rewards.npy under "_weights" at play time, and
    since section 4.4 configures every replay from the run's own params/env.yaml, the
    weight logged IS the weight the run was trained under except where that file was
    absent, in which case the log records the tree's weight and says so by recording what
    was applied. Where no weight is available at all the unweighted records are omitted
    and the weighted ones stand alone.
    """
    group = "rewards"
    if not data.rewards:
        return []
    terms = {k: v for k, v in data.rewards.items() if k != "total_reward"}
    if not terms:
        return []
    means = {k: float(np.mean(v)) for k, v in terms.items()}
    positive = sum(m for m in means.values() if m > 0) or 1.0
    negative = sum(m for m in means.values() if m < 0) or -1.0
    net = positive + negative

    out = [
        record(group, "positive budget", "rate", positive, "1/s"),
        record(group, "negative budget", "rate", negative, "1/s"),
        record(group, "net budget", "rate", net, "1/s"),
        record(group, "term count", "value", len(terms), ""),
    ]
    if "total_reward" in data.rewards:
        out += summarise(group, "total reward per step", data.rewards["total_reward"], "")
        out.append(record(group, "total reward", "rate",
                          float(np.mean(data.rewards["total_reward"])) / data.dt, "1/s"))
    for name, series in sorted(terms.items(), key=lambda kv: -abs(np.mean(kv[1]))):
        mean = means[name]
        out += summarise(group, f"{name}", series, "1/s")
        out.append(record(group, name, "share of total budget",
                          100.0 * mean / (positive - negative), "pct"))
        out.append(record(group, name, "share of its own side",
                          100.0 * mean / (positive if mean > 0 else negative), "pct"))
        weight = data.reward_weights.get(name)
        if weight:
            # The unweighted function value, which is what makes two configurations
            # with different weights comparable at all.
            out += summarise(group, f"{name}, unweighted", series / weight, "")
            out.append(record(group, name, "weight", weight, ""))
        else:
            out.append(record(group, name, "weight unavailable", np.nan, ""))
    return out
```

```python
def compute_all(data: GaitData, families: tuple[str, ...] | None = None) -> dict:
    """Every statistic, as a flat record set with metadata.

    A family that raises is caught and reported rather than aborting the whole
    computation, because this runs at the end of a play whose rollout cost minutes and
    whose raw dump must be written regardless. The failure is recorded as a record so
    that a reader of the table learns which family is missing and why.
    """
    records, failures = [], {}
    for name, function in _FAMILIES:
        if families is not None and name not in families:
            continue
        try:
            records.extend(function(data))
        except Exception as error:  # noqa: BLE001
            failures[name] = repr(error)
            records.append(record(name, "family failed", "error", np.nan, ""))
    return {
        "records": records,
        "meta": {
            "num_steps": data.num_steps,
            "num_envs": data.num_envs,
            "num_joints": data.num_joints,
            "num_feet": data.num_feet,
            "dt": data.dt,
            "body_mass": data.body_mass,
            "joint_names": list(data.joint_names),
            "feet_names": list(data.feet_names),
            "has_sole_clearance": data.has_sole_clearance,
            "has_orientation": data.base_quaternion is not None,
            "has_limits": data.joint_effort_limits is not None,
            "has_peak_contact_force": data.has_peak_contact_force,
            "contact_force_history_length": data.contact_force_history_length,
            "contact_force_threshold": CONTACT_FORCE_THRESHOLD,
            "impact_window_s": IMPACT_WINDOW_S,
```

The module ends there and offers no path based entry point. A first draft carried a `compute_from_paths` convenience function that loaded the two dumps, read the run parameters and computed the record set from files, and it existed to serve the dashboard's compute on load fallback. With the computation confined to `play.py`, which holds the arrays in memory and never loads them from disk, that function has no caller, and a function written against a caller that does not exist is the code this plan is meant to stop accumulating. The consequence is recorded rather than left implicit. A play whose statistics block failed, which the guard of section 4.4 permits without costing the raw dumps, is recovered by replaying rather than by recomputing from the dumps it left behind, and where that cost is judged too high the remedy is to give `stats.py` a command line entry point deliberately, as a change with a stated caller, rather than to keep an unused function against the possibility.

The metadata block is not decoration. Eight of its entries are the conditions under which the records may be read, the control period, the contact threshold, the impact window, the history depth and the four availability flags, and a table that presents a peak contact force without stating whether the source was the instantaneous channel or the sensor history, or a clearance percentile without stating whether the source was the true sole or the frame proxy, invites exactly the confusion that produced the 1.68 against 1.551 discrepancy in the record.

### 4.4 Wiring into `play.py`

Three wirings, taken in this order. The analysis package is put on the path and the metadata tuple imported from its single home, the replay is configured from the run's own parameters, and the statistics dump is written at the end of the play.

The path insertion follows the precedent the file already sets. `play.py` imports `cli_args` at `scripts/rsl_rl/play.py:12` without qualification, which resolves because a script's own directory is on the path, so the sibling analysis directory is added in the same spirit rather than through a packaging change. It must sit in the block below the application launch, since `os` is not imported until `scripts/rsl_rl/play.py:64`.

The replay configuration is the automation this plan exists to deliver, and it is unconditional. A flag was specified in the first revision and is withdrawn, on the reasoning that a default reproducing the present conduct would leave the manual step standing for every invocation that forgot to pass it, and the manual step is the defect. Every evaluation wants the configuration the policy was trained under, no evaluation wants whatever the tree happens to hold on the day, and a setting whose correct value is the same in every case is not a setting. This is a departure from rule 4 of [../CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md), which prefers an optional argument whose default preserves the old behaviour, and it is taken deliberately under review direction. The behaviour of an existing caller does change, in that a replay now applies the run's own reward set, weights, decimation, episode length and physics step in place of the tree's, and where the two agree, which is the case whenever the tree has not moved since training, nothing observable changes at all. Where they disagree the new conduct is the correct one and the disagreement was the silent failure.

Two properties bound the change. The parameter file is read from the run directory already resolved for the checkpoint, so a replay that finds no such file falls back to the tree with a warning rather than failing, which keeps a checkpoint whose parameters were never dumped playable. And the applied configuration is dumped again into the replay's own `params/env.yaml` by the existing machinery, so what was applied is recorded in the evaluation's artefact exactly as rule 4 intends of a choice made in a calling configuration.

```python

def load_statistics(seed_dir):
    """Return the statistics record set one play wrote, or None where it wrote none.

    Reads and nothing more. The record set is computed by play.py at the close of a play,
    from the arrays it holds in memory, so this function's only responsibility is to find
    the file and hand it on. A dashboard that recomputed would be a second implementation
    of every statistic, free to disagree with the first, and the point of the pipeline is
    that a figure has one origin.

    An absent file means the play predates the pipeline or its statistics block failed,
    and in both cases the remedy is to replay rather than to compute here.

        raw = np.load(path, allow_pickle=True)
        return raw.item() if raw.ndim == 0 else raw
    except Exception as error:
        print(f"[WARN] Failed to load statistics.npy at {path}: {error}")
        return None
```

```python
def get_env_data(raw_data, env_id):
    """Extract one environment's series from a dump channel.

    Every per step channel is a stacked array of shape (Time, Env, ...), so this is a
    slice on the environment axis and nothing more. CHANGED, the previous form also
    accepted the list of arrays that DataLogger.plot used to write and the singleton
    wrap it put in front of most channels. Neither is written any longer, and a dump
    that still carries them predates the pipeline and must be replayed to be read here.

    Returns: (Time, ...) for the chosen environment, or an empty array where the channel
    is absent, empty, or has too few environments.
    """
    if raw_data is None:
        return np.array([])
    data_np = np.asarray(raw_data)
    if data_np.ndim < 2 or env_id >= data_np.shape[1]:
        return np.array([])
    return data_np[:, env_id]
```

## 5. Interpretation, Statistic by Statistic

A table of numbers is not an analysis, and the record of this project supplies repeated evidence that the same figure admits opposite readings depending on what stands beside it. A double support fraction near zero was read as an athletic gait until the thirteenth pass established it as the signature of a one legged hold. A feet distance penalty reading zero was read as a wide stance until the twentieth pass established that the term measured an axis a long stride satisfies. A rising clearance reward was read as progress until the fifth pass established it as a foot rocking onto its edge. This section therefore gives, for each family, what the statistic means, what the record has taken it to mean in the past, what it should be taken to mean in future, and the value a well behaved biped should exhibit.

### 5.1 Temporal statistics

The cadence, the stance duty and the support occupancies together constitute the gait's identity, and they should be read before anything else because every other statistic is conditioned on them. A human at a comfortable speed walks at 113 steps per minute with a stance duty of 62 percent and a double support fraction of 24 percent [1], and a machine tracking a one hertz clock should present a median cycle period of one second with a stance duty above one half.

The double support fraction is the single most diagnostic entry in the family, and its history in this project is instructive. It stood at 2.3 percent in the four run ablation of the twentieth pass, at 0.84 after Phase 1, and at 11.62 after Phases 1b and 2, against a commanded 20.0 and a human 24. A value near zero does not mean the gait is athletic. It means the weight transfer is impulsive, and the arithmetic is unforgiving, since at a one hertz cadence a 2.3 percent double support allows eleven milliseconds per transfer during which the whole body weight must move, and no swing trajectory makes such a transfer gentle. Read a value below five percent as a prediction that the peak contact force will be high, and read the two together rather than separately.

The flight fraction should be zero for a walk and any nonzero value is either a run or a stumble, which the cycle period distribution tells apart, a run presenting a regular period and a stumble a short cycle tail.

The coefficient of variation of the cycle period is the entry that distinguishes a limit cycle from a sequence of recoveries [16], and the record supplies the case it would have caught, a median of exactly 1.000 seconds coexisting with a tenth percentile of 0.720. Read a coefficient below five percent as a settled limit cycle, between five and fifteen as a gait under mild disturbance, and above fifteen as a policy that is recovering rather than walking.

### 5.2 Impact statistics

The peak contact force is the headline transferability statistic, and its standard is the 1.0 to 1.5 body weights a human walking gait develops, against 2.0 to 2.9 for running [3]. A walking machine exceeding three body weights on an average step is loading its structure beyond what running imposes on a human, which is the observation that opened the whole efficiency work stream. The trajectory of this figure through the record is the clearest single account of what the phased remedies bought, 2.354 body weights at the baseline, 2.191 after the failed Phase 1, and 1.584 after Phases 1b and 2, which is inside the human band.

Two cautions govern the reading and both are structural rather than incidental. Every peak this project has quoted to date is a lower bound, because the dump recorded the instantaneous force while the impact reward read the sensor's force history whose maximum sees transients falling between two control steps, and the twenty first pass measured the discrepancy at a factor of twelve on the excess above threshold. Section 4.2.5 moves the channel onto that history maximum, so a run played after the change carries the true peak and a run played before it should be read as a floor, the two being distinguishable by the `contact force history depth` record which is zero for the latter. And the worst case matters as much as the mean, the record quoting 13.76 body weights at the worst against 3.08 on the mean, since a structure fails on the worst load and not the average one.

The two touchdown velocities must never be conflated. The frame vertical speed is what the superseded landing term charged and the sole approach speed is what the collision is governed by, and their ratio is the diagnostic. A ratio near unity means the foot descends flat. A ratio rising above 1.4, which is what the twenty first pass measured under the v1 term, means the policy is rotating its foot into the ground because the term charges translation alone, and the ankle saturation that accompanies it is that substitution paid for in actuator effort. Read a rising ratio as an exploit in progress rather than as noise.

The loading rate separates a collision from a weight acceptance where the peak alone cannot, since a human weight acceptance rises to its first peak over roughly a quarter of stance [3] whereas the record measures 733 body weights per second, which is a rise to full load in a few tens of milliseconds. Expect a well behaved gait to present a loading rate under about 50 body weights per second.

### 5.3 Swing statistics

The four swing descriptors must be read as a set, and the reason is that the apex alone is nearly uninformative. Between the baseline and Phases 1b and 2 the apex clearance barely moved while the apex position moved from 0.265 of the swing to 0.463 and the raised cosine correlation from 0.421 to 0.917, which is a complete change of trajectory shape that the apex did not register.

The expectation for a well behaved swing is an apex position near 0.5, a raised cosine correlation above 0.85, a single clearance peak per swing, and an apex modestly above the target clearance rather than far above it. A correlation below 0.6 with an apex position below 0.35 is the trapezoid signature, and its cause is a clearance reward whose integrand depends on the instantaneous height alone, so that the trajectory maximising its integral over a swing of fixed duration is the one reaching the target soonest, holding longest and leaving latest. That maximiser earns 46 percent more than a sinusoidal arc of the same apex and is paid for by nothing unless a landing term prices the descent that ends it.

A count of clearance peaks above two per swing indicates the foot is being waved rather than carried, and the record measures 3.644 peaks per swing at the baseline against 1.702 after the corrected phases.

One property of the human standard is worth stating because it is counterintuitive and no reward in this project expresses it. Human minimum toe clearance occurs at or very near mid swing at under two and a half centimetres [1], so the human clearance profile has an interior minimum rather than a plateau or a single maximum, and the event is the one most associated with tripping and therefore the most tightly regulated. A machine presenting a smooth single peaked arc is closer to the standard than one presenting a plateau, but neither reproduces the human profile, and a future term that did would be measured here.

### 5.4 Joint and actuator statistics

The percentiles are taken on the magnitude and the ninety ninth is the one to read, because the traces are impulse trains whose mean understates the demand by an order of magnitude. A joint whose ninety ninth percentile torque approaches its effort ceiling is a joint that will saturate on hardware, and the fraction of steps above 98 percent of the ceiling is the direct measure of how often it already does.

The record's use of this family is the model. The twentieth pass found the ankle roll above 98 percent of its 131 newton metre ceiling for 6 to 7 percent of all steps with the joint simultaneously at its mechanical limit and its velocity near zero, which is an actuator pressing a joint into its stop and holding it there, and the arithmetic that confirms the reading is that reaching that torque through the proportional term alone would require a position error of 6.55 radians against a joint whose whole travel is 0.70. Read simultaneous limit residence, effort saturation and near zero velocity as the stop being used as a structural element rather than as a joint being controlled.

The limit residence fraction carries its own reading. A well behaved joint should stand within 0.02 radians of a limit for a negligible fraction of steps. The record measures the knee at 37 percent with a bimodal distribution, 35 percent below 0.05 radians against a hard extension stop at zero and 13 percent above 1.40 against a flexion stop at 1.483, with only 15 percent in the intermediate band, which is a joint being switched between two constraints. The biomechanical standard is a knee flexing to roughly fifteen degrees during weight acceptance and sixty at peak swing [1], so neither locked nor folded to its limit.

The fraction exceeding a declared ceiling is a signal about the simulation rather than the policy. The twentieth pass records the ankle pitch reaching 0.619 radians against a declared limit of 0.454 and both ankles exceeding their velocity ceilings, and attributes it to the position solver yielding at an iteration count of two. A nonzero value here means the physics is not enforcing the constraint the configuration declares, and any conclusion resting on that joint's behaviour is suspect.

### 5.5 Energetic statistics

The absolute cost of transport is the headline efficiency figure and its scale is wide, human walking near 0.2, the Cornell and Delft passive walkers at 0.055 and 0.08, and the Honda humanoid of the previous generation at roughly 1.6 to 2 [6]. A learned policy may land anywhere across that range and nothing in a velocity tracking objective selects the efficient end.

The antagonism ratio is the entry that says whether a high cost is a property of the trajectory or of the manner of producing it, and it is the most valuable single addition this pipeline makes to the project's habitual reading. The record measures 850 watts of absolute joint power against 89 of net, a ratio of 9.5, with an absolute cost of transport of 2.38 against a net figure of 0.25 which is close to human. The trajectory the base follows is not expensive. What the joints are made to pay to produce it is. Read a ratio above three as antagonism and localise it through the pairwise opposed power entries, which the record uses to show the hip pitch and the knee expending 170 watts against one another falling to 43 once the impact was priced.

### 5.6 Posture and stability statistics

The torso tilt should sit within a few degrees of vertical, the record having begun this work stream at forty to seventy degrees and reaching 5.6 to 7.3. This is now a measurement rather than an inversion, and the two should agree, so a disagreement between the measured tilt and the value implied by the flat orientation reward rate is evidence that the play configuration and the trained configuration differ.

The base height should hold within a few centimetres of its target, and its excursion across a stride should be near the five centimetre human figure, which is the pendular exchange of potential against kinetic energy that a walk performs. A markedly smaller excursion indicates a policy holding its base rigidly level, which costs energy and is one signature of the straight legged gait the twenty second pass diagnosed.

The margin of stability entries answer a question none of the above answers, namely how near a fall the gait runs [8]. A negative mediolateral margin during single support is normal in walking, since walking is a controlled fall caught by the next step, and the magnitude of the negative excursion is what the next foot placement must cover. Read a large negative lateral margin together with a narrow stance as a policy with no room to recover, which is exactly the configuration the frontal plane literature warns of [9], the lateral excursion required per step being half the stance width. The base of support here is a lower bound because the sole extent is not recorded, so the margins are conservative and the trend across runs is more trustworthy than the absolute value.

### 5.7 Symmetry, smoothness and variability

The sagittal symmetry indices should remain in single figures, which section 3.4 of [GAIT_EFFICIENCY_PLAN.md](GAIT_EFFICIENCY_PLAN.md) already states as a constraint on any change. The value of the family is localisation rather than scoring, the record citing sagittal indices of 4.7, 9.1 and 6.3 percent as evidence the symmetry augmentation works while a frontal ankle roll range index of 41 percent is cited as evidence of a lateral balance defect. Read a large index on one joint pair against small indices elsewhere as a defect in the plane that pair acts in, not as a failure of the augmentation.

The index must be read as a mean magnitude over environments and never as a pooled figure, and the reason was established by measurement on 2026-08-04 rather than taken from the literature. Pooling the environments before forming the index cancels the asymmetry, because the left and the right pooled statistics each take the union over environments whose asymmetries point in opposite directions, and on run `2026-07-28_06-37-24` the ankle roll range of motion index reads 4.68 percent pooled against a mean magnitude of 13.4 and a value of 14.09 in environment zero alone. A pooled index near zero is therefore not evidence of symmetry, and the signed mean is reported beside the magnitude precisely so that a reader can tell a consistent limp, which shows in both, from a scattered one, which shows only in the magnitude.

The spectral arc length is negated by convention so that a value nearer zero is smoother [14]. It has no absolute standard here and its use is comparative, a run whose joint velocity arc length is markedly more negative than its predecessor's having acquired roughness that no reward penalty registered. The caution that belongs with it is that a smoothness metric which improves under a reward penalty and not under policy space regularisation [15] is measuring the penalty rather than the gait.

The episode outcome entries belong beside every behavioural statistic and not in a footnote, because a policy compared on its behaviour alone is compared on the episodes it survived, which is a selection effect. The twenty second pass supplies the case, every impact statistic improving while episodes ending on the low height condition nearly doubled at the matched iteration, and a table omitting the second would have reported an unqualified success.

### 5.8 Reward budget statistics

The budget is the family the record has found most often decisive, every one of the five obstacles enumerated in section 1.1 of [GAIT_EFFICIENCY_PLAN.md](GAIT_EFFICIENCY_PLAN.md) having been diagnosed from a budget rather than from a behaviour, and the reason is that a budget states why the policy chose to be what it is whereas a behaviour states only what it chose.

Three readings recur and each should be looked for. A term supplying a large share of the positive budget while the behaviour it was meant to produce is absent is a term being farmed, and the fifth pass supplies the archetype, `rew_foot_clearance` reading 10.07 per second while `rew_no_fly` read minus 0.033, which are jointly possible only if the clearance is being collected without a foot leaving the ground. A term reading near zero is either dead or satisfied, and the discriminator is the unweighted value, a func near zero meaning dead and a func near one meaning satisfied, a distinction that matters because a dead term must be rebuilt while a satisfied one may be left alone. And a set of penalties whose total is a few percent of income is a set of penalties that will be paid rather than avoided, the record measuring the six effort and smoothness terms at 6.0 percent of the positive budget of which five sixths was one term.

The unweighted comparison is what makes two configurations with different weights comparable, and it is the reason the weights must be logged. Read the unweighted value as the physics and the weighted rate as the incentive, and note that a term may be strong in one and weak in the other, which is the condition under which a reward is loud and ineffective.

One general signature closes this section because it is the most reliable single indicator of reward hacking in the record. An agent whose effort and quality penalties worsen monotonically while one positive term climbs is hacking that term rather than acquiring a skill, and the fifth pass documents it exactly, the clearance reward climbing from 0.08 to 13.94 while action smoothness went from minus 0.22 to minus 1.65, joint torque from minus 0.12 to minus 1.25, angular velocity from minus 0.06 to minus 0.41 and flat orientation from minus 0.30 to minus 0.88.

## 6. Guidelines for Analysing a Legged Locomotion Policy

The guidelines below are the operative residue of twenty two passes of investigation and of the literature surveyed in section 2. They are ordered as an analysis should proceed, and each carries the evidence that established it, so that a reader who disagrees with one can find what it rests on.

### 6.1 Before any statistic is computed, establish what is being measured

Confirm the run configuration from the run's own dumped `params/env.yaml` and never from the working tree. A play dump's reward weights are those of the live tree wherever the replay was not configured from the run, since `play.py` rebuilds its environment through `parse_env_cfg`, and the twenty first pass records a case in which three parameters differed between the two. Section 4.1 removes the manual step, the parameter file carrying the import path of every reward function and therefore permitting a term the tree has since deleted to be restored, and section 4.4 applies it on every replay rather than on request. The behavioural channels are unaffected and record what the trained policy did, but any per term function value must be recovered by reconstruction rather than by division through an assumed weight.

Confirm the index order from the dump's own name lists and never from the asset. The twentieth pass proved the articulation enumerates left before right at every depth while the URDF declares the whole right chain first, so a name list derived from the asset is transposed, and every per joint plot this project exported before 2026-07-31 carries a swapped side label. The order is not derivable from the asset by any reasoning available, which is why it is now recorded.

Confirm the control period rather than assuming it. Every cadence, duty fraction, cost of transport and loading rate scales with it, and it is 0.01 seconds here against 0.02 for the Isaac Lab G1 reference.

Confirm what the contact threshold and the impact window were. The twentieth pass reports 1.68 metres per second where the twenty first reports 1.551 for the same run and the same quantity, the difference being an unrecorded threshold, so a figure quoted without its threshold cannot be compared against another.

Confirm whether the clearance channel is the true sole or the frame proxy. On a sole footed robot the frame overestimates the true clearance by 23.5 millimetres on average and 74.4 at the ninety fifth percentile, so a gate or a percentile computed on the frame is a different quantity from one computed on the sole.

### 6.2 Compare only what is comparable

Compare at matched iteration counts with the curriculum values verified equal. The tracking error metrics do not converge downward while a curriculum is active, since the reward kernel contracts and the command range widens as training proceeds, and the twenty second pass records a run whose planar error rose from 0.173 at eleven thousand iterations to 0.183 at thirty thousand for that reason alone. Reading a metric at the end of one run against the end of another is unsound wherever a curriculum is active.

Compare over the same population. Every figure should state whether it is taken over all environments or over one, the twentieth pass recording a yaw error of 0.741 over thirty two environments against 0.62 over environment zero alone.

Compare against a sibling rather than against a memory. The strongest evidence in this record is the four run ablation of the twentieth pass, four configurations trained in parallel and evaluated on the same day, and it settled in one pass questions that successive single runs had left open for six.

Distinguish a confound from a cause before attributing an improvement. The twenty second pass attributes a halved base roll rate not to the grace window it was testing but to an angular velocity penalty weight that changed in the same run for an unrelated reason, and says so.

### 6.3 Read the behaviour and the budget together, never separately

A behaviour without a budget cannot be explained, and a budget without a behaviour cannot be validated. Every root cause in this record was found by placing one against the other, the tilted foot exploit by placing a rising clearance reward against a no fly term reading zero, the parked foot exploit by placing a high air time reward against a video showing no alternation, the trapezoid by placing a swing profile against the integrand that maximises it.

Compute the marginal arithmetic before changing a weight. Ask what the policy would gain and lose by adopting the behaviour you want, at fixed cadence and fixed everything else, and sum the affected terms. The twentieth pass computed compliance with the commanded double support to be worth 1.87 per second against a positive budget of 63, which is three percent and therefore a gradient a converged policy will not resolve, and the twenty first computed that under the configuration actually trained a softer landing was a net loss of 0.81 per second, so the policy was correct to keep stomping and no amount of training would have changed it.

Check whether a term is dead before strengthening it. A Gaussian kernel placed far from the operating point delivers a gradient of order ten to the minus six, which the Phase A knee reward of [NATURAL_GAIT_PLAN.md](NATURAL_GAIT_PLAN.md) demonstrated, and a dead term must be rebuilt rather than reweighted. Check whether it is saturated before raising its coefficient, the twenty second pass declining a proposal to double a coefficient on the measurement that its branch was true on 99.8 percent of steps and therefore saturated in both arms of the comparison it would have to discriminate.

Verify that a term measures the quantity the defect consists of. This is the single lesson the record states most often and in the most currencies. A term that measures a proxy will be satisfied by whatever the proxy fails to see, and the record supplies four instances, a clearance reward measuring a frame height satisfied by tilting, a landing term measuring a time integral satisfied by loitering, the same term measuring a frame velocity satisfied by rotating the sole in, and a distance hinge measuring a planar norm satisfied by a long stride.

### 6.4 Watch for the known exploit signatures

A positive term climbing while every effort and quality penalty worsens monotonically is that term being hacked rather than a skill being acquired.

A contact force reported on a foot whose sole clearance is well above the ground is forged contact, and its cause in this project was a permanent shank into foot interpenetration in the mesh asset which silenced every contact keyed term for eight passes.

A double support fraction near zero with a high peak contact force is the impulsive transfer, and the two are one defect rather than two.

A term reading near zero whose behaviour is nonetheless bad is a term measuring the wrong axis or the wrong frame, and the feet distance hinge is the archetype, reading 0.00014 while the feet crossed the midline.

A joint at its mechanical limit with its actuator saturated and its velocity near zero is a stop being used as a structural element, and it is cheaper than control whenever the limit penalty is a small fraction of the tracking income.

A rising ratio between a sole quantity and its frame proxy is a rotational substitution in progress, the policy discharging a penalty in the currency the term cannot see.

### 6.5 Judge transferability separately from performance

A gait that tracks its command is not thereby a gait that could be placed on hardware, and none of the properties that decide transfer appears in the tracking error the training curves report. Five should be checked explicitly. The peak contact force against the human band of 1.0 to 1.5 body weights [3]. The actuator saturation fraction, since a policy relying on a torque the hardware cannot deliver will not reproduce. The joint velocity and position excursions beyond their declared ceilings, since those indicate the simulator is not enforcing what the hardware will. The action and torque smoothness, since a policy commanding a 470 newton metre change within one control step is commanding a bandwidth the drive does not have. And the cost of transport, since thermal limits bind on sustained current rather than on peak torque.

### 6.6 Preserve what has been won

Any change must be checked against the properties already achieved, and section 3.4 of [GAIT_EFFICIENCY_PLAN.md](GAIT_EFFICIENCY_PLAN.md) states five for this robot, the cadence and phase correlation with the clock, the planar tracking error within twenty percent of its established value, the base height regulation and torso attitude, the sagittal symmetry indices in single figures, and the undesired contacts penalty near zero. A remedy that spends one of these to buy another has not improved the gait, and the twenty second pass records exactly such a trade, a softer landing bought by straightening the legs, which migrated the sagittal work to a hip pitch actuator at 82 percent of its ceiling and left the gait without the compliance to absorb a disturbance.

### 6.7 The checklist

The following is the operative form of the above, to be worked through in order.

1. Replay the run, which reads its own `params/env.yaml` and applies it, and read the console report to confirm which terms were applied, added, removed or failed to import, so that the reward set, the weights and the control period are known to be those the policy was trained under rather than those the working tree holds today. Record the weights, the command ranges, the curriculum values and the actuator gains and ceilings from that file and not from the tree.
2. Confirm the dump carries `joint_names`, `body_names` and `feet_names`, and use them. Where it does not, the dump predates 2026-07-31 and the fallback order applies.
3. Record the control period, the contact threshold, the impact window and the clearance source, and quote them with every figure.
4. Establish the episode boundaries and exclude any statistic that spans one.
5. Read the temporal family first. Cadence, stance duty, double support, single support, flight, and the cycle period coefficient of variation.
6. Read the impact family second. Peak force in body weights, both touchdown velocities and their ratio, and the loading rate.
7. Read the swing family third, as a set of four and never by its apex alone.
8. Read the per joint family fourth, at the ninety ninth percentile of the magnitude, with the saturation and limit residence fractions beside it.
9. Read the energetic family fifth, in all three cost conventions, and localise any antagonism through the pairwise opposed power entries.
10. Read the posture, stability and symmetry families sixth, and localise any asymmetry to a plane.
11. Read the reward budget last, and for each of the three signatures of section 5.8.
12. Compute the marginal arithmetic for any change contemplated, at fixed cadence, summing every affected term.
13. Check the proposed remedy measures the quantity the defect consists of, and not a proxy for it.
14. Check the five preserved properties of section 6.6 against the proposed change.
15. Verify the caller set of every shared function the change touches, and prefer an optional argument whose default reproduces the old behaviour, per [../CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md).
16. Record the outcome, the divergence from the plan, and any newly discovered fact, in the governing context document.

That measurement is the strongest available argument for section 4.4 and it qualifies the regeneration this revision assumes. One qualitative correspondence is worth as much as any of the above, because it validates the family whose implementation the plan had to correct. The twentieth pass reports the sagittal symmetry indices in single figures against a frontal ankle roll index of 41 percent, and cites the contrast as evidence that the augmentation works while a lateral balance defect remains. The corrected per environment implementation reproduces exactly that structure on the same run, a hip pitch torque index of 5.20 percent mean magnitude against an ankle roll torque index of 27.03 with a ninety ninth percentile of 66.42, whereas the pooled implementation the plan carried before the correction reported 4.68 percent for the ankle roll and would have contradicted the record entirely.

The swing family is the one place where the specification does not reproduce the record, and the 2026-08-06 re-run establishes the gap as larger than the first draft supposed. On the untouched `2026-07-28_06-37-24` dump the code of section 4.3.6 returns a raised cosine correlation of 0.4741 against the 0.421 the record states, a swing apex position of 0.4217 against 0.463, and 3.657 clearance peaks per swing against 1.702, the last being a factor of two rather than a rounding. The cause is the swing segmentation convention rather than the shape descriptors, since the same dump reproduces every touchdown and temporal figure exactly, and the candidates are the treatment of the first and last sample of a swing, the inclusion of swings truncated by the dump boundary, and the prominence filter applied before a clearance peak is counted, that last being the only one capable of doubling a count. The implementation must resolve it against the twentieth pass rather than adopt whichever convention is convenient, and must record the convention it adopts, since a peak count is meaningless without one. This is the single open defect the plan carries into execution, and it is confined to three records of one family.

One qualification governs the whole table, it is stronger than the first revision recorded, and it must be applied before any figure in it is quoted against a run played after this plan lands. Two changes separate the dumps these figures came from and the dumps that will replace them. Section 4.2.5 substitutes the maximum over the sensor's force history for the instantaneous contact channel, so the peak force mean of 1.584 body weights, the worst of 4.939 and the loading rate derived from them will all rise. And the regeneration itself is a replay rather than a conversion, so the rollout behind a regenerated dump is a fresh one, and every figure in the table above, not only the force rows, is subject to whatever the replay differs by. One thing bounds that difference and it does not eliminate it. The replay is configured from the run's own parameters by section 4.4, which removes the largest source of divergence. Nothing else does, and the measurement recorded below shows that a replay of one checkpoint moved its mean absolute joint power by twenty eight percent and its double support fraction by a third, so the assumption that a converged policy reproduces its own rollout closely is not supported by this evidence and must not be relied upon.

The reproduction gate is therefore two sided and the sides answer different questions. The implementation must reproduce the table exactly when run against the surviving dumps in the shape they were prototyped in, which is a gate on the arithmetic and is discharged below. And the regenerated dumps establish the new baseline for the record, against which every future comparison is made, so the table above becomes the historical figure rather than the live target on the day the regeneration completes. The `contact force history depth` record distinguishes the two eras without ambiguity, reading zero for the old channel and four for a current SD_BRS1 run, and the divergence between the recorded and the regenerated figures must itself be recorded in [../context/gait_metrics.md](../context/gait_metrics.md) rather than absorbed silently, since an unexplained movement in a figure is the thing this pipeline exists to make visible.

Four of the gates below were discharged in advance, by extracting the code blocks of this document verbatim and running them, so that the specification is known executable rather than merely plausible. Recorded in cluster 13 of `../context/literature.md`, which took them from a published clinical normative table and from Winter, D. A., Biomechanics and Motor Control of Human Movement, Wiley, the table itself not having been fixed to a citable paper.

3. Nilsson, J., and Thorstensson, A. Ground reaction forces at different speeds of human walking and running. Acta Physiologica Scandinavica 136(2), 1989. Cluster 13. The walking peak of 1.0 to 1.5 body weights against 2.0 to 2.9 for running, and the double humped profile whose first peak is reached over roughly a quarter of stance.
4. Vukobratović, M., and Borovac, B. Zero-Moment Point, Thirty Five Years of its Life. International Journal of Humanoid Robotics 1(1), 2004, pages 157 to 173. Newly surveyed for this document. The criterion that the contact remains unilateral while the point of vanishing horizontal ground reaction moment lies within the support polygon, and therefore the warrant for reporting the distance from that point to the nearest polygon edge as a margin.
5. McGeer, T. Passive Dynamic Walking. International Journal of Robotics Research 9(2), 1990. Cluster 10. The result that the heel strike collision is where a walking gait loses its energy.
6. The cost of transport figures, 0.2 for human walking, 0.055 and 0.08 for the Cornell and Delft passive walkers against an estimated 0.05 for the mechanical component of human walking, and roughly 1.6 to 2 for the Honda humanoid. Cluster 13, which records them as the figures reported in the energy efficient locomotion literature without fixing an individual work, so the entry stands for that body of work.
7. Benchmarking Model Predictive Control and Reinforcement Learning Based Control for Legged Robot Locomotion in MuJoCo Simulation. arXiv:2501.16590. Newly surveyed, authors not verified. Cited for its statement of the headline metric set used in quadrupedal locomotion benchmarking, the success rate, the velocity tracking reward and the inverse cost of transport, and for its definition of the dimensionless cost of transport as the positive mechanical actuator power per unit weight and unit speed.
8. Hof, A. L., Gazendam, M. G. J., and Sinke, W. E. The condition for dynamic stability. Journal of Biomechanics 38(1), 2005, pages 1 to 8. Newly surveyed. 9. The frontal plane balance and foot placement estimator literature, from which the finding is taken that the lateral centre of mass excursion required per step is half the stance width, so that a narrow stance is balanced by torso roll alone, and that recovery from a lateral disturbance requires the foot to be placed further laterally than the centre of mass. 10. Pratt, J., Carff, J., Drakunov, S., and Goswami, A. Capture Point, A Step toward Humanoid Push Recovery. IEEE-RAS International Conference on Humanoid Robots, 2006. Newly surveyed. The capture point as the ground point at which a step brings the body to rest, coinciding under the linear inverted pendulum with the divergent component of the motion.
11. The nonlinear gait stability literature, comprising the finite time maximum Lyapunov exponent as a measure of local dynamic stability and the maximum Floquet multiplier as a measure of orbital stability, a value below unity indicating an orbitally stable stride to stride map. Newly surveyed, the foundational application to human locomotion being attributed throughout that literature to Dingwell and colleagues, whose individual papers were not fixed during this survey, so the entry stands for that body of work.
12. Robinson, R. O., Herzog, W., and Nigg, B. M. Use of force platform variables to quantify the effects of chiropractic manipulation on gait symmetry. 13. The harmonic ratio literature, comparing the even against the odd harmonics of the stride frequency in the trunk acceleration spectrum as a step to step symmetry and smoothness index, together with the documented caution that the measure has been paraphrased variously as smoothness, harmony, rhythmicity and dynamic stability.
