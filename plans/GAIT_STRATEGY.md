This document diagnoses the degraded gait recorded at the end of the run sd_brs1_flat/2026-07-15_06-59-45 and lays out a prioritised, evidence-grounded strategy for correcting it. The diagnosis rests on frame analysis of that run's final training video, no longer retained on disk, a full review of the environment, reward, actuator, and algorithm configuration, and a literature survey of gait shaping techniques for bipedal reinforcement learning. ## 1. What the video shows

Across the twenty second episode the robot tracks the commanded heading yet walks in a deep crouch with the torso pitched forward by forty to seventy degrees, at times rolled sideways, with the feet swept quickly along the ground rather than lifted. One robot appears fully collapsed mid episode while the episode continues, which demonstrates that the present termination set does not prune such postures. The three complaints, feet not raised, gait asymmetry, and a torso that is not vertical, are therefore all visible in the recording, and the analysis below shows that each one is traceable to identifiable configuration causes rather than to insufficient training time.

## 2. Diagnosis from the codebase

### 2.1 The foot clearance reward is miscalibrated and now rewards the shuffle

The reward rew_foot_clearance measures the height of the Link6 body frame, which sits at the ankle joint, and the sole plate extends 0.124 m below that frame, so the frame stands at roughly 0.124 m when the foot is flat on the ground. The configured target_height of 0.12 m is therefore below the standing height of the measured point, and since the term multiplies a Gaussian on height error by a tanh of horizontal foot speed, its maximum is attained by sweeping the foot quickly at ground level. The term does not merely fail to encourage lifting, it actively pays for the fast flat shuffle seen in the video, and raising its weight from 2 to 20 in the working tree amplifies the defect. The comment beside the parameter assumes a 0.03 m foot radius, which belongs to the TRON1 point foot and not to this robot. The correct target for about 8 cm of sole clearance is approximately 0.20 m.

The same imported constant infects a second term, recorded here on 2026-07-17 after it was found while applying the phase 1 changes. feet_regulation takes an explicit foot_radius parameter, used to convert the measured body height into a ground clearance, and the BRS configuration passed 0.03, so the term believed a foot resting flat on the ground stood 0.094 m in the air. The figure 0.124 has since been confirmed directly against the collision mesh, meshes/Link6R.STL and Link6L.STL both report z_min exactly −0.1240 in the link frame, and it reconciles with the kinematic chain, 0.181 + 0.44 + 0.43 + 0.124 = 1.175 m, the straight leg torso height. Any term that converts a Link6 body height into a sole clearance must therefore use 0.124, and the audit note is that foot_landing_vel carries the same parameter and would need the same treatment were it ever enabled for this robot.

### 2.1b The clearance reward measures the wrong quantity, and tilt farms it (added 2026-07-17 after run 2026-07-17_07-49-02)

Raising the target from 0.12 to 0.20, as section 2.1 recommended, did not repair the term, it merely moved the exploit, and the reason is that the measured quantity is wrong rather than the constant. foot_clearance_reward reads body_pos_w[Link6].z, the height of the link frame origin, which sits at the ankle, while the sole plate extends 0.3156 m fore and aft. Tilting the foot about the ankle therefore raises the frame while the sole edge remains on the ground. Rotating the collision mesh and taking its lowest point gives the frame height of a foot that is still touching the ground, 0.124 m level, 0.170 m at 20 degrees, 0.186 m at 30 degrees, 0.197 m at 40 degrees and 0.201 m at 45 degrees, against a maximum of 0.2245 m. The configured target of 0.20 m with std 0.035 is thus scored at kernel 0.994 to 0.999 by a grounded foot rocked onto its edge, and since the term sums over both feet with no alternation requirement, the policy collected it from both feet at once while never entering single stance.

The run's own logs confirm this independently of the geometry. Every Episode_Reward is a rate per second, rew_no_fly at -0.033 against weight 15 implies both single stance and flight are essentially zero so the robot is in double support 99.9 percent of the time, feet_air_time reads 2e-5 against a maximum of 5.0, and yet rew_foot_clearance reads 10.07 and climbing. Inverting feet_slide (-3.36, weight 5) gives a per foot contact speed of 0.336 m/s, and feeding that through feet_regulation (-0.0098, weight 0.2) yields a height gate of 0.217, hence a frame height of 0.170 m while in contact, which is about 20 degrees of tilt. A level grounded foot would give exactly 0.124 m, so a frame above 0.124 m while in contact is direct log side proof of tilting.

This is why the term works on TRON1 and cannot work here. A point foot of radius 0.03 has a centre height equal to its clearance under any orientation, so the frame height is an exact proxy. The BRS sole breaks the assumption, pure tilt moves the frame by up to 0.10 m, comparable to the entire intended swing clearance, so no choice of target_height can separate lifting from tilting. The same flaw propagates to feet_regulation, whose height gate also derives from the frame, and the phase 1 change to that term consequently released the anti slide penalty exactly where the exploit lives, applying 0.216 of the penalty at the observed 20 degrees against 0.884 under the original settings.

The fix is to stop inferring clearance from the frame height. The first and cheapest measure, implemented on 2026-07-17, gates the term on contact, a foot touching the ground earns nothing whatever its tilt, which takes the toe rock from 13.03 per second to zero while leaving a genuine swing at 10.74 per second untouched. It is not a complete fix, because the reward still reads the frame once the foot is airborne, so a foot tilted 40 degrees still scores near maximum while hovering 0 to 1.4 cm above the ground against 6.5 to 8.7 cm for a level foot.

The complete fix, implemented on 2026-07-17 as foot_clearance_reward_v2 and now bound in the BRS configuration, measures the true sole clearance. It transforms a table of sole points through body_quat_w, adds body_pos_w, and takes their minimum world height, a quantity exactly invariant to how the foot is tilted, so target_height becomes the desired sole clearance and reads 0.08 rather than the frame target of 0.20, the two agreeing to within 4 mm for a level foot. A v2 was warranted here rather than an optional argument precisely because target_height changes meaning, a semantic shift that no default can carry safely. The sole table was measured from the collision mesh, not from the primitive URDF whose sole box would have introduced 14.31 mm of error, and the mesh reveals chamfered fore and aft edges, so the support set is an arc rather than a corner, four flat corners give 7.74 mm of error, eight points give 2.43 mm, and the twelve point table adopted gives 0.85 mm over the reachable orientation range. The measured outcome, a grounded foot now reports at most 0.71 mm of clearance at any tilt, and the residual exploit that survived the contact gate, a foot hovering 1 cm while tilted 40 degrees, falls from 10.28 per second to 0.20 while a genuine level 8 cm swing is preserved at 10.74. The same sole clearance quantity should now be given to feet_regulation, which retains the frame height gate and therefore still releases the anti slide penalty on a tilted grounded foot. A feet level term, van Marum's Table I Feet orientation at 0.05 against 0.3 combined linear velocity, about 8 at this scale, is no longer necessary to close the exploit but remains the cheapest way to make tilting itself costly.

### 2.2 The air time reward is a negative barrier at weight 500

This section walks through both implementations in full, derives the payoff structure of the current term, and explains in learning dynamic terms why it obstructs the discovery of stepping while the IsaacLab biped variant promotes it.

#### 2.2.1 What the current implementation computes

The contact sensor is configured with track_air_time and updates at the simulation step of 0.005 s, it maintains per body state machines that accumulate current_air_time while a body is out of contact, latch that accumulator into last_air_time at the instant of touchdown, and symmetrically maintain current_contact_time and last_contact_time (isaaclab/sensors/contact_sensor). The custom reward in mdp/rewards.py lines 49 to 76 then evaluates, at every control step of 0.01 s, first_contact = compute_first_contact(env.step_dt), a boolean that is true only on the single control step in which a foot's contact age is younger than the step, and pays reward = clamp(last_air_time − threshold_min, max = threshold_max − threshold_min) × first_contact, gated to zero when the command norm is below 0.1 m/s. With threshold_min 0.2, threshold_max 0.5, weight 500, and the manager's dt scaling of 0.01, the payoff per touchdown event as a function of the completed swing duration τ is

```
r(τ) = 500 × 0.01 × (τ − 0.2),  clamped above at +1.5
r(0.05) = −0.75    r(0.10) = −0.50    r(0.15) = −0.25    r(0.20) = 0    r(0.50+) = +1.50
```

and a foot that never leaves the ground generates no touchdown events and therefore collects exactly zero from this term, forever.

#### 2.2.2 Why this payoff profile is a barrier to discovering stepping

Three properties combine into the barrier. First, the term is event sparse, the policy gradient receives information about it only through touchdown events, so a policy that does not step receives no gradient toward stepping from this term at all, the term can only ever push a policy that is already stepping. Second, every touchdown that early exploration can produce is negatively priced. An untrained policy exploring around a double support stance produces swing durations far below 0.2 s, because a 0.2 s swing at walking cadence is essentially a completed, balance transferred step, and the payoff at those durations is −0.25 to −1.0 per event against a maximum tracking income of +0.65 per control step, so one clumsy 0.05 s hop erases more than a full step's worth of perfect velocity tracking. The continuous path in policy space from τ = 0 to the profitable region τ > 0.2 therefore passes through a valley in which every increment of stepping behaviour lowers the return, and gradient ascent does not volunteer to cross such a valley. Third, of the two ways to raise this term's expected value, lengthening τ requires a coordinated multi joint balance transfer onto single support that the untrained policy cannot yet execute, whereas reducing the frequency of touchdown events requires only suppressing foot lifts, a small and immediately available policy change, so the gradient reliably selects the degenerate direction and the term teaches the robot not to lift its feet. The same mechanism prices contact chatter, brief force dropouts while a foot drags register as micro air times far below 0.2 s, so even the shuffle is pushed toward firmer, more continuous dragging. In reward shaping terms the term is not a potential based shaping function, and by the classical policy invariance result of Ng, Harada, and Russell (ICML 1999) such shaping is permitted to change the optimum of the shaped problem, which is precisely what is observed, the shaped optimum is the shuffle.

It is worth recording why the same construction is successful in its original home. The term descends from the quadruped reward of Rudin et al. (Learning to Walk in Minutes, CoRL 2021, arXiv:2109.11978, threshold 0.5 s in legged_gym), and a quadruped can lift one leg while remaining statically stable on the other three, so exploration crosses the short swing valley cheaply and constantly, the barrier is shallow. A biped must actively transfer its weight before any swing can lengthen, which is exactly the coordination the untrained policy lacks, so the identical term that shapes quadruped gaits gates biped learning. van Marum et al. (arXiv:2404.19173, section IV.B) reach the matching conclusion from the humanoid side, their air time term is deliberately recast as a touchdown penalty counteracted by accumulated air time and is explicitly the only sparse term in their reward, and they report that dense single foot contact shaping, not sparse air time magnitude, is what reliably converts velocity tracking into walking.

#### 2.2.3 What the replacement computes and why it inverts the incentive

feet_air_time_positive_biped (isaaclab_tasks velocity mdp rewards.py lines 49 to 68) evaluates at every control step, in_mode_time = contact_time if the foot is in contact else air_time, single_stance = exactly one foot in contact, reward = clamp(min over feet of in_mode_time, max = threshold) × single_stance, gated by the same command norm. The consequences invert every pathological property above. The term is dense, it pays on every control step of single support, so the very first instant in which the robot shifts weight onto one foot begins to earn, before the swing foot has risen a millimetre, and the earning grows monotonically as the swing matures because the minimum of the stance foot's contact age and the swing foot's air age rises with both. The term is non negative everywhere, there is no valley between the shuffle and the walk, every incremental lengthening of single support raises the return, so the gradient points from the shuffle toward stepping along the entire path. The single stance gate additionally prices double support shuffling and flight at zero, subsuming the intent of rew_no_fly, and the clamp at threshold 0.4 s regularises cadence exactly as van Marum's touchdown constant does, air beyond 0.4 s earns nothing so there is no incentive toward exaggerated leaps. The weight must be recalibrated when switching, the value 500 was sized against a payoff that arrives once per step cycle, whereas the dense variant pays up to threshold × weight × dt on each of roughly forty control steps per swing, the G1 reference weights it at one quarter of the linear tracking weight and the equivalent here is 12.5 against rew_lin_vel_xy at 50.

### 2.3 Nothing stops the robot from leaning

The orientation penalty in the recorded run was −1.0, against tracking terms worth up to +0.65 per step, and at a forty five degree lean the orientation cost was roughly −0.01 per step, two orders of magnitude below the tracking gain. The working tree raises it to −5.0, which is still roughly seven to ten times below the ratios used by the reference configurations, the G1 config sets flat orientation at parity with its linear tracking weight (both magnitude 1.0, rough_env_cfg.py:26 and :135) and van Marum weights roll and pitch orientation at 0.2 against 0.15 for each of the two velocity components, that is two thirds of the combined linear velocity weight and heavier than either component alone. Mapped onto this configuration's scale those ratios imply an orientation weight between −33 and −50, not −5. Moreover there is no orientation termination and low_height terminates only below 0.5 m, half the nominal height, so bent and collapsed postures survive and keep harvesting tracking reward. The curriculum that anneals the tracking std down to 0.09 sharpens this exploit further, a sharply peaked tracking kernel makes leaning into the command even more profitable relative to posture.

### 2.4 The default pose, what the reset randomisation fixes and what it cannot

The default joint pose plays three separable roles in this stack, and keeping them separate resolves the apparent conflict between the earlier draft of this section and the deliberate initial pose randomisation.

The first role is the centre of the initial state distribution. The reset events in brs_base_env_cfg.py sample joint specific uniform offsets around the default through the custom reset_joint_by_offset (hip roll ±0.1295, hip pitch −0.25 to 0.42 mirrored per leg, knee 0 to 0.475, ankle roll ±0.1, ankle pitch −0.113 to 0.25, all clamped to the soft limits), and these ranges were deliberately widened after earlier runs in which the hips and knees barely moved. That intervention is sound and should stay. Diverse initial states are a recognised exploration device, reference state initialisation in DeepMimic (Peng et al., SIGGRAPH 2018, arXiv:1804.02717) rests on the same principle that starting episodes across the useful state manifold hands the critic and policy experience they cannot cheaply reach on their own, and the observed improvement, knees that now flex and velocities that now track, is consistent with that mechanism. Nothing in this document proposes narrowing the reset distribution.

The second role is the action offset. JointPositionActionCfg is configured with use_default_offset=True, so at every control step the drive target is default_pose + 0.25 × action, regardless of where the episode started. The zero action fixed point of the closed loop is therefore the straight leg pose in every episode, wherever the reset scattered it, and whenever the network output is small the PD controllers pull the robot back toward straight knees. Reaching the stance the 1.14 m height target implies (about 0.57 rad of knee flexion, see the arithmetic below) requires the policy to hold a standing bias of roughly 2.3 action units on each knee at all times, a bias it must first discover and then maintain against its own exploration noise (std 1.0, or 0.25 rad at the joint). The randomised starts do not alter this attractor, they only change where each episode begins its fall toward it.

The third role is the geometric reference frame. The straight knee default sits exactly on the knee's hard lower limit 0 and outside the soft band of roughly 0.074 to 1.409 implied by soft_joint_pos_limit_factor 0.9, so the attractor pose of the second role is itself inside the pen_joint_pos_limits penalty region, and joint_pos_rel reports stance flexion as a large permanent deviation rather than as zero. The init_state comment claiming soles at ground with the base at 1.1 m is stale, with thigh 0.44 m and shank 0.43 m the straight leg torso height is about 1.175 m, and the spawn height of 1.3 m drops the robot roughly 12 cm at every reset. For the height targets, 1.1 m demands about 0.84 rad of knee flexion whereas 1.14 m needs only about 0.57 rad, which is the basis of the phase 0 target change already applied.

The decision for phase 0 is recorded as follows. The initial joint position randomisation stays as designed, the default pose stays at zeros for now, and the second and third roles convert into a monitored risk rather than an immediate change. The discriminating observation is cheap, if the new baseline's stance knee distribution (from the DataLogger joint position dump, knee columns per ../context/sd_brs_urdf.md) still collapses toward zero during double support and standing command envelopes, the attractor explanation is confirmed, and the remedy is to move only the action offset to the stance pose while recentring the reset offset ranges so the sampled initial state distribution remains exactly the one that is working, the two are independently adjustable precisely because the reset events sample offsets relative to the default. The spawn height correction from 1.3 m to about 1.16 m touches no joint value and can be taken at any time.

### 2.5 The proximal actuators were drastically underdamped, with calculations

Each joint under PD position control behaves near its operating point as the second order system I q̈ = K (q* − q) − D q̇ plus gravity and coupling terms, where I is the effective inertia of everything distal to the joint. The standard quantities follow.

```
natural frequency        ωn = sqrt(K / I)          [rad/s]
damping ratio            ζ  = D / (2 sqrt(K I))
step overshoot fraction  M  = exp(−π ζ / sqrt(1 − ζ²))      (underdamped, ζ < 1)
oscillation envelope     T  = 1 / (ζ ωn) = 2 I / D          [s]
```

Taking the stance configuration effective inertias derived in BRS.md section 4 (hip pitch 6.494, hip roll 6.499, knee 1.272, ankle pitch 0.039, ankle roll 0.033 kg m²), the gains that produced the recorded video evaluate as follows.

| Joint | K | D | ωn = sqrt(K/I) | ζ = D/(2 sqrt(KI)) | Overshoot M | Envelope T |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hip pitch | 200 | 5 | sqrt(200/6.494) = 5.55 | 5/(2 sqrt(1298.8)) = 5/72.1 = 0.069 | exp(−0.218) = 80 % | 1/(0.069 × 5.55) = 2.6 s |
| Hip roll | 150 | 5 | sqrt(150/6.499) = 4.80 | 5/(2 sqrt(974.9)) = 5/62.4 = 0.080 | 78 % | 2.6 s |
| Knee | 200 | 5 | sqrt(200/1.272) = 12.54 | 5/(2 sqrt(254.4)) = 5/31.9 = 0.157 | 61 % | 0.51 s |
| Ankle pitch | 20 | 4 | sqrt(20/0.039) = 22.6 | 4/(2 sqrt(0.78)) = 4/1.77 = 2.26 | none, overdamped | sluggish |
| Ankle roll | 20 | 4 | sqrt(20/0.033) = 24.6 | 4/(2 sqrt(0.66)) = 4/1.62 = 2.46 | none, overdamped | sluggish |

The physical reading is stark. A hip pitch position command overshoots its target by about 80 percent and rings inside an envelope that takes 2.6 seconds to decay, while a complete swing phase at walking cadence lasts 0.3 to 0.4 seconds, so every swing target the policy issues is answered by an oscillation seven times longer than the swing itself, and the knee rings for a further half second on top. Precise foot placement is not learnable on such a plant, and the behaviour that minimises the ringing is exactly the low amplitude shuffle the video shows, since small targets produce small oscillations. The overdamped ankles compound the posture problem from the other side, a 60 kg sole footed robot balances upright primarily through fast ankle torque modulation, and ankles that respond sluggishly push the optimiser toward crouching and torso lean as substitute stabilisation strategies. BRS.md recommended both stiffness and damping ranges, the stiffness recommendations were adopted into the configuration but the damping recommendations were not, which is how this state arose.

The phase 0 corrections now applied in the working tree land almost exactly on the ζ ≈ 0.7 target for the proximal joints.

| Joint | K now | D now | ζ now | Residual note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hip pitch | 200 | 50 | 50/72.1 = 0.694 | on target |
| Hip roll | 150 | 45 | 45/62.4 = 0.721 | on target |
| Knee | 200 | 22 | 22/31.9 = 0.690 | on target |
| Ankle pitch | 50 | 4 | 4/(2 sqrt(50 × 0.039)) = 4/2.79 = 1.43 | still overdamped, D ≈ 2 × 0.7 × sqrt(1.95) ≈ 2.0 gives ζ 0.7 |
| Ankle roll | 20 | 4 | 2.46 unchanged | D ≈ 2 × 0.7 × sqrt(0.66) ≈ 1.1 gives ζ 0.7 |

The raised ankle pitch stiffness of 50 lifts ωn to sqrt(50/0.039) = 35.8 rad/s, comfortably above the 100 Hz control relevant band, and the only residual plant work is the two ankle damping reductions in the table, which restore ankle bandwidth for push off and lateral balance. A saturation sanity check passes throughout, at the largest soft limit excursions the proportional torques K × Δq remain below the configured effort limits (knee 200 × 1.41 = 282 < 600, hip pitch 200 × 1.15 = 230 < 500, ankle pitch 50 × 0.70 = 35 < 262). One caveat belongs on record, the effective inertias are configuration dependent, the tabulated values are stance representative figures from BRS.md, and ζ during swing (leg folded, lower I) will sit somewhat higher than tabulated, which is acceptable since overshoot shrinks as ζ rises.

### 2.6 Nothing in the stack expresses left-right symmetry

There is no mirror loss, no symmetric data augmentation, no phase variable, and no per leg regularisation, so nothing penalises the limping asymmetric solution once discovered. The vendored rsl_rl PPO already implements the symmetry interface of Mittal et al. 2024 (symmetry_cfg with data augmentation and mirror loss), so enabling symmetry is plumbing work, not research work. Smaller compounding factors are the slow command range (lin_vel_x up to 0.5 m/s before iteration 6000, slow targets are known to favour shuffles), the keep_ankle_pitch_zero_in_air term that pays its maximum when both feet stay planted, and the feet_regulation term whose exponential length scale of 1.1 m makes it a near constant penalty on swing foot velocity that fights the clearance objective.

### 2.7 What phase 1 achieved, and the new failure it exposed (added 2026-07-20 after run 2026-07-20_07-52-14)

Two things happened between the recording diagnosed above and this run. Phase 1 was applied to the reward set, and, separately, the permanent shank into foot self penetration recorded in ../context/brs_gait.md was found and removed, which restored every contact keyed term to working order for the first time in this work stream. The results must therefore be read as the first honest measurement of the phase 1 reward design rather than as a continuation of the earlier series.

Judged on its own terms phase 1 succeeded completely. Single support occupancy went from essentially zero to at least ninety one percent, the air time term went from 2e-5 to 3.43 per second, posture holds at an RMS torso tilt of 9.1 degrees, base height sits within 2.8 cm of the 1.14 m target, velocity tracking reaches 0.886 of its kernel, and episodes now run to the twenty second timeout in ninety seven percent of cases against a low height termination rate that fell from 0.95 to 0.03. The shuffle, the collapse and the double support lock are all gone.

What replaced them is a different degeneracy. Inverting the logged rates through the weight as reward rate per second basis gives no_fly 0.909, which forces single support occupancy between 0.909 and 1.0 with flight below 0.018, and gives feet_air_time 0.274, which after correcting for the 0.803 command gate implies that the minimum in mode time averages 0.34 to 0.38 s against a clamp at 0.4 s and therefore that each single support phase lasts between 1.4 and 3.3 seconds, most plausibly about 2.5. The robot is not failing to step, it is stepping about six times more slowly than a human, roughly 0.2 to 0.4 steps per second against a natural 1.8. Meanwhile the clearance term supplies 0.697 from the single airborne foot, which at the 0.08 m target implies a foot speed of 0.86 m/s against a base speed near 0.25, so that foot is being waved rather than carried. The video corroborates all of this independently, the robot stands on one leg with the other extended forward and clear of the ground and holds that configuration without a swap through a 2.56 second window.

The cause is that all three gait terms are maximised by exactly this posture, and the arithmetic is decisive. Modelling a cycle as two single support phases of length T_ss and two double support phases of T_ds, and allowing the swing foot either to pass through the clearance band or to park inside it, the park and hold strategy at T_ss 2.5 s pays 3.62 from air time, 14.71 from no_fly and 13.66 from clearance for a total of 31.99 per second, which reproduces the logged 3.43, 13.63 and 13.94 totalling 31.01 to within three percent and so validates the model. A genuine walk at T_ss 0.40 s pays 1.61, 12.00 and 5.02 totalling 18.62. Walking therefore costs 13.3 per second, sixteen percent of the 85.6 per second gross income, and with the adaptive learning rate already settled at 3e-5 and mean reward flat since iteration 2658 that barrier will not be crossed by further training.

Each term prefers the hold for its own reason, and the reasons must be kept separate because the remedies differ. no_fly carries no grace window, so every instant of double support is unpaid and the term strictly prefers fewer transitions. feet_air_time_positive_biped clamps at its threshold instead of peaking there, so its mean over a phase rises monotonically toward 0.4 as the phase lengthens, 0.20 at 0.4 s against 0.38 at 2.5 s, and each swap additionally resets both accumulators and costs a dip, so alternation is penalised twice over. foot_clearance_reward_v2 is stance agnostic and velocity multiplied, so a foot parked at the target height and waved collects the kernel continuously whereas a real swing crosses the band for only about forty five percent of a far shorter phase. The common structural fault is that not one term in the set has a value that depends on the number of steps taken.

A sweep of the candidate repairs against both strategies settles the sequencing. A 0.2 s grace window on no_fly leaves the hold ahead by 9.9 per second. That plus a peaked air time kernel leaves it ahead by 7.7. The ranking inverts only when the clearance term stops paying a parked foot, and that gate applied alone inverts it by 0.32 per second, which is inside noise, whereas the three together give walking a margin near 9 per second. The clearance term is thus necessary but not sufficient, and the three must move together.

One corroborating observation belongs on record because it is the general signature to watch for. Across training rew_foot_clearance climbed from 0.08 to 13.94 while every effort and quality penalty worsened monotonically, action smoothness from -0.22 to -1.65, joint torque from -0.12 to -1.25, angular velocity from -0.06 to -0.41 and flat orientation from -0.30 to -0.88. An agent paying steadily rising costs in order to farm one rising term is hacking that term rather than acquiring a skill.

Finally, the relation to section 2.2 must be stated plainly rather than left implicit. That section argued the original sparse air time term was an exploration barrier and replaced it with the dense variant, and that judgement was correct and the change worked. The dense term bought single support and has now saturated. This is a curriculum transition rather than a reversal, and what must be added is a term that prices step events, which the dense term by construction cannot.

## 3. What the literature prescribes

The survey (full notes in ../context/literature.md cluster 7) converges on four families of technique, ordered here from cheapest to heaviest.

1. Minimal dense contact shaping. van Marum et al. (arXiv:2404.19173) train robust standing and walking on Digit with a single foot contact reward, a touchdown regulated air time term, orientation weighted above velocity, and explicitly no clock, reporting that this family is the most reliable and requires no tuning. The IsaacLab G1 reference config embodies the same ratios, dense positive biped air time at one quarter of the linear tracking weight, flat orientation at parity with it, joint deviation penalties at one tenth of it on the non sagittal joints, and a termination penalty worth about 1.3 seconds of perfect tracking income.
2. Symmetry enforcement. Mittal et al. (arXiv:2403.04359) compare mirror loss and symmetric data augmentation on PPO, and Yu et al. (SIGGRAPH 2018) established the mirror loss for reference free symmetric gaits. Both mechanisms exist in the vendored rsl_rl behind symmetry_cfg.
3. Periodic clock rewards. Siekmann et al. (arXiv:2011.01387) span all common bipedal gaits with phase interval costs on swing foot force and stance foot velocity, and the repo already contains an unused Walk These Ways style GaitReward class implementing this construction, needing only a gait parameter command term. van Marum argues clocks impede disturbance rejection, so this family is a second resort if the clock free stack undershoots on gait regularity.
4. Contact scheduling. Added 2026-07-20, since the failure of run 2026-07-20_07-52-14 is one that families 1 to 3 above do not address. Humanoid-Gym (Gu et al., arXiv:2404.05695) defines a periodic stance mask over two double support and two single support phases per cycle and rewards the measured contacts for matching it, at weight 1.0 which is the largest term in their set, with the clock pair supplied as a policy input. This is the only surveyed construction that states WHEN each foot should be down, and it cannot be farmed by holding because holding is off schedule by definition. Its cost is the clock input and van Marum's disturbance rejection objection.
5. Imitation style rewards. AMP (arXiv:2104.02180) and HumanMimic (arXiv:2309.14225) obtain human likeness from retargeted mocap through adversarial style rewards, at the price of dataset preparation, retargeting to a 10 DOF sole foot biped, and adversarial training stability. This is the escalation path if reference free shaping proves insufficient, and the LIPM guided stability reward already planned in /ws/plans/LIPM_REWARD.md is a lighter model based intermediate.

Two structural facts about van Marum's own table, retrieved in full on 2026-07-20 and recorded in ../context/literature.md cluster 8, bear directly on the phase 1b design below and were not visible in the earlier paraphrase. Their feet airtime term is sparse and touchdown triggered, the sum over feet of (t_air - 0.4) times a touchdown indicator, and it carries the largest weight in their reward, so it pays for step events and pays nothing whatever to a robot that holds a foot up without landing it. Their feet contact term carries a 0.2 s grace window, so brief double support during weight transfer is not punished. This configuration inverted both choices, paying single contact occupancy densely and heavily while paying nothing at all for a landing. One caution belongs beside this, neither their term nor Humanoid-Gym's mask rewards the step count directly, and van Marum's (t_air - 0.4) in isolation pays more for one long hold followed by a landing than for several short swings, so its protection comes from being touchdown gated in combination with the grace window rather than from the term alone.

### Phase 1, repair the reward and termination structure

Before the table, the conversion basis must be stated, because the reference weights cannot be copied literally. The RewardManager computes value = func × weight × dt (reward_manager.py:144), so for any term whose function saturates at 1 the weight is exactly the reward rate per second, and weights are therefore comparable across configurations of differing control rates without any dt bookkeeping. This configuration pays linear tracking at 50 per second against the G1 reference's 1.0 and van Marum's 0.15 plus 0.15, so the G1 weights map onto this scale by a factor of 50 and van Marum's by a factor of about 167. Terms that fire once per event rather than continuously, notably is_terminated, must instead be converted through the income they forfeit, since for those the dt multiplication makes the raw penalty weight × dt rather than a rate.

The termination penalty deserves its arithmetic in full, since it is the one entry where copying the reference verbatim inverts the intent. G1 terminates at weight −200 with a step of 0.02 s, a raw penalty of 4.0, and G1 earns at most 3.0 per second of perfect combined tracking, so its termination costs about 1.3 seconds of ideal income, or about 6.7 percent of a perfect 20 second episode. This configuration earns at most 65 per second combined and steps at 0.01 s, so reproducing that same 1.3 seconds of forfeited income requires a raw penalty of about 85, hence a weight near −8000, which lands at about 6 percent of a perfect episode and cross checks against the G1 fraction. Taking the literal −200 instead would yield a raw penalty of 2.0, roughly three hundredths of a second of tracking income, which is indistinguishable from no penalty at all. One caveat belongs on record, a single step penalty of −85 against a typical per step reward near 0.65 is a large spike that may inflate value loss variance early in training, so if Loss/value_function degrades relative to the baseline, halve the weight to −4000 and re examine.

The feet_regulation row also deserves its arithmetic, because its two parameters are coupled and changing the length scale alone would silently disable the term rather than focus it. The gate is exp(−(body_z − foot_radius) / scale), so with the imported foot_radius of 0.03 a grounded foot reports a clearance of 0.094 m rather than 0, and tightening the scale to 0.03 would evaluate that as exp(−3.13) = 0.044, retaining only about four percent of the configured weight where the penalty is supposed to bite hardest. ### Phase 1b, price cadence and close the parked foot exploit (added 2026-07-20)

The organising principle is that the reward set contains no term whose value depends on the number of steps taken, so the optimiser is free to drive the transition count towards zero while every gait term keeps paying. Three changes restore that dependence, and the sweep recorded in section 2.7 shows they must be taken together, since no one of them inverts the ranking by a margin outside noise. All three are new functions in the project mdp package rather than edits to existing ones, because feet_air_time_positive_biped belongs to the shared IsaacLab tree and because the clearance change alters what the term means, which per the workspace rules on backwards compatibility warrants a v3 rather than a default carrying argument.

| Change | Now | Proposed | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| clearance, parked foot income | foot_clearance_reward_v2, kernel times tanh(foot speed), stance agnostic | gate the term on the swing being genuine, either by multiplying the kernel by a peaked function of the foot's air age centred on the target swing duration, or by tracking a reference swing height profile as arXiv:2512.01996 does | a parked and waved foot currently collects 13.66 per second of the 31.99 that the hold earns, and removing it is the single necessary condition for the ranking to invert |
| air time shape | clamp(min in mode time, 0.4) | replace the clamp with a Gaussian peaked at 0.4 s of width about 0.15 s | the clamp is flat above the threshold and its per phase mean rises with phase length, so long holds pay more, a peak makes 0.4 s the argmax rather than a plateau floor |
| no_fly grace window | pays only during single contact | pay if single contact occurred at least once in the preceding 0.2 s, van Marum's construction | without it every instant of double support is unpaid, and since no walk avoids double support the term prices transitions themselves |
| new touchdown reward | absent | sum over feet of (t_air - 0.4) at touchdown, weight set by the income conversion below | restores a term whose value depends on step events, van Marum weights this above every other term in their set |

The weight for the touchdown term follows the income forfeiture conversion already used for the termination penalty, not a literal transfer. At the target cadence of two touchdowns per second, the term should be worth enough to cover the walking deficit with margin, and the deficit after the other three changes is about 9 per second in walking's favour, so a modest weight suffices and the term's role is to hold the cadence up rather than to create the incentive by itself. Begin at a weight that pays roughly 3 to 5 per second at the target cadence and raise it only if the measured cadence undershoots.

The residual risk is that the peaked air time kernel reintroduces the valley of section 2.2.2, since a policy whose swings are far from 0.4 s earns little. That risk is acceptable now and was not acceptable then, because the robot already holds single support for seconds at a time and therefore approaches the peak from above rather than from zero, which is the descending side of the kernel and is monotone in the right direction. If the cadence nonetheless collapses back towards holding, widen the kernel before weakening it.

Sequencing note recorded 2026-07-20. The contact schedule described in the next paragraph as the escalation has been wired and enabled first, at the user's direction, as an overnight experiment ahead of phase 1b. The reasoning is sound, the clock is the one intervention that attacks the missing cadence dependence directly rather than by removing income from the hold, so if it works it also tells us how much of phase 1b is actually needed. The wiring is in brs_base_env_cfg.py, a gait_command term in CommandsCfg at 1.0 Hz with offset 0.5 and stance duration 0.6, a gait_phase observation in both the policy and critic groups of ObservationsCfg, and rew_gait in RewardsCfg at weight 40. The weight was set by reimplementing compute_contact_targets and both reward halves over one cycle, which gives the hold -0.488 and a matching walk -0.079, a discriminating gap of 0.408 rather than the 0.5 estimated by hand, so 40 yields 16.3 per second against the 13.3 per second barrier while 30 would have yielded 12.3 and fallen short. Note that this experiment leaves the three phase 1b defects standing, so it tests whether an explicit schedule can override them rather than whether they are repaired.

If phase 1b does not produce alternation, the escalation is the contact schedule of family 4 rather than more weight tuning. The GaitReward class in mdp/rewards.py already implements the Walk These Ways form of Siekmann's construction and needs only a gait parameter command supplying frequency, offset and duration, and a constant command of about 1.3 Hz with offset 0.5 and duration 0.55 expresses anti phase walking directly. That family is the only one surveyed that specifies when each foot should be down, and it therefore cannot be farmed by holding.

### Phase 2, symmetry

Enable the rsl_rl symmetry pathway with both mechanisms, data augmentation for sample efficiency and the mirror loss (coefficient near 0.5) to shape the optimum itself. The joint mirror map in the trained DOF order [HipRollR, HipRollL, HipPitchR, HipPitchL, KneePitchR, KneePitchL, AnkleRollR, AnkleRollL, AnklePitchR, AnklePitchL] is, swap each R and L pair, negate the HipRoll, HipPitch, and AnkleRoll pairs, keep the sign of the KneePitch and AnklePitch pairs, this follows from the reflection algebra applied to the URDF axes and matches the mirrored reset ranges already in the config. The observation mirror negates the y linear velocity, the x and z angular velocities, the y gravity component, the y and yaw command components, applies the joint map to joint positions, velocities, and last actions, and must be applied per frame across the 10 step stacked history. The augmentation function receives the flattened policy and critic groups, so implement it once against the ObservationsCfg layout and add an assertion on group widths to survive future config edits.

### Phase 3, escalation if gait regularity or human likeness still lags

First option, activate the existing GaitReward with a constant gait command term (frequency ≈ 1.3 Hz, offset 0.5, duration ≈ 0.55, force and velocity sigmas tuned on a short run), Siekmann's construction with these parameters expresses anti phase walking directly and the class already exists in mdp/rewards.py, accepting van Marum's caveat that clocks trade some disturbance rejection. Second option, adopt the LIPM guided stability reward already planned in /ws/plans/LIPM_REWARD.md as a model based stability shaping term. Third option, AMP or HumanMimic style adversarial imitation from retargeted human walking data, the heaviest path and only warranted if the reference free stack plateaus below the desired aesthetics.

## 5. Validation protocol

Evaluate every phase with the play script's DataLogger, setting FEET_LINK_NAMES to "Link6[LR]" since the default targets TRON1, and judge against these quantitative gates rather than visual impression alone. Mean swing air time per foot at or above 0.3 s at 0.5 m/s, sole apex clearance at or above 6 cm from the feet position logs, projected gravity xy norm below 0.15 in steady walking, base height within 3 cm of 1.14, left to right air time and peak contact force asymmetry below ten percent, and linear velocity tracking error within the pre change baseline. The feet contact force and feet velocity channels added to the DataLogger provide the air time, impulse, and slide measurements directly, and the existing dashboard joint order in ../context/sd_brs_urdf.md applies unchanged.

Three gates are added on 2026-07-20, because run 2026-07-20_07-52-14 passed every gate above while holding a one legged stance, which shows the original set does not discriminate cadence. Step frequency at or above 1.2 steps per second at a commanded 0.5 m/s, equivalently a single support phase below 0.8 s. Double support occupancy between five and twenty five percent, since a value near zero now indicates the hold rather than an athletic gait. Swing foot sole clearance that returns below 2 cm at least once per second, which distinguishes a foot that swings from a foot that parks. All three read directly off the contact force channels already logged. Two cheap TensorBoard side checks apply without any rollout, feet_air_time divided by its weight should sit near 0.2 rather than near 0.4, since the dense term's per phase mean at human cadence is half its clamp, and rew_no_fly divided by its weight should sit near 0.8 rather than near 0.91, since a real walk spends time in double support.

## 6. Risks and sequencing notes

The damping change alters the plant that the old checkpoints were trained on, so no resumed run is comparable across phase 0, start fresh. The default pose change alters the meaning of joint_pos_rel and last_action, likewise incomparable, which is why phases 0 and 1 should be a single new baseline run. The orientation termination can suppress early exploration if set too tight, 0.8 rad is deliberately permissive and can be tightened by curriculum later. The single largest scientific risk is that several defects (clearance target, air time barrier, underdamped hips) each independently suffice to produce the observed shuffle, so if the phase 0 plus 1 run still shuffles, ablate by checking the per term reward logs in TensorBoard, the episode reward decomposition will show whether the air time and clearance terms have become positive income, which is the signature of stepping being discovered.
