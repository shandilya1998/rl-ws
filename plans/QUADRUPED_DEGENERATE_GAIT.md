## 1. Introduction

The quadruped introduced by `plans/QUADRUPED_INTEGRATION_PLAN.md` and recorded as implemented on 2026-08-19 has now been trained, and the resulting policy does not walk on four legs. The run at `IsaacLab/logs/rsl_rl/quadruped_flat/2026-08-25_10-15-10` produces, by the visual evidence of `videos/train/rl-video-step-410000.mp4`, a degenerate gait in which the machine supports itself on two legs while continuing to track the commanded base velocity, and the dumped configuration that produced it is `params/env.yaml` in the same directory. The behaviour is degenerate in the precise sense that it satisfies the reward the environment states while failing the objective the reward was written to express, which is the classical shape of a specification failure rather than of a training failure, and it is therefore the reward configuration rather than the learning algorithm that this document interrogates first.

Three facts about the run must be established before anything is inferred from it, because each of them corrects an assumption a reader would otherwise carry in. The first is that the run did not train on flat ground. The dumped configuration names `QuadrupedPFBlindRoughEnvCfg` at `params/env.yaml:1` and carries a six by sixty four terrain generator with stairs, slopes and waves at `params/env.yaml:245-341`, the directory name `quadruped_flat` being the fixed experiment string of `environments/environments/tasks/locomotion/agents/quadruped_rsl_rl_ppo_cfg.py:20` rather than a record of the task launched. The second is that the video the observation rests upon is not a snapshot of a converged policy. One unit of the TensorBoard step axis is one proximal policy optimisation iteration, the recorded video counter advances by `num_steps_per_env` of twenty five per iteration, and step 410000 is therefore iteration 16400 of a run that had reached 17267 of a configured thirty thousand when this investigation read it. The third is that the run was in decline at that point rather than at rest, mean return having peaked at 646 near iteration 5275 and fallen to 234 by iteration 17267. The run has since stopped, at iteration 17443 with a mean return of 253, short of its configured thirty thousand and by no mechanism this document has established, so the curves quoted throughout, extracted at iteration 17267, cover 98.99 per cent of the run that exists and none of the run that was planned.

The measured evidence sharpens the observation in a way the video alone cannot. Every reward term of this framework is logged as its per episode accumulated sum divided by the fixed constant `episode_length_s` of twenty, so a logged series may be converted back to the physical quantity the term integrates by multiplying by twenty and dividing by the product of the term's weight and the control period of 0.02 seconds. The extracted series, the summary statistics over them and the two scripts that perform the extraction and the conversion are preserved at `../context/artefacts/quadruped_flat-2026-08-25_10-15-10/`, since the log tree that holds the event file is transient and every quantitative claim below is taken from that dump rather than from the tree. Performing that conversion over the last three hundred logged iterations gives a mean trunk tilt of 10.2 degrees, a figure that excludes a sustained rear onto the hind pair, since a trunk pitched thirty degrees would register a projected gravity magnitude of 0.5 against the 0.178 measured. What the same conversion does show is a root mean square vertical velocity of 1.290 metres per second against 0.313 early in training, a root mean square roll and pitch rate of 2.15 radians per second against 1.18, and a torso contact termination rate risen from four per cent to nineteen. The machine is therefore not standing on two legs with a level back, it is bouncing violently on a reduced set of contacts while holding its trunk approximately level, which is the hopping mode of the degenerate gait taxonomy rather than the rearing mode, and the distinction matters because the two have different remedies.

The implemented quadruped is closely comparable to the Unitree Go1, which makes the Go1 the natural yardstick throughout. Both machines carry twelve actuated joints in an abduction, hip and knee arrangement over segments of 0.213 metres, both stand near 0.3 metres, and the quadruped's declared torque ceilings of 23.622511 and 35.238 newton metres match the GO-M8010-6 ratings of 23.7 and 35.55 newton metres that the Go1 carries to within one per cent, which is the evidence that the design derives from that actuator family. Isaac Lab ships a Go1 velocity task whose reward configuration is public, tested and known to produce a trot, at `IsaacLab/source/isaaclab_tasks/isaaclab_tasks/manager_based/locomotion/velocity/config/go1/rough_env_cfg.py`, and the comparison of the two configurations is therefore a comparison against a working control rather than against an abstraction. Section 2 performs that comparison on both the physical and the configurational axes, section 3 establishes what the literature prescribes and what it reports about this class of failure, section 4 assembles the candidate root causes and their failure mechanisms, and section 5 specifies the two experiments that would discriminate among them.

The prior belief this investigation was handed, that actuator parameterisation is not the primary cause because two legs demonstrably suffice to balance the machine and to hold its commanded velocity, is examined in section 2.1 and is upheld there, and upheld more strongly than the belief itself proposed, since every actuator quantity computed comes out close between the two robots or favours the Go1 where it differs.

## 2. Implemented Quadruped Against the Unitree Go1

### 2.1 Morphological and Actuator Attributes

The mass distribution is where the two machines part company most visibly, and it parts in a direction opposite to the one intuition suggests. The quadruped totals 16.077077 kilogrammes over a trunk of 0.674397 (`environments/environments/assets/urdf/quadruped/quadruped.urdf:6`) and four legs of 3.850670 each, so the trunk carries 4.195 per cent of the machine, where the Go1 totals 13.099448 kilogrammes over a 5.204 kilogramme trunk (`unitree_ros/robots/go1_description/urdf/go1.urdf:436`) and carries 39.727 per cent of itself in that trunk, a factor of 9.5 between the two fractions. The intuition this invites, that a robot carrying almost all its mass in its legs can shift its centre of mass by swinging them and so buy itself two legged balance, is refuted by direct computation. Ninety point six seven per cent of the quadruped's leg mass sits in three cylindrical actuator housings at or within a few centimetres of the joint axes (`quadruped.urdf:28,55,85`) rather than out along the limb, so sweeping the rear pair across its entire joint range moves the whole body centre of mass by 0.0016 metres, less than a fifth of the 0.0078 metres the Go1's lighter but distally distributed pair achieves over the same manoeuvre. Neither figure approaches the 0.155 to 0.207 metre fore aft overhang that a static two legged stance would require either machine to close, so ballast shifting is not the mechanism on either robot, and the heavy legs that make this design unusual confer no advantage toward the behaviour observed.

What does distinguish the two machines is the aspect ratio of the support polygon. Forward kinematics through the declared joint origins places the quadruped's foot at 0.20188, minus 0.14200, minus 0.28711 metres relative to its base at the nominal crouch of `environments/environments/assets/config/quadruped_identified_cfg.py:90-94`, giving a stance rectangle 0.362 metres fore to aft on a straight leg of 0.4839 metres, a ratio of 0.748. The Go1's equivalent ratio is 0.981, its hips being spaced very nearly its own leg length apart. The quadruped's footprint is therefore 23.8 per cent shorter, relative to its own leg, than the Go1's, and its lateral stance 16.9 per cent narrower by the same normalisation, which is the one purely geometric fact in this survey that favours collapsing the working footprint toward a line and balancing along it. Its abduction range of plus and minus 1.0 radians about a nominal of exactly zero (`quadruped.urdf:79`) exceeds the Go1's plus and minus 0.863 about a nominal offset of 0.1 (`go1.urdf:471`, `IsaacLab/source/isaaclab_assets/isaaclab_assets/robots/unitree.py:123-128`), and its knee range of 2.4 radians exceeds the Go1's 1.93 by 24 per cent, so the quadruped enjoys more kinematic freedom in exactly the two axes an atypical stance would draw upon.

The actuator comparison does not distinguish the machines at all, which is the substantive finding rather than an absence of one. The quadruped's identified gains give natural frequencies of 5.96, 6.37 and 9.18 hertz at damping ratios of 0.485, 0.483 and 0.600 across abduction, hip and knee, against 5.10, 5.68 and 6.49 hertz at 0.321, 0.357 and 0.408 for the Go1 read through the A1 and Go2 analytic comparator, the Go1's own configuration being a learned actuator network at `unitree.py:32-44` that declares no stiffness from which a frequency could be taken. Every joint of both machines sits within a single octave of every other, so response bandwidth is not a discriminator. Nor is torque capacity. The tightest joint on either machine under two legged support, its own abduction, clears its requirement by between 2.92 and 5.66 times on the quadruped and by 3.33 times on the Go1, and every other joint clears by a wider margin, so a two legged stance is comfortably admissible on both by this measure and forbidden on neither. The joint torque penalty rises by a factor of 2.22 to 2.95 on the quadruped and 2.00 on the Go1 in moving from four legged to two legged support, a real cost in the right direction but a small one, of order a thousandth of a reward unit per step against a tracking term whose ceiling is 0.5.

## 3. Literature Survey

### 3.1 Reward shaping practice for quadruped locomotion

The reward vocabulary every configuration in this workspace speaks descends from one source. Rudin, Hoeller, Reist and Hutter established the base table that the massively parallel training regime made practical, tracking linear velocity at 1.0 and angular at 0.5 through exponential kernels, vertical velocity at minus 2.0, roll and pitch rate at minus 0.05, torque at minus 1e-5, joint acceleration at minus 2.5e-7, action rate at minus 0.01, air time at 1.0 and collision at minus 1.0, with base height, orientation and joint position limits present but unweighted [1]. Two features of that work govern everything built on it. The first is the pair of curricula, terrain level and command range, promoted on a tracking criterion rather than on a fixed schedule. The second is the `only_positive_rewards` flag, which clips the summed step reward at zero before the termination term is added, and which is the mechanism that makes the comparatively large penalty weights safe, since a policy facing a heavily penalised state can otherwise reduce its accumulated loss by ending the episode sooner, an arithmetic a return floored at zero removes.

Isaac Lab reproduced that table in its manager based velocity task and, decisively for this investigation, reproduced it without the clip, an absence search of `IsaacLab/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/managers/reward_manager.py` finding no clamp on the summed reward anywhere [2]. Its Unitree ports narrow the table in a consistent direction, raising tracking to 1.5 and 0.75, tightening torque to minus 2e-4, cutting air time from 0.125 to 0.01 on rough terrain and restoring it to 0.25 on flat, disabling undesired contacts entirely and halving the action scale to 0.25 [2]. The ANYmal line diverges from the Unitree line on precisely the terms this quadruped adds, leaving base height and orientation unweighted on rough terrain where the Unitree flat variants weight orientation at minus 2.5, a split `context/literature.md` cluster 17 already records and which the present survey confirms extends to the flat variants as well. Unitree's own `unitree_rl_gym` Go2 configuration corroborates the torque figure of minus 2e-4 to four significant figures across two codebases sharing no source, and adds a joint position limit weight of minus 10.0 that the base table does not carry [3].

The Unitree line's alternative reward design abandons air time altogether. HIMLoco carries orientation at minus 0.2, base height at minus 1.0, foot clearance at minus 0.01 and joint power at minus 2e-5, with air time, collision, torque and joint position limits all present as scored terms set to zero, and it sets `only_positive_rewards` to false [4]. DreamWaQ's published table is identical to HIMLoco's in every scale save one addition, a power distribution penalty at minus 1e-5 on the variance of instantaneous power across the twelve motors, a coincidence between two independently published tables that the present survey establishes and that neither work's text explains [5]. These two configurations obtain gait quality from posture, height and clearance rather than from cadence, which is a coherent alternative to the air time mechanism rather than a variation upon it.

The third family conditions the gait explicitly. Margolis and Agrawal train one policy over a family of commanded gait parameters, frequency, footswing height and body posture among them, and their effective reward retires the flat orientation and base height penalties to zero in favour of a commanded orientation tracking term, drops vertical velocity two orders of magnitude to minus 0.02, zeroes air time outright, and carries as its two largest weights a pair of shaped contact force and contact velocity terms at 4.0 each, gated by a per foot commanded phase [6]. Extreme Parkour departs further still, rewarding progress toward a terrain relative goal rather than a commanded body velocity, carrying no air time term at all, and pricing the abduction degree of freedom directly through a `hip_pos` term at minus 0.5, which the present configuration's `pen_abad_deviation` follows [7]. RMA's ten term composite prices work rather than squared torque and carries a foot slip term at 0.8, and anneals every penalty coefficient from near zero to its tabulated value over training rather than annealing the task [8]. Lee, Hwangbo, Wellhausen, Koltun and Hutter place their reward specification in a supplement this survey could not obtain, and no weight from that work is quoted here, though its architecture, a privileged teacher distilled into a proprioceptive student over a history of observations, is the ancestor of the critic and history arrangement this repository uses [9]. A recent Isaac Lab Go1 configuration is worth recording for two figures at the extreme of the surveyed range, a joint position limit weight of minus 100.0 and an explicit gait term at 10.0 accompanied by an air time variance penalty at minus 1.0, the largest single weight in its table being the gait term [10].

Three observations follow from the assembled tables that no single work states. Joint acceleration sits at exactly minus 2.5e-7 in every legged_gym descendant surveyed without exception, which marks it as a figure copied rather than retuned. The torque weight separates into two bands with no intermediate value, a legged_gym band at minus 1e-5 and a Unitree hardware band at minus 1e-4 to minus 2e-4, and three unrelated ports converge independently on the latter for machines of this mass. And only three of fourteen surveyed configurations carry a non zero joint position limit weight, all three of them hardware targeted Unitree builds, which suggests the term is carried against the risk of a real actuator being driven into a mechanical stop rather than against any simulated failure mode.

### 3.2 The emergence of degenerate gaits and the remedies adopted

The formal reason a velocity tracking objective admits a degenerate gait is that it is a proxy, and Skalse, Howe, Krasheninnikov and Krueger prove that a proxy is unhackable with respect to a true objective only under a condition so strong that, over the full space of stochastic policies, two mutually unhackable rewards require one of them to be constant [11]. Applied here, nothing in the algebra of a velocity error term distinguishes the four beat support pattern of a trot from the two beat pattern of a hop, both tracking a commanded speed equally well over the interval the reward integrates. Krakovna and colleagues name the general phenomenon specification gaming [12], and Pan, Bhatia and Steinhardt supply the empirical complement, that a more capable agent exploits a misspecified reward more severely and may undergo a sharp behavioural transition as training time increases, a caution whose transfer to legged locomotion is an inference rather than a demonstrated result, since none of their four environments is a locomotion task [13].

Within legged locomotion the sharpest primary account of collapse to a degenerate posture is Reda, Tao and van de Panne, whose ablation over survival bonus magnitude is explicit, a bonus of zero making gait discovery too hard while a large bonus is exploited by a character that balances and never steps, their own summary stating that values too small or too large lead to local minima corresponding to falling forward and standing still respectively [14]. The closest precedent for a reduction in the number of support limbs is van Marum and colleagues, who report directly that on a humanoid a reward built from velocity tracking with no explicit per foot contact term converges to two footed hopping, and who supply the single foot contact term with a 0.2 second grace window that repairs it [15]. Fu, Kumar, Malik and Pathak establish the complementary result from the other direction, that an energy minimisation term is what turns an otherwise undirected velocity objective into a family of biomechanically recognisable gaits, so that the absence or underweighting of such a term leaves gait shape unconstrained exactly as the unhackability argument predicts [16].

The literature on deliberate quadruped bipedalism sharpens rather than dissolves the puzzle. Xiao, Dong, Lam and colleagues built a system specifically to make a quadruped walk on two legs and state plainly that the transition is extremely challenging, the robot losing half its ground contacts while its body axis rotates from horizontal to vertical and its centre of mass rises, requiring a purpose built centre of mass and centre of pressure estimator feeding a dedicated balance reward [17]. Zhang, Corcodel and Zhao require a risk adaptive distributional formulation to stabilise the same behaviour on a Go2 [18], and Peng, Bao, Humphreys, Delfaki, Kanoulas and Zhou require adversarial motion priors over a bipedal reference trajectory, on the stated ground that the bipedal locomotion mode for quadruped robots remains unverified without such machinery [19]. If that apparatus is what a clean rear demands, then a policy that has arrived at two legged support without any of it has almost certainly not found a rear, which is exactly what the 10.2 degree measured trunk tilt of section 1 independently establishes.

The remedies divide into families of differing cost. Periodic contact reward composition gates foot force penalties during swing and foot velocity penalties during stance behind a phase clock spanning standing, walking, hopping, running and skipping [20], and central pattern generators embed the oscillator in the action space directly [21], both at the cost of committing to a contact schedule in advance, a commitment van Marum and colleagues argue against on the ground that an undefined clock during standing or disturbance recovery constrains foot motion exactly when the robot most needs it free [15]. Symmetry augmentation and mirror losses improve gait quality [22][23], but a caution this investigation records and which no surveyed source states is that the symmetry group these works enforce is the left and right sagittal reflection, and a quadruped supporting itself symmetrically on one pair of limbs is invariant under exactly that reflection, so the fault axis here is fore and aft and symmetry augmentation cannot by itself forbid it. Reference motion imitation and adversarial motion priors supply the missing preference over gait shape directly, at the cost of a reference clip and a discriminator [24][25]. Explicit per foot contact and duty balance is the most directly targeted family, van Marum's contact term and Humanoid-Gym's stance mask comparing measured contact against a clock defined schedule being its two working constructions [15][26]. And the positive reward clip of Rudin and colleagues is the framework level device that makes a penalty dominated budget safe [1].

| Pathology | Mechanism | Remedy family | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Support pattern collapse to two limbs | velocity tracking is agnostic to which limbs achieve it, nothing prices the number of grounded feet | per foot contact or duty term, gait phase conditioning, quadrupedal motion prior | [15][17][20][26] |
| Pronking and synchronised hopping | energy term absent or underweighted leaves gait shape unconstrained | energy or power term at a weight an ablation confirms is felt | [16] |

| Mirror symmetric but fore aft degenerate gait | sagittal mirror losses constrain the wrong axis | a support pattern term orthogonal to the symmetry axis | [22][23], this document's synthesis |

### 3.3 Augmented survey, the published budgets against which this configuration is judged, added 2026-09-02

The first two subsections establish what the reward vocabulary is and what the literature reports about its failure modes. This subsection supplies the numbers that section 5 needs in order to choose weights, drawn from every Isaac Lab quadruped configuration on disk and from four published tables retrieved in full rather than paraphrased.

The Isaac Lab Unitree line is narrower than the earlier pass established. The Go1, Go2 and A1 rough configurations are identical in every reward weight, all three setting linear tracking at 1.5 and angular at 0.75, air time at 0.01, torque at minus 2e-4 and joint acceleration at minus 2.5e-7, disabling undesired contacts outright, and inheriting vertical velocity at minus 2.0, roll and pitch rate at minus 0.05 and action rate at minus 0.01 from the shared base [2]. Summing the regularisation magnitudes against the linear tracking weight gives a ratio of 1.387, of which the air time term contributes 0.0067. The corresponding flat variants raise flat orientation to minus 2.5 and air time to 0.25, taking the ratio to 3.15. The ANYmal family diverges exactly as cluster 17 of `../context/literature.md` records, all three of b, c and d carrying no reward override at all and therefore inheriting tracking at 1.0 and 0.5, undesired contacts left enabled at minus 1.0 where the Unitree line removes it, torque at the legged_gym figure of minus 1e-5 and air time at 0.125, for a ratio of 3.185, and their flat variants reach 8.560 on the strength of an orientation penalty five times the Unitree figure [2].

Isaac Lab's Spot configuration is not in the Rudin lineage at all and had not previously been surveyed here, and it is the most instructive comparator this document has found, because it is the only configuration on disk whose gait terms are its largest. Its two tracking rewards are custom exponential kernels weighted 5.0 each, its air time reward is itself weighted 5.0, and a clock free contact synchrony term is weighted 10.0, the largest single weight in its table, so it devotes 4.742 times its own tracking prize to shaping and regularisation together, the opposite proportion from the Unitree line [2]. Margolis and Agrawal's own table, retrieved in full in this pass, is more extreme still, setting velocity tracking at 0.02 and yaw at 0.01 against six gait dependent terms of which footswing height tracking alone, at minus 0.6, outweighs tracking thirtyfold, for a combined ratio near 74 [6]. That retrieval also corrects this document's own section 3.1, which quotes a contact force and contact velocity weight of 4.0 from a narrative summary where the paper's Table 1 reads minus 0.08, and the earlier figure should be treated as superseded pending a check against the paper's PDF. A mixture of experts configuration on a Unitree Go2, new to this workspace, supplies a third convergent point at a ratio of 6.17 [29].

The pattern across nine independent configurations is that no published quadruped reward, whether already surveyed here or newly retrieved, sets its gait shaping term as small relative to tracking as this configuration does, and every one that trains a genuine four legged gait rather than a single forward heading carries at least a 1.4 fold and as much as a 74 fold multiple of shaping and regularisation against its own tracking prize.

On foot clearance the survey resolves a question of form and discovers an omission. None of the three clearance variants this workspace carries is wired into the quadruped at all, their only callers being the TRON1 and KScale biped configurations, so a robot training over 0.08 metre stairs has never priced the height a swinging foot reaches. Of the three, `foot_clearance_reward` at `rewards.py:265-316` is exactly the bounded exponential and hyperbolic tangent form Spot carries at target height 0.10 metres, standard deviation 0.05, tangent multiplier 2.0 and weight 0.5 [2], itself the bounded descendant of the unbounded quadratic HIMLoco carries at minus 0.01 [4], with one addition of the workspace's own, an optional contact gate closing the exploit of a grounded foot earning the reward by geometry. Variant two's sole geometry refinement is inert on a spherical foot, and variant three follows the phase referenced raised cosine form of Humanoid-Gym [26] and of Seo and colleagues [31], requiring the gait phase clock this configuration leaves disabled and which section 3.2 argues against [15]. A fourth term in the same module, `feet_regulation` at `rewards.py:1193-1229`, is a stance slip suppressor rather than a clearance target and reproduces term for term the foot regulation reward of the Concurrent Teacher-Student work [30], from which the mixture of experts configuration takes it at minus 0.05 [29], and it is likewise unused by this robot. Both variants one and two measure an absolute world height rather than a height above the terrain beneath the foot, a defect the code's own docstring admits at `rewards.py:362-365` and one that compounds the blind actor of RC-5.

On clock free support pattern terms the survey locates the quadruped construction the earlier pass lacked, and finds it structurally insufficient against this precise failure. Spot's `GaitReward` pairs the two diagonals explicitly, front left with rear right and front right with rear left, rewarding synchrony within a pair and opposition between pairs while reading only the contact sensor's own timers and committing to no clock [2]. The two feet run A retracts are exactly one of those pairs. But it clips its squared errors before the exponential, so once two feet's timings diverge beyond 0.2 seconds the term stops distinguishing a moderate desynchronisation from an unbounded one, and a diagonal pair held aloft together with large and roughly equal air times can still score near its ceiling. This is the same defect, a clamp that removes the gradient exactly where a runaway should be punished more, RC-1 identifies in this workspace's own air time term, now observed to recur independently in a published construction, and the observation is this document's own analysis rather than a claim its authors make. Spot's `air_time_variance_penalty` at minus 1.0 prices duty imbalance across all four feet at once, but reads the touchdown latched `last_air_time` and so shares the blindness to a limb that has stopped landing altogether [2].

On power and jitter the survey supplies the figures section 5 needs. HIMLoco's full table sets joint power in the L1 form at minus 2e-5, joint acceleration at minus 2.5e-7 and both action rate and smoothness at minus 0.01 [4], DreamWaQ's is identical save a power distribution variance term at minus 1e-5 [5], and the mixture of experts Go2 configuration converges independently on the same minus 2e-5 for power and minus 2.5e-7 for acceleration [29]. This workspace's own `joint_powers_l1` at `rewards.py:513-518` is that exact L1 form, is active on the TRON1 PF task and disabled on the SF task at the same minus 2e-5, and is absent entirely from the quadruped's reward set. On the specific question of a term to stop a policy freezing or splaying a proximal joint, the mixture of experts configuration carries a hip position deviation term at minus 0.05, introduced by its authors expressly to mitigate outward thigh abduction during rapid locomotion [29], forty times smaller than the minus 2.0 this configuration carries on the abduction axis, and Spot restricts its joint acceleration and joint velocity penalties to the hip degrees of freedom alone rather than summing across the articulation [2], which is the closest published precedent for the asymmetry `pen_abad_deviation` already applies to position but which no term here applies to velocity or acceleration.

## 4. Root Cause Analysis

The candidates below are ordered by the weight of evidence behind them, and each is stated as a mechanism that could be checked rather than as a conclusion. They are not alternatives. The account this section arrives at is that one term pays for the behaviour, no term forbids it, the terms that would restrain its consequences are a fraction of reference strength, and a curriculum then pushes the policy off whatever four legged solution it had found into a regime an unclipped return permits it to occupy.

RC-1, the gait shaping term is inverted in effect and pays for retracting a limb. The local `feet_air_time` at `environments/environments/tasks/locomotion/mdp/rewards.py:196-223` computes, at each touchdown, the quantity `last_air_time - threshold_min` clamped above at `threshold_max - threshold_min`, and sums it over feet. With `threshold_min` at 0.25 and `threshold_max` at 0.40 (`base_env_cfg.py:1168-1177`), a foot landing after less than a quarter second of flight pays a negative excess that is not floored, a foot landing after more than 0.25 pays a positive excess capped at 0.15, and a foot that never lands triggers no touchdown and pays exactly zero. The three outcomes are therefore ordered as follows, a foot cycling faster than the design cadence is worse than a foot held permanently in the air, which is in turn worse than a foot cycling slower. Converting the logged series by the factor established in section 1, the term's raw per episode value is minus 43.19 seconds early in training, minus 31.05 at the reward peak near iteration 5000 and minus 33.60 in the last three hundred iterations, so it is negative across every one of the 17267 iterations and never once enters the region it was written to reward. Because each touchdown can contribute at most minus 0.25, the late figure implies at least 134 touchdowns per twenty second episode, or 6.7 per second aggregated over the feet, so the feet are landing frequently and with air times far short of the floor. The policy's cheapest available improvement on this term is to stop landing feet, and retracting one pair removes roughly half the accrual, worth about 0.63 reward units per episode. That is a small quantity against a return of several hundred, but it is strictly positive, monotone in the number of retracted limbs, and opposed by no term of comparable structure.

RC-2, no term prices the support pattern. The two tracking terms read base linear and angular velocity alone and are indifferent to which limbs produce them [2]. `pen_undesired_contacts` meters contact and pays a raised limb nothing, `pen_abad_deviation` meters one joint of three per leg and is blind to a posture reached through hip and knee flexion, and `feet_air_time`, as RC-1 establishes, pays a raised limb zero rather than a penalty. This is the condition that makes the behaviour reachable at all, independent of what makes it attractive, and it is the condition the literature identifies as the direct cause of the same collapse on a humanoid [15].

RC-3, the penalties that would price the consequences are at six per cent of reference strength. `pen_lin_vel_z`, `pen_joint_torque` and `pen_joint_accel` are carried at the Go1's literal absolute weights against a tracking budget 16.67 times larger, so relative to the prize they oppose they are an order of magnitude weaker than on the reference machine, as section 2.2 tabulates and as `tron1-rl-isaaclab-cozum/context/quadruped.md` section 3.3 records deliberately. The measured consequence is direct. Root mean square vertical base velocity runs at 0.313 metres per second early, 0.356 at the reward peak and 1.290 in the last three hundred iterations, and root mean square roll and pitch rate at 1.18, 0.90 and 2.15 radians per second over the same windows. A gait that bounces at 1.29 metres per second root mean square is being charged almost nothing for it.

RC-4, the reward budget was transplanted from a lineage that clips and into a framework that does not. Every legged_gym descendant carrying large penalty weights sets `only_positive_rewards` [1], and Isaac Lab provides no such mechanism [2]. This configuration carries penalty weights larger than Isaac Lab's own defaults on an unclipped signed sum, which is the arithmetic under which an accumulating penalty can make an earlier termination preferable to continuing, and the measured torso contact termination rate rising from four per cent in mid training to nineteen per cent late is consistent with that arithmetic, though not by itself proof of it.

RC-5, the actor is blind to the terrain it stands on while its critic is not. The height scan appears only in the critic group (`base_env_cfg.py:335-339`), the trained class being `QuadrupedPFBlindRoughEnvCfg`, and the Go1's rough policy sees the terrain directly [2]. Two consequences follow. A policy that cannot see an approaching stair edge cannot place an unseen foot to clear it, so reducing the number of feet committed to unseen ground is a defensible strategy under any contact penalty. And the information asymmetry between an actor that cannot see the terrain and a critic that can bounds how well the value function can explain the actor's returns, which is consistent with `Loss/value_function` rising from single digits in mid training to a mean of 443.94 and a peak of 4689.9 by the end.

RC-6, the collapse is dated to the onset of the push and command curricula, not to early exploration. Mean return peaks at 646 near iteration 5275 and declines monotonically thereafter to 234, and the push force and linear command curricula leave their floor values at essentially that same iteration, 5600 by a two hundred iteration bucketed reading, where the terrain level curriculum had already begun climbing from iteration 2000 without any accompanying decline. `Policy/mean_noise_std` falls to 0.351 by iteration 1301 and then climbs back to 1.094, above its initialisation value, and `Loss/entropy` follows the same trough and recovery. A policy re expanding its exploration under a growing value error is a policy being driven off a solution it had found, not one converging onto a new one, and this dates the failure as a late collapse rather than an early local minimum. That dating is provisional, since it rests on aggregate curves rather than on a measured contact pattern, and phase 1 of section 5 exists to settle it.

RC-7, the episode never begins settled. Every reset samples up to 0.5 metres per second and radians per second on all six base axes and 0.2 radians on every joint (`base_env_cfg.py:1064-1086`), where the Go1 zeroes all of it [2]. A policy that must recover balance from an arbitrary initial condition on every episode is rewarded for whatever posture is easiest to stabilise from broadly rather than for the coordinated trot that is optimal only once settled.

RC-8, the morphology lowers the barrier without supplying a motive. The fore aft stance is 0.748 of leg length against the Go1's 0.981, and the abduction and knee ranges are wider, as section 2.1 establishes. This explains why the behaviour is available on this machine more readily than on the Go1, and explains nothing about why it is preferred.

RC-9, excluded. Actuator parameterisation is not a cause. Natural frequency, damping ratio, torque margin at two legged support and the marginal torque penalty all come out close between the two machines or favour the Go1, and the heavy leg fraction confers less centre of mass authority than the Go1's lighter legs, as section 2.1 computes. The prior belief handed to this investigation is upheld.

One measurement constrains every account above and must be carried into section 6. The mean trunk tilt is 5.82 degrees at the reward peak and 10.23 degrees in the last three hundred iterations, so the trunk is approximately level throughout and a sustained rear onto the hind pair is excluded. Whichever pair bears the load, the failure is a collapse of the support pattern under a level trunk, accompanied by violent vertical motion, which places it in the hopping family of section 3.2 rather than the rearing family.

RC-1 requires correction in its central claim. The term is no longer the `feet_air_time` of the earlier run but `feet_air_time_v2` at `rewards.py:225-254`, and `threshold_min` is now 0.0 rather than 0.25 (`base_env_cfg.py:1168-1177`), an intervention made expressly to counteract the behaviour this document investigates. That intervention removed the term's only teeth. With a floor of zero the payoff at each touchdown is the air time itself, the v2 penalty branch above `threshold_max` firing on only 0.05 per cent of run A's 25290 measured air intervals and being dead code in practice, so the term is non negative on 99.95 per cent of landings and its measured contribution is positive, plus 0.065 per landing and plus 0.733 per episode. The earlier claim that the term is negative at every logged iteration was true of the earlier configuration and is false of this one, the TensorBoard record showing it negative only across iterations 1640 to 3865 and positive at every one of the 6171 iterations from 3866 to the end of run A, and never negative at any point of run B.

The mechanism the floor of zero produces is worse than a weak term. The per foot pay rate is the air time multiplied by the landing rate, which is identically the fraction of time that foot spends off the ground, so the term is exactly blind to cadence and measures only how little time a foot spends in stance. A foot cycling at 6.6 hertz with 0.08 seconds of air earns the same rate as one cycling at 1.25 hertz with 0.40 seconds. The episode payoff reduces to 0.04 times the summed airborne duty of the feet that land, which reproduces the measurement to within the two per cent of environments the command norm gate suppresses, 0.841 predicted against 0.733 measured. The consequence is the decisive figure of this investigation. A perfect four beat trot at a duty factor of one half would pay 1.600 per episode against the observed hop's 0.733, so the entire difference between the degenerate gait and an ideal one, as scored by the only gait shaping term the configuration contains, is 0.867 reward units against a return of 636.26, that is 0.14 per cent. The term cannot steer the policy even when played perfectly.

A further property of the term must be recorded because it defeats monitoring. A retracted limb triggers no `compute_first_contact` and contributes exactly zero rather than a penalty, so once the retraction is total the term reads only the brisk cycling of the survivors and reports its healthiest values precisely when the failure is complete. It is not merely too weak to steer, it is an inverted diagnostic, and the sign flip back to positive at iteration 3866 must not be read as a recovery.

The corrected reward budget supersedes the estimates of section 2.2, which normalised weights rather than measuring contributions. Per term series in `rewards.npy` and in the `Episode_Reward` tags are rates per second, so a per episode figure is the mean multiplied by `episode_length_s` of 20.0. Run A's final training budget, at iteration 10036, ranks `rew_lin_vel_xy` at plus 293.77, `pen_action_smoothness` at minus 169.74, `rew_ang_vel_z` at plus 108.41, `pen_action_rate` at minus 58.29, `pen_ang_vel_xy` at minus 56.89 and `pen_abad_deviation` at minus 33.54, with `feet_air_time` sixteenth of seventeen at plus 0.53. This corrects the picture section 2.2 draws in one important respect. The jitter penalties are not weak, they are the second, fourth and fifth largest lines in the budget and together reach minus 284.92 against a tracking sum of plus 402.18, so RC-3's account applies to the gait shaping and support pattern terms and not to the smoothness and rate terms, which are already enormous and are being paid as an unavoidable tax rather than steering anything.

The tracking term is saturated, which reframes the failure. The linear tracking kernel evaluates to a mean of 0.9614 at the configured standard deviation of 0.4 on run A's own evaluation trajectory, against a logged realised value of 0.9634, so the standard deviation curriculum had barely moved and the policy earns 481.69 of a 500.00 ceiling while hopping on two legs, at a mean tracking error of 0.063 metres per second against a mean commanded speed of 0.692. The degenerate gait therefore costs the tracking objective essentially nothing. This is not a trade of gait quality for tracking accuracy, it is a gait the reward never priced, and the distinction matters because the remedy for the first is a reweighting and the remedy for the second is a term that does not exist.

RC-6 is refuted as stated and replaced. The curricula do not date the collapse, they begin far too early, `modify_push_force` first moving at iteration 95 and the three command velocity curricula and the terrain levels at iterations 191 and 192, all of them identical to the iteration between runs A and B and therefore threshold driven rather than weight driven. The return collapse therefore precedes the retraction by roughly six thousand iterations and cannot be caused by it.

RC-10 is added, the optimisation collapse, and it is the failure that arrives first. `Loss/entropy` is the diagonal Gaussian policy entropy in nats over twelve action dimensions, which the identity for such an entropy confirms against the logged pairs at three separate points of both runs to within five per cent. At the `entropy_coef` of 0.01 that `quadruped_rsl_rl_ppo_cfg.py:33` sets, the entropy bonus entering the loss at run A's final iteration is 0.215 against a concurrent `Loss/surrogate` of 0.0082, a ratio of 26.2, so over the last two thirds of the run the optimiser is maximising policy entropy rather than return. `Loss/learning_rate` is pinned near its floor of 1e-5 for effectively the whole run after a brief early spike, which is the adaptive Kullback Leibler scheduler's response to a policy it cannot stabilise, and the return never recovers across the nine thousand three hundred iterations following the peak. Section 2.2's table row recording an entropy coefficient of 0.005 for this robot is wrong, the configuration reads 0.01, matching the Go1 rather than halving it.

RC-11 is added, the perturbation ceiling, and it dates the retraction specifically. `Curriculum/modify_push_force` reaches its configured ceiling of 3.0 metres per second at iteration 7700 and stays pinned there, and the retraction first appears in the training video at iteration 8000, a gap of three hundred iterations. The reading this document adopts, and which phases 1 and 2 of section 5 are designed to separate, is that an already degraded high entropy policy under a maximal disturbance settles into the hop as a low dimensional attractor that is robust to being shoved and that keeps the saturated tracking term paid, and that nothing in the reward opposes it because nothing in the reward prices which feet are on the ground.

Two arithmetic conventions used above are recorded so that a later reader does not mistake them. One TensorBoard step is one policy optimisation iteration, and the video step counter advances by `num_steps_per_env` of twenty five per iteration (`quadruped_rsl_rl_ppo_cfg.py:18`), which the curriculum onset confirms independently, a `starting_step` of 2400 firing at iteration 96. And the per episode conversion is the logged rate multiplied by `episode_length_s` of 20.0, not by the thousand steps of an episode, a distinction worth twenty fold and one that inflates every penalty fiftyfold if taken the other way.

## 5. Remediation Programme

The first edition of this section proposed two experiments to take measurements that the workspace did not yet have. Those measurements have since been taken, from four independent surfaces, and this section is therefore rewritten as a remediation programme rather than as a diagnostic one. What follows assumes section 4.1, and in particular its two central conclusions, that the run suffers two separable failures which arrive in a fixed order, an optimisation collapse from iteration 707 and a support pattern collapse from iteration 8000, and that the configuration contains no term of any weight that prices which feet are on the ground.

Five phases are specified. Phases 1 and 2 are jointly necessary and neither is sufficient alone, phase 1 arresting the collapse that precedes the retraction and phase 2 forbidding the retraction itself, and they are therefore run as one experiment with two arms rather than in sequence. Phase 3 supplies the foot clearance term that the terrain generator has required since the quadruped was integrated and that no configuration has ever carried. Phase 4 shapes the support pattern positively, once phases 1 and 2 have established that a four beat gait survives at all. Phase 5 records the structural changes that remain, each of which invalidates comparison against every existing checkpoint and none of which should be attempted before the reward specification is settled.

Every change below obeys the change discipline of `CLAUDE.md`. Where a behaviour can be carried by an argument whose default reproduces the old behaviour it is carried that way and set only in the quadruped's own configuration, where it cannot the function is appended rather than edited, and no existing caller of any shared function in the `mdp` package changes, since SD_BRS1 and the TRON1 SF and PF tasks read the same module and an in place edit would silently alter their in flight and historical runs.

The baseline against which every phase is measured is the tree as it now stands, which carries `rew_lin_vel_xy` at 10 and `rew_ang_vel_z` at 5 (`base_env_cfg.py:1110-1119`), that is, the configuration of run B and not of run A. Run A's weights of 25 and 12.5 are history and no phase proposes returning to them.

### Phase 1, arrest the optimisation collapse and restore the reward budget

What is being asked. Does the run still lose ninety one per cent of its return when the entropy bonus no longer dominates the objective, when the perturbation curriculum stops at a magnitude the machine can absorb, and when the three oversized jitter penalties are returned to their reference values. This phase changes no reward function and adds no term, it changes weights and hyperparameters alone, which is why it comes first, a configuration whose optimiser is diverging cannot be used to evaluate a reward.

Change 1.1, the entropy coefficient. The measured mechanism is arithmetic. `Loss/entropy` is the diagonal Gaussian policy entropy in nats over twelve action dimensions, which the identity `H = 12(½ln 2πe + ln σ)` confirms against the logged pairs to within five per cent at three separate points of both runs, 9.111 predicted against 8.738 measured at run A's minimum and 22.622 against 21.496 at its final iteration. At `entropy_coef` of 0.01 (`quadruped_rsl_rl_ppo_cfg.py:33`) the entropy bonus entering the loss at run A's final iteration is 0.215, against a concurrent `Loss/surrogate` of 0.0082, a ratio of 26.2. The optimiser is therefore maximising entropy rather than return over the last two thirds of the run, which is precisely what a noise standard deviation climbing to 1.594 and still rising at the final logged point reports. Set the coefficient to 0.001, which is the figure Rudin and colleagues carry [1] and one tenth of the value transplanted from the biped.

```python
# environments/environments/tasks/locomotion/agents/quadruped_rsl_rl_ppo_cfg.py, in
# PFQuadrupedPPORunnerCfg.algorithm. The comment at that class's head records that the
# hyperparameters are deliberately identical to the biped's, and this change breaks that
# deliberately, so the comment must be amended in the same pass to record why.
entropy_coef = 0.001
```

Change 1.2, the perturbation ceiling. `modify_push_force` ramps toward 3.0 metres per second and reaches that ceiling at iteration 7700, and the retraction first appears in the training video at iteration 8000, a gap of three hundred iterations. A three metre per second impulse on a 16.077 kilogramme machine is a large disturbance, and Isaac Lab's own Go1 rough configuration disables the push entirely on the ground that a light robot on generated terrain is already sufficiently perturbed [2], a position the quadruped's own configuration already half accepts, its interval push having been halved against the biped's for that stated reason (`base_env_cfg.py:1088-1090`). Lower the ceiling to 1.0.

```python
# environments/environments/tasks/locomotion/cfg/quadruped/base_env_cfg.py, in CurriculumCfg
"max_velocity": (1.0, 1.0),   # was (3.0, 3.0)
```

Change 1.3, the three oversized jitter penalties. These are not weak, they are the second, fourth and fifth largest lines in run A's final budget at minus 169.74, minus 58.29 and minus 56.89 reward units per episode, together minus 284.92 against a tracking sum of plus 402.18. Their magnitude is a consequence of the pathology rather than a design choice, each having stood at roughly a third of that value at iteration 2509 while the gait was still healthy, but their weights are five to ten times the reference figures regardless. `pen_ang_vel_xy` at minus 0.5 stands against the minus 0.05 that Go1, Go2 and A1 all carry [2], `pen_action_rate` at minus 0.05 against their minus 0.01, and `pen_action_smoothness` at minus 0.075 against the minus 0.01 that HIMLoco [4] and the mixture of experts Go2 configuration [29] independently carry. Returning them to reference frees roughly two hundred reward units of budget for the terms that phases 2 and 4 add, and it is the single largest reallocation available without touching the tracking prize.

```python
pen_ang_vel_xy = RewTerm(func=mdp.ang_vel_xy_l2, weight=-0.05)          # was -0.5
pen_action_rate = RewTerm(func=mdp.action_rate_l2, weight=-0.01)        # was -0.05
pen_action_smoothness = RewTerm(func=mdp.ActionSmoothnessPenalty, weight=-0.01)  # was -0.075
```

Change 1.4, the abduction deviation weight. `pen_abad_deviation` at minus 2.0 is the largest penalty in the evaluation budget at minus 21.11 per episode, forty times the minus 0.05 that the mixture of experts configuration carries for exactly the purpose this term was written for, mitigating outward thigh abduction during rapid locomotion [29], and four times Extreme Parkour's minus 0.5 [7] which the term's own comment cites as its model (`base_env_cfg.py:1156-1158`). ### Phase 2, forbid the retraction by arithmetic rather than by threshold

What is being asked. Does a term that reads the running air time, and therefore accrues without bound against a limb that has stopped landing, prevent the collapse that every touchdown gated term in the surveyed literature is structurally unable to see.

The case for this phase is the strongest in the document, because it rests on a property of the sensor rather than on a tuning judgement. Isaac Lab's contact sensor exposes `last_air_time`, frozen at the previous touchdown (`IsaacLab/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/sensors/contact_sensor/contact_sensor_data.py:119`), and `current_air_time`, running since the last detach (`:128`). Every gait term surveyed across both passes of this investigation reads the former, this workspace's `feet_air_time` and `feet_air_time_v2` (`rewards.py:196,225`), its `unbalance_feet_air_time` (`rewards.py:522`), and Isaac Lab Spot's own `air_time_variance_penalty` [2]. A limb that never touches down triggers no `compute_first_contact`, contributes exactly zero, and is invisible to all of them. Worse, as section 4.1 establishes, `feet_air_time_v2` reports its healthiest values precisely when the retraction is total, because the two surviving feet are then cycling briskly and the two held feet contribute nothing rather than a penalty.

The clipping defect compounds this and is independent of it. Spot's `GaitReward`, the largest single term in its table at weight 10.0 and the only clock free quadruped construction the survey located that pairs the two diagonals explicitly, clips its squared errors at `max_err**2` before the exponential [2], so once two feet's air times diverge beyond 0.2 seconds the term stops distinguishing a moderate desynchronisation from an unbounded one, and a diagonal pair held aloft together, their air times large and roughly equal, can still score near its ceiling. This is the same defect, a clamp that removes the gradient exactly where a runaway should be punished more, that section 4 identifies in `feet_air_time`, now observed to recur independently in a published construction, and it is why phase 2 adopts an unclipped running quantity rather than adopting `GaitReward` alone.

Change 2.1, the anti hold term. Appended to `rewards.py`, no existing function edited.

```python
# environments/environments/tasks/locomotion/mdp/rewards.py, appended.
def feet_hold_penalty(
    env: ManagerBasedRLEnv,
    sensor_cfg: SceneEntityCfg,
    command_name: str,
    air_time_ceiling: float,
) -> torch.Tensor:
    """Penalise a foot held in the air beyond a ceiling.

    Unlike feet_air_time and feet_air_time_v2, which are evaluated only at touchdown and
    therefore pay a foot that never lands exactly zero, this term reads the RUNNING air
    time every step, so a retracted limb accrues without bound. Deliberately unclipped,
    since a clip would remove the gradient exactly where a runaway retraction should be
    punished more, which is the defect this term exists to avoid. Gated on the command
    norm so that a standing robot is not charged for holding still.
    """
    contact_sensor: ContactSensor = env.scene.sensors[sensor_cfg.name]
    current_air_time = contact_sensor.data.current_air_time[:, sensor_cfg.body_ids]
    excess = torch.clamp(current_air_time - air_time_ceiling, min=0.0)
    reward = torch.sum(excess, dim=1)
    reward *= torch.norm(env.command_manager.get_command(command_name)[:, :2], dim=1) > 0.1
    return reward
```

Change 2.2, the weight and the ceiling. The ceiling is set at 0.45 seconds, matching phase 1's `threshold_max` so that the two terms meet without a gap in which a foot is neither rewarded for a long swing nor charged for a hold. The weight is derived rather than chosen, and the derivation must account for the term accruing every step rather than at an event, which makes its episode integral quadratic in the duration of a hold. A limb retracted from the start of a twenty second episode accumulates an excess of `(20 - 0.45)^2 / 2`, that is 191.10 second seconds, so the weight sets the price of a total retraction directly. At minus 0.1 the term charges 19.11 reward units per episode for one retracted limb and 38.22 for two, which places the cost of run A's observed failure at roughly forty units, comparable with `pen_abad_deviation` at its phase 1 weight and two orders of magnitude above the 0.87 units by which the existing gait term separates that failure from an ideal trot. A four beat trot swinging at 0.25 seconds pays exactly zero, since no swing reaches the ceiling, and a limb retracted only for the final quarter of an episode pays 1.04, so the term is gentle on a transient and severe on a settled hold, which is the gradient shape wanted.

The quadratic growth is deliberate and is the property that distinguishes this construction, since a hold twice as long costs four times as much and no magnitude of retraction reaches a region where the gradient flattens. It carries one consequence to be recorded, that the term's magnitude scales with the square of `episode_length_s`, so the weight must be rederived rather than carried across if that constant ever changes.

```python
# environments/environments/tasks/locomotion/cfg/quadruped/base_env_cfg.py, in RewardsCfg

    func=mdp.feet_hold_penalty,
    weight=-0.1,
    params={
        "sensor_cfg": SceneEntityCfg("contact_forces", body_names="foot_.*_Link"),
        "command_name": "base_velocity",
        "air_time_ceiling": 0.45,
    },

```

Mechanism. The term is zero for any gait whose swings complete within 0.45 seconds, so it costs a healthy policy nothing and cannot distort a gait it does not forbid, and its per step charge grows linearly and without bound in the duration of a hold, so that its episode integral grows quadratically and no magnitude of retraction escapes it. This is the property no surveyed alternative has. If the retraction disappears under phase 1 and 2 together and the per foot duties read near one half on all four feet, the reward specification defect is closed. If the retraction persists at a weight of minus 0.1, the weight is raised rather than the construction abandoned, since the construction's correctness does not depend on the weight and only its authority does.

Expected outcome and cost. One further training run of the same length as phase 1's, run as a second arm under an identical seed so that the two are directly comparable. Two arms, phase 1 alone and phase 1 with phase 2, are the minimum that separates the budget hypothesis from the specification hypothesis, and running phase 2 without phase 1 is not proposed, since a diverging optimiser would confound it.

Literature link. The construction is the quadruped analogue of van Marum and colleagues' single foot contact term, adopted for the same stated reason, that a humanoid trained on velocity tracking with no explicit per foot contact term converges to two footed hopping [15]. It commits to no contact schedule, which preserves the objection that same work raises against the phase clock family [15][20], and it therefore remains compatible with the gait conditioning that phase 4 may later require.

### Phase 3, price the swing foot over the terrain the robot actually trains on

What is being asked. The quadruped has trained on a generator carrying 0.08 metre stairs, 0.3 slopes and 0.12 metre waves since it was integrated (`params/env.yaml:293-341`), and its reward set has never contained a term of any weight pricing the height a swinging foot reaches. This phase supplies one.

The survey establishes three facts that together settle the choice of form. First, none of the three clearance variants the workspace already carries is wired into the quadruped at all, their only callers being the TRON1 SF and PF and the KScale biped configurations, so this is a wiring exercise and not new mathematics. Second, `foot_clearance_reward` at `rewards.py:265-316` is exactly the bounded exponential and hyperbolic tangent form that Isaac Lab's Spot carries [2], with one addition of the workspace's own, an optional contact gate at `rewards.py:308-315` that closes the exploit of a grounded foot earning the reward by geometry alone, and it descends from the unbounded quadratic that HIMLoco carries at minus 0.01 [4]. Third, the sole geometry refinement of `foot_clearance_reward_v2` buys nothing on this robot, whose foot is a 0.022 metre sphere with no extended sole, and `foot_clearance_reward_v3` requires the gait phase clock that sits disabled in this configuration and that section 3.2 argues against on van Marum's authority [15]. Variant one, with its contact gate enabled, is therefore the correct choice, and it is the only one of the three that is both compatible with a clock free policy and not inert.

Change 3.1, wire the existing term. Spot's own parameters are adopted directly, `target_height` 0.10 metres, `std` 0.05, `tanh_mult` 2.0 and weight 0.5 [2], the target being appropriate against a 0.08 metre stair.

Defect recorded rather than repaired. ### Phase 4, shape the support pattern positively and price the power

What is being asked. Phases 1 to 3 remove the incentives to fail and add one term that forbids the specific failure observed. This phase adds the terms that positively describe the gait wanted, and it is run only once a four beat gait exists to shape.

Change 4.1, diagonal synchrony without a clock. Spot's `GaitReward` pairs `(FL, RR)` and `(FR, RL)`, which under this robot's naming are exactly the two diagonals of a trot, and rewards synchrony within a pair and opposition between pairs while reading only the contact sensor's own timers, committing to no phase clock [2]. The two feet run A retracts, FR and RL, are precisely one of those pairs. The term must be ported with its clip removed or widened, for the reason phase 2 gives, and it must therefore be written as a new function rather than transplanted, since the clipped form would score the observed failure near its ceiling. Retain the velocity gate at 0.5 metres per second so that a standing command earns nothing rather than being penalised.

Change 4.2, the power term the configuration has never carried. `joint_powers_l1` already exists at `rewards.py:513-518` in the exact L1 form that HIMLoco [4], DreamWaQ [5] and the mixture of experts Go2 configuration [29] all carry, and it is active on the TRON1 PF task and present but disabled on the SF task at minus 2e-5, the same figure all three published sources independently converge on. It is absent from the quadruped's reward set entirely. This is the term that section 3.2 identifies as the one that turns an undirected velocity objective into a family of biomechanically recognisable gaits [16], and its absence is why the two load bearing knees are permitted to draw 67.6 per cent of the machine's mechanical power. Wire it at minus 2e-5.

Change 4.3, duty balance across the four feet. Spot's `air_time_variance_penalty` at minus 1.0 [2], or the workspace's own `unbalance_feet_air_time` at `rewards.py:522-528` which computes the same variance, prices a foot resting far from the other three's cadence irrespective of which foot it is. Both read `last_air_time` and therefore share the touchdown blindness of phase 2's analysis, so this term complements `feet_hold_penalty` rather than replacing it, catching the partial desynchronisation that precedes a full retraction while the phase 2 term catches the retraction itself.

Mechanism and reading. The discriminating measurement throughout is the per foot stance duty and the feet grounded histogram of section 4.1, taken from a fresh evaluation dump. A four beat trot reads approximately 0.5 on all four feet with the histogram concentrated on two and three feet grounded, against run A's 0.425, 0.001, 0.000, 0.524 and its 46.7 per cent at zero feet grounded. The secondary readings are total mechanical power, which should fall well below run A's 123.9 watts, and the knee to hip power ratio, which stands at 2.36 in run A against 1.67 in run B and which a healthy gait should bring near unity.

### Phase 5, deferred structural changes

Four changes are named here so that their scope is recorded rather than rediscovered, and none should be attempted before phases 1 and 2 have returned, since each invalidates comparison against every existing checkpoint.

The first is promoting the height scan from the critic group to the policy group, which addresses RC-5 directly and which phase 3 has just given a second reason to want, since a terrain referenced clearance term and a terrain blind actor are a poor pairing. It changes the observation dimension and therefore admits no warm start.

The second is the positive reward clip of Rudin and colleagues [1], which every legged_gym descendant carrying large penalty weights sets and which Isaac Lab does not provide [2]. Phase 1's reallocation reduces the urgency by cutting the penalty stack roughly in half, but the arithmetic under which an accumulating penalty makes early termination preferable remains available in principle, and the measured base contact termination rate rising from 0.05 to 0.205 across run A is consistent with it. Implementing it requires a change in the runner rather than in the configuration.

The third is the episode initialisation, RC-7, every reset sampling up to 0.5 metres per second and radians per second on all six base axes where the Go1 zeroes all of it [2].

The fourth is the experiment name. Both runs under study are filed under `quadruped_flat` while training the rough terrain generator, a defect of `quadruped_rsl_rl_ppo_cfg.py:20` that will misdirect any later reader of the log tree, and that this document has now had to caveat in three separate places.

### What this programme does not attempt

Two things are deliberately excluded. No reference motion, adversarial motion prior or gait phase clock is proposed, since each supplies a preference over gait shape at the cost of a commitment this robot's disturbance recovery would then carry [15][20][24][25], and the evidence does not yet show that the clock free constructions of phases 2 and 4 are insufficient. And no further reduction of the tracking weights is proposed. The measured linear tracking kernel evaluates to 0.9614 at the configured standard deviation of 0.4 on run A's own trajectory, earning 481.69 of a 500.00 ceiling while hopping on two legs, so the term is saturated and its local gradient is near zero, which means the gait failure is not bought with tracking accuracy and cannot be unbought by making tracking cheaper. What the saturation does suggest, and what phase 1 does not act on but a later phase might, is that sharpening the kernel would restore a gradient the term has lost, the same trajectory scoring 0.9243 at a standard deviation of 0.2 and 0.7980 at the curriculum's floor of 0.09.

The first is that the gait shaping term is defective under both of the configurations it has been run in, independently of whether it is the cause of the observed gait. Under the earlier `threshold_min` of 0.25 it paid a penalty at every one of 17267 logged iterations of run `2026-08-25_10-15-10` and never once entered its rewarding region, and under the present `threshold_min` of 0.0 it has become a bounty on airborne duty fraction that separates the observed hop from an ideal trot by 0.14 per cent of the return, as section 4.1 computes. ## 7. Conclusion

The quadruped's degenerate gait is not explained by its actuators. Every actuator quantity computed against the Unitree Go1, natural frequency, damping ratio, torque margin under two legged support and the marginal cost of that support under the torque penalty, comes out close between the two machines or favours the Go1, and the heavy leg fraction that looked most likely to matter confers less centre of mass authority than the Go1's lighter and more distally distributed legs. The prior belief that framed this investigation is therefore upheld by measurement rather than merely accepted. What morphology does contribute is permission rather than motive, a fore aft stance 0.748 of leg length against the Go1's 0.981 and wider abduction and knee ranges, which lower the barrier to an atypical support pattern without giving the policy any reason to seek one.

The reason is in the reward, and it is composite. One term pays for the behaviour, the touchdown gated air time term charging every landing that falls short of a 0.25 second floor and charging a retracted limb nothing, a term measured negative across the entire run and never once positive. No term prices the support pattern, so nothing opposes that payment. The three penalties that would price the consequences of a bouncing gait are carried at six per cent of the reference machine's relative strength, and the measured root mean square vertical velocity of 1.29 metres per second is the direct evidence that they are not being felt. And the whole budget sits on an unclipped signed sum in a framework that provides no positive reward clip, having been derived from a lineage whose weights presuppose one.

The measurement that most sharpens the diagnosis is the one that contradicts the natural reading of the video. The trunk holds at 5.8 to 10.2 degrees of tilt throughout training, which excludes a sustained rear onto the hind pair and places the failure in the hopping family rather than the rearing family, a reading independently supported by the literature on deliberate quadruped bipedalism, which finds a clean rear to require purpose built balance machinery that this run does not have. The failure is a collapse of the support pattern under a level trunk, accompanied by violent vertical motion and, from iteration 5200 onward, by a monotone two thirds erosion of the return coincident with the onset of the push and command curricula.

What this document does not yet establish is which feet bear load and from which iteration, every quantity in section 4 having been inferred from aggregate reward series rather than observed directly. Phase 1 of section 5 supplies that measurement at the cost of a profile registration and five checkpoint dumps, no retraining required, and its outcome selects between the two reward hypotheses that phase 2 then separates. Until phase 1 has run, no change should be made to the configuration, since a configuration whose failure has not been located is a configuration whose repair cannot be verified.
